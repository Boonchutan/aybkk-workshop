# Gervais: the moves, in his own words

Quotes below were checked against the named source in September 2026. Where the exact wording was not seen in the source, the quote is marked "reported".

## On the target

- "Offence often occurs when people mistake the subject of a joke with the actual target. They're not always the same." — his own tweet, 2017.
- "Do I pander to the 200 privileged egos in the room, or do I try and entertain 200 million people at home who aren't winning awards? I try and play the outsider." — Golden Globes 2020 opening, as transcribed by The Hollywood Reporter.
- "I'm very careful about my jokes. I factually check them. I never want to lie about someone to get a laugh." and "Stupidity mixed with arrogance is even funnier." — The Talks interview, 2016.
- "I can't laugh with people I don't like. I think you should never be above the audience." — Big Think, "The Principles of Comedy", 2011.

What it means for Boonchu: the joke is on the powerful, the pompous, the fashionable, or on himself. The subject can be anything, including a beginner's wobble. The target is never the beginner.

## On the flat face and sincerity

- "We tease our friends. We use sarcasm as a shield and a weapon. We avoid sincerity until it's absolutely necessary." — TIME essay, "The Difference Between American and British Humour", 2011.
- "Our brashness and swagger is laden with equal portions of self-deprecation. This is our license to hand it out." — same essay.

What it means: mock yourself first, then you are allowed to mock the industry. Keep the face flat. Let the sincere line arrive late, and let it be the point.

## On taboo and the sincere core

- "I deal in taboo subjects because I want to take the audience to a place it hasn't been before, even for a split second." — BBC The One Show, 2023, as quoted by TODAY.
- "I think you can get away with anything because real life is worse in many ways ... people are crying out for taboo subjects dealt with sensitively." — Gold Derby interview on After Life, 2020.
- "It's almost an inoculation to real life." — CBC Radio q with Tom Power, 2020.
- "It doesn't get lighter, but you get better at carrying it." — on grief, Deadline, January 2022.
- "He was Scrooge, and kindness made him realize he was wrong. But of course, Christmas Carol ends on Christmas Day. What happens on Boxing Day?" — on After Life's structure, Deadline, June 2019.

What it means: the sincere line under the joke is not decoration. It is the reason the joke was allowed. For Boonchu the sacred floor is the practice and the lineage.

## On voice and starting late

- "To do comedy, I think you need to find a voice, and probably a discontented one. With me, it was getting fat and approaching middle age." — Harvard Business Review, 2011.
- "If you're not doing that, you're not really a comedian, you're a clown." — same interview, on making people think, not only laugh.

What it means: a teacher starting video at fifty, with twenty years of things he has noticed and not said, has exactly the voice Gervais says comedy needs.

## The seven craft cards (from Boonchu's study deck, topic "Sarcasm That Does Not Cost You")

1. **Only make fun of ideas that are too big for their boots.** Punch up, never down. In yoga there is a lot to punch up at: the fancy words, teachers who say practice is magic, people selling calm. A student who cannot touch their toes is never a target.
2. **Say the joke like it is the weather.** Deadpan means a plain face and no wait for the laugh. Bruno (Vibe Samurai) does it in every reel: the joke is said the same way as the serious line before it. When the speaker laughs first, the joke dies.
3. **Two voices.** Say the silly thing in its voice, then the true thing in yours. The gap is the joke. "My practice is a journey. (pause) It is a mat, a room, and six in the morning."
4. **Laugh at yourself first, and nobody can use it against you.** Habits, mistakes, bad days. Never the thing that gives you the right to teach. The lineage is not a joke. The morning coffee is.
5. **The honest thing, said plainly, is the joke.** Most of Gervais is not wordplay. It is saying the thing everyone thinks. The test: is it true, and would a ten-year practitioner nod?
6. **Something must stay sacred.** Gervais is deadly serious about comedy itself. Bruno never jokes about the idea underneath. Boonchu's floor is the practice and the lineage. If everything is a joke, nothing is funny and nobody trusts you.
7. **No swearing, no cruelty, and the face-to-face test.** Swear words get reels hidden on Chinese apps and sound cheap to the serious practitioner. Would you say this line to that person, in your shala, to their face? If not, cut it.

## Where Gervais and Bruno meet

Both deliver jokes with a flat face and no change of voice, and both keep a sincere thesis under the jokes. Bruno's closes are often a joke on himself that calls back the first laugh. That is the register Boonchu is aiming for: sincere by default, dry when it counts, never at the expense of the floor.

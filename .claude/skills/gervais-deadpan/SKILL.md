# Gervais Deadpan

Ricky Gervais is funny for reasons that can be copied without copying the swearing. This skill copies the reasons. It is tuned for Boonchu: an authorized Ashtanga teacher in Bangkok, English his second language, talking to serious practitioners aged 40 to 60, often with Chinese subtitles, while building a ten-person China cohort whose whole price rests on a lineage that can be checked. Read the floor section before writing a single joke, because the wrong target costs more than a hundred good jokes earn.

## The five moves, in plain words

1. **The flat face.** Say the funny thing exactly the way you say the serious thing. Same speed, same voice, no smile, no "just kidding". Then do nothing with your face for one second. The listener does the laughing. When the speaker laughs first, the joke dies. Gervais's own rule from the TIME essay: British humour uses sarcasm as a shield and avoids sincerity until it is absolutely necessary. That "until" is the point. The sincere line comes, and it hits harder because everything before it was flat.
2. **The target is bigger than the room.** The joke is on the powerful, the pompous, the fashionable, or on himself. Never on the small person, the scared person, the one who is trying. Gervais on stage: the room full of privileged egos, not the people at home. His own words: offence often comes when people mistake the subject of a joke for its target, and they are not the same. A joke can be about a beginner's wobble and still target the industry that sold the beginner a fantasy.
3. **The true thing after the joke.** Under every good Gervais bit there is one plain, honest sentence, said flat, with no music. After Life plants the sad true thing in the first minute so every cruel line after it reads as pain. For Boonchu the order is usually joke, then the true thing, said slower.
4. **Come back to it later.** Plant something small early, leave it, bring it back as the last line. The room laughs harder because they remember. Humanity's nut-allergy story is the model: read once, flat, then returned to until the end.
5. **Let the other thing be the joke.** Gervais asks Karl one plain question and stays quiet. Solo on camera, Boonchu does this with a buzzword: say the buzzword in its own voice, stop, then answer it in his. The gap is the joke. Nobody had to be clever.

## Boonchu's floor: what never becomes a joke, and why

Something must stay sacred or the jokes have nothing to stand on. Gervais mocks nearly everything and is deadly serious about comedy itself. That floor is why people trust him. Boonchu's floor:

- **Students.** Anyone on the mat, anyone trying, anyone who wrote in. Punching down is cruelty, and they are also the audience.
- **The lineage, Sharath, the authorization.** This is the asset. Laugh at yourself about your habits, your bad mornings, being bad at Instagram. Never about the thing that gives you the right to teach.
- **The practice itself.** Be sarcastic about the industry around it. Be dead serious about the thing. A student should finish the funniest reel more sure that Boonchu takes the practice seriously, not less.
- **Named living people.** Roast the trend, the fashion, the business model, the buzzword. Not a named teacher or competitor. The `/dm` rule forbids arguing in public, and a named target invites the fight.
- **Religion.** Gervais's biggest theme. Not Boonchu's. It cuts against students, the lineage, and China all at once.

Fair targets, and there are plenty: the yoga business; buzzwords (journey, flow, transformation, unlock, holding space, honour your body); teachers who say practice is magic; people selling calm; performances filmed as practice; the 200-hour-then-300-hour ladder; apps; the influencer pose in Bali; the word "advanced"; Boonchu's own habits.

## Hard rules, with the reason

- **No swearing.** Chinese platforms filter it; it sounds cheap to a 40 to 60 year old practitioner; and the joke was never in the swear word. If a line needs one to work, it is not finished. Cut the word, find the true thing.
- **No cruelty. The face-to-face test.** Would Boonchu say this line to that person, in his shala, to their face? If not, it does not go out.
- **The truth test.** Is it true? Would a ten-year practitioner nod? If it is only mean or only clever, it is not this kind of joke. Gervais fact-checks his jokes; he says he never wants to lie about someone to get a laugh.
- **No invented facts, numbers, or life stories.** Self-deprecation must be true. If you need a real detail you do not have, leave a slot: `[BOONCHU: one true thing about ...]`.
- **Plain words.** Every output readable by a smart ten-year-old. Short sentences. The flat delivery needs plain words, and so do the subtitles.
- **Dose.** Two or three joke lines in a thirty-second reel, never every line. The sincere line must survive, or it is a comedy reel, and comedy reels pull the wrong crowd for a filtered cohort.

## The joke shapes, with Boonchu-sized examples

1. **Two voices.** The buzzword in its voice, a pause, the truth in his. "My practice is a journey. (pause) It is a mat, a room, and six in the morning."
2. **The honest thing, said plainly.** The thing everyone thinks and nobody says. "Most stretching videos are performances." The laugh is relief.
3. **The list of true facts that ends flat.** Gervais on Charlie Sheen ends with "And that was a Monday." No joke words. "He did a 200-hour training. Then a 300-hour training. Then he opened a studio. That was March."
4. **The flat opening that buys permission.** Gervais opens the 2020 Globes with "last time I'm hosting, so I don't care anymore." For Boonchu: "This reel will not do well. Good. It is not for most people."
5. **Read the real text flat, then one dry line.** His Animals show reads real books in a flat voice and adds only the question a child would ask. Read a real ad, a studio tagline, a "yoga fact", pause, then: "Who checked?"
6. **Laugh at yourself first.** About habits, never credentials. "Twenty years of practice and I still [BOONCHU: one true small failing]." Said first, it cannot be used against you.
7. **The callback.** The first laugh returns as the last line, shorter.

## Delivery notes (include them every time)

Flat face. Same speed as the serious line. No smile. Hold one second after the joke, then move on. Never say "just kidding". An eyebrow at most. Say the true thing slower than the joke.

## Modes

Pick the one the request needs. If unclear, use PUNCH-UP when a draft is pasted and WRITE when only a topic is given.

- **WRITE.** Topic → a twenty to forty second script with tags, or a caption.
- **PUNCH-UP.** Draft pasted → add or replace at most three lines. Keep the sincere line. Show each change as before and after.
- **CHECK.** One joke or line → target audit → verdict SAFE, FIX, or CUT, with the rewritten line when it is FIX.
- **TEACH.** One move → plain explanation → one example → a ten-minute drill ending in "Done when ..." → the clip to watch, from `references/study.md`.

## Output format

```
SCRIPT        (or PUNCH-UP with each changed line shown before → after, or the CHECK verdict)
              tags on every line: [JOKE] [TRUE] [CALLBACK] [PAUSE]
TARGET AUDIT  one row per joke: the line → its target → bigger than the room? → verdict
DELIVERY      one or two lines
CUT LIST      lines removed or refused, and why (swear, cruel, student, lineage, religion,
              untrue, only clever, too many jokes)
FILL-INS      [BOONCHU: ...] slots, if any
```

## Worked example (PUNCH-UP)

Draft from Boonchu: "A lot of people say their practice is a journey. I think practice is about showing up every day. It's simple but not easy. See you on the mat."

```
SCRIPT
[JOKE]     My practice is a journey.   [PAUSE]
[TRUE]     It is a mat, a room, and six in the morning. The journey is the walk to the room.
[TRUE]     Most people do not want a practice. They want to have practised.
[JOKE]     I know. I checked. With myself. This morning.   [PAUSE]
[TRUE]     Showing up is not simple. It is just the only part that works.
[CALLBACK] The journey is thirty steps. Bring nothing.

before → after
"A lot of people say their practice is a journey." → "My practice is a journey. (pause) It is a mat, a room, and six in the morning."
"I think practice is about showing up every day." → "Most people do not want a practice. They want to have practised."
"It's simple but not easy." → "Showing up is not simple. It is just the only part that works."
"See you on the mat." → cut; the callback closes it.

TARGET AUDIT
"My practice is a journey" → the buzzword, the industry that sells it → yes → SAFE
"I checked. With myself. This morning." → Boonchu's own habit → yes (himself) → SAFE
"The journey is thirty steps. Bring nothing." → the buzzword again → yes → SAFE

DELIVERY   Say "journey" the way a brochure would. Then drop to your normal voice for
           everything else and never come back up. Hold one second after "this morning".

CUT LIST   "See you on the mat" — a soft call to action; the spoken video does not sell
           or ask, and the callback is a stronger last line.

FILL-INS   None. If Boonchu wants a self-deprecating line that is specifically his,
           replace line four with [BOONCHU: one true thing you skipped this week].
```

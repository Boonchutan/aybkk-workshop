# Brief: 20 quotes, "physical exercise is the beginning of brain development" (10 Nov 2026)

## Who and why
Boonchu, owner of AYBKK (Ashtanga Yoga Bangkok), asked: "research 20 quotes about.. how physical exercise is the beginning
of the brain development." He turns quote research into Instagram carousels and Reels. Audiences: English, Thai, Chinese, Russian.
Readers: adults who practise or want to start. The message he sells: a daily physical practice (same practice, same time, with a teacher).

## The theme (strict)
Each quote must say, in its own words, that MOVEMENT / PHYSICAL EXERCISE / THE BODY COMES FIRST for the mind or brain:
- the brain grows, learns or develops through movement (children, adults, old age);
- the brain exists for movement; thinking starts in the body;
- training the body is the first step of training the mind (old philosophers, educators, yoga texts);
- a measured finding that exercise changes the brain (a real paper).
"Exercise is healthy" alone is NOT enough. "Walking gives me ideas" is weak but allowed for at most 2 of the final 20.

## Fact rules (strict: the owner's hard rule is "never invent quotes; fact-check every quote; label paraphrases")
- A quote counts ONLY if you opened the PRIMARY source yourself (the book page, the paper, the lecture transcript, the original-language
  text) and copied the exact words. Quote sites (BrainyQuote, Goodreads, AZQuotes, Pinterest, blogs) are never proof.
  If only a secondary source exists, mark status "secondary" and say which.
- Give: exact words; author with one plain line on who they were; work, year, chapter/section/page; translator and edition for translations;
  the original-language text when the original is not English (Greek, Latin, Sanskrit, Chinese, Russian, German, French, Italian, Japanese, Thai);
  the URL you opened; 2 to 3 sentences of context (what the passage is about) so nobody twists it.
- Famous misattributions must be caught (e.g. lines given to Plato, Hippocrates, Einstein, Aristotle with no source). If the popular wording
  differs from the real text, give the real text.
- For a study: quote a sentence from the paper itself (abstract or body), give authors, journal, year, DOI, sample (who, how many, how long),
  and the exact number with its unit. Say "in this study", never a general promise. No number without its source line.
- A paraphrase is allowed only when the exact words are too long or not in English; label it "retold" and still give the exact source words.

## Safety (the business runs in China, Russia and Thailand)
OUT: politicians and government leaders of any country and time (no presidents, prime ministers, chairmen, ministers, kings, emperors),
royalty, war, religious conflict, anything that mocks a god, a sage or a religion. Careful and respectful with the Buddha.
No medical promise (never "exercise prevents/cures dementia, depression, ADHD"). Pick study lines that describe what was measured.
Avoid authors whose name itself is a scandal today. Avoid living authors only if the line cannot be checked in their own book or talk.

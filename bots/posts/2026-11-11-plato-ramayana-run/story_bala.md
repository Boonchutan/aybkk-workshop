## The 8 picks (story order)

### 1. Valmiki : the sentence he could not stop looking at
- Who: the forest sage who becomes the first poet. Here he is only a teacher walking to the river with one student, Bharadvaja.
- Where: 1.2.9-18 (birds, the sentence, he measures it), 1.2.22-38 (Brahma), 1.2.39-41 (students repeat it). Griffith Book I, Canto II "Brahma's Visit".
- Moment: he goes to bathe in the river Tamasa. Two water birds are calling together. A hidden hunter's arrow takes one, the other cries.
  Pity bursts out of him as one angry sentence at the hunter. FORK: walk on and bathe, or stop and look at what just came out of his own mouth.
  He stops (1.2.16). He measures it: four parts, the same number of syllables in each (1.2.18). He is still turning it over at home when Brahma the creator
  comes and sits in front of him (1.2.27-29). Brahma smiles (1.2.30): that came by my wish, now tell all of Rama's story in that shape.
  Then the students sing the one verse "time and again" and by being repeated it becomes a real verse (1.2.39-40). Grief (shoka) became verse (shloka).
- source_words (Rao, 1.2.16 and 1.2.40, 49 words): "Thus speaking and pondering in his heart, he became thoughtful: "What is this utterance by me in grief and anguish
  for this bird?"" ... "Equally lettered, four-footed is that verse when great Sage Valmiki articulated it, and when repetitively recited by one and all,
  it attained prominence as verse proper."
- Griffith (second witness): "He pondered long upon the speech, / Recalled the words and measured each," and
  "There sate before Válmíki's eyes / The Father of the earth and skies; / But still the hermit's thoughts were bent / On one thing only, all intent / On that poor curlew's mournful fate"
- Surprise: the poem began with one sentence the poet said in anger and was shaken by, not with a plan. With the creator god as his guest his mind was still on the bird.
  It became "verse proper" only after the students repeated it.
- Danger: a hidden hunter, one bird falls, the other cries. Inner danger: a holy man has just spoken in anger.
- Practice: he looked closer at the SAME sentence (fits "curiosity = look closer", never "ask why"); repetition by the students made it a form. Same asana again tomorrow.
- Not in Valmiki: the robber Ratnakara, "mara mara", the anthill (later: Adhyatma Ramayana 2.6.64-92 per the Indology list; often credited to the Skanda Purana, not checked).
  Bird: krauncha. Rao and Griffith say "curlews", many modern writers say sarus crane. Say "two water birds".
- Safety: REJECTED on 9 Nov for a Krounchasana script (bots/posts/2026-11-09-plato-hidden-stories-harvard/research_inter.md, lines 51 and 287: killing with blood + a curse).
  Under the 11 Nov brief: the death in one gentle line, no blood (cut 1.2.11-12), the curse told as "angry words" (it kills nobody). Say "a hunter" (not "outcast", "tribal"). Owner's call.

### 2. Dasharatha : the promise he made before he heard the wish
- Who: Rama's father, who waited very long for a son. Tell him as a father, not as a king.
- Where: 1.18.38-59 (arrival, the open promise), 1.19 (the wish, he faints), 1.20 (he refuses; Ravana named 1.20.16-20), 1.21 (anger, Vasishtha), 1.22.1-6 (hands him over),
  1.22.24 and 1.23.2 (grass bed, dawn call). Griffith XX-XXIV.
- Moment: Vishvamitra at the door. Dasharatha promises first (1.18.52-58; Griffith: "Without reserve I grant it now"). The wish: Rama for ten nights,
  to guard the fire rite from Maricha and Subahu (1.19.4-18). He faints (1.19.20-21). FORK: his word or his boy. He says no: not yet sixteen (1.20.2); take me and my army (1.20.3-10);
  and when he hears Ravana is behind them: "I myself am not capable of standing against that evil minded Ravana" (1.20.20). Vishvamitra: you promised and now go back on it;
  "I wish to go away as I have come" (1.21.2-3). The earth trembles (1.21.4). Vasishtha: keep your word; he can stop them himself, he came for your son's good (1.21.21).
  Dasharatha agrees "gladly and wholeheartedly" (1.21.22), kisses Rama's forehead, hands him over; Lakshmana follows (1.22.1-6).
  Tail for the payoff: first night on a grass bed made pleasant by the teacher's kind words (1.22.24); at dawn "get up oh, tigerly-man, eastern aurora is emerging" (1.23.2).
- source_words (Rao, 1.20.2 and 1.21.21, 49 words): "Less than sixteen years of age is my lotus-eyed Rama, and I see no warring aptitude to him with the demons." ...
  "This son of Kushi, Vishvamitra, can as well control those demons by himself, but intending to accord beneficence to your son he approached you and imploring upon you."
- Griffith: "Without reserve I grant it now: / My deity, O Lord, art thou." ; refrain "Ráma, my child, thou must not take." ; "Didst thou, O King, a promise make, / And wishest now thy word to break?"
- Surprise: the teacher did not need the boy. He asked for the boy's own good. A promise given before the wish is heard is the same thing that breaks this father later (Ayodhya Kanda).
  Ravana is named here, long before Sita enters the story.
- Danger: two shape-changing demons who ruin the sacred fire, Ravana behind them, an angry sage at the door, the earth shaking, a father who faints.
- Practice: keeping your word on the day it costs something. The teacher takes the student out of the easy house for the student's good, and the first lesson is small: sleep on grass, get up at first light.
- Differences: age "Less than sixteen" (Rao) / "This year he will be just sixteen" (Griffith) / "less than twelve" (Maricha's memory, Aranya 3.38.6). Say "not yet sixteen".
  Father's age: sixty thousand years (Rao 1.20.10) / "Nine thousand circling years" (Griffith). Give no number.
  1.21.21 ("can as well control those demons by himself") is in Rao's Sanskrit (teSaam nigrahaNe shaktaH svayam) but NOT in Griffith's recension: cite Rao.
  1.23.2 is today the opening verse of the Venkatesa Suprabhatam, a 15th-century morning hymn (Wikipedia "Suprabhatam"): the hymn is not Valmiki, only that verse is.
- Safety: father and son, never throne. No battle. Do not make the father look weak. Vishvamitra and Vasishtha have older scripts (28 Sep; Arundhati; Bala 70) but not this scene.
  The Ayodhya section may also pick Dasharatha (the promise to Kaikeyi): use him once, or tell this as the first of his two promises.

### 3. The Astras : the weapons that fold their hands
- Who: the divine weapons Vishvamitra holds. In Valmiki they are persons: sons of Krishashva and Daksha's daughters, "a hundred", fifty and fifty (1.21.13-17).
- Where: 1.27 (given, 1.27.22-27) and 1.28.1-15 (the calling-back set). Griffith XXIX "The Celestial Arms", XXX "The Mysterious Powers".
- Moment: the morning after the Tataka forest. Vishvamitra faces east and gives Rama every weapon he holds (1.27.22). They come and stand before the boy in shining bodies:
  "here are your thralls" (1.27.25, word-by-word line). Rama "patted them with his palm" and says: stay in my mind (1.27.26-27).
  FORK: he is now "unassailable even for gods". Walk on, or learn the other half first? He asks for the calling-back too (1.28.2). A second set is taught.
  They too stand there, "Some are fire-like, some smoke-like, and some moon-like and some sun-like", palms joined: "what can we do for you" (1.28.11-13).
  He answers (1.28.14). They walk around him once and go (1.28.15).
- source_words (Rao, 1.28.2 and 1.28.14, 49 words): "Oh, godly sage, I have taken the missiles and became unassailable even for gods, but oh, eminent sage, I wish to know the
  annulment of missiles too." ... "While remaining in my memory you assist me as and when needed, and till then you may go away as you wish,"
- Griffith: "Now, best of saints, I long to gain / The powers that can these arms restrain." ; "Thy thralls, O Ráma, here we stand: / Command, we pray, thy faithful band" ;
  "Depart," he cried, "where each may list, / But when I call you to assist, / Be present to my mind with speed, / And aid me in the hour of need."
- Surprise: the weapons are alive and polite. And a boy just handed everything asks for the brake. Nothing is used in the whole scene.
- Danger: they have just left Tataka's forest and walk toward the demons; a boy holds powers that humble gods and, for one moment, no way to stop them.
- Practice: coming out is taught, same as going in. And "stay in my memory": in a Mysore room nobody calls the asanas out; the series lives in you and comes when called.
- Not here: no weapon is used in 27-28. "A weapon that cannot be called back" (Ashvatthama) is Mahabharata. Names differ by translator (mothers: Jaya and Suprabha in Rao, Jaya and Vijaya in Griffith).
- Safety: bright helpers who bow, no war, no use shown. Avoid the word "missile" (Rao's word). Low danger: open on the forest behind them.

### 4. Maricha : six nights, and the one Rama let live
- Who: a night-walking demon, son of Tataka (1.24.26, 1.25.9), sent with Subahu to spoil the fire rite (1.19.4-6; "instigated by" Ravana, 1.20.18-19). Later he becomes the golden deer.
  He was not born a demon: he was a yaksha woman's son "who by curse became a demon" (1.25.9), cursed by the sage Agastya when he and his mother rushed at the sage (1.25.10-12).
- Where: 1.30.1-26. Griffith XXXII. Echo: Aranya Kanda 3.38.16-22.
- Moment: the teacher begins his rite and goes silent (a vow). The other sages give the order (1.30.4). The boys guard "wakefully" (1.30.5). Five days: nothing.
  Day six: "you be prepared and be on alert" (1.30.7). The altar flares, a roar in the sky, two demons like storm clouds (1.30.8-12).
  FORK: which arrow. The Manava arrow hits Maricha in the chest and throws him "a hundred yojana lengths" into the ocean (1.30.18), "without taking away his life" (1.30.20).
  The rite is completed and the teacher speaks again (1.30.25-26).
- source_words (Rao, 1.30.4 and 1.30.20, 57 words): "you have to safeguard this ritual for six nights and days from today, and this Sage Vishvamitra who is under the vow of ritual
  goes into muteness as required by the vow." ... "Lakshmana, see the Cold arrow belonging to Maanava missile expounded by Manu which is taking him away on baffling,
  but without taking away his life."
- Griffith: "The youths began, disdaining sleep, / Six days and nights their watch to keep." ... "And, hurled a hundred leagues away, / In ocean's flood he fell." ...
  "Hath struck him senseless with the smart, / But left him breathing still."
- Surprise: nothing for five days, teacher silent, danger on the last day. And Maricha lives: years later he tells Ravana "That Rama did not wish to kill me then,
  and I am thus saved" (Aranya 3.38.20, Rao) and "such a boy, such a Rama spared me" (3.38.22). He is the one who becomes the golden deer (Aranya section has that source).
- Danger: night-walkers out of the sky like storm clouds, come to put out the sacred fire. Two boys on watch, and the teacher cannot speak.
- Practice: showing up on the days nothing happens. A silent teacher is still a teacher. Six days of watch and Ashtanga's six practice days are a number echo, not a source link. Say so.
- Careful: Subahu IS killed (1.30.22) and the rest driven off (1.30.23): leave out or one gentle line, never say "nobody was hurt".
  Griffith's hermits say "Till the fifth day be come and past" but the watch is six days and nights in both. The silence is a ritual vow, not a test of the boys.
  "I am reluctant to eliminate" (Rao 1.30.15) has no match in Griffith. "Rama spared him because of the deer to come" is a comment on the site, not the verse.
  Maricha (the demon) is NOT the sage Marichi of Marichyasana. Never link them.
- Safety: OUT the rain of blood on the altar (1.30.12-13): say "they came to put out the fire". No killing shown.

### 5. Ahalya : the woman nobody could see
- Who: wife of the sage Gautama, mother of Shatananda who is Janaka's priest (1.50.6, 1.51.1-2).
- Where: 1.48.11-16 and 1.48.29-33 (the hermitage, the curse), 1.49.11-22 (they enter; she is seen), 1.51.1-11 (her son). Griffith XLVIII-XLIX.
- Moment: near Mithila, an old hermitage, lovely, empty. Rama asks whose (1.48.11-12). [ONE SAFE LINE for what happened.] Her husband's words (1.48.29-30);
  when Rama comes it ends and I come back (1.48.31-32). He leaves for the Himalayas (1.48.33).
  FORK: an empty yard. Pass by, or go in. The teacher: "enter the hermitage" (1.49.11). She is there, bright from her tapas, "like the befogged and beclouded
  moonshine of a full moon" (1.49.13-15). The brothers bow to her feet (1.49.16-17). She gives them water and welcome (1.49.18). Flowers fall (1.49.19).
  Gautama returns (1.49.21). Her son: "my glorious mother ... Really!" (1.51.4-7).
- source_words (Rao, 1.48.29-30 and 1.49.16-17, 56 words): "you shall tarry here for many thousands of years to come without food and consuming air alone, and unseen by all beings
  you shall live on in this hermitage while contritely recumbent in dust." ... "On reaching the end of curse she came into the view of Raghava-s, and they too gladly touched her feet in reverence."
- Griffith: "Thy bed the ashes, air thy food, / Here shalt thou live in solitude. / This lonely grove thy home shall be, / And not an eye thy form shall see." and
  "The sons of Raghu clapped her feet;" (Gutenberg spelling).
- Surprise: NO STONE in Valmiki. She is unseen, not stone. And Rama does not touch her with his foot: the brothers bow to HER feet.
- Danger: thousands of years alone, unseen, lying in dust, eating air. A place even the sages have left.
- Practice: practice nobody sees still does its work (she comes out bright from it); it changes when someone notices. The teacher is the one who says go in and look.
- Not in Valmiki: turned to stone; freed by the touch or dust of Rama's foot. The site's own comment on the sarga 49 page: "This is not evident in Valmiki Ramayana.
  The idea of petrifaction of Ahalya is brought in from PadmaPuraana". Later tellings with the stone: Tulsidas, Kamban (secondary).
  Also NOT Valmiki: "she was tricked". In this text she knows who it is (1.48.19). So the safe line must not say "tricked".
- Safety: OUT Indra, the disguise, what happened, Indra's punishment (1.48.17-28, 1.49.1-10). One line only:
  "Something wrong happened in that house, and her husband, a great sage, was very angry." No judging words. Gautama is not cruel: he sets the end himself and comes back.

### 6. Janaka : the father who did not make the bow lighter
- Who: ruler of Mithila, a sage among kings, Sita's father. He found her as a baby in the earth.
- Where: 1.66.7-26, 1.67.1-27. Griffith LXVI-LXVII. Sita's own telling: Ayodhya 2.118.27-54 (K. M. K. Murthy).
- Moment: Janaka tells it himself. "when I was ploughing the ritual field then raised by the plough [from the furrow is a baby girl...]" (1.66.13-14, Rao; brackets are his).
  He raised her as his own. In his house: a bow of Shiva kept by his family. FORK (years ago): what to ask of the man who marries her. One bar: strength (1.66.14-15).
  Kings came, could not even move it (1.66.18-19). Angry, they camped round his city for a year until supplies ran low (1.66.20-23). He kept the bar.
  Now the bow comes in an "eight-wheeled coffer", "tugged by five thousand tall men" (1.67.3-4). Rama opens the lid and says to his teacher: "Now I wish to get the feel
  of this supreme bow" (1.67.13-14). "All Right!" say sage and father; he "playfully grabbed the bow" (1.67.15), strings it, draws, and it breaks in the middle (1.67.17),
  a sound like thunder, all fall except four: the teacher, Janaka and the two brothers (1.67.19). Janaka: my promise has come true (1.67.23).
- source_words (Griffith, Cantos LXVI, 55 words): "Once, as it chanced, I ploughed the ground, / When sudden, 'neath the share was found / An infant springing from the earth, /
  Named Sítá from her secret birth." ... "Not one of all the royal band / Could raise or take the bow in hand." ... "There, wasting all, a year they lay, / And brought my treasures to decay,"
- Griffith also: "Five thousand youths in number, all / Of manly strength and stature tall, / The ponderous eight-wheeled chest that held / The heavenly bow, with toil propelled."
- Surprise: no contest day with rival princes in the hall. The suitors failed long before. Rama only came to see the bow. Sita is not in the scene.
  And the father kept the test hard with an army round his walls for a year.
- Danger: angry suitors around the city for a year, food running low; a bow five thousand men must drag; when it breaks the earth shakes and the hall falls down.
- Practice: the bar stays, you grow to it; the next asana comes when this one is done. And he said what he wanted to try and waited for the yes.
- Not in Valmiki: Ravana trying the bow (folk and later tellings); child Sita lifting the bow; Rama and Sita meeting in the garden first; the garland (Tulsidas and regional).
  CAREFUL: do not say "no swayamvara in Valmiki". Sita uses the word herself: "I shall inaugurate a Svayamvara" (2.118.38), "in that Svayamvara" (2.118.54).
  What is true: "the princes offered salutation to it and went away" (2.118.43) and "After a very long time, this Rama ... came" (2.118.44).
  The two tellings differ: siege in Bala 66, a polite leaving in Ayodhya 118. "Up to his ear" (1.67.17) is Rao's bracket, not the verse.
- Safety: father, daughter, promise. The siege: one line or cut; cut the heaven-sent army (1.66.23-25). Cut Shiva's threat to the gods (1.66.9-10). The breaking told with awe, never as a win over Shiva.

### 7. Parashurama : the old champion who chose what to lose
- Who: the older Rama, "Rama of the Axe", son of the sage Jamadagni. He once won the whole earth and gave it to the sage Kashyapa.
- Where: 1.74.8-24, 1.75.1-28, 1.76.1-24. Griffith LXXIV-LXXVI.
- Moment: road home from the wedding. Birds cry; Vasishtha: something frightening comes but it can be eased (1.74.8-13). Wind, darkness, dust; the army stands stunned (1.74.13-16).
  Out of the dark: Rama of the Axe, with a second great bow. Dasharatha, palms joined: they are youngsters, give them safety (1.75.5-9). He is not even answered (1.75.10).
  The challenge: string this bow, then fight me (1.75.3-4, 26-27). Rama takes it from his hand, the arrow already on it (1.76.4-5): I will not send this at your life,
  but it cannot go back unused; "either this motility of yours at the speed of your mind, or even those unparalleled realms of heavens which you have earned
  by the power of your ascesis, I will eliminate whichever you wish" (1.76.6-8). FORK is the old man's. Slowly and softly (1.76.12): I gave the earth to Kashyapa
  and by his order I do not spend a night on it; leave me my going; take the worlds I won (1.76.13-16). "it is malapropos to say that this is a disgrace to me" (1.76.19).
  The arrow flies, the dark lifts, he walks round Rama in respect and goes to his mountain (1.76.21-24).
- source_words (Griffith, Canto LXXVI, 57 words): "Obedient to my guide's behest / On earth by night I never rest. / My choice is made, I will not dim / Mine honour and be false to him. /
  So, son of Raghu, leave me still / The power to wander where I will," ... "No shame is mine, I ween, for thou, / Lord of the Worlds, hast dimmed my brow."
- Rao for the same: "in my observance of my mentor Kashyapa's order I do not spend nights on this earth from then afterwards" (1.76.14).
- Surprise: the loser chooses his loss, and gives up what he earned to keep an old promise. Two Ramas in one scene. Losing "is no shame".
- Danger: dark and dust at midday, an army frozen, a father begging, the most feared fighter alive in the road, an arrow that cannot be put back.
- Practice: when you cannot keep both, what you collected or the daily promise? He kept the promise. For older practitioners: being passed by a younger one is no shame.
- Not in Valmiki: the court scene right after the bow breaks, with Lakshmana's sharp words (Tulsidas, Ramcharitmanas, per secondary sources). In Valmiki it is on the road and Lakshmana does not speak.
  Number of his wars: the Rao page prints "thirty-seven times" (1.75.24-25); tradition says twenty-one. Use no number.
- Safety: OUT his killing of the warrior clans and his father's murder (1.74.21-22, 1.75.23-26); OUT the Shiva and Vishnu contest over the two bows (1.75.14-20). Nobody is hurt in the scene.
  Say "the older Rama, Rama of the Axe". He leaves with honour. Never "King Rama".

### 8. Trishanku : the man who went to find a teacher who would say yes
- Who: an ancestor in Rama's own family line. A story inside the story: Shatananda tells it to Rama in Mithila, as part of how Vishvamitra became a great sage.
- Where: 1.57.10-22, 1.58.1-24, 1.59.1-5, 1.60.8-34. Griffith LVII-LX.
- Moment: he wants a rite to reach heaven in his own body. His teacher Vasishtha: impossible (1.57.11-13). FORK: accept, or look for a yes.
  He goes to the teacher's hundred sons (1.58.2). He says he will go elsewhere; overnight he loses his fine looks and his people leave him (1.58.8-12).
  Vishvamitra takes him in and promises (1.59.2-5). The gods do not come. Vishvamitra lifts him by his own tapas; he rises (1.60.12-16). Indra: fall, "with your head inversed" (1.60.17-18).
  He falls crying "save me". Vishvamitra: 'wait... wait...' (1.60.19-20) and he stops in mid sky. New stars are made in the south. The gods agree he stays there "upside-down" among them (1.60.30-32),
  because the sage says "I have promised ... and I am not interested to make it untrue" (1.60.27).
- source_words (Rao, 1.58.2 and 1.60.30-32, 43 words): "You slow-witted king, your veracious mentor has denounced you. But transgressing him how do you approach another branch of the same
  tree of wisdom, when that very tree rejected you?" ... "Trishanku will also remain in the circle of stars you created, but upside-down"
- Griffith: "Dost thou transgress his prudent rule, / And seek, for aid, another school?" and "And 'mid them hung, as one divine, / Head downward shall Triśanku shine;"
- Surprise: he is still hanging there, head down. He got what he asked for and not what he wanted. Both teachers kept their word.
- Danger: a man falling head-first out of the sky crying "save me"; before that he loses his looks and his people in one night.
- Practice: patience with a teacher's no or not yet. Going from teacher to teacher to collect a yes can leave you between two places.
- Not in Valmiki: that this is the Southern Cross (modern Indian star lists call Crux "Trishanku"; Valmiki only says new stars in the southern sky).
  "Trishanku's heaven" as a saying is later.
- Safety: OUT the curse into a low caste (1.58.8-11; never use Rao's words for it), OUT Vishvamitra's curse on Vasishtha's sons (1.59), soften the threat to make a new Indra.
  Do not make either sage look small. Theme is close to unfilmed Ep. 6 "The Teacher Who Said No": do not run them back to back. No "Trishanku" in bots/posts (grep).

## Bench (read, usable or ruled out, not in the 8)
- Rishyasringa (1.9.3-19, 1.10.1-33; Griffith IX-X). A boy raised in the forest who has never seen a town or a sweet. A land has no rain. The ministers are afraid of his father
  (1.9.16) so singers from the city are sent. "Having relished the sweets, that resplendent sage supposed them to be fruits only, for he did not taste sweets earlier" (1.10.21).
  Next day he goes back to find them (1.10.24-25). As he is brought into the land "the Rain-god quickly showered rain" (1.10.29). Why bench: the engine of the story is
  temptation by courtesans (1.10.20), weak fork. Safe only as "singers and sweets".
  Not in Valmiki: born of a deer, the horn (Mahabharata Vana Parva 110 ff., secondary). Shanta as Dasharatha's own daughter: the verse says Romapada's daughter (1.11.19).
- Tataka (1.24.25-32, 1.25, 1.26; Griffith XXVI-XXVIII). Once a beautiful yaksha girl with the strength of a thousand elephants (1.25.5-7); cursed into a monster after she
  rushed at the sage Agastya (1.25.10-13). Dust storm, rain of stones (1.26.15-16). Rama: "I do not really attempt to kill her" (1.26.12). Vishvamitra: enough pity, the sun is setting,
  "demons at dusk and afterwards become unassailable" (1.26.21-23). That day "that forest of Tataka too is released from the curse" (1.26.35).
  Why bench: gore in the middle (1.26.17-18) and it ends in the killing of a female figure with the teacher arguing for it. Riskiest of the section.
- Sumitra and the last share (1.16.11-32, 1.18.14, 1.18.28-33; Griffith XV, XIX). The father gives half to Kausalya, then shares to Sumitra and Kaikeyi,
  "Then thinking for a while gave the remaining ... again to queen Sumitra" (1.16.27-29). Two shares, twins: Lakshmana who attaches himself to Rama, Shatrughna to Bharata.
  Not in Valmiki: the bird that carries a share to Anjana; the two queens handing their shares over (Tulsidas). Why bench: no danger, small fork.
- NEW. The hundred daughters of Kushanabha (1.32.11-26, 1.33.1-25; told by Vishvamitra about his own family). The Wind asks to marry them. They say: ask our father.
  They could push him from his place by their tapas but hold back "to conserve our ascetic values" (1.32.20). He bends their bodies crooked (1.32.23).
  The father: "Forgiving is the duty of imperturbable and you have done it." (1.33.6). At their wedding, "By mere touch of hand ... their misshape" is gone (1.33.23).
  Strong body picture (bent, then straight) and a real fork (hit back or hold). Why bench: it shows the god Vayu, father of Hanuman, doing wrong to girls. Too risky under "never mock a god".
- NEW. Shunahshepa (1.61-62). UNSAFE: a boy sold by his parents as the offering for a rite, tied to the post; Vishvamitra curses his own sons. Good ending (two hymns free him) but not a bedtime story.
- Vishvamitra's first night and the dawn call (1.22.10-24, 1.23.1-4): folded into pick 2. Shatananda (1.51.1-11): folded into pick 5.
- The wedding (1.73): four brothers marry four girls of Janaka's house in one hour. No fork. Vasishtha's family-line speech (1.70) is already used.

# Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. ? · "Two Sounds in the Water" · Asana: Viranchyasana A (and B) (Advanced A, 3rd series, Sthira Bhaga A) · Lane B · ask word: aybkk

Writer's script, 9 Nov 2026. Story id C2 (key advA2). No episode number until it is filmed. Beat order H, DH, T1, RH, T2, P.

Lane B, and why: the point is the habit. Same practice, same seat, stop looking for the next method. That is the Habit Gap. The story gives no fix for an asana people are stuck in, and Viranchyasana is an Advanced A asana that almost no viewer is working on. Lane A needs a real technique fix and the STUCK ask, and this story has none. Hook #13 is tagged goal=trust and #60 goal=shala, both habit lines. So the caption asks for "aybkk" (keyword test, frozen until 22 Nov).

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If you keep changing method, you never stay on one long enough for it to work. Next year you are still looking. And you start again from day one.

Why care: Maybe you tried apps, programs and new teachers. Each one felt like the answer for two weeks. This story shows the first teacher of the whole world started with less than you have. Two sounds, and nobody to explain them. It was enough to begin, because he stayed on his seat.

## 3. Toolkit

Toolkit pick: /hook (contrarian lever, bank #13) + Hero's Journey (short form).
Why: The story is a small, clean hero's journey. A call (make the world), no map (two sounds, nobody there), a step he takes alone (he sits down), a long ordeal (a thousand years of the gods) and a new place at the end (now he is the greatest of all who practise). That is Boonchu's Fail, then Hero, then Payoff. The Harvard fork (#1) sits at the moment he finds nobody, so the viewer chooses before Brahma does. The other five scripts this week all run on Fork-First Story, so this one keeps the fork but changes the body tool.

Toolkit choice:
- Question-First ("Two sounds. Nobody to explain. What you do?" as line 1) + Fork-First Story. A strong fork, but a question opener is less contrarian than a flat claim, and it asks the fork before we know who is on the lotus.
- Vibe Samurai (flat statement "The first teacher of the world did not know how to begin.", credit the source, ladder of 3 (heard, searched, sat), turn, flip). Very clean, but the turn pulls toward the banned "not X but Y" line, and opening on "did not know" risks sounding like we laugh at Brahma.

## 4. Hook bank

Main hook, #13 [teach] fit=adapt goal=trust habit=9:
- template: "The complete guide to getting better at {skill}"
- aybkk: "The complete guide to getting better at Ashtanga: show up tomorrow"
- note: "A Reel cannot be 'complete', so turn it into a dry one-line answer that keeps the title honest."

How I adapted it:
- I kept the dry "complete guide" title and made the answer the story itself. "Complete guide to Ashtanga? Two sounds long." It flips the belief that you need a bigger, better method. It makes the viewer ask "which two sounds?" That loop is paid at lines 13 to 15.
- The bank's own answer ("show up tomorrow") is the same as our payoff ("your own seat"). That keeps the title honest, as the note asks.
- His voice: the article is dropped and it is a question, the way he talks. Said to camera, the same words on screen from frame 1, two fingers up on "two sounds".

Double hook, from #60 [warn] fit=adapt goal=shala habit=10:
- template: "You're wasting {number} hours a week on {thing}"
- aybkk: "You're wasting time every week deciding which workout to do today"
- note: "Drop the made-up number and the vague 'hours'; Mysore is the same practice every day, so there is nothing to choose."

How I adapted it:
- No number and no "wasting" (it scolds). It becomes a mirror in his voice: "Every year, new method. Right?"
- The bank's "every week" becomes "every year". A serious practitioner does not change method every week, so "week" sounds wrong to him. "Every year" sounds true. It also sets up the close, line 24 "This year, how many new method?".
- Then the stakes jump to the biggest teacher there is. "World's first teacher? Two sounds." It opens the loop "so what did he do?", paid at line 17 (he sits down) and line 19 (what he is now). The point is out by about 0:06.1.

Not used: #121 and #101 (Ep. 6).

## 5. Harvard tricks used

- #1 Decide before you know the ending: line 12, "What you do?", then a 1 s pause, eyes in the lens. He has heard two sounds, looked everywhere and found nobody. Line 17 answers it ("He sit down on his own seat.").
- #21 Teach them something about their own problem: lines 2 and 3 set it up, line 20 lands it. The viewer thinks the problem is the method. The first teacher of the world had two sounds, and it was enough to begin because he stayed. Lines 24 and 25 hand the same insight back as a question.
- #27 Every claim needs a because: line 15 is the because for the hook's claim ("two sounds long"): "That's all the voice say" (2.9.6 to 2.9.7). Line 19 gives the because for his title ("So now he is...", the text's own "thus", 2.9.8). Line 20 gives the because for "enough" ("because he stay").
- #30 Sell only the next step: the spoken video sells nothing. No price, no course, no link line. The caption has ONE ask (comment aybkk).
Not used: #2 (no result is promised to the viewer) and #20 (line 16 has a cost inside the story, but there is no cost question to the viewer).

## 6. The script

Timing: the story (lines 4 to 19) is in the bedtime voice, timed at 2.6 spoken words a second. The adult lines (1 to 3 and 20 to 25) are timed at 2.8. Fixed beats are added on top (see part 7). Story pictures go over his head as in Ep. 5. Pictures never show Brahma's face (Thai and Chinese viewers pray to Phra Phrom). Use a lotus on dark water, soft light from four sides, a small figure seen from behind (he sits down only at line 17).

26. 0:57.4 [P] (P) แล้วเจอกันครับ
    (small subtitle: see you)

Story pattern: Fail (he wants to make the world and does not know how to begin, he gets two sounds and finds nobody), then Hero (he sits down on his own seat and practises a thousand years of the gods), then Payoff (now he is the greatest of all who practise, then the viewer's own seat).

One thread check: lines 1 to 3 talk to adults. Lines 4 to 19 tell the story straight, start to end, with no jumping out. "What you do?" stays in the bedtime voice. Lines 20 to 25 come back to the adults: the point, the asana, the close.

5-year-old test (lines 4 to 19 alone): Brahma is the first teacher of the whole world. His seat is a lotus flower. He wants to make the world. How to begin? In the water there is a voice. Ta pa. Ta pa. He looks around and nobody is there. What do you do? Put together, it is tapa. Tapa means heat, the hard practice. That is all the voice says. Nobody to explain. Only him, on the water. He sits down on his own seat, firm. He practises a thousand years of the gods. So now he is the greatest of all who practise. It makes sense alone. The question in line 8 (how to begin?) is answered inside the story (he sits down and practises), and line 16 no longer opens a new goal the story never pays.

Contrarian check: line 1 flips the belief that you need a bigger method (the guide is two sounds long). Line 2 says the viewer's own habit out loud. The close is two short questions, not an answer.

Retention check: one OL before 0:08 (line 3, plus "which two sounds?" from line 1). Rehooks at 0:18.4 (the whisper), 0:24.6 (the twist), 0:36.6 (the thousand years) and 0:47.3 (the asana shot, a new picture). The slow set-up (0:08 to 0:16) gets two new pictures, the push in on the lotus at 0:10.7 and the hard cut to the ring on the water at about 0:16. A silent beat before the adults come back (after line 19). Every loop is paid: line 1 at line 15, line 3 at lines 17 and 19, the fork at line 17. The loop in line 9 (whose voice?) is left open on purpose, because the book says he found nobody.

How it is timed: story lines 4 to 19 at 2.6 words a second, adult lines 1 to 3 and 20 to 25 at 2.8. If the take runs over 60 s, cut in this order:
1. "Right?" from line 2 (about 0.4 s).
2. Shorten the fork pause and the whisper gap to 0.6 s and 0.4 s (about 0.6 s).
3. Line 16 becomes "Nobody to explain. Only him." (about 1.2 s).
The old cut "He want to make the world. How?" is gone. "But" is already out, and "to begin" must stay for line 20's callback.
Never cut line 1 (the hook), line 3 (the loop), line 10 (the two sounds), line 12 (the fork), lines 13 to 15 (the twist), line 18 (the thousand years), line 19 (the title) or lines 24 and 25 (the close).

## 10. Flags

1. Respect for Brahma (Phra Phrom). Thai and Chinese viewers pray to him. Line 8 "How to begin?" is what 2.9.5 says, and nothing more. It is said softly. No joke anywhere is about him. The hook joke is about the "complete guide", not about Brahma. No face in any picture. Ask a Thai reader to watch the cut before posting.
2. The hook is a dry joke, and a purist can push on it. "Complete guide to Ashtanga? Two sounds long." means "the guide is tapa, do the practice". Ashtanga has more in it than that. Also, after 2.9.9 the same chapter goes on, and the Lord teaches Brahma more. I have not opened those later verses, so the script never says two sounds were all he ever learned. It says "That's all the voice say" (true for 2.9.6 to 2.9.7) and "enough to begin".
3. "First teacher of the whole world" joins two words in 2.9.5. "ādi-deva" means the first god, and "jagatāṁ paro guruḥ" means the supreme teacher of the worlds. The translation says "the first spiritual master, supreme in the universe". So "first teacher" is fair, but it is a merge.
4. The voice is never named. The verse says he found nobody. Only the commentary (purports to 2.9.6 to 2.9.8) says the Lord, or Krishna, spoke. The loop "whose voice?" stays open on purpose. If a viewer asks in the comments, answer with the verse: "the book says he looked everywhere and found nobody."
5. "Tapa" or "tapas". The MW headword is tapas. The verse gives only two syllables, ta and pa, and the purport writes "tapa". The meanings (heat, austerity, severe meditation) come from MW "tapas". The translation says "penance" every time. We never say "penance" or "punishment", only "heat, the hard practice".
6. "He practise" means tapa, the hard practice. It never means asanas. Never say Brahma did Viranchyasana or any asana. The name is the link, and the half lotus "seat" is our picture of his lotus seat. The text says neither.
7. "Sit" and "firm". "Firm" (line 17) is the translation's word "firmly". The Sanskrit "āsthāya" is not a strong word. MW gives ā-sthāya as "having recourse to, using, employing ... having ascended ... standing, standing by". The vedabase glosses of the same word differ by verse. In 2.9.7 it is "āsthāya — sit down", but in 2.9.5 it is "to find the source of it" ("could not trace out the source of his lotus seat"). So line 6 no longer says he sits. It says only that his seat is a lotus flower, which the gloss "his lotus seat" in both verses supports. The one sitting-down moment is line 17, from 2.9.7. It is fine as a retelling.
8. 2.9.9 is left out. The story ends on his title from 2.9.8, as the do-not-say list asks. This also avoids any "practise and God will appear" reading, and the line "the supreme planet above all others". Note that the story beats say "Vishnu". 2.9.9 says "bhagavān", the Lord, and does not name Vishnu. If Boonchu wants that line back, say "the Lord", in the caption only.
9. No event from the second telling (3.8) is told. There is no stem, no four faces, and no "100 years". Two setting words lean on it. "Lotus flower" (line 6): the verse word svadhiṣṇya means "his own seat" (MW dhiṣṇya "site, place, abode ... the seat of a god"), "lotus" is the vedabase gloss, and 3.8.15 to 3.8.16 say "lotus flower". "On the water" (line 16): 2.9.6 puts the voice "in the water", close by, and 3.8.17 to 3.8.18 put the lotus in the water. 2.9.5 to 2.9.8 alone never say the lotus is on the water. If a purist pushes, line 16 becomes "Nobody to explain. Only him." (also cut 3 in part 7). The picture note "light from four sides" is only a quiet nod to the four faces. It is never said.
10. Brahma's name in the asana. On screen and in the caption we now use VIRAÑCI, not VIRIÑCA. MW p. 982 gives virañca as "N. of Brahmā, BhP., Sch." and virañci as "id." (the same). MW's other spelling, viriñca (p. 983), is "also applied to Viṣṇu and Śiva", so it is not used. Virañci plus āsana gives virañcyāsana, the AYI spelling (my own reading of the sound change, i before ā becomes y). AYI ties the asana to Brahma ("Virañcya ist ein Beiname von Brahmā"). Spellings differ (Viranchyasana in Sweeney, Virañcyāsana at AYI). For the asana name on screen use VIRANCHYASANA, the spelling his students know.
11. "The greatest of all who practise" stands for "tapas tapīyāṁs tapatāṁ" (the hardest-practising of all who practise). The translation says "the greatest of all ascetics". Ours is a plain-word retelling, not a quote, so it has no quote marks.
12. Asana safety and the open door. Viranchyasana A puts a leg behind the head. AYI warns: "Schlecht vorbereitet landet die Belastung schnell im Knie oder im unteren Rücken" (badly prepared, the strain lands fast in the knee or the low back). The script never says "try this". Show it done well, and keep the shot short. A Lane B beginner may feel this is "advanced only". So line 22 says only "Half lotus. Your own seat.", and the close asks about mornings on the mat, not about the asana. The optional cutaway of students on their own mats (with their OK) helps the welcome.
13. The teacher who notices is missing from the story. Brahma had nobody, and someone could read the moral as "you don't need a teacher". The caption closes that gap: "You don't have to sit alone like Brahma. At AYBKK, a teacher notices when you miss class." It is kept out of the spoken lines to protect the one thread and to keep the video from selling.
14. "Every year, new method. Right?" is a mirror, not a fact about the viewer. Say it with a smile, never as a scold.
15. Platforms and names. plato.md (Two Lanes) says "Lane B never goes on WeChat or Xiaohongshu", and this story would do well there. A Chinese version would need no aybkk ask. Ask Boonchu first. In Chinese write 梵天 for Brahma. Many Chinese visitors call the Bangkok Brahma shrine 四面佛, so get a native reader's check before using that name. In Thai write พระพรหม. Thai ตบะ may come from the same word as tapa. That is not checked, so check it in the Royal Institute dictionary before any Thai caption uses it.
16. Never turn "a thousand years of the gods" into human years. The purport to 2.9.8 gives a conversion, and we do not use it. Never say "100 years" for any part of this story.
17. Vishvamitra wording is not reused. That script (28 Sep) spoke of tapas as "focus" ("lost his focus"). Here tapa is only "heat, the hard practice".

22. The checker quoted MW as "virañci, same as virañca, 'N. of Brahmā, BhP.'". That is close, but not exact. MW cites BhP for virañca. For virañci it gives "id." with Kāv. 23. The on-screen riddle at line 13.

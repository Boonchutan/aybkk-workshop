# Yoga, fitness and Ashtanga in Dali (Tali), China

Research report for Boonchu Tanti, AYBKK (Ashtanga Yoga Bangkok). Written on 22 Nov 2026. "Tali" was read as Dali (大理), in Yunnan province, China. The tags used below (FACT, VENDOR CLAIM, ESTIMATE, [S12]) are explained right after the summary.

## 0. One-page summary

**Answer: do not teach in Dali in 2027 unless a host brings a room, papers and paid deposits, because the serious Ashtanga base there is small: one listed Mysore room and visiting groups of 12 to 25.**

Dali is a real long-stay and "healing" town. Its serious Ashtanga base is a different and much smaller group. A deposit is money paid up front to hold a place. "Papers" means a business licence (the paper that lets a firm trade). "Listed" means on the Sharath Yoga Centre teacher list.

**One action:** run the 19-day paper test (three counts, two messages, one post) and read it once on Fri 11 Dec 2026. No trip, no money.

**Stakes:** cohort 3 (the next 30-day group) starts in 50 days with 0 new leads (people who asked about the course) on 21 Nov; an empty seat costs 180,000 THB, and a whole 5-day Dali workshop would take in only about 1.4 to 2.9 seats before the host's share and tax (ESTIMATE) [S135][S140][S133].

**Why care:** the belief is "Dali is full of wellness people, so it is a market", but a community founder there says healing brings mostly passers-by, the budgets found are small, and the real wall is teaching days, not towns [S8][S17][S134].

**The 3 biggest risks**

1. Work rules. Visa-free entry (coming in without a visa) does not cover work. Whether a short paid workshop counts as work is not settled. A ban would end every China stop, not only Dali [S119][S120].
2. Small base, small budgets. One listed room with unknown class size. Visiting events of 12 to 25 people. Long-stayers budget about 2,200 to 7,300 CNY a month (rough blog figures) [S59][S67][S17].
3. Distraction. Dali planning takes days away from filling cohort 3. The best Dali case only matches one cohort teaching day, before the host's share and tax (ESTIMATE, section 7).

**The cheapest test**

Three counts, two messages, one post. Cost 0 THB. About 30 minutes of Boonchu's time. Read once on Fri 11 Dec 2026. The steps and the pass and kill rules are in section 8. The test cannot say whether a paid teaching visit is legal. That needs a lawyer.

## 2. Yoga and the healing scene

### The picture

- The New York Times wrote in Feb 2024 that "healing" is the buzzword in Dali: "Healing yoga, healing camping trips, even healing coffee shops" (FACT) [S24].
- A business-registry count gave 61 yoga-related studios and shops in "Dali" (FACT as cited by the press, Mar 2023; city or prefecture not stated) [S38]. For scale, Meituan listed over 300 yoga studios in Kunming (FACT, May 2023, a different method) [S39]. No newer count was found.
- The healing trade sells short things. Healer trainings last 3 to 5 days, or 1 to 2 weeks for advanced levels (FACT, a journalist's summary, Mar 2023) [S38]. Drama-healing stays start at about 1,000 CNY per person per day (FACT, drama healing only, Mar 2023) [S38]. There is no sourced Dali-wide price per day for healing stays.
- The Chinese press treats this trade with doubt. One report quotes a 5-day course in Dali at 6,980 CNY, with a 3,980 CNY add-on to become a "promotion ambassador" (VENDOR CLAIM, Apr 2024) [S45].
- The government's wellness plans are about medicine, tourism and outdoor sport, not yoga. The prefecture was named a "World Wellness City" pilot (FACT; the body that gave the title is not confirmed) [S10][S55]. In the two prefecture papers read, the 2026 work report and the outdoor sport plan, the word yoga appears once, as "forest yoga" (FACT, Aug 2025) [S10][S86]. A long official review of Dali never uses the words yoga or healing (FACT, Dec 2025) [S12]. Xinhua says sport and medical long-stay products are in short supply (FACT, Nov 2026) [S7].
- Trust has been broken before. An Old Town studio with over 300 members closed in Sep 2020 when the owner vanished, and Dali City police opened a case (FACT) [S41]. One refund complaint about a 6,400 CNY yoga card is on public record (FACT, one buyer's account, Aug 2024) [S91].

### Box: visa, work and tax rules for a foreign teacher

> [!warning] I am not a lawyer. This is not legal or tax advice.
>
> **What is law** (texts re-opened on 22 Nov 2026)
>
> - Entry. Thai passport holders may enter China without a visa: 30 days per stay, and no more than 90 days in any 180. This does not cover work (FACT, in force 1 Mar 2024) [S119].
> - Work. A foreigner who works in China needs a work permit and a work-type residence permit. The law does not define "work" and sets no shortest length (FACT) [S120].
> - Visa types. The M visa is for trade, the F visa for exchange and visits, the Z visa for work (FACT, 2013 rule) [S143]. A law-firm guide says the M visa cannot be used for employment (FACT) [S144]. So neither visa-free entry nor an M visa is a work permit.
> - Purpose. Doing things that do not match the stated reason for the stay can bring an order to leave (FACT) [S120].
> - Fines. Illegal work costs the foreigner 5,000 to 20,000 CNY (25,000 to 101,000 THB), with 5 to 15 days of detention in serious cases. Whoever hires pays 10,000 CNY per person, up to 100,000 (FACT) [S120][S133].
> - Bans. Repatriation (being sent home) brings a ban on entry for 1 to 5 years. Deportation brings 10 years (FACT) [S120].
> - Short work. The Bangkok visa centre shows a short-term work approval, for under 90 days, only for commercial shows. It does not say where a paid yoga workshop fits (FACT, read 22 Nov 2026) [S126].
> - Tax treaty (a deal between two countries on who may tax what). A fee for teaching in China that is paid by a resident of China may be taxed in China, even for a 5-day visit. Staying under 183 days does not change this (FACT, 1986 agreement, Article 14; later changes were not checked) [S121].
> - Withholding (the payer keeps the tax back and sends it to the tax office). Income from work done in China counts as China income wherever it is paid. For a visitor, 20% of the fee is taken off, then a rate of 3% to 45% applies. The 45% rate starts above 80,000 CNY taxable in one month. If the payer fails to withhold, the teacher still owes the tax (FACT) [S122][S123][S125].
> - VAT (a sales tax). On a person's service fee it is 1% in 2026 to 2027, and 3% in normal years (FACT, per PwC) [S124].
> - Adverts. Training adverts may not promise results or use student praise. Words such as "national-level", "highest" and "best" are banned in all adverts, with a fine of 200,000 to 1,000,000 CNY for the advertiser (FACT, read on a copy of the law) [S127]. AYBKK's own ad plan reads this as: no 最 (most) and no 第一 (number one) [S134].
>
> **What is my estimate**
>
> | Fee paid by a Chinese host in one month | Income tax withheld | Share of the fee |
> |---|---|---|
> | 50,000 CNY | 7,590 CNY | 15.2% |
> | 100,000 CNY | 20,840 CNY | 20.8% |
> | 200,000 CNY | 56,840 CNY | 28.4% |
>
> The sum: fee x 80% x rate, minus the fixed amount in the tax table. Example: 100,000 x 80% = 80,000. Then 80,000 x 35% minus 7,160 = 20,840. VAT of about 1% comes on top (ESTIMATE from [S123][S124]).
>
> **What is common practice** (no source found)
>
> - The host studio is the legal organiser. It takes the students' money, gives the fapiao (China's official tax receipt), withholds the tax and pays the teacher.
> - Common practice is not a legal shield. Nothing found says this set-up makes a paid teaching visit legal.
>
> **What a Chinese lawyer or the host must confirm**
>
> 1. Does a paid workshop of 3 to 10 days count as "work"? Which entry status does the teacher need?
> 2. Will the host write an invitation that matches what is really done?
> 3. How is the tax worked out, who bears it, and will the host hand over the tax certificate (proof that the tax was paid)?
> 4. Has a later change altered Article 14 of the treaty? How does Thailand give credit for tax paid in China? This one is for a Thai accountant.
> 5. Who files the VAT, and who gives fapiao to students?
> 6. Do the venue or the local police want notice of an event led by a foreigner?
> 7. If students pay a Thai account from abroad, the tax picture may differ. Ask. Do not assume.

## 7. Options for AYBKK

### Two tools, in plain words

- **Hormozi's value equation.** An offer is worth more when the dream outcome is big and the buyer believes it will work. It is worth less when the wait is long and the effort is high. As a sum: value = (dream outcome x belief it will work) / (time delay x effort and sacrifice).
- **Munger's inversion.** Do not ask "how do I win?" first. Ask "what would kill this?" Then stay away from those things.

Effort: the highest of all options. A week of leave, 8,000 to 9,000 CNY plus travel, and two slow days at altitude.
- What could kill it. It is the most visible form of paid teaching by a foreigner: a public advert across China. Place rules: the guesthouse law, the Yunnan list, the lake rules [S32][S35][S34]. A crowded field of at least seven organisers. Rooms double at Chinese New Year [S130]. Deposits go to a venue while buyers can ask for refunds within 7 days [S108]. A retreat is a trip, not a habit. It is a new product, and the plan says "nothing new after 12 Dec" [S134]. A large fee in one month climbs the tax table [S123].
- If a paid week that people travel to is wanted, build it in Thailand, where the work rules are not in question and the Bangkok room is the proof.

### D. Hold part of a future 180K cohort in Dali

- Sum. Income does not change: 12 x 180,000 = 2,160,000 THB in Bangkok or in Dali. Only cost and risk change.
- Added. Hall hire (no rate found). Travel and rooms for two teachers. Possible Chinese tax on fees paid by Chinese residents (ESTIMATE: 28.4% on a 200,000 CNY fee = 56,840 CNY). Weeks of paid teaching look like work. Each Dali day comes out of the 90 visa-free days.
- Saved, for students only. One flight: Kunming to Bangkok from US$82 to 86 one way [S28]. They did not ask for it.
- Value equation. Belief is lower in Dali, because the proof is the Bangkok room and its regulars. Effort is lower for Chinese students: no passport, own language. That is the only gain.
- What could kill it. A ban on entry. Tax withheld on a large fee. Winter: cohort 3 runs 11 Jan to 9 Feb, when Dali mornings are near 3 C [S23]. A month of strong practice at 1,975 m. No room of his own. The Bangkok shala loses its teachers. New signed terms are needed.

### E. Dali as a content and audience angle only

- What. No trip. Use Dali as a story, and as a way to find serious practitioners who live or long-stay in Dali and Yunnan. One action per post: name your city. Plato drafts. Boonchu posts. No ads.
- Sum. Cash 0. Pool: nearly 200,000 long-stay people in Dali City in the first half of 2026 [S8]. One seat equals 18.75 Bangkok month passes at 9,600 THB (180,000 / 9,600) [S137].
- Value equation, for the viewer. Dream: a practice that outlasts the stay. Belief: plain facts, no promises. Delay: they can start tomorrow morning in the Mysore room where they live. Effort: follow, and name a city.
- What could kill it. The wrong crowd: Dali pictures pull tourists and trip buyers. Unchecked numbers in public. "Healing" words creeping in. It can look like fishing in the Dali teacher's pond: ask her first and point people to her room. It steals post slots from cohort 3: cap it at 2 posts.

### F. Bring the Dali kind of person to Bangkok

- What. Dali's long-stayers already move for one to three months and work from a laptop. Offer them the next stop: one month of Mysore in Bangkok, in the room where the 180K course is taught. It uses the 1 month pass and the Chinese welcome page that already exist [S137][S136]. No new product. No teaching inside China.
- Sum. 9,600 THB + 7% VAT = 10,272 THB [S137]. Train from Dali to Kunming: 105 to 155.5 CNY [S29]. Kunming to Bangkok: 2 h 21 min, from US$163 return [S28]. Entry to Thailand: 30 days visa-free for a visit, not for work; check the current Thai rule [S119]. Ten such students a year = 96,000 THB before VAT = 0.53 of one seat. Small by itself. The value is the missing paid step below 180K.
- Value equation. Dream: a month in the teacher's own room. Belief: high, because it is the working shala. Delay: 30 days give a habit a real start. A week does not. Effort: a flight and a month of Bangkok rent. They already live this way.
- What could kill it. Small numbers. Bangkok costs more than Dali, where rooms run 500 to 2,000 CNY a month [S15]. It grows into a new product with its own page and admin: keep it to one line under the posts. Who answers Chinese visitors on WeChat? Entry rules can change.

### G. If Yunnan at all, Kunming before Dali

- Why. Kunming has the nonstop flight from Bangkok [S28], more Ashtanga rooms [S73], and a Mysore room that already hosts visiting listed teachers [S72].
- Sum. Five teaching days mean about 6 days away, not 7. Meituan listed over 300 yoga studios in Kunming in 2023 [S39]. One Kunming room charged 4,600 CNY list for a 5-day visitor in 2024 [S72]. It also ran a one-month course for 6 students [S71]. Dali people can come by train in about 2 hours for 105 to 155.5 CNY [S29]. Gross = heads x 4,100 CNY. Heads unknown.
- Value equation. The same as B, with a shorter trip and a bigger city base. Less scenery, which is fine: students come for the room.
- What could kill it. No graduate or host is known there either. The same work-rule, tax and day-budget risks as B. Unproven headcount.

## 8. Recommendation

**The plan in one sentence:** fill cohort 3, settle the visa and tax question once for every China stop, and let Dali prove itself on paper before it costs a teaching day.

The real find is not Dali. Two things hang over every China stop: the work rules and the tax on a host-paid fee. Fix those first.

# AYBKK Assistant Duty

For assistants in the Mysore room at AYBKK. Part 1 is the job. Part 2 is the count.

## Part 1: The assistant's role

You are a second pair of eyes and hands for the main teacher, so every student in a full room gets seen. Your real job is noticing who is here, who is missing, who is hurt and who is new, and telling the main teacher. The main teacher decides each student's practice; you help them do the postures they already have, safely, breath first. Touch is offered, never taken: ask first, use light hands, and a no is always fine. In Boonchu's words, adjustment is "necessary once in a while", and "the practice is still your own."

### Before class

**Do**

- On a 05:30 morning the door check-in starts around 05:15. Be in and ready before it opens. [Boonchu to confirm: what time assistants arrive, and on which days and batches they assist]
- Ask the main teacher where they want you today, and which students they keep for themselves.
- Read the morning teaching brief, so you know who is new, who is hurt and who is coming back. [Boonchu to confirm: are assistants in the teacher LINE group that gets the 05:00 teaching brief, and is the brief switched on]
- Greet people by name at the door. Noticing who is here is half the job.
- Show a first-timer where to wait and where the mat goes. Tell them the teacher will guide them step by step.
- If someone came without a booking, add them at the door with "+ Add someone who is here". [Boonchu to confirm: do assistants run the door check-in at /door, or only the teacher]
- If a student tells you about an injury or a pregnancy, tell the main teacher before practice starts.
- Dress simply and skip the perfume. You will be close to people all morning.

**Don't**

- Don't walk in with the students. The room should already be calm when they arrive.
- Don't start teaching a newcomer the whole sequence at the door. The teacher starts them from zero.
- Don't quote prices or sell passes. Prices change, so send those questions to the desk. [Boonchu to confirm: who answers price and pass questions in the morning]
- Don't make a fuss when someone comes back after a break. Just say good morning.
- Don't tell anyone their body type or dosha at the door.
- Don't carry your phone in the practice room during class.

### During Mysore

**Do**

- Listen to the breath first. You can hear who is struggling from across the room.
- Go where the main teacher sends you, and go straight away.
- Help each student with the postures they already have.
- When someone fights a bind with a red face and no breath, bring them back to a shape they can breathe in.
- Give fewer corrections as a student gets better, so they learn to feel it without you.
- Speak low and close, to one student at a time.
- Keep one eye on the whole room, even with your hands on one student.
- Notice who is missing today, and tell the main teacher after class.

**Don't**

- Don't give a new posture or take one away. That is the main teacher's call.
- Don't adjust everyone in every posture. Boonchu says adjustment is needed once in a while, and the practice stays their own.
- Don't adjust and walk off. Stay until the breath is back.
- Don't let the shape win over the breath.
- Don't chat with other assistants across the room. Use your eyes and small gestures.
- Don't count for a student or rush them. It is their pace and their breath.

### Adjustments: touch and consent

**Do**

- Ask before you touch, every time, even someone you adjusted yesterday.
- Say where your hand will go and why, in a few words.
- Look first, then touch. In public posts, students praise the teacher who "observes first" and is "never forceful".
- Start light and stable, on the big bones, so the student feels steadier.
- Use the breath as your stop sign: release, get the breath back, then think about depth.
- Check for a consent card or chip, and still ask out loud. [Boonchu to confirm: does AYBKK use consent cards or chips, and where are they kept]
- If fingers go tingly or numb in a bind, the student comes out at once. Keep your hands off that arm until the main teacher decides.
- Take a no as the full answer, and stay just as warm with that student.

**Don't**

- Don't force anyone into a posture, ever.
- Don't push someone deeper while they hold their breath for a bind.
- Don't touch a part of the body you haven't named first.
- Don't take yesterday's yes as today's yes. A student can say no at any point.
- Don't use your body weight, or any adjustment the main teacher hasn't taught you. [Boonchu to confirm: may assistants give dropback assists, and does AYBKK use the Mysore towel or top rule]
- Don't touch an injured or pregnant student until the main teacher says how, or whether.
- Don't make a student explain why they said no.
- Don't film or photograph an adjustment without the student's OK.

### Safety and injuries

**Do**

- If someone feels faint, lie them down with the legs raised, or sit them with the head low.
- If someone faints during practice, or doesn't come round within a minute, call 1669. [Boonchu to confirm: list 1646 (Bangkok) beside 1669? Only 1669 is confirmed by official sources]
- If someone is hot, dizzy or sick, move them somewhere cool, give water and cool the skin.
- Confusion, a seizure, or hot skin that has stopped sweating is an emergency. Call 1669.
- Watch knees in lotus and half lotus. If the knee hurts, they come out.
- Watch the neck in headstand and shoulderstand. The head stays still.
- Write down what you saw in plain words, and give it to the main teacher the same morning.
- Know where the first aid kit is, and the address to give: S31 Sukhumvit Hotel, Sukhumvit 31. [Boonchu to confirm: where the first aid kit is, the floor and room to give 1669, and who makes the call]
- An injured student still comes at the same hour. The main teacher gives them what the body can do today.

**Don't**

- Don't diagnose or name an injury, even when you're sure.
- Don't prescribe anything: no medicine, no diet, no treatment plan.
- Don't give anyone a recovery timeline.
- Don't offer to fix a sore shoulder after class. That is treatment, and we are not licensed for it.
- Don't soften the adjustment and carry on when a symptom runs down the arm. Stop and pass it on.
- Don't let someone who fainted get straight back up and practise.
- Don't leave an unwell student alone, and don't let them drive themselves to hospital.
- Don't wait until next week on numbness or night pain. It goes to the main teacher today.

### Respecting the main teacher

**Do**

- The main teacher decides each student's practice. We help them do it.
- If the main teacher steps in on your adjustment, step back and watch. Being corrected well is how an assistant earns trust.
- Use the main teacher's words for cues, and check anything you're unsure of after class.
- Pass on everything a student tells you about pain, injury, pregnancy or worry.
- When you don't know, say "I don't know yet, I'll ask the teacher."
- Talk to students about pace, depth and when to stop, the way the main teacher does.
- Save your questions for after class.

**Don't**

- Don't give a new posture without the main teacher.
- Don't tell a student to stop at a posture or skip ahead on your own.
- Don't correct the main teacher in front of a student.
- Don't make a line sound stronger than the teacher said it. When a student checks, it has to be true.
- Don't hand out labels like "you are a Vata".
- Don't argue when the main teacher sends you somewhere else. Just go.

### Shala etiquette

**Do**

- No phones, no photos, no talking during practice. That goes for us too.
- Know the moon days. The shala is closed on full moon and new moon days.
- Let students wait their turn, and bring them in when a spot is free. [Boonchu to confirm: how spots are given when the room is full]
- Respect a ladies' holiday. Traditionally women rest the first three days, and it is her choice. [Boonchu to confirm: AYBKK's guidance on ladies' holiday]
- Say "shala" in English. In Thai, write ชาลา.
- Move quietly: no running and no loud sounds in the room.
- Say "See you tomorrow" when students leave.

**Don't**

- Don't photograph or film students on your own phone.
- Don't ask a woman why she is resting or skipping inversions.
- Don't share student names, notes or numbers outside the team.
- Don't say "we miss you". Say "See you tomorrow" and expect them back.
- Don't eat or drink in the practice area. That is the rule at the Sharath Yoga Centre in Mysore.
- Don't step over or onto a student's mat. Walk around it.

### Closing the room

**Do**

- Make sure everyone does the finishing postures and takes rest, even when they're late for work.
- When a student reaches their last posture, send them to backbends if they have them, then to finishing.
- Keep the resting area quiet, and let people rest.
- Remind students to clean their mat and the floor around it with the spray.
- Check that everyone who practised is checked in before the class ends. After that, the door won't take a walk-in.
- Write your notes in the teaching notes: one thing to work on, and what the team should know. [Boonchu to confirm: do assistants get a passcode for the teaching notes page, or do they tell the main teacher]
- Before you leave, tell the main teacher who was missing, who got hurt and who was new.
- Close the room the way the main teacher shows you. [Boonchu to confirm: closing jobs: lights, fans or air, windows, props and mats, and who locks up]

**Don't**

- Don't start loud cleaning while people are still resting.
- Don't hurry anyone out of rest so you can close up.
- Don't leave "Let the student read the notes too" ticked on a team-only note. It is ticked by default.
- Don't write a diagnosis in the notes. Write what you saw, like "shoulder still guarding on the right".
- Don't message a missing student yourself. The team sends the "See you tomorrow" notes on set days. [Boonchu to confirm: may assistants message students, or does only the team send the 'See you tomorrow' notes]
- Don't leave before the main teacher is finished with the room.

## Part 2: Primary Series vinyasa count

### How to read the count

- A vinyasa is one movement with one breath.
- Count 1 starts from Samasthiti. Samasthiti itself has no number.
- The state of the asana is the posture itself: the count where you stay for the breaths.
- Totals are full vinyasa. The last count is the forward fold. The inhale back up to Samasthiti is not counted.
- In half vinyasa you go from Downward Dog straight into count 7 of the next posture (Kino MacGregor, Petri Räisänen).

### Where the numbers come from

- This sheet follows the Pattabhi Jois count in Yoga Mala. In his foreword to Yoga Mala (2009), Sharath writes: "The method explained in this book is identical to the method taught at his institute in Mysore, the method we follow today."
- Sharath's own count book, Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, could not be found for this check. His led count is known only from second-hand notes. So no cell is checked against Sharath directly.
- Where Yoga Mala is silent (most gazes, most breath numbers), the cell shows Lino Miele's count book, Astanga Yoga. Where neither book has it, the cell or the flag names the source.
- Breath numbers like "5 breaths" are Lino Miele's. Yoga Mala usually says to breathe in the state "as much as possible", and its notes say 5 to 8 breaths is enough. This holds in every table, so it is not flagged row by row. Where Yoga Mala gives its own number or time, the cell shows it.
- Every other difference has a ⚑ marker and is explained under the table. Sharath's led count (second-hand) is in the flags where it differs.

Plain steps for a spa format:

1. Call the Department of Health Service Support (DHSS) on 1426. Ask for your category in writing.
2. Ask the district office about change of use, fire rules and the bath and steam permit [S56].
3. Hire a licensed operator: 20 or older, with a DHSS certificate and a passed DHSS test [S54]. The operator must be on site in every opening hour [S57].

7. Never claim a service treats or prevents disease [S57].

**A. Start your own.** MID is the best format, and the answer is still no. Building work is sunk, and visit numbers are guesses. PREMIUM also needs a doctor and clinic licences at a time when enforcement is active [S10].

**B. Buy a franchise.** A franchise means paying a brand for its system and name.
- No US recovery brand is for sale in Thailand.
- °CRYO already has a Bangkok operator [S79]. Its only public cost figure is USD 550k to 750k, from 2021 (ESTIMATE [S80]).
- Restore charges a 7% royalty plus a 2% brand fund [S67]. Fees of 9% push MID payback from 47 to 71 months (ESTIMATE).
- Check every franchise claim. In 2026 the US FTC made the Club Pilates franchisor return USD 17M to franchisees [S81].

**C. Stake in FORM.**

- What it is: a clinic for physio, Chinese medicine and applied Thai traditional medicine [S4], [S82]. Its machines are compression boots, red light and a laser add-on [S4]. It has 4 sites open and 2 "coming soon" [S12]. No owners or revenue are published.
- How it earns: say a physio on 30,000 plus 15% commission sells 6 hours a day. That costs about 507 THB an hour. About 76% of the 2,100 price is left for rent, overhead and profit (ESTIMATE).
- A fair price: small private healthcare service firms sell for 2.7 to 3.9 times yearly EBITDA. That range is for firms whose key staff often leave (ESTIMATE, US-weighted study [S83]).
- Never pay more than it would cost to build the same profit yourself. MID costs 12.73M for 3.75M a year, about 3.4 times (ESTIMATE).
- Formula: your share x (2.7 to 3.9 x real EBITDA, minus debt and unused wallet credit, plus cash).
- A small stake gives you no control. Ask for monthly accounts, a veto on new debt and new sites, and the right to sell on the founders' terms.

**D. Fit with AYBKK.**

- D1, referral both ways. Zero cash. Students go to FORM. FORM's patients who finish physio come to the shala through aybkk.net/start/form, which the site already counts. The second direction is the one that makes you money. Your course already teaches teachers to "refer the medical question out" [S84]. One kept 12-month member is worth 59,900 THB plus VAT [S3]. Take a student rate, not a referral fee, until a lawyer checks the clinic rules.
- D2, recovery corner. One sauna, one plunge, two sets of boots and one trained attendant in 15 m2 (ESTIMATE). It is the only format that survives all 3 shocks. But 0.41M a year is about 2.3 seats of the 180K course. If you give it free to members, it pays for itself only if 7 to 13 students a month stay who would have quit (ESTIMATE).
- No source shows that a sauna or plunge helps keep members. One chain's survey shows interest only [S85]. In a 2026 US survey, 53% of lapsed gym members just drifted away, and about half were never contacted [S86].

- Your rule: "No equipment race (cold plunge, sauna, reformers): chains win there. We win on habit and teacher." [S3]
- Your shala page, habit 7: "Heat from inside, cooled by your breath. Your own warmth, no machine." Habit 6: "Calm you train, not buy." [S87]

**Hormozi value equation.** Value = (dream outcome x how likely it works) ÷ (time to result x effort and cost).

| Part | Recovery studio | AYBKK |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | recover, sleep, feel calm: wanted, but the same everywhere | stronger, calmer, better sleep |
| How likely it works | low to medium: thin science; Thai law bans treatment claims [S57] | an authorised teacher; proof checked at weeks 0, 6 and 12 [S87] |
| Time to result | good: you feel it in the session | slower: weeks |
| Effort and cost | high: travel, cold, a price every visit | same time, same place, a teacher who notices |

Every studio buys the same kit and faces the same limits on claims. So the only way a studio can win is by cutting effort, which means being where people already are. Chains do that at scale.

**Munger inversion.** Ask how this would fail, then avoid it.

1. Pay for building work before demand is proven.
2. Fight on price in a tier that starts at 200 THB.
3. Add IV drips or a doctor while enforcement is active.
4. Skip oxygen checks, grounding or insurance, or let anyone plunge alone.
5. Buy a stake, or a franchise, on numbers no one has checked.
6. Take your eyes off the 180K course for a year.

Avoid all 6 and you are left with D1.

1. DRIFT: At the same effort, after 10 to 20 minutes the heart beats a bit faster and pumps a bit less blood each beat. A review, not yoga. Scientists still argue why.
2. PEAK: In a 90-minute flow yoga class (not Ashtanga), heart rate was highest on average in the sun salutations, about 72% of its top speed, then about 69% for the next 45 minutes.
3. SWEAT: 8 cyclists rode 2 hours in a 33 °C lab. The less water they drank, the hotter they got and the harder their hearts worked. Small study, not yoga, not drinking advice.
4. FUEL: In a small lab study (6 people, not yoga), after 40 easy minutes the legs ran more and more on fat in the blood, about 60% by hour 4. Not fat loss.
5. MOOD: In a fair test with 63 people, a medicine blocked the effect of endorphins, the body's own painkillers. A 45-minute run still lifted mood and eased worry more than walking.
6. BLISS: In 19 trained runners, the blood level of a 'bliss' chemical kept rising through a marathon. Only the blood was checked: a link, not proof.
7. QUIET: A review of brain-scan studies (sport and lab, not yoga) found clear signs that, in the zone, brain parts that think about 'me' quiet down. The authors call it early science.
8. GROW: In a small study of 8 people rowing 4 hours, the brain let out 2 to 3 times more 'brain fertiliser' than at rest. It helps cells grow, but growth was not tested.
9. SHARE: After the primary series, a small scan of 10 Ashtanga students: the balance centre took a slightly bigger share of brain fuel than at rest. Actual use, there and overall, did not change.
10. CALM: After an hour of yoga, 8 regulars had about 27% more GABA, a calm-down chemical, in the brain area scanned. A different group who read did not. Tiny study, not a fair test, mood not checked.

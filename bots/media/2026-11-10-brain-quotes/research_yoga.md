## How each source was read
- FULL TEXT = I downloaded the text of the edition (archive.org scan text, Wikisource, publisher PDF) and found the words in it.
- SNIPPET = lending copy on archive.org. I read the words through archive.org full-text search inside that scan, stitching overlapping snippets. The words are from the book, but I did not see the whole page. Page numbers come from page-break digits in the OCR.
- sacred-texts.com returned HTTP 403 to every request, so I used archive.org scans and Wikisource instead.
- Sanskrit comes from sa.wikisource.org pages (named below). It is a transcription, not a critical edition.

## The 10 candidates

### 1. kpj-yogamala-asana-first (SNIPPET) fit 9
K. Pattabhi Jois, Yoga Mala (Kannada 1962; English 1999; read: North Point Press 2002 scan `yogamala0000jois_h8s8`).
> It is, after all, not possible to practice the limbs and sub-limbs of yama and niyama when the body and sense organs are weak and haunted by obstacles. To destroy diseases of the body and sense organs, asana must be studied and practiced, which is why Swatmarama speaks of it as the limb to be undertaken first.

- Page: p. 17. The OCR shows "...and dharana. Since the [16] views of Swatmarama and the Upanishadic sages are in agreement on this point, their view is also acceptable to us. It is, after all, ...". Page numbers sit at the foot of the page in this edition (checked against "15 ISHWARAPRANIDHANA"), so the passage starts the page after 16.
- Next sentence: "From its practice, the body will be conditioned and this, in turn, will lead to improved health."
- Counter-line in the same book, pp. 11-12: "From the scriptural statement, "Nayam atma balahinena labhyah [The Self cannot be gained by the weak]," we see that mental strength is greater than physical strength." So he puts the body first in order, not in rank.
- Also pp. 21-22: "A man cannot achieve anything in the world if his sense organs are weak. The experience of the Self, for the weak, is simply not possible."
- Translator not named in the snippets I saw. Front matter says: "Every change and addition, however, has been reviewed by Jois".
- URL: https://archive.org/details/yogamala0000jois_h8s8

### 2. vivekananda-gita-biceps (FULL TEXT) fit 9
Swami Vivekananda, "Vedanta in its Application to Indian Life" (lecture, Madras, 1897), Complete Works vol. 3, Lectures from Colombo to Almora.
> You will understand the Gita better with your biceps, your muscles, a little stronger. You will understand the mighty genius and the mighty strength of Krishna better with a little of strong blood in you. You will understand the Upanishads better and the glory of the Atman when your body stands firm upon your feet, and you feel yourselves as men.

- Just before: "What is the cause of that? Physical weakness. This sort of weak brain is not able to do anything; we must strengthen it. First of all, our young men must be strong. Religion will come afterwards. Be strong, my young friends; that is my advice to you. You will be nearer to Heaven through football than through the study of the Gita. These are bold words; but I have to say them, for I love you."
- Do NOT use the football sentence (reads as sport above scripture).
- URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Complete_Works_of_Swami_Vivekananda/Volume_3/Lectures_from_Colombo_to_Almora/Vedanta_in_its_Application_to_Indian_Life
- Page in the printed Complete Works not checked.

### 3. chandogya-strength-before-understanding (FULL TEXT) fit 8
Chandogya Upanishad 7.8.1. Tr. F. Max Müller, The Upanishads Part I (Sacred Books of the East vol. 1), Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1879, p. 116.
> Power (bala) is better than understanding. One powerful man shakes a hundred men of understanding. If a man is powerful, he becomes a rising man. If he rises, he becomes a man who visits wise people. If he visits, he becomes a follower of wise people. If he follows them, he becomes a seeing, a hearing, a perceiving, a knowing, a doing, an understanding man.

Sanskrit (sa.wikisource, छान्दोग्योपनिषद्/अध्यायः ७, खण्ड ८):
> बलं वाव विज्ञानाद्भूयोऽपि ह शतं विज्ञानवतामेको बलवानाकम्पयते । स यदा बली भवत्यथोत्थाता भवत्युत्तिष्ठन्परिचरिता भवति परिचरन्नुपसत्ता भवत्युपसीदन्द्रष्टा भवति श्रोता भवति मन्ता भवति बोद्धा भवति कर्ता भवति विज्ञाता भवति ।

- Context: Sanatkumara teaches Narada a ladder; each rung is "better than" the one before. The next rung (7.9.1): "Food (anna) is better than power." The ladder ends in the Infinite.
- Shankara's gloss (sa.wikisource, छान्दोग्योपनिषद्भाष्यम्): "बलमित्यन्नोपयोगजनितं मनसो विज्ञेये प्रतिभानसामर्थ्यम्" = strength is the mind's power to grasp, born from food; he adds it is also the body's power to stand up.
- URL: https://archive.org/details/upanishads01ml

### 4. sushruta-gentle-walk-memory (FULL TEXT) fit 8
Sushruta Samhita, Chikitsasthana ch. 24, verse 80 (standard Sanskrit numbering). Tr. Kaviraj Kunja Lal Bhishagratna, An English Translation of the Sushruta Samhita, vol. II, Calcutta, 1911, p. 491 (he numbers the group 54-57).
> A gentle walk or stroll, which is not very fatiguing to the body, tends, on the contrary, to improve his memory, strength, digestive capacity (Agni) and the functions of the sense-organs. It increases also the duration of life.

Sanskrit (sa.wikisource, सुश्रुतसंहिता/चिकित्सास्थानम्/अध्याय २१-२५):
> यत्तु चङ्क्रमणं नातिदेहपीडाकरं भवेत् । तदायुर्बलमेधाग्निप्रदमिन्द्रियबोधनम् ॥ ८० ॥

- Literal: walking that does not pain the body too much gives long life, strength, intellect (medha) and digestive fire, and wakes the senses.
- The line before it says too much walking on the road causes weakness. "on the contrary" answers that.
- OCR shows a stray ";" after "not" in the scan text.
- A different line of this chapter was used before ("Diseases fly...").
- URL: https://archive.org/details/englishtranslati02susr

### 5. hyp-asana-described-first (FULL TEXT) fit 8
Svatmarama, Hatha Yoga Pradipika, ch. 1, verse 17 in the common numbering (19 in Pancham Sinh's edition and on Sanskrit Wikisource). Tr. Pancham Sinh, Panini Office, Allahabad, 1915 (preface dated Ajmer, 31 January 1915).
> Being the first accessory of Hatha Yoga, asana is described first.

Sanskrit (sa.wikisource, हठयोगप्रदीपिका/उपदेशः १):
> हठस्य प्रथमाङ्गत्वादासनं पूर्वं उच्यते । कुर्यात्तदासनं स्थैर्यं आरोग्यं चाङ्गलाघवम् ॥ १.१९ ॥

- The second sentence of this verse ("It should be practised for gaining steady posture, health and lightness of body.") is ALREADY USED. Only the first sentence is new.
- Print has diacritics (Haṭha, âsana); the scan text loses them. Checked in two scans.
- URL: https://archive.org/details/dli.csl.7087

### 6. hyp-staircase-to-raja-yoga (FULL TEXT) fit 7
Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.1. Tr. Pancham Sinh, 1915, p. 1.
> Salutation to Adinatha (Siva) who expounded the knowledge of Hatha Yoga, which like a staircase leads the aspirant to the high pinnacled Raja Yoga.

Sanskrit:
> श्रीआदिनाथाय नमोऽस्तु तस्मै येनोपदिष्टा हठयोगविद्या । विभ्राजते प्रोन्नतराजयोगम् आरोढुं इच्छोरधिरोहिणीव ॥ १.१ ॥

- 1.2: "Yogin Swatmarama, after saluting first his Guru Srinatha explains Hatha Yoga for the attainment of Raja Yoga."
- 2.76 (reserve): "No success in Raja Yoga without Hatha Yoga, and no success in Hatha Yoga without Raja Yoga. One should, therefore, practise both of these well, till complete success is gained." Sanskrit: हठं विना राजयोगो राजयोगं विना हठः । न सिध्यति ततो युग्मं आनिष्पत्तेः समभ्यसेत् ॥ २.७६ ॥

### 7. sharath-body-is-the-home (FULL TEXT, publisher's excerpt PDF) fit 7
R. Sharath Jois with Isha Singh Sawhney, Ageless: A Yogi's Secrets to a Long and Healthy Life, Juggernaut Books, New Delhi, 2018, Introduction, p. xxiv.
> One's journey to immortality begins with a strong body. Your body is the home you live in. Without a strong foundation, your house will crumble. Of course, you also need a steady spiritual base.

- p. xix: "From that change first came stability. ... And through all this came stability of my mind."

### 8. yogasutra-2-48-vyasa (FULL TEXT) fit 6

> Thereafter he is unassailed by extremes. As a result of mastering the postures he is not overcome by the extremes, by cold and heat and by the other [extremes].

Sanskrit (sa.wikisource, पातञ्जलयोगदर्शनम् - व्यासभाष्यसमेतम्/पादः २):

- 2.49 (pp. 192-193): "When there is this [stability of posture], the restraint of breath cutting off the flow of inspiration and expiration [follows]. After the mastery of posture [follows the restraint of the breath]." Sanskrit: तस्मिन्सति श्वासप्रश्वासयोर्गतिविच्छेदः प्राणायामः ॥४९॥ सत्यासनजये ...
- Woods's own heading for 2.46-48: "Postures ... Bodily conditions favourable to concentration." 2.53: "For fixed-attentions also the central organ becomes fit."

### 9. svetasvatara-first-results (FULL TEXT) fit 6
Shvetashvatara Upanishad 2.13. Tr. F. Max Müller, The Upanishads Part II (Sacred Books of the East vol. 15), Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1884, p. 242.
> The first results of Yoga they call lightness, healthiness, steadiness, a good complexion, an easy pronunciation, a sweet odour, and slight excretions.

Sanskrit (sa.wikisource, श्वेताश्वतरोपनिषत्/द्वितीयः अध्यायः):
> लघुत्वमारोग्यमलोलुपत्वं वर्णप्रसादं स्वरसौष्ठवं च । गन्धः शुभो मूत्रपुरीषमल्पं योगप्रवृत्तिं प्रथमां वदन्ति ॥ १३ ॥

- Müller's "steadiness" stands for alolupatvam, which literally means freedom from craving.
- URL: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.22552

## Reserve (verified, not in the 10)
- Mundaka Upanishad 3.2.4, Müller SBE 15 p. 40: "Nor is that Self to be gained by one who is destitute of strength, or without earnestness, or without right meditation." Sanskrit: नायमात्मा बलहीनेन लभ्यो न च प्रमादात् तपसो वाप्यलिङ्गात् । Fit 6. Risk: Shankara reads "strength" as the vigour born of steadiness in the Self (बलग्रहीणेनात्मनिष्ठाजनितवीर्यहीनेन), and Pattabhi Jois reads it as mental strength first.
- Gheranda Samhita 1.9-11, tr. Srisa Chandra Vasu (first published 1914-15; read in the 1979 Sri Satguru reprint scan): "2nd — Asana or posture gives Driddhata or strength ... 6th — Dhyana gives perception (Pratyakshatwa) of Self". Sanskrit: षट्कर्मणां शोधनञ्च आसनेन भवेदृढम् । ... Fit 6. Shows the order (body steps before meditation) but names only bodily strength for asana.
- HYP 2.76 (above). Fit 6. It cuts both ways: no hatha without raja either.
- Taittiriya Upanishad 2.2, Müller SBE 15 p. 55: "Different from this, which consists of the essence of food, is the other, the inner Self, which consists of breath. The former is filled by this." And 3.2 (p. 64): "He perceived that food is Brahman". And 1.4: "May my body be able, my tongue sweet, may I hear much with my ears!" Fit 4: the body-sheath is first in the list, but the text does not say movement or exercise.

## Dropped
- Charaka Sutrasthana 7.31-33: 7.32 is already used. 7.31 defines exercise as bodily action "sthairyartha balavardhini" (for firmness, growing strength). It names the body, not the mind. I saw the Sanskrit of 7.31 only in a Wikisource search snippet of a scanned edition; no named English translation opened.
- Sushruta Chikitsa 24.38-43 (the exercise verses): body only (growth, firmness, lightness, no laziness). No mind word. The walking verse 24.80 is the one that names medha.
- Sharath Jois conference lines ("you have to do asana, pranayama first to bring steadiness"): only in students' notes on blogs (archive.joisyoga.com 2013). Not his own published text.
- KPJAYI website line "A person must first take up daily asana practice to make the body strong and healthy. With the body and sense organs thus stabilized, the mind can be steady and controlled.": written from memory, page not opened; unsigned institute text. Use the Yoga Mala p. 17 lines instead.
- Vivekananda "You will be nearer to Heaven through football than through the study of the Gita": real, but unsafe.
- B.K.S. Iyengar: owner's order. Kalidasa Ks 5.33: already used.
- sacred-texts.com: blocked (403); not used as proof.

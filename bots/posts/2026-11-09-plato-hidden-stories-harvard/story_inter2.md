# Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. ? · "The Horses That Looked Weak" · Asana: Vatayanasana (Intermediate Series, Nadi Shodhana, 2nd series) · Lane B · ask word: aybkk

Story id inter-C-thin-horses (key inter2). Draft of 9 Nov 2026. It gets no episode number until it is filmed. Beat order: H, DH, T1, RH, T2, P.

Why Lane B and not Lane A: the story is about the first fall and about a man who knows the marks. It is not a fix for a stuck asana. That is the Habit Gap: people quit after the first fall because they take it as proof they are too weak. Lane A (STUCK) needs a real technique fix for Vatayanasana from Boonchu, and this story has none. The hook bank lines it is built on (#102, #42) are both tagged goal=shala. In week 3 of the keyword test, the Lane B slots are Tue 10 and Sun 15 Nov.

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: if you wait until you feel strong before you start, you may never start. And if you take your first fall as proof that you are weak, you quit in week one and never find out what you could do.

Why care: the hook bank names "too stiff" as the beginner fear (#20, #42, #102; line 60 "too stiff, too strict"). "Too weak" is my change to fit the story's words. No line in the bank says "weak". The nearest fear on record is the plato.md Lane B fear buster "Too hard, too serious". In this story the best horses look weak, and they even fall at the start. The owner sees only thin horses. The horse man sees the marks. If you have ever called yourself too weak to start, this story asks who made that call.

## 4. Hook bank

Main hook, #102 [bold] fit=use goal=shala habit=8: "Why wait to get flexible when you can start Mysore stiff?" (template "Why {default} when you can {alternative}?"; bank note: "Hits the top beginner fear".)
Second source, #42 [warn]: "The problem with 'wait until you're flexible enough to start yoga'".
The fear these bank lines name is "too stiff" (#20, #42, #102; line 60 "too stiff, too strict"). "Too weak" is my change to fit the story's words. No bank line names it.

How I adapted it:
- "Flexible / stiff" becomes "strong / weak", to match the story's own words ("weak in strength and breath", "lean-fleshed, yet strong").
- The default (wait until you are ready) becomes the viewer's own question, in his voice: "Too weak for yoga?" (line 1, the lean).
- The alternative (start as you are) becomes the story's proof, not a promise: "Best horses here look weak." (line 2, the flip). He says it over the picture of the four thin horses, so "here" points at them. Ganguli calls them "those best of horses" at the very moment they fall (LXXI).
- "Mysore" is dropped. It needs explaining, and there is no time in the first second.
- Double hook (line 3, open loop): "First, they fall on their knees." It raises the stakes (they look weak, and they also fall) and opens the loop "so how are they the best?", which is paid at lines 15 to 17.
- 11 bank words become 4 + 5. The three point lines are 15 words, out by 0:05.4.

## 10. Flags

1. Hidden context, all left out on purpose: Vahuka, the horse man, is King Nala in disguise, "of unsightly appearance" (LXXI, line 35794). The rider is King Rituparna of Ayodhya. The rush is for Damayanti's second choosing. Indian viewers who know Nala and Damayanti may say "you left out the best part". The cuts follow the brief (no royalty as the point, no looks joke) and do_not_say ("Nala" is also the name in the bridge story already used). Do not explain it in replies either, unless Boonchu says so.
2. "Chariot" is my word for Ganguli's "car" (a horse chariot). It hints at a rich man, but nobody is called king. Keep crowns and palaces out of the pictures.
3. "His horse man", never "new". He is hired in LXVII, and then he works there a long time before the trip. The end of LXVII says he "continued to live unknown in the abode of that monarch" (line 35474-35475). He says his sloka "every evening", and Jivala asks "whom dost thou lament thus daily?" (35452, 35456). LXX opens with "After a long time had passed away" (35672) and calls Vahuka "the charioteer of that king" (35680-35681). The trip in LXXI comes after that. The old draft said "new", from the picker's note, and that was wrong. Do not say "new" in the caption or in replies either.
4. Thin, not weak: the text says the horses were "lean-fleshed, yet strong". Only the owner calls them weak. The script says they LOOK weak (line 2), and "Too weak?" (line 14) is a question. Never say they were weak.
5. "The man not happy" stands for "somewhat angrily" (a little angry). Stronger words like "very angry" or "shout" are not in the text.
6. "Weak in strength and breath" is the owner talking about horses. It is not a breath lesson for the viewer. Make no breath or health promise next to it, on screen or in the caption.
7. Curls: the same paragraph says "ten hairy curls" but then lists twelve (1+2+4+4+1). Never give a count, on screen, in the caption or in a reply. "In the right places" (caption) is my retelling of the list plus "free from inauspicious marks".
8. The fall: the text gives no reason. Never add "too heavy" or "first load". Never say "he did not whip them". The text says only that he soothed them and raised them with the reins. The AI pictures must show no whip and no hurt animal. The horses kneel and get up.
9. Flying: LXXI says the horses "rose to the sky", but the script must not say they flew. "Like a bird in the sky" is from LXXII, where the words describe Nala driving. Pictures show the chariot on the ground, fast, with a bird above it. Never horses in the air.
10. The shawl is Ganguli's "upper garment" and "sheet". "Already far behind" stands for "dropped down far away ... one yojana thence". No km for the yojana. It is not checked.
11. The asana link is the word only. Monier-Williams gives vātāyana (masculine) = "a horse", "moving or fleet as wind", tagged L. (found in word lists, with no text cited). The neuter means "a window", so a Sanskrit purist could say the name means "window asana", and some books say "horse face". The script says neither. It also never says the asana is named after this story, and it is not.
12. "One knee go down. Then you rise." is my plain picture of the asana. The AYI sheet gives only the names, so no checked source covers the movement. Boonchu checks it against how he teaches Vatayanasana and changes it to his own words if it is off. His cue goes in line 21.
13. Lane B audience against a second-series asana: Lane B speaks to beginners, who will not do Vatayanasana for years. Line 18 ("Every new asana, you fall first. Normal.") speaks to both. To a beginner every asana is new. A long-time student knows the first try at a new asana. It also leads into the second-series asana at line 19. The asana is a picture of falling and rising, not a fix, so the Reel stays Lane B with the aybkk ask. If Boonchu wants it beginner-only, line 18 can be "First week, you feel weak too. Normal." (same length).
14. "Every new asana, you fall first. Normal." is a teacher's view from the room. It is not a study and has no number.

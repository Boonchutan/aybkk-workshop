## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If you practise to beat the next mat, you grow the wrong way. Each morning turns into a contest. The contest turns into anger, like Vindhya's, and one day you stop coming.

Why care: Everyone compares their mat with someone. The one who binds, the one who got the next asana first. This story shows a great mountain that grew from comparison. The old books remember him for something else. He kept the sage's word, and he still keeps it.

Translation rule for the ZH, TH and RU captions (Flag 18 still applies: ask Boonchu before the ZH caption and the XHS title). "Never come back" means he stayed in the south. Never use a word that can also mean died. A native speaker checks each one (Boonchu for Thai).
- ZH: 他再没有回到北方，在南方安了家。 Never 走了, 离去, 离世, 不在了 or 再也没有回来 alone (it can be read as died).
- TH: ไม่เคยกลับขึ้นเหนืออีกเลย ไปสร้างบ้านใหม่ทางใต้ Never จากไป, ไม่อยู่แล้ว or ล่วงลับ.
- RU: Он так и не вернулся с юга. Он построил там новый дом. Never "ушёл навсегда", "не стало" or "покинул нас".
- "Bow down flat" for a mountain is a picture of a respectful bow to the ground, so keep a plain word for that (ZH 俯伏在地 or 平平地伏在地上, never 躺平, which is slang for giving up; TH หมอบราบลงกับพื้น; RU поклонился и лёг ровно на землю). The title "The Mountain That Lay Down" keeps lying down.
- "Bow" here is always the act of bowing, never the archer's bow (dhanura, as in Dhanurasana). ZH 拜 or 跪拜, never 弓. TH a กราบ word (the bow of respect), never คันธนู. RU поклон, never лук. This matters most for "Remember this bow" and "that bow sashtanga". A native speaker checks each one.

## 10. Flags

1. Two books joined. The Mahabharata (3.102) and the Ramayana (3.10.83) have the jealousy, the growing, the gods, the request, the agreement and "does not grow". The shake, the bow down flat, "like a stick", "sashtanga" and "child" are from the Devi Bhagavata only (10.7.16-18), a later book. So line 13 carries the small card ONE OLD BOOK SAYS · DEVI BHAGAVATA, line 18 says "One old book", and the caption names all three books.
2. Story order in lines 11 to 14 follows the Devi Bhagavata: he arrives, the mountain sees him standing in front, shakes and bows, then he speaks. In the Mahabharata he speaks first (he asks for a path), and there is no bow. "Only stand in front" is the Devi Bhagavata's "dṛṣṭvāgre sthitaṃ munim" (seeing the sage standing in front). "Only" is my word: nothing else happens before the bow in that book. Never make "talking fails, standing works" the lesson. If a checker wants it plainer: "Stand in front." (3 words, the echo of line 2 stays).
3. The gods. They came TO him (MBh 3.102.6 "samāgamya", Ganguli "came to Vindhya"). Never "advice from far away". The true contrast is many gods failed and one sage came. In the Devi Bhagavata the gods never speak to the mountain, so line 9 is from the Mahabharata only. Indra is with them in the verse ("sendrāḥ"). Never draw him with a crown.
4. "Old" (lines 3 and 10). No text gives Agastya's age. The Ramayana calls him "dīrghāyus" (long-lived, 3.10.84, read by me, not on the checker's list) and the Devi Bhagavata has him call the mountain "child" and "little son". Never call him small or short (MW p. 4 "of short stature" is only a summary of tradition). Draw a man of normal height.
5. "Block Sun and Moon" (line 8). The text says he wanted to block the PATH of the Sun and the Moon (3.102.5 "mārgaṃ roddhum"). The caption says "the path". The picture shows the Sun and Moon stopped behind the mountain, never swallowed. The CIII line about darkness and death is left out.
6. "Never come back" (line 16) is MBh 3.102.13 "adyāpi ... na nivartate" (even today he does not return). It is not a trick or a lie. He went south and made his home there (DBh 10.7.20-21). Never tell him as a trickster. Never call him "teacher" or "guru" (no text does). Never make "the teacher never came back" the lesson, and never tie it to the Mysore "stop here" rule. The spoken close is about the viewer's mat, and line 17 keeps the payoff on the sage's word that the mountain still keeps.
7. "Still low" and "still keep sage's word" (line 17). The texts say he does not grow (MBh 3.101.15, 3.102.14, Ramayana 3.10.83, all present tense), and the sage has not come back "even today" (3.102.13). The old "his word" was unclear: in the spoken story the mountain never promises anything, and only the sage gives an order (line 14), so a 5-year-old would ask "what word?". "Sage's word" is what the texts say he keeps: MBh 3.101.15 "vacas tavānatikrāman" (not going past your word, said to Agastya; Ganguli CIII "unable to disobey thy command") and Ramayana 3.10.83 "saṃdeśaṃ pālayan" (keeping his instruction). "Look at a map" is cut to the add-back list. MW p. 972 calls the Vindhya "a low range of hills" (read by me, not on the checker's list). Never give a height or say "lowest". Keep MW's "separating Hindūstān proper from the Dekhan" and any north versus south reading out.
8. "Ashtanga. Same word." sāṣṭāṅga is sa (with) + aṣṭāṅga (MW p. 1212, p. 117). Never say Ashtanga means bowing. Never say the eight limbs of Ashtanga yoga are body parts. Never say anyone linked the asana to this story. The caption adds "Only the word is the same." Option only with a checker's OK: the same Devi Bhagavata uses the word for the eight limbs of yoga at 7.35.4-5 ("aṣṭāṅgāny āhur etāni yogināṃ yogasādhane"; Vijnanananda "Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana, and Samadhi, these are the eight limbs of Yoga", dbh_en.txt line 28405-28406). I read it, but it is not on the verified list, so it is not in the script or caption.
9. Asana. The link is one step: mountain asana, mountain story. Sweeney calls Tadasana "Mountain posture", and MW has tāḍa = "a mountain, L." (old word lists only). Never say it is named after Vindhya, and never say the sequence was made to match the story. No asana number on screen (it changes with how you count A, B and C). Outside Ashtanga many classes use "Tadasana" for plain standing. A practitioner may say "that's a beginner asana". That is the hook's point, so it stays. Never say it is the same as Samasthitih.
10. "You only stand" (lines 2 and 20). Sweeney p. 75 is standing with palms together for 5 breaths, then a lift (In. 8) and down to the floor (Ex. 9).

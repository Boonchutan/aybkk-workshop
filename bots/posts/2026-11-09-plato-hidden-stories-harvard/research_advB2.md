## N1. Tadasana: the mountain who lay down flat for a small teacher (Vindhya and Agastya)

### Name check (honest)
- Sweeney, Astanga Yoga As It Is, asana index p. 83 (OCR "Tii{liisana"): Tāḍāsana = "Mountain posture", pages 46, 75.
- The asana in Advanced B (Sweeney p. 75 photo, I looked at it): stand straight, feet together, palms together at the chest, "In. + 5 Br. + Ex. (7)".
  Then down to a crow-like hold and jump back. In the hardest series, the "mountain" is just standing still with hands together.
- Monier-Williams p. 441 col. 3, tāḍa m.: "a blow ... whipping ... sound, noise ... a handful of grass &c. ... a mountain, L." (L. = found only in old word lists).
- Apte p. 526a, tāḍa: "1 A blow ... 2 Noise, sound. 3 A sheaf. 4 A mountain."
- Vacaspatyam p. 3265, tāḍa: "5 parvate hemaca0" (mountain, from Hemacandra's word list). Böhtlingk pw: "* Berg" (the * means lexicon only).
- tāla = palmyra palm, also "a measure of height" (MW). No "mountain". Some other schools call Tadasana "palm tree pose" from tāla. tāḍā: no entry.
Verdict: "mountain" is a real old Sanskrit meaning of tāḍa, but a rare one, found only in old dictionaries, never in a story. The main meaning is "a blow".
The Ashtanga book's own name is "Mountain posture". No text names the asana after Vindhya. Say "Mountain asana. And the most famous mountain story
in old India." Never say "named after Vindhya". Good enough: one step (asana = mountain, story = a mountain), like Kapotasana and the doves.

### What the texts really say
Main text: Mahabharata, Book 3 Vana Parva, Tirtha-yatra Parva, Ganguli section CIV (Gutenberg #15474, Volume 1, which holds Books 1 to 3;
#15476 is Books 8 to 12 and has no Vindhya). Critical Edition 3.102.1-14 (GRETIL mbh03). Lomasa tells it to Yudhishthira.
- Why he grew (CIV): "The sun between his rising and setting used to revolve round that monarch of mountains--the great Meru of golden lustre.
  And seeing this the mountain Vindhya spake to Surya saying, 'As thou every day goest round Meru and honourest him by thy circumambulations,
  do thou even the same by me, O maker of light!'" CE 3.102.3 "nityaśaḥ parigamyate" (is gone round every day).
- The Sun (CIV): "I do not of my own will honour this mountain by my circumambulations. By those who have built this universe hath that path been
  assigned to me." CE 3.102.4 "nāham ātmecchayā ... eṣa mārgaḥ pradiṣṭo me".
- "Thus addressed the mountain suddenly began to increase from wrath, desirous ... of obstructing the path of the Sun and the Moon."
  CE 3.102.1 and 3.102.5 "sahasā" (suddenly), "krodhāt" (from anger).
- "all the assembled gods came to Vindhya ... and tried to dissuade him from his course. But he heeded not what they said."
  CE 3.102.6 "na ca sma teṣāṃ vacanaṃ cakāra" (he did not do what they said).
- The gods go to Agastya. "the Brahmana came to the mountain. And he with his wife, having arrived there, came near Vindhya and spake to him"
  CE 3.102.10 "sadāraḥ samupasthitaḥ" (stood near, with his wife).
- What Agastya asked (CIV): "'O thou best of mountains! I wish to have a path given to me by thee, as, for some purpose, I shall have to go to the
  southern region. Until my return, do thou wait for me. And when I have returned, O king of mountains, thou mayst increase in bulk as much as
  thou pleasest.'" CE 3.102.12 "yāvadāgamanaṃ mahyaṃ tāvat tvaṃ pratipālaya" (until I come back, wait / keep it so).
  MW p. 667 prati-pāl: "to protect, guard, keep ... to observe, maintain ... to wait, wait for".
- Did he return? (CIV): "having made this compact with Vindhya up to the present day Varuna's son doth not return from the southern region.
  Thus have I ... narrated to thee why Vindhya doth not increase in bulk, by reason of the power of Agastya."
  CE 3.102.13 "evaṃ sa samayaṃ kṛtvā ... adyāpi dakṣiṇād deśād vāruṇir na nivartate" (samaya = agreement; adyāpi = even today).
- Section CIII (the gods to Agastya): Vindhya "hath ceased to increase, as he was unable to disobey thy command."
- Second witness, Valmiki Ramayana (Critical Ed.) 3.10.83, GRETIL ram_03_u, Rama speaking: "mārgaṃ niroddhuṃ satataṃ bhāskarasyācalottamaḥ /
  saṃdeśaṃ pālayaṃs tasya vindhyaśailo na vardhate" = the Vindhya, best of mountains, keeping his instruction (saṃdeśaṃ pālayan), does not grow
  to block the sun's path (my translation).
- The BOW is only in a later book: Devi Bhagavata Purana 10.7.16-19 (Sanskrit, archive.org "SrimadDeviBhagavatamSanskrit"; English Swami
  Vijnanananda, archive.org "SrimadDeviBhagavatamEnglish"):
  10.7.16 "cakampe cācalas tūrṇaṃ ... giriḥ kharvataro bhūtvā" (the mountain trembled and became very low)
  10.7.17 "daṇḍavat patito bhūmau sāṣṭāṅgaṃ bhaktibhāvataḥ" (he fell on the ground like a stick, with the EIGHT-LIMB bow, out of devotion)
  10.7.18 "vatsa evaṃ tiṣṭha tāvat tvaṃ yāvad āgamyate mayā" (Child, stay like this until I come back)
  10.7.19 "aśakto 'haṃ ... ārohaṇe tava putraka" (I cannot climb your heights, little son)
  Vijnanananda: "became low and dwarfish and bowed down to the Muni and fell down with devotion in sastangas ... just like a stick dropped flat
  on the ground before the Muni ... 'O Child! Better remain in this state until I come back. For, O Child! I am quite unable to ascend to your lofty heights.'"
  MW p. 1212 sāṣṭāṅga: "performed with eight limbs ... a reverential prostration of the body so as to touch the ground with the hands, breast,
  forehead, knees, and feet". Same word as Ashtanga (aṣṭa-aṅga, eight limbs).
  10.7.21: Agastya goes south, builds his hermitage on the Malaya mountain. Later "the Devi ... went to the Bindhya Mountain and settled there and
  became known, in the three worlds, by the name of Bindhyavasini". 10.7.13-14: he was very sad to leave Kashi "and he remembered it always".
- MW p. 4 agasti: "of short stature ... compelled the Vindhya mountains to prostrate themselves before him". MW p. 972 vindhya (summary of the MBh
  legend): "begging at the same time that he would retain a low position till his return; this he promised to do, but Agastya never returned".
  The Vindhya is a real range, "a low range of hills" (MW). You can show it on a map.
Answers to the brief's questions:
- Why did Vindhya grow? He asked the Sun to walk round him every day like Meru. The Sun said the path is not his choice. Vindhya grew from anger,
  suddenly, to block the Sun and Moon. (MBh. The "jealous of Meru" and Narada's gossip are only in the Devi Bhagavata, 10.2-10.3.)
- What did Agastya ask? A path south, and "wait for me until I come back; then grow as you like". In the MBh he does NOT ask him to bow.
- Did he bow? Not in the MBh or the Ramayana. Only in the Devi Bhagavata (and MW's summary). Say "one old book says he lay down flat".
- Was Agastya his teacher? No text I opened says guru. The Devi Bhagavata has the mountain bow with bhakti and Agastya call him "child" and
  "little son". So "like a student before a teacher" is a fair picture, not a text claim. Do not say "his teacher Agastya" as a fact.
- Did Agastya return? No. MBh: "up to the present day" he does not return. Kept his word: MBh "compact" (samaya), Ramayana "keeping his instruction".

### Story (retold, bedtime)
Every day the Sun walks around a golden mountain, Meru. The mountain Vindhya says: walk around me too. The Sun says: this path is not my choice.
Vindhya gets angry. He grows. Suddenly. Taller and taller. Soon he blocks the Sun and the Moon. All the gods come and talk to him. He does not listen.
So they go to a small sage, Agastya. Agastya walks to the mountain with his wife. He stands in front of him. And the huge mountain trembles and lies
down flat, like a stick on the ground, with eight body parts touching the earth. Agastya smiles: "Child, stay like this until I come back."
He walks over the mountain to the south. He makes his home there. He never comes back. And Vindhya? Still low. Still keeping his word. Look at a map today.

### Surprise
Outside India almost nobody knows Vindhya (Thai and Chinese viewers know Meru as เขาพระสุเมรุ / 须弥山, which helps). The image is big and new:
a whole mountain lying down in front of one small man. The word is the twist for Ashtanga people: the old book calls his bow "sāṣṭāṅga", eight limbs.
And the hardest series has a mountain asana where you just stand still with hands together. Indians know the outline from school and comics;
almost none know the sāṣṭāṅga line.

### Practice and Habit Gap
- Asana: the mountain in Advanced B is not about growing. You come up out of hard asanas and simply stand, palms together, 5 breaths.
  Tall body, bowed hands. [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less].
- Habit Gap (best frame): advice from far away did nothing. All the gods talked; he did not listen. One teacher walked up and stood in front of him;
  he lay down at once. People do not lack advice (apps, videos). They lack someone who stands in front of them. At AYBKK a teacher notices when you miss class.
- Second value: keeping your word every day (MBh "compact", Ramayana "keeping his instruction"). Insider reading for Ashtangis: the Mysore
  "stop here until I give you the next one". Use with care (see risks).
- Why he grew is comparison: he wanted the daily honour Meru got. Comparing your mat with the next mat makes you grow the wrong way.

- #56 adapted: "Advice from far away? Even a mountain don't listen." (bank note: videos alone; keep it about presence, name no app).

### Safety
Gentle on its own (MBh CIV first half). Leave out everything around it: the Kalakeyas killing sages at night and "heaps of bones" (CII), Vritra slain and
Nahusha "thrown down from his throne of heaven" (CIII), the gods killing the demons after the ocean is drunk (CIV second half, CV), and the Devi Bhagavata's
"some of them died" when the sun stopped (10.3). Say "great mountain", not "king of mountains". Never tell Agastya as a trickster who lied:
say "He went south. He made his home there." Do not mock Narada (skip him). Respectful to the Devi if the Vindhyavasini ending is used.
Ep. 4 (posted) already used Agastya drinking the ocean: an on-screen "same sage as Ep. 4" is fine, but keep one thread in the spoken story.

1. Same shape the judge disliked in Kapila: the teacher leaves and never comes back. Mitigation: land the adult point on the moment the teacher

2. The bow, "eight limbs" and "child" come from the Devi Bhagavata, a later Purana; the MBh and Ramayana have only the request, the agreement and the
   stopping. Mixing is fine if the caption names both books.
3. Name link: "mountain" is a lexicon-only meaning of tāḍa. Honest wording needed on screen.
4. Indians know the outline. 5. "Stop here until I come back" read as the Mysore rule could sound like a teacher who abandons; keep it out of the spoken close.

## N2. Buddhasana: 499 were chosen, and his friends said "What about Ananda?" (the Buddha is not a character)

### Name check
buddha = "awakened, awake" (MW p. 733). Sweeney p. 82: Buddhasana "Enlightened posture", p. 46, 72. AYI: "the posture of the Buddha".
The link is the word "awake". Ananda wakes up in this story. Never say Ananda "became a Buddha": in Theravada he became an arahant ("a perfected one").
Say "his mind became free" or "he woke up". Can also serve Shayanasana (śayana = lying down, MW p. 1055), because it happens as he lies down,
but Buddhasana is the better link.

### What the texts say
Main text: Vinaya Pitaka, Cullavagga XI = Kd 21 "The chapter on the first council", tr. Bhikkhu Brahmali, SuttaCentral (api bilarasuttas pli-tv-kd21), Pali on the same page.
- 1.2.2 "Mahākassapa then selected four hundred and ninety-nine perfected ones." (ekenūnapañcaarahantasatāni)
- 1.2.4 "There's Venerable Ānanda who, although still a trainee, is incapable of acting out of desire, ill will, confusion, or fear."
- 1.2.5 "He has learned many teachings and much Monastic Law from the Buddha." 1.2.6 "Please invite him as well." 1.2.7 "And he did."
- 1.6.2 "It wouldn't be proper for me to go to the assembly tomorrow if I'm still a trainee." (sve sannipāto)
- 1.6.3 "After spending most of the night with mindfulness directed to the body, early in the morning he bent over to lie down." (kāyagatāya satiyā)
- 1.6.4 "In the interval between his feet coming off the ground and his head hitting the pillow," (Appattañca sīsaṁ bibbohanaṁ, bhūmito ca pādā muttā)
- 1.6.5 "his mind was freed from the corruptions through letting go."
- 1.6.6 "And Venerable Ānanda went to the assembly as a perfected one."
Second text: Theragatha 17.3 (Ananda's own verses), tr. Sujato, SuttaCentral thag17.3:
- 11.1-11.3 "82,000 from the Buddha, and 2,000 more from the monks: 84,000 teachings I've learned"
- 26.1-26.2 "In the twenty five years that have passed since I became a trainee"
- 28.1-28.4 "For twenty-five years I attended on the Buddha with loving deeds, like a shadow that never left."
- 32.1-32.2 "I'm a trainee, who has more to do; my heart's desire is still unfulfilled."

### Story (retold, bedtime)
Ananda knows more teachings than anyone. 84,000. For twenty-five years he stayed near his teacher, like a shadow. But he is still a learner.
Now there is a big meeting. 499 monks are chosen. All of them already free. Ananda is not chosen. His friends say: "What about Ananda?
He learned so much. Invite him too." So he is number 500. The meeting is tomorrow. Ananda thinks: I cannot go like this. He practises most of the night,
feeling his body, breath by breath. Early morning. Nothing. He gives up and bends down to lie on his bed. His feet leave the floor.
His head has not yet touched the pillow. Right there, in between, his mind becomes free. In the morning he walks into the meeting, awake.

### Surprise
The man who knew the most was the last one to wake up. Knowing 84,000 teachings was not enough. And it came in the space between two positions,
feet off the floor, head not yet on the pillow, after he stopped trying. Thai Buddhists often know it ("in none of the four postures");
Chinese, Russian and Western practitioners almost never do.

### Practice and Habit Gap
- Habit Gap thesis in one story: people do not lack information, they lack practice. He had every teaching; what worked was one more night of practice
  with the body, then letting go. Harvard #21: "You already know enough about yoga."
- Noticing: 499 were picked; the others noticed the one left out and spoke up. That is the "we miss you" list. (Say "his friends noticed",
  not "the teacher": in this text it is the monks.) AYBKK line kept separate: at AYBKK a teacher notices when you miss class.
- Asana: Buddhasana comes late, when you are tired. The moment often comes when you stop fighting and keep breathing. [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less].
  Optional insight for Ashtangis: the practice is also between the asanas (vinyasa); he woke up between two positions.

### Hooks (bank)
- #56 adapted (contrarian): "He learned 84,000 teachings. It was not enough." DH: "What worked? One night. And a pillow."
- #117 adapted: "499 were chosen. Somebody noticed who was missing."
- Fork (#1): "Big test tomorrow. You are not ready. What you do? Go anyway? Stay home?" Then tell.
- #20 cost: "If you quit the night before, you never know." #27 because: "It came because he kept practising 25 years, and one more night."
- Close question: "How much more do you need to know before you practise?" Lane B (comment aybkk).
Stakes: if you wait until you know enough, you never start, because there is always more to know.
Why care: most viewers have watched more yoga videos than they have practised mornings.

### Safety
Respectful only. The Buddha is not a character: he has passed away before this meeting. Do not say why the meeting was held (it was after the Buddha's
death, and Kd 21 1.1.11 has monks crying and rolling on the ground). Leave out the later part of Kd 21 where the council makes Ananda confess faults.
"Four postures" is commentary and Thai tradition, not Kd 21: say only "feet off the floor, head not yet on the pillow". Never call Ananda lazy or slow:
the text calls him a trainee. Never compare Boonchu or AYBKK to Ananda or the Buddha. No AI image of the Buddha; show Ananda only from behind
(robe, bare feet leaving the floor, a pillow), or no person at all: Thai viewers dislike monks in sales content.
Overlap: advA's Kaundinya (C3) is also a Buddhist story; not in the same week.

### Risks
1. A revered monk next to a sales ask is still a Thai risk, smaller than the Buddha but real. The caption ask must stay far from his name.
2. Thai viewers may know it. 3. "Buddhasana" link: say "buddha means awake", never "Ananda became Buddha".
4. "Shadow that never left" was used for Dilipa (Gomukhasana): skip that line.

## N3. Akarna Dhanurasana: the stranger who got a ball out of a well with a chain of grass (Drona)

### Name check
Sweeney p. 82: Akarna Dhanurasana "Archer's (bowstring to ear) posture", p. 46, 73. MW p. 126 ā-karṇam: "'up to the ear' ... said of an arrow reaching
to ... the ear in drawing a bow". Drona is the archery teacher. The "grass" is iṣīkā, MW p. 168: "a reed, rush, stem or stalk of grass used as an arrow".
He "pierces" (vetsyāmi). So it is an archer's trick. Link: archer asana, archer-teacher story. Medium.

### What the texts say
Mahabharata, Adi Parva, Sambhava Parva, Ganguli section CXXXIII (Gutenberg #15474). Critical Edition 1.122.12-21 (GRETIL mbh01).
- CE 1.122.12-13: the boys come out of the city "krīḍanto vīṭayā" (playing with a vīṭā, a small piece of wood; Ganguli "a ball"), "papāta kūpe sā vīṭā" (it fell in the well),
  "na ca te pratyapadyanta karma vīṭopalabdhaye" (they found no way to get it).
- Ganguli: "They then began to eye one another bashfully, and not knowing how to recover it, their anxiety became great. Just at this time they beheld a
  Brahmana near enough unto them, of darkish hue, decrepit and lean ... Drona ... seeing the princes unsuccessful, and conscious of his own skill, smiled a little"
  (CE 1.122.14 "prahasya mandaṃ", laughing softly).
- CE 1.122.16 "eṣa muṣṭir iṣīkāṇāṃ mayāstreṇābhimantritaḥ" = "This handful of long grass I would invest, by my mantras, with the virtue of weapons." (Ganguli)
- CE 1.122.17 = Ganguli: "I will, with one of these blades, pierce the ball, and then pierce that blade with another, and that another with a third, and thus shall I,
  by a chain, bring up the ball."
- CE 1.122.18: "vismayotphullalocanāḥ" = Ganguli "the princes were all amazed and their eyes expanded with delight".
- CE 1.122.19: "abhivādayāmahe brahman naitad anyeṣu vidyate" = Ganguli "We bow to thee, O Brahmana! None else owneth such skill."
- CE 1.122.20 and Ganguli: "Do ye repair unto Bhishma and describe to him my likeness and skill." Ganguli: Bhishma, "thinking that he would make the best
  preceptor for the princes, went in person unto him".
- IMPORTANT: the ring, the "dry well", the dinner he asks for, and shooting the ring up with a bow and arrow are NOT in the Critical Edition text.
  They are star passages (*1365, *1368, only some manuscripts). Ganguli prints them. Say "some old copies add...".

### Story (retold, bedtime)
Some boys play with a little wooden piece. It falls into a deep well. They try and try. Nothing. They look at each other, ashamed.
A thin, dark, old stranger is standing nearby. He sees them. He smiles a little. He picks a handful of long grass. He says a few words over it.
He throws one blade. It sticks in the wood. He throws a second blade. It sticks in the first. A third into the second. Blade by blade, a chain grows.
He pulls the chain up. The wood comes out. The boys' eyes go wide. "Who are you?" "Tell your grandfather Bhishma about me." The stranger is Drona.
That day he becomes their teacher of the bow.

### Surprise
The chain of grass is a strong, new picture for Thai, Chinese, Russian and Western viewers (Indians know it from TV). And this is how the famous
archery teacher got the job: by noticing children stuck at a well.

### Practice and Habit Gap
- One blade cannot reach the bottom. Each blade holds the one before. That is a streak: one morning holds the next. (AYBKK builds the attendance streak.)
- The teacher noticed the boys who were stuck before they asked.
- Asana: Akarna Dhanurasana A comes before B; each piece holds the next. [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less].

### Hooks (bank)
- #49 adapted: "One blade of grass can't reach the bottom of a well." DH: "The old man who knew that became the most famous archery teacher in India."
- Fork (#1): "Wood at the bottom of a deep well. No rope. What you do?"
- #27 because: "It reached because every blade held the one before." #20 cost: "If every morning starts from zero, you never reach the bottom."
Lane B (comment aybkk). Stakes: if every practice starts from zero, nothing joins, and you quit. Why care: most people restart, they don't continue.

### Safety
Kids playing, no fight. Leave out Drona's words "Shame on your Kshatriya might" (caste and scolding), his grudge with Drupada right after (revenge),
and anything about war. "Princes" = say "boys". Bhishma = "their grandfather Bhishma" (he is a great-uncle; "grandfather" is the text's "pitāmaha").

### Risks
1. In the text the chain works because of a weapon mantra (1.122.16), not only joining. A strict checker may say the habit lesson hides the magic.
   Keep "he says a few words over the grass" in the story.
2. Drona was already the teacher in the Dhanurasana story (bird test), and many viewers link him to Ekalavya's thumb. Comments may go there.
3. The bow and arrow part (the ring) is not in the Critical Edition, so the "archer" link rests on reed-arrows, not a drawn bow.

## R1 (reserve). Akarna Dhanurasana: the oil seller and the proud archer (Chinese)
Source opened: Ouyang Xiu, Guitian Lu 歸田録, juan 1 (zh.wikisource, raw page, line 74):
"陳康肅公善射，當世無雙，公亦以此自矜。嘗射於家圃，有賣油翁釋擔而立，睨之，久而不去，見其發矢十中八九，但微頷之。康肅問曰：「汝亦知射乎？吾射不亦精乎？」
翁曰：「無他，但手熟爾。」... 乃取一葫蘆置於地，以錢覆其口，徐以杓酌油瀝之，自錢孔入，而錢不濕，因曰：「我亦無他，惟手熟爾。」康肅笑而遣之。"
Plain: an archer hits 8 or 9 of 10; an old oil seller only nods a little. "Nothing special. Just a practised hand." He pours oil through the hole of a coin
without wetting it. "Me too. Just a practised hand." The archer laughs and sends him off.
Why reserve: every Chinese viewer learned it in school (卖油翁), so it fails "surprise" for China; the moral is close to "practice makes perfect".
Chen Yaozi was a real Song official (not a modern leader, but an official). Fine for Thai, Russian, Western viewers. Lane B.

## Not found / checked and dropped
- Omkarasana: Chandogya 1.2 (gods vs demons), 1.4 (gods afraid of death), Katha (Yama): out. Chandogya 1.12 (white dog, dogs chant "Om, let us eat"): funny,
  but reads as mocking the chant; not opened today, not recommended. Mundaka 2.2.3-4 (Om is the bow, the self the arrow) is a teaching image, not a story,
  and I did not open it today.
- Shayanasana other than Shesha: Vishnu's sleep (demons killed), Muchukunda (a man burned), Bhishma's bed (death), Kumbhakarna (used). Ananda (N2) also fits "lying down".
- Samanasana: Sweeney "Straight/balanced posture" (p. 83). samāna = "same, identical" or the vital air (MW p. 1160, 1154). No story; the breath-and-senses story was Ep. 4's
  unused alternative.
- Paryangasana: Sweeney glosses it "Revolving limb posture" (pari + aṅga), not paryaṅka "couch". Name unclear, no story.
- Akarna archers excluded or unsafe: Arjuna, Ekalavya, Rama, Karna (armour cut from his body: blood), Kama (burned), Ji Chang 纪昌 (shoots a louse, plots to kill his teacher).

# Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. 6 · "The Teacher Who Said No" · Asana: Samasthitih (Primary series, the standing start and end of every practice) · Lane B · ask word: aybkk

Writer a. Rewrite of v4 (4 Nov) in Boonchu's beat order H, DH, T1, RH, T2, P, with Harvard tricks.

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If nobody notices you stop, one missed week turns into a month. Next year you start again from day one.

Why care: If you quit yoga before, you probably blamed the practice ("too hard") or yourself. This story shows a different reason. Nobody noticed. And the strict teacher you were scared of is the one who came looking.

## 3. Toolkit

Toolkit pick: Triple Hook + Fork-First Story.
Why: The Triple Hook gives the point in 2 short lines (about 5 s), so it meets Boonchu's 6 second rule. The fork (Harvard #1) stops the story right after the third "no". The viewer thinks "I quit". Then the surprise is "He stay." The viewer just picked what many do in real life (quit), and the story shows the other choice.

Toolkit choice:
- /hook (contrarian lever) + /story 4-beat. Cleaner, but it has no stop for the viewer to choose, so Harvard #1 gets lost.
- Vibe Samurai. The three no's (food, milk, foam) make a ready ladder of three. But its turn ("it isn't X, it's Y") is close to the banned "not X but Y" trick, and the flat opening is slower than the Triple Hook.

## 4. Hook bank

#121 [triple]: "This is why most people quit yoga. You think it's because yoga is too hard. It's not. It's because nobody notices when you stop coming."

How I adapted it:
- Context kept: "This is why people quit yoga." I cut "most" because it is an unchecked claim (v4 also cut it).
- Lean and snapback pressed into one line in his voice: "Too hard? No. Nobody notice you stop." The bank's 25 words become 13. The lean is now the viewer's own thought ("Too hard?") and the snapback is one word ("No.").
- Line 1 does all three in the first second. SAY: to camera. SHOW: Boonchu next to one empty mat in the shala. WRITE: "THIS IS WHY PEOPLE QUIT YOGA" on screen from frame 1.
- New double hook (line 3) adds a second flip. People believe a strict teacher drives you away. Here the strict one is the one who notices. This opens the loop "how?", which lines 15 and 19 pay.

## 5. Harvard tricks used

- #21 Teach them something about their own problem: line 2. The viewer thinks quitting was about hard. The real gap is that nobody noticed.
- #1 Decide before you know the ending (the fork): line 11, "Everything, no. What you do?" Then a 1 s silence, then the answer at line 12 ("He stay.").
- #20 The cost of not fixing it: line 14 inside the story (blind, in a well, sun going down, "Who gonna notice?"). Line 21 to the adults ("You stop... who notice? Next year, day one again?").
- #30 Sell only the next step: the caption has ONE ask (comment aybkk). There is no pitch, price, course or link line in the spoken video.

Story pattern: Fail (he goes blind and falls in a well, alone at sunset), Hero (the teacher who said no comes looking, the boy prays and sees again), Payoff (line 19, then the viewer's own mat).

One thread check: lines 1 to 3 speak to adults, lines 4 to 18 tell the story straight from start to end, and lines 19 to 21 come back to the adults. The fork at line 11 stays inside the bedtime voice, like asking your child "what you do?". 5-year-old test: read lines 4 to 18 alone and they make sense.

## 7. Length

Spoken words: 147 (the Thai sign-off not counted).
Estimated time: about 57 to 61 s at 2.9 words a second, with the two 1 s beats, the stand and the sign-off. At a slower bedtime pace (2.6 words a second) it runs about 66 s.
If the take runs over 60 s, cut in this order:
1. Line 14, drop "Who gonna notice?" (about 1 s).
2. Line 4, drop "From the Mahabharata" and keep it on the title card (about 1 s).
3. Line 9, drop "Cow milk. No." The milk then lives only in the caption (about 1.5 s).

## 10. Flags

1. Lines 1 and 2 ("This is why people quit yoga. Nobody notice you stop.") are Boonchu's view from teaching, not a study. "Most" is cut. Never add a number. The caption frames it as "I think".
2. "Too hard? No." is the approved #121 snapback. Some could read it as a soft "not X but Y" move. If he dislikes it, a softer line is "Too hard? Maybe. But nobody notice you stop." (+2 words).
3. The teacher looks cruel unless you know it is a test. The book says "this was the trial of Upamanyu". The caption says it; the spoken script does not, to save time. A purist could say the teacher's rules caused the blindness. Line 15 lets the teacher own it ("I say no to everything"), which is true to the text.
4. Ganguli's sunset line is broken ("is, of course, and therefore, doth not come home"). A word is missing. The Sanskrit (GRETIL 1.3.53) has "sa niyataṃ kupitaḥ", "he is surely angry". So the teacher thinks the boy is angry with HIM and still goes looking. That is strong for the "strict teacher" point. But it is not in Ganguli, so the script leaves it out. Option, if Boonchu wants it: line 15 becomes "Teacher notice. I say no to everything. He must be angry. Let's go find him." (+4 words, source GRETIL 01,003.053).
5. NEVER say the teacher pulled him out. The book never says how he got out of the well. The script says only "He pray. He see again." In the book he prays on the teacher's advice, the Aswins come, and only then does he see again and go to his teacher.
6. Left out, not changed: the second begging (the teacher says it takes food from other beggars, adi.txt 1838-1844) and the Aswins' cake test (he refuses to eat the cake before offering it to his teacher, adi.txt 1958-1970). The cake is a strong second twist, but one ordeal per video. It could be its own episode later.
7. "Fall in a well": the book first says "fell into a pit", then the boy calls it a well twice. The script uses well, like the boy.
8. "So well fed" softens the book's "exceedingly plump" and "fat". No body talk on screen.
9. Wild leaves: the book's Arka (Asclepias gigantea, now called Calotropis gigantea, the crown flower, ดอกรัก in Thai). Spoken as "wild leaves" only. It is a story, not advice. No health claim about the plant.
10. Asana: Samasthitih is kept. Why: the one action the story repeats every evening is "standing in front of the teacher". The Sanskrit word (sthitvā) shares the root of sthiti in Samasthitih. Nothing else in the story (cows, well, leaves, the Aswins) names a Primary asana honestly. Gomukhasana is already used (Dilipa). The bow is Boonchu's gesture, not part of the asana. A purist could say Samasthitih is standing still with arms by the sides, not a bow. So the spoken line says only "you stand", and the bow stays a small gesture.
11. Samasthitih repeats: it is in stories-used (the earlier versions of this same story), it starts and ends Surya Namaskara A (the 10 Oct Aruna Reel), and it was proposed for the 12 Oct guru Reel options. Check the guru Reel was not posted with it, and post apart from the Aruna Reel.
12. The close "Next year, day one again?" is a question, not a shame. It is the bank's own stakes line. Many China and Russia viewers practise at home or online, so the caption invites and does not scold.
13. "Who gonna notice?" uses slang. If it does not sound like him, use "Who notice?" (same meaning, one word less).
14. Length is near the top of the range (147 words). Use the cut order in part 7 if the take runs over 60 s.
15. Content safety: no killing, no curse, no politics, no royalty. A boy alone in a well is mild danger and ends well. The Aswins are gods and are named with respect ("healers of the gods").
16. Episode number Ep. 6 follows the standing order (last posted Ep. 5). Hook #121 is close to #116 and to the 6 Oct doves Reel hook (v1 flag), so post apart.
17. "From the Mahabharata" is spoken because Boonchu asked for the source in v2. The name is explained on the title card ("India's great old story").

## 20 ideas
1. You don't need the splits (our takeaway: splits were not tested). In a study of 13,423 people aged 65 to 90, the stiffest were linked with a higher risk of dying, but more than a moderate range showed no extra survival benefit. (JAMA Network Open, 2026)  
   Source: JAMA Network Open 9(8):e2628227, 2026 (Wu et al.), DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.28227
2. In a Japanese study of young adults, tying shoelaces used about 90 degrees of hip bend. That is more than walking or stairs ask. (Hyodo et al., Brazilian Journal of Physical Therapy, 2017)  
   Source: Hyodo et al., Braz J Phys Ther 21(3):159-166, 2017, DOI 10.1016/j.bjpt.2017.03.012
3. In a lab test, reversing a car used 92% of the neck's full turn. A typical daily task used about a fifth. (Bible et al., Journal of Spinal Disorders & Techniques, 2010)  
   Source: Bible et al., J Spinal Disord Tech 23(1):15-21, 2010, DOI 10.1097/BSD.0b013e3181981632
4. Soviet heart surgeon Nikolai Amosov guessed that a desk worker bends his back only 10 to 20 times a day, for example to tie his shoes. (Amosov, Thoughts on Health, 1987)  
   Source: Н. М. Амосов, Раздумья о здоровье, 2nd ed., Sverdlovsk 1987, p. 126
5. In 436 Canadians aged 55 to 86, each decade older had about 6 degrees less shoulder and hip range. The authors call it small. (Stathokostas et al., Journal of Aging Research, 2013)  
   Source: Stathokostas et al., J Aging Res 2013:743843, DOI 10.1155/2013/743843
6. People around 85 had the back of one thigh (the hamstrings) stretched 5 times a week for 6 weeks. 60 second holds gained 2.4 degrees a week, 15 second holds only 0.6. (Feland et al., Physical Therapy, 2001)  
   Source: Feland et al., Phys Ther 81(5):1110-1117, 2001, DOI 10.1093/ptj/81.5.1110
7. 56 women aged 50 to 79, new to yoga, came to one 90 minute class a week for 20 weeks. On average, their spine range grew 6.9 to 12.5 degrees, depending on direction. There was no comparison group. (Grabara & Szopa, J Phys Ther Sci, 2015)  
   Source: Grabara & Szopa, J Phys Ther Sci 27(2):361-365, 2015, DOI 10.1589/jpts.27.361
8. For healthy adults, the ACSM recommends a minute of stretching in total for each main muscle group, on at least 2 days a week. (ACSM, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 2011)  
   Source: Garber et al., ACSM Position Stand, Med Sci Sports Exerc 43(7):1334-1359, 2011, DOI 10.1249/MSS.0b013e318213fefb
9. Young adults stretched their legs 5 days a week for 12 weeks. Six weeks after they stopped, the new range was gone. (Bisconti et al., The Journal of Physiology, 2020)  
   Source: Bisconti et al., J Physiol 598(17):3645-3666, 2020, DOI 10.1113/JP279866
10. China's 2021 guideline tells people 65 and over to keep up balance, agility and flexibility work. Its first rule ends 'keep at it' (贵在坚持). (Physical Activity Guidelines for Chinese, 2021)  
   Source: 中国人群身体活动指南（2021）, Chin J Public Health 38(2):129-130, DOI 10.11847/zgggws1137503; English version: Biomed Environ Sci 35(1):1-3, 2022
11. Laozi wrote that we are born soft and supple and die hard and stiff: stiffness goes with death, softness with life. (Laozi, Daodejing 76, tr. Legge 1891, retold)  
   Source: Daodejing 76 (Wang Bi text); Legge, Sacred Books of the East 39, 1891, p. 118
12. 3,139 people aged 46 to 65, tested at an exercise clinic in Brazil, were followed about 13 years. The stiffest men were linked with about 1.9 times the death risk of the most flexible men. A link, not proof. (Araújo et al., Scand J Med Sci Sports, 2024)  
   Source: Araújo et al., Scand J Med Sci Sports 34(8):e14708, 2024, DOI 10.1111/sms.14708
13. In 526 Japanese adults, people 40 and older who reached less far toward their toes had stiffer arteries than others their age. Under 40 there was no clear difference. (Yamamoto et al., Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol, 2009)  
   Source: Yamamoto et al., Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol 297(4):H1314-H1318, 2009, DOI 10.1152/ajpheart.00061.2009
14. In a small Canadian trial, 40 adults around 62 with slightly high blood pressure exercised 30 minutes, 5 days a week, for 8 weeks: half stretched, half walked. In the stretching half, the top number fell from 146 to 140. (Ko et al., J Phys Act Health, 2021)  
   Source: Ko et al., J Phys Act Health 18(1):21-28, 2021, DOI 10.1123/jpah.2020-0365
15. When walking, the back leg's hip opened about 20 degrees in young adults, 14 in healthy older people and 11 in older people with repeated falls. A link only. (Kerrigan et al., Arch Phys Med Rehabil, 2001)  
   Source: Kerrigan et al., Arch Phys Med Rehabil 82(1):26-30, 2001, DOI 10.1053/apmr.2001.18584
16. Joseph Pilates wrote: 'If your spine is inflexibly stiff at 30, you are old; if it is completely flexible at 60, you are young.' (Pilates, Return to Life Through Contrology, 1945)  
   Source: Joseph H. Pilates with William John Miller, Return to Life Through Contrology, 1945
17. No old source has been found for the Chinese saying 筋长一寸，寿延十年 (literally, sinews one inch longer, life ten years longer), and nobody ever measured that number. (Chinese folk saying, earliest print found 2009)  
   Source: Folk saying; earliest dated print found: Xiao Hongci, 医行天下, 2009, ISBN 9787218060873
18. In 26,727 Americans followed 17 years, people who said they stretched were linked with about 10% lower odds of dying, even after counting their other exercise. (Sheehan & Li, Med Sci Sports Exerc, 2020)  
   Source: Sheehan & Li, Med Sci Sports Exerc 52(12):2554-2562, 2020, DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002406
19. Half of 40 working women aged 40 to 61 stretched 10 minutes before bed for 3 weeks (about 5 nights a week). Their menopause symptom score fell from about 29 to 16; the waiting half stayed near 32. (Kai et al., Menopause, 2016)  
   Source: Kai et al., Menopause 23(8):827-832, 2016, DOI 10.1097/GME.0000000000000651
20. An old yoga book puts asana first, practised for steadiness, health and light limbs. (Svatmarama, Hatha Yoga Pradipika ch. 1, retold; it overlaps a line used before)  
   Source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.19 (GRETIL, Pancham Sinh 1915), which is 1.17 in the Bihar School numbering

## The 8 picks (story order)

### 1. Kaikeyi (with her maid Manthara) : the queen who was happy first
- Who: Rama's stepmother, mother of Bharata. Manthara is the old maid who has lived with her since her birth (2.7.1).
- Where: 2.7.1-36, 2.8.1-39, 2.9.1-37 and 54, 2.10.10. Griffith II, Cantos VII-IX. Echo: 2.112.27-28.
- Moment: the day before Rama's great day (2.7.11). Manthara goes up to the palace roof "accidentally" (2.7.1), sees flags, flowers, wet streets, asks another maid, hears the news,
  comes down burning (2.7.12-13). She wakes the queen: get up, you are in danger (2.7.14). Kaikeyi's first answer is joy: she gets up "full of delight" and
  gives the maid a jewel for the good news (2.7.31-34): "I do not see any difference between Rama and Bharata" (2.7.35). FORK: the jewel or the whisper. Manthara
  "threw down the ornament" (2.8.1). Kaikeyi still defends Rama: "For me, Rama is as lovable as Bharata and even more. Is he not doing more service to me than to Kausalya?" (2.8.18).
  Manthara tries a third time: your son will be nothing, you will be a servant (2.8.10-11, 2.8.22-39). Then the turn (2.9.1-2). The poet's own judgement:
  she was "of right thinking in nature" and "like a small girl, got into a wrong path" (2.9.37).
  The tool: two wishes her husband owed her from long ago, when she carried him unconscious off a battlefield (2.9.16-17). She had put them aside and it is the maid who
  reminds her: "Have you forgotten about it" (2.9.6). Manthara's proverb: "When water has already flown out, there is no use constructing a dam." (2.9.54).
  Irony: at that hour the father is walking to her rooms to give her the good news himself (2.10.10).
- Source words (Murthy): "I do not see any difference between Rama and Bharata. That is why, I am happy that Rama is being anointed for crown by the king." (2.7.35) ...
  "Manthara, becoming indignant with Kaikeyi, threw down the ornament given to her and with anger and distress spoke the following words:" (2.8.1)
- Griffith: "And joyous at the tidings gave / A jewel to the hump-back slave;" ... "I joy that Ráma gains the throne: / Kauśalyá's son is as mine own." ... "She answered, as the gift she spurned:"
- Surprise: the "wicked stepmother" loved Rama and paid for the good news with a jewel. It took three tries in one sitting to turn her.
- Danger: one whisper in one sitting undoes a whole family. Fairy-tale level: a jewel thrown on the floor, then a queen lying on the bare floor of the "chamber of wrath" (2.9.55-57, 2.10.8).
- Practice: your first feeling on your own mat was right. Then a voice says "someone else is getting more, you are behind", and one sitting of listening can undo years.
  Stay on your own mat. (Link type: meaning.)
- Not in Valmiki: Kaikeyi holding the chariot wheel or axle with her finger or hand (later tellings; secondary sources name the Brahma Purana and regional versions). In Valmiki she
  takes the unconscious king off the field (2.9.16). The gods sending Sarasvati to twist Manthara's mind is Tulsidas (Ramcharitmanas), per secondary sources. An old grudge of Manthara
  against the child Rama is later telling too. In Valmiki she goes up "accidentally" and no reason is given.
- Safety: never mock a body: on camera she is "an old maid called Manthara", not the source's word. No throne talk: "a great day for Rama, the eldest son" and "two wishes the
  father owed her". The old battle = one line ("long ago she saved his life"). OUT: the father's desire (2.10.17, 2.11.1), talk of killing (2.8.27), her threats to die (2.9.58-59),
  the long praise of the hump (2.9.40-52). Do not make her a monster: 2.9.37, and Rama's own word to Bharata later: "Protect your mother, Kaikeyi. Do not get angry with her." (2.112.27-28).
- Asana hook: none found. Say so.

### 2. Dasharatha (and Rama's answer) : "stay one more night"
- Who: Rama's old father. In Bala pick 2 he promised a sage before he heard the wish. Here it happens again, and this time nobody can undo it.
- Where: the oath 2.11.3-10; the pigeon king 2.12.43 and 2.14.4; the one word 2.18.3; the farewell 2.34.16-60 (Griffith XXXIV); after 2.40.28-51, 2.42.1-3 and 33.
- Moment: Kaikeyi asks him to promise first (2.11.3). He swears on the dearest thing he has: "I can't live even for a moment without seeing Rama ... By that Rama, I am taking
  my oath" (2.11.7). Then he hears the two wishes. He begs, even at her feet (2.12.36), and prays to the night itself not to turn into dawn (2.13.17). She holds him to it with the story of the king who kept his promise to a bird: "a dispute between a
  hawk and a pigeon" (2.12.43; 2.14.4). In the morning he cannot say it: he "spoke only one word "Rama!"" (2.18.3) and Kaikeyi tells Rama herself (2.18.20-40).
  The farewell: he runs to his son and falls before he reaches him (2.34.17). FORK, and it is the father who offers the way out, twice. First: "Oh, Rama! I was stupefied by
  Kaikeyi through a boon. Now, by confining me, be you the king of Ayodhya." (2.34.26). Rama: "For my sake, do not generate untruth about you." (2.34.28), and
  fourteen years from now "I shall clasp your feet once more" (2.34.29). Second, smaller: stay just tonight (2.34.33-34). Rama: "It is not possible for me, Oh father, to stay on
  even for a moment." (2.34.49). The father, "tied up by fetters of truth" (2.34.30), blesses the road (2.34.31). He follows the chariot on foot until his ministers say
  "We should not follow to a long distance him whom we wish to see come back." (2.40.50). He watches the dust until it is gone (2.42.1-3). At midnight:
  "My sight has extinguished along with Rama and not returned ... Touch me with your hand." (2.42.33).
- Source words (Murthy): "Oh, son! Do not leave in any case today, now at night time. Even by seeing you for one day, I may live happily." (2.34.33)
  "Looking at your mother and myself, stay at least for this night today. Satiated by all the desired objects, you may leave tomorrow at dawn." (2.34.34)
- Griffith: "But O, a little longer stay, / Nor turn thy steps this night away," ; "A captive in the snare of truth," ; the oath: "I swear by Ráma my dear son, / Speak, and thy bidding shall be done."
- Surprise: the father himself begs the son to break the father's promise, first big (hold me back), then small (one night). The son says no to both, to protect the father's word.
  And the father never speaks the order: he says one word.
- Danger: an old father loses the son he swore by. A promise made in a happy minute, paid on the worst morning.
- Practice (keeping your word): a promise does not end with a big no. It ends with "just tonight", "just this morning". Rama refuses the small exception. Also: the word you
  gave on a good day is tested on a bad day. (Link type: meaning.) Trick #1 fits: stop at "stay one more night" and ask "What you do?"
- Asana hook (UNCHECKED against the PDF list): Raja Kapotasana or Eka Pada Raja Kapotasana. raja = king, kapota = pigeon. The promise scene itself cites the king and the
  pigeon (Sanskrit gloss on the page: "shyena kapotiiye", 2.12.43). WORD link only: the asana means "king pigeon", it is not named after that king. Say so.
- Differences: Griffith softens 2.34.26 to "Unfit to reign, by her misled: / Be ruler in thy father's stead." The site's gloss has "nigR^ihya ... maam" = "by confining me". Use the site, flag Griffith.
- Not in Valmiki: the name "Shravana Kumara" and the parents carried in baskets. In Valmiki (2.63-64) the boy at the river is an unnamed young ascetic. That whole story is
  UNSAFE here anyway (an arrow in the dark, a boy dies, a curse of death).
- Safety: a father's promise and a son's choice. On camera 2.34.26 becomes "Stop me. Hold me here. And stay." Never "be king". The king-and-pigeon story: one safe line
  ("a king who kept his word to a small bird"), no flesh, no eyes (2.14.5 OUT). His death (2.64) only as one gentle line or not at all. OUT: 2.42.6-9 (he disowns Kaikeyi).
  Overlap: Bala pick 2 is also "promised before he heard". Do not run both back to back, or make this one the callback ("he did it again").

### 3. Kausalya : the mother who said "I forbid it", then tied a herb on his wrist
- Who: Rama's own mother, the eldest queen. She keeps vows and morning rites (2.20.14-19).
- Where: 2.20.14-36, 2.21.20-29 and 50-52, 2.24.9-14 and 31-37, 2.25.1-47. Griffith XX, XXI, XXIV, XXV. Later: 2.40.43-45, 2.75.10-11 and 63.
- Moment: dawn. She has "spent the whole night with steadfastness" and is at her fire, in white, praying for her son's big day (2.20.14-15). He walks in. She offers a seat and
  food. He touches the seat only: "Why this seat for me? Time has come for me to sit on a seat made of Kusha grass." (2.20.28). She falls (2.20.32).
  FORK: hold him or bless him. First she holds: stay and serve your mother, a sage once won heaven that way, "Formerly Kasyapa was staying in his own house itself ...
  serving his mother" (2.21.23); "I am also as respectable a person to you as your father ... I do not give permission to you." (2.21.51). Then: take me too, "A cow follows its
  calf wherever it goes." (2.24.9). He says his father needs her (2.24.11-13). The turn: "I am not able to avert your well-made resolution" (2.24.32). She sips water,
  puts her grief away (2.25.1) and gives the long blessing: may the thing you practise guard you (2.25.3), may Skanda guard you (2.25.11), may you have the luck Vishnu had
  "while making his three strides" (2.25.35). She puts rice on his head and ties a healing herb on his wrist "by way of an amulet" (2.25.37-38).
  "Though filled with grief inside, she appeared to be over-joyed outside" (2.25.39). When the chariot leaves she runs after it "as a cow fond of her calf" (2.40.43).
- Source words (Murthy): "Oh, Rama! Your departure cannot be restrained, depart now, return soon. Abide in the footsteps of the virtuous." (2.25.2)
  "Oh, Rama! Let that righteousness, which you are fostering with courage and discipline , protect you". (2.25.3)
- Griffith: "Thou must not seek the forest wild." (XXI) then "May virtue on thy steps attend, / And be her faithful lover's friend." and "And spoke glad words though crushed by woe," (XXV).
- Surprise: the mother forbids him first, with a mother's full authority. Then the same woman sends him with the strongest blessing in the book. And she cannot give him
  a guard or a map. She gives him one thing: what you keep will keep you.
- Danger: a mother at her morning prayer learns her son leaves for a forest of wild animals for fourteen years, today.
- Practice: she cannot go with him, like a teacher cannot practise for you. All she can say: the practice you keep with courage and discipline is what protects you. And the news
  found her already at her dawn practice. (Link type: meaning.) Also a true word link to "asana" itself: he refuses the fine seat for a grass seat (gloss: "visTaraasana").
- Asana hooks (UNCHECKED against the PDF list; all three are NAME links inside her own speeches): Kasyapasana (she names Kasyapa, 2.21.23), Skandasana (2.25.11),
  Trivikramasana or Supta Trivikramasana (tri + vikrama = three strides, 2.25.35; Griffith: "When with his triple step he paced,"). Pick one.
- Differences: 2.21.23 Murthy "Kasyapa"; Griffith "Kaśyap's great ascetic son". Say "a sage of Kashyapa's family" or check before naming.
- Not in Valmiki: the famous Sanskrit line "dharmo rakshati rakshitah" is NOT this verse (usually cited from Manusmriti 8.15; unchecked). Quote only the translation above.
- Safety: OUT her threat to starve (2.21.26-27), her bitter words about Kaikeyi and about her own marriage (2.20.36-55). One line: "she said she could not live without him".
  No throne: "your big day" not "your coronation". Respectful: she is a mother, never weak or silly.

### 4. Sita : "I will walk in front of you"
- Who: Rama's young wife, daughter of Janaka, raised in a palace.
- Where: 2.26.19-38, 2.27.1-23, 2.28.1-26, 2.29.1-24, 2.30.1-47; the bark dress 2.37.9-20; her real clothes 2.37.34-36, 2.39.14-18, 2.40.13-15; walking order 2.52.95-96.
  Griffith XXVI-XXX, XXXVII, XXXIX.
- Moment: he comes home with a changed face. He tells her: stay, look after my parents (2.26.28-38). She answers "angry out of love alone" (2.27.1).
  FORK is Rama's: take her or leave her safe. He says no and lists the forest: lions in caves, crocodiles, thorns, a bed of leaves, fallen fruit, darkness, hunger, snakes,
  scorpions, mosquitoes (2.28.7-23). She: all that turns good if you are there (2.29.2), and as a girl she was told she would live in a forest and
  "I was always eager to stay in a forest." (2.29.9). The grass "will touch my soles like a heap of cotton or soft deerskin." (2.30.12). He says no again (2.29.22-24).
  She weeps in his arms (2.30.22-26). Then he says why he had refused: "without knowing your entire opinion, I do not like to take you to the forest." (2.30.28);
  "it appears that you are created indeed for dwelling in the forest with me." (2.30.29). She gives her things away, glad (2.30.43-47).
  Small true scene after: bark clothes are brought. Rama and Lakshmana change. Sita in silk "erred again and again" (2.37.12): "Keeping one piece of bark on her neck
  as well as holding it with her hand, Seetha stood abashed, not adept as she was in wearing it." (2.37.13). Rama ties it over her silk himself (2.37.14).
- Source words (Murthy): "Oh, Rama! If you set forth now itself to the forest, which is difficult to be travelled, I shall come before you, by trampling down thorns and grass
  with long pointed stalks." (2.27.6) ... "know all those disadvantages you mentioned about dwelling in the forest become advantages to me, if your affection is placed before them." (2.29.2)
- Griffith: "My foot before thine own shall pass / Through tangled thorn and matted grass."
- Surprise: she was not taken along. She was told no, twice, and argued her way in. He said no until he knew her whole mind. And the brave one could not tie the dress on day one.
- Danger: his own list (2.28). A girl who has never walked on rough ground chooses fourteen years of it.
- Practice (showing up, small steps): day one you do not know how to tie it, and someone ties it for you. You go anyway. Also: a "no" can be the way the other one learns how much
  you mean it. (Link type: meaning.) Careful: the "no first" angle is close to unfilmed Ep. 6 "The Teacher Who Said No". Do not run them back to back.
- Honest limits: she SAYS she will walk in front. In the forest the order is Lakshmana first, Sita in the middle, Rama last (2.52.95-96). Never show her leading the walk.
- Not in Valmiki: Sita leaving in bark or orange robes. In Valmiki the family priest stops it (2.37.21 and 34-36), the father sends clothes and jewels for all the years (2.39.15-16)
  and she leaves wearing them (2.39.17, 2.40.13-15). Urmila asking to come: not in this departure (in this kanda Urmila is named once, 2.118.53). The line on the ground is Aranya, and not in Valmiki at all.
- Safety: respectful, never a joke: the bark scene is tender, not funny. OUT: her taunt (2.30.3), her threats of poison and fire (2.29.21, 2.30.19-20), the "husband is a god"
  teaching (keep "where you go, I go"). OUT the priest's anger at Kaikeyi (2.37.22-33).
- Asana hook: none found. Say so.

### 5. Sumantra : the man who had to drive the empty chariot home
- Who: the old charioteer and counsellor of Rama's father. He drives the three out of the city.
- Where: 2.40.10-17 and 46-48; 2.46.11-16; 2.52.12-64; 2.57.1-26; 2.58; 2.59.1-3 and 10-12; later 2.84.11-13. Griffith XL, LII, LVII, LIX, LXXXIV.
- Moment: he starts the clock as he takes the reins: fourteen years, and "Those years ... are to be considered having commenced." (2.40.12). The father cries stop, Rama says
  go, and his mind is "as in between two (opposing) whirl pools" (2.40.46). At the great river a boat waits. He asks: "What shall I do?" (2.52.12). Rama touches him:
  "Go now, that your service to me has been completed." (2.52.14). FORK: he begs to come: the horses can serve you; let me bring you home "in this very chariot" in
  fourteen years (2.52.47-58). Rama gives the reason: only when Kaikeyi sees you back will she believe I have gone, and stop doubting my father's word (2.52.61-62).
  So the harder job is his. He waits by the river hoping to be called (2.59.3). The horses stand "shedding warm tears" and will not start (2.59.1).
  At evening he reaches the city (2.57.5): "hundreds and thousands of people ran towards him asking, "Where is Rama?"" (2.57.9). He drives on
  "by covering his face" (2.57.16). "Nobody greeted me" (2.59.10). Kausalya names what he did: he "has done difficult things" (2.57.29).
  Quiet payoff: later the same man is on the same road again, with Bharata, going to find Rama, and it is he who presents Guha (2.84.11-13).
- Source words (Murthy): "The cry of distress raised by the citizens (of Ayodhya) will be increased a hundred-fold, when they see me with an empty chariot." (2.52.44) ...
  "With the hope that I shall be called again by Rama, I stayed there along with Guha for three days." (2.59.3)
- Griffith: "Woe will be theirs too deep to brook / When on the empty car they look," and "With Guha all that day I stayed, / Still by the earnest hope delayed"
- Surprise: the hard job in this story was not going to the forest. It was going back. And the reason: the empty chariot is the proof that the father kept his word.
- Danger: a whole city waiting at the gate, and he is the one who must answer "where is he". Fairy-tale level: horses that cry and will not move.
- Practice (coming back): after a long gap the hard part is not the asana. It is the walk back in, where people ask where you were. He covered his face and drove in anyway.
  And the city noticed who was missing: true link to "a teacher who notices when you're gone". (Link type: meaning.)
- Differences: how long he waited: Murthy "three days"; the Sanskrit gloss on the same page says "bahuun = for many (three) ... days"; Griffith "all that day". Say "he waited". Give no number.
- Not in Valmiki: nothing famous is added to him. Do not give the horses speech. Do not tell 2.40.47 (Rama telling him to say he did not hear).
- Safety: safe. OUT his line about entering a fire (2.52.49). The father fainting: "the old father could not speak". No throne.
- Asana hook: none by name. Only a loose meaning link to a "turned back" asana (parivrtta = turned round), if the PDF has one. Weak. Say so.

### 6. Guha : five hundred boats, and one question
- Who: chief of the Nishadas, the forest and river people at the Ganga, and Rama's old friend (2.50.33). Not a prince, not a sage.
- Where: first meeting 2.50.33-51, 2.51.1-9, 2.52.5-10 and 65-81; the boats 2.84.1-18, 2.85.1-13; the grass bed 2.86-87, 2.88.1-2 and 13-15. Griffith L-LII, LXXXIV-LXXXVIII.
- Moment 1 (set-up): Rama arrives. Guha brings food, drink and beds: "All this land is yours." (2.50.38-39). Rama takes nothing: "I desire nothing but a little forage for the horses."
  (2.50.45); "We are not allowed to receive anything we must always be givers." (2.87.17). He drinks water and lies on the ground. Guha offers Lakshmana a bed:
  "All of us are habituated to hardships. You are habituated to comforts. We shall keep awake this night" (2.51.3). Lakshmana will not lie down. So both stand
  guard till morning (2.50.50, 2.51.26, 2.87.23-24). His men row them across (2.52.77-81).
- Moment 2 (THE FORK): later a huge army stands on his bank with Bharata's flag. Guha thinks the worst (2.84.3-5). Block the river, or find out? He does both, in the right
  order: five hundred boats, a hundred men each, ready (2.84.8), then he walks to Bharata with gifts of food (2.84.10) and says it to his face: "This great army of yours is
  begetting an apprehension in my mind." (2.85.7). Bharata, "whose heart resembled a taintless sky" (2.85.8): I go to bring him home (2.85.9-10). Guha's face is "radiated with
  joy" (2.85.11). That night he shows Bharata the grass where Rama slept: the blades pressed flat, "some particles of gold" and silk threads from Sita's cloth (2.88.13-15).
- Source words (Murthy): "Let five hundred boats, each manner by a hundred youthful ferrymen, be stationed and made ready" -thus announced Guha. (2.84.8)
  "If Bharata is favourably disposed now towards Rama, then only his army may (be allowed to) cross the River Ganga in safety." (2.84.9)   ["manner" is the site's slip for "manned"]
- Griffith: "A hundred fishers man each boat / Of the five hundred here afloat," and "But this thy host so wide dispread / Wakes in my heart one doubt and dread,"
- Surprise: the famous picture, a boatman washing Rama's feet before he lets him on the boat, is not in Valmiki. In Valmiki the river chief's gifts are refused, so he gives the
  one thing nobody can refuse: he stays awake. Later he is ready to stop an army for his friend, and he goes and looks first.
- Danger: an army "like an ocean" (2.84.2) on one bank, five hundred boats on the water, and one wrong guess starts a fight between friends of the same man.
- Practice: curiosity = look closer. He did not decide from the far bank. He went near and looked. Same on the mat: the asana that looks like an enemy today, go near, look closer
  in the same asana (never "ask why"). Second true link (showing up): when your gift is not needed, you can still stay and stand there. (Link type: meaning.)
- Not in Valmiki: Kevat the boatman who washes Rama's feet first, for fear the boat turns into a woman: Tulsidas, Ramcharitmanas (secondary sources). In Valmiki Guha orders a boat
  with a helmsman and oarsmen (2.52.6-7, 77, 81) and does not row; the feet are washed by Lakshmana, at night, on the ground (2.50.49; 2.87.21).
- Safety: call him "chief of the river people" or "Guha, a chief of boatmen": not "king" (the source's word), no caste talk. No war: boats ready, nobody fights. The gifts: say
  "food from the forest" (the text has fish, meat and honey, 2.84.10). OUT "bind us with chains or slay us" (2.84.4): say "he may mean harm".
- Asana hook (UNCHECKED): Skandasana only as a SHARED NAME: Guha is also a name of the god Skanda (Monier-Williams, from memory, not opened today). The asana is named for the god,
  not for this Guha. Weak. Better none, or the boat (nau), which is not an Advanced A or B asana.
- Overlap: Moment 2 is also beat 2 of pick 8 (Bharata). Use it in one of them only.

### 7. Lakshmana : the brother on the tree
- Who: Rama's younger brother, son of Sumitra, who chose to come along. His mother's order as he left: "Do not neglect your half-brother Rama, who is going to the forest, my son!" (2.40.5).
- Where: 2.96.3-21, 2.97.1-28 (Griffith XCVII-XCVIII). Background: his own promise 2.31.3 and 23-25; the night watches 2.46.15-16, 2.51.1-9 and 26, 2.53.3.
- Moment: their hill, Chitrakuta. Dust in the sky, a roar, elephants and deer running (2.96.3-8). Rama: go and see what it is (2.96.9-10). Lakshmana climbs a flowering sal tree
  (2.96.11). An army, and on a chariot Bharata's flag with a tree on it (2.96.12, 18). He calls down: "Extinguish the fire. Let Seetha occupy a hiding place. Make your bow ready" (2.96.14).
  He is sure: Bharata comes to hurt us (2.96.17). FORK: string the bow, or wait and see. Rama calms him (2.97.1): "Has Bharata at any time done any harm to you previously?" (2.97.14).
  Then the line that stops him: if it is the kingdom you want, I will tell Bharata to hand it to you, and he "will answer, So, be it." (2.97.17-18).
  Lakshmana, "abashed, shrank into himself." (2.97.19). He changes the subject: maybe it is our father, come to see you (2.97.20). And Rama, "Observing the emberassed"
  [his brother], agrees out loud: yes, maybe it is father (2.97.21-23). Then, quietly: "You get down from the top of the tree." (2.97.27). He comes down and stands
  "with joined palms" beside him (2.97.28). Below, Bharata has ordered his army not to step on the hermitage ground (2.97.29).
- Source words (Murthy): Listening to these words ashamed, answered: "I think that our father Dasaratha came here to see you himself." (2.97.20)
  Observing the emberassed , Rama said, "I think the mighty armed Dasaratha came here to see us." (2.97.21)   [spelling and the missing name are the site's]
- Griffith: "And Lakshmaṇ keenly felt the blame, / And shrank within himself for shame:" ... "Thus Lakshmaṇ spoke and stood ashamed, / And Ráma saw and thus exclaimed:" ; "Quick, quick, my lord, put out the fire, / Let Sítá to the cave retire."
- Surprise: the most loyal man in the story is the one who is wrong. And Rama does not win the point. He sees the shame, and goes along with the change of subject so his brother can climb down with his face.
- Danger: an army at the foot of the hill, and a brother in a tree who has already decided. One arrow from that tree and two brothers are at war.
- Practice (a teacher who notices): he noticed the moment his student was ashamed and did not make him smaller. Second: from the top of the tree on a bad day you see flags and dust, not
  the person. Come down, look closer. (Link type: meaning.) At AYBKK "a teacher notices" is a confirmed line.
- ALT moment for Lakshmana (if the asana step needs a link): the bed he would not lie on. His promise: "I shall do everything while you are waking or sleeping." (2.31.25). First night
  at the Tamasa he keeps "awake that whole night" (2.46.16). At the Ganga Guha offers a bed (the Sanskrit verse printed on the page has the word shayya, bed, in Devanagari, 2.51.2; the
  word-by-word gloss skips it); he says "how is it possible for me to sleep" (2.51.9) and stands until dawn (2.51.26).
  Asana hook (UNCHECKED): Sayanasana, MEANING link (shayana = lying down, same root as shayya = bed; root not checked in a dictionary today): the rest he did not take.
- Not in Valmiki: Lakshmana never sleeping for fourteen years while his wife Urmila sleeps for him (Telugu folk songs and the Ranganatha Ramayana, per secondary sources).
  Valmiki has the night watches above, and Rama says "we must both keep awake during nights, from today Lakshmana!" (2.53.3): they share it. The tree scene IS in Valmiki.
- Safety: never a joke about Lakshmana: he is wrong out of love. OUT every threat he speaks (2.96.22-30), the word blood, the meal in 2.96.1-2. No war: nobody shoots. Keep the kingdom
  line small: "If you want what he has, I will ask him. He will say yes."
- Overlap: this scene is also beat 4 of pick 8 (Bharata). Use it in one of them only.

### 8. Bharata : the brother nobody believed
- Who: Kaikeyi's son. He was far away at his uncle's house when it all happened (2.19.10, 2.75.2-4). He comes home to find it was done in his name.
- Where: 2.75.1-20 and 60-63; 2.85.7-13; 2.88.26-27; 2.90.1-3 and 10-21; 2.96.17; 2.112.9-29; 2.113.1; 2.115.1-27. Griffith LXXV, LXXXV, XC, CXII, CXV.
- Moment: FORK at the start: keep what was given, or go and give it back. He goes. And nobody believes him. A ladder:
  1) Kausalya: "you, who have a desire for kingdom, have got this invincible kingdom" (2.75.10-11). He falls at her feet; she takes him "to her lap" (2.75.63).
  2) Guha at the river readies five hundred boats and asks (2.84.8, 2.85.7). 3) The sage Bharadvaja asks the same (2.90.10-13). Bharata: "If you the Blessed One, regard me like this,
  I am lost indeed!" (2.90.15). The sage: "I was aware of your purpose in your mind. But, I desired to confirm it" (2.90.21). 4) Lakshmana, in the tree, reaches for his bow (2.96.17).
  Each time he answers softly and keeps walking. He goes to the sage on foot, "discarding his weapons and ornaments" (2.90.1-2). Seeing the grass bed he vows:
  "From today onwards, I shall sleep on the earth or on the grass, with my locks matted, wearing robes of bark" (2.88.26). Rama still says no (2.112.18).
  So he asks for one small thing: "Pray mount with your feet on the wooden sandals adorned with gold!" (2.112.21). Rama steps on and off and gives them (2.112.22).
  Bharata carries them on his head (2.113.1, 2.115.12). He does not go to live in the palace: "I am going to Nandigrama village." (2.115.2). There, in bark, for fourteen years,
  he holds an umbrella over the sandals (2.115.16, 25) and tells them every matter first (2.115.27).
- Source words (Murthy): "Whatever a small affair of the state came up or whenever a high-valued gift was offered, Bharata used to report that matter to the wooden sandals and then only
  dealt with it in the proper way afterwards." (2.115.27)
- Griffith: "The rule and all affairs of state / I to these shoes will delegate." ; "Suspect me not: ne'er come the time / For me to plot so foul a crime!" ; "And with the sandals on his head / To Nandigrám he quickly sped."
- Surprise: the one who was handed everything is the one nobody trusts: a mother, a friend, a sage and his own brother all think the worst. He does not win by arguing. He wins by
  what he does every single day for fourteen years. And he lives the same hard life as the brother in the forest, by choice, in a village outside the city.
- Danger: he walks toward people who are ready to stop him: boats on the river, a bow in a tree. And the worst: the brother he loves may believe it too.
- Practice (patience, keeping the place, same thing every day): you cannot talk people out of what they think of you. You show up to the same small act every day. He reported to the
  sandals first, every time. That is a habit system: same act, same place. And he kept his brother's place open until he came back: what a teacher does with your spot when you are away.
  (Link type: meaning.)
- Differences: "wooden" is Murthy's word (gloss: "paaduke = (these) wooden sandals", then "hemabhuuSite" = adorned with gold); Griffith "with gold bedecked". Say "sandals with gold on them" or just "sandals".
- Not in Valmiki: the sandals kept in the palace of Ayodhya. In Valmiki they are at Nandigrama, a village to the east (2.115.10), and Bharata does not live in the palace.
  Do not say he took the sandals off Rama's feet: the text says Rama stepped onto them and off (2.112.22; 2.113.13 "turning to the East").
- Safety: thrones are never the point: say "his brother's place" or "the high seat", not throne, crown or regent. Tell it as a brother keeping a place. NEVER write "King Rama"
  (Griffith's Canto CXV has "Until King Ráma come again": do not quote that line). OUT: the vow to enter fire (2.112.25): say "I wait fourteen years and not one day more".
  OUT his curses (2.75.21-58) and hard words to his mother. No war: the army never fights, and he orders it not to trample the hermitage (2.97.29).
- Asana hook: none found by name. Say so.

## Bench (read, usable, not in the 8)
- Sumitra, mother of Lakshmana (2.40.3-9; 2.44.1-31; Griffith XL, XLIV). The one parent who does not ask anyone to stay. Weeping, she repeats "fare forth, fare forth!" (2.40.8) and gives the
  order: "Know Rama to be Dasaratha. Look upon Seetha the daughter of Janaka as myself. Consider the forest as Ayodhya and depart happily, my son!" (2.40.9). Then she is the one who comforts
  Kausalya: "While people are to be consoled by you at this moment, why are you fostering grief in your heart?" (2.44.25). Griffith: "Let Ráma Daśaratha be, / Look upon Sítá as on me, /
  And let the cot wherein you dwell / Be thine Ayodhyá. Fare thee well." (Griffith says "cot"; the Sanskrit gloss has "aTaviim = forest".) Practice: take home with you: the same practice wherever
  you are. Why bench: no danger in the scene, small fork, no asana name. Best use: the opening of a Lakshmana episode. Safety: skip 2.40.7 (dying in battle). Her two shares of the gift are in the Bala bench.
- Rama, the choice (2.18.1-41, 2.19.1-40; Griffith XVIII-XIX). The father says one word. Rama promises before he hears: "Rama does not speak two things." (2.18.30). Kaikeyi tells him.
  He: "Let it be, as you said it." (2.19.2). He walks round the things laid out for his big day (2.19.31) and "The people adjacent to Rama could not visualise any change in the face of Rama" (2.19.36).
  Folded into pick 2. Best-known character, so not a pick of its own.
- Bharadvaja at the meeting of the rivers (2.54.8-43, 2.55.1-22; 2.90; 2.91; Griffith LIV-LV, XC-XCI). He offers his own ashram: "You stay here comfortably." (2.54.22). Rama says no: people
  will find me "easy to see at this place" (2.54.24-25). The sage names a far hill (gloss "dashakroshe"; the translator makes it sixty miles, a note on the page says eighty: give no number),
  walks them part of the way "as a father would do for his sons" (2.55.2), gives the road: follow
  the Yamuna west, make a raft yourselves, greet the dark banyan: "It is the way to Chitrakuta. I visited that place many times." (2.55.9). They build the raft with their own hands (2.55.14).
  Later he asks Bharata the hard question though he already knows the answer (2.90.21), and feeds the whole army until the soldiers say "We shall not go either to Ayodhya or to Dandaka forest."
  (2.91.60). Practice: a teacher who has walked the road many times gives the next step and turns back; the first river you cross in a friend's boat, the second on a raft you build.
  Why bench: Bharadvaja was used twice (Ep. 3; Yavakri story), Bharadvajasana is Intermediate, and the feast has wine and dancers (tell as "food and music" only). In this site's text
  Rama also meets Valmiki himself at Chitrakuta (2.56.16-17): recension detail, check before using.
- Anasuya, the very old wife of the sage Atri (2.117.5-28, 2.118.1-30; Griffith CXVII-CXVIII). Her body "constantly shook like a banana tree in a storm" (2.117.17-18); in a ten-year drought she
  made fruit grow and the river flow (2.117.9-10). She offers Sita a wish. Sita: "Everything stands fulfilled" (2.118.16). She gives gifts that never fade (2.118.18-19), then asks the young
  woman for a story: "I wish to hear that narrative in detail." (2.118.25), and Sita tells her own beginnings until night. A bedtime story inside the story. Why bench: her teaching is the
  duty of wives (2.117.20-28), hard to tell today; small fork. Note for Bala pick 6: "placing a garland" in 2.118.24 is the translator's footnote on the word svayamvara, not the verse.
- The citizens at the Tamasa (2.45, 2.46.17-34; Griffith XLV-XLVII): the whole city follows and sleeps under the trees; before dawn Rama has the chariot driven north and back so they lose the track
  and go home. Good image, but the hero slips away from people who love him: easy to tell wrong.
- Vasishtha and the bark dress (2.37.21-36): the family priest stops the dress. Folded into pick 4. Vasishtha scenes were used before (check stories-used).
- UNSAFE, do not use: Dasharatha and the hermit's son (2.63-64; Griffith LXIII-LXIV): a young prince shoots at a sound in the dark, "the sound of a pitcher being filled up", thinking it an elephant.
  A boy dies, blind parents, a curse that kills. Breaks three safety rules.

# AYBKK shala wall: quote shortlist

## Recommended pick: Yoga Sūtra 1.14

**Long time. No break. With care.**
स तु दीर्घकालनैरन्तर्यसत्कारासेवितो दृढभूमिः
*Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra 1.14*

This sutra states the Habit Gap in Patañjali's own words. Practice becomes firm ground (dṛḍhabhūmiḥ) only when it is kept up for a long time (dīrghakāla), without breaks (nairantarya) and with care (satkāra). That is the AYBKK system: the same practice at the same time for years, with a teacher who notices. It is also Guruji's teaching without his name on the wall. In his 1994 Yoga International interview he gave this sutra in his own English ("Practicing a long time with respect and without interruption brings perfection ... your entire life long, you practice"). A former student, Corigliano (2017), writes that she heard it "countless times" in his afternoon conferences. Sharath's Pure Dedication Award honours this same kind of long, unbroken practice. So the line carries the lineage but avoids the two problems with "all is coming": the abuse record now attached to Jois's name, and the ironic reading since Remski's 2019 book. The Sanskrit matches Wikisource, sanskritdocuments and GRETIL letter for letter. It says nothing about God as long as satkāra is rendered "care" and not "devotion". The six English words read from the back of the room and use basic vocabulary, so they translate cleanly into Thai, Chinese and Russian. A beginner reads it as instructions for the next 12 weeks. A 10-year practitioner reads it as a description of their own practice. If Boonchu wants Guruji's own voice instead, take #2. If the wall has room for two lines, put #1 with #4: together they say "Traditional Ashtanga. Open door." (a strict method, and no effort is wasted).

### 1. Yoga Sūtra 1.14 (recommended)
- **Original:** स तु दीर्घकालनैरन्तर्यसत्कारासेवितो दृढभूमिः ॥ १.१४ ॥
- **IAST:** sa tu dīrghakāla-nairantarya-satkārāsevito dṛḍhabhūmiḥ (the hyphens only help reading; GRETIL prints it without them)
- **Short wall version:** paint the whole sutra on one line. You can drop ॥ १.१४ ॥ if space is tight. Cutting it to the three stems दीर्घकाल · नैरन्तर्य · सत्कार leaves something that is not a sentence, so do that only if a Sanskrit teacher approves.
- **Plain English line:** Long time. No break. With care. (6 words). If the wall has room: Long time. No break. With care. Then it stands firm. (10 words)
- **Source line:** Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra 1.14
- **Notes:**
  - On this wall satkāra means care, respect or earnestness. Do not use "devotion", which is how Bryant and Hariharananda translate it.
  - Nairantarya means not dropping out. It does not mean "no rest days", so tell the translators that Saturdays and moon days stay.
  - If you want a translator's English with a credit, use Charles Johnston (1912, public domain): "This becomes a firm resting-place, when followed long, persistently, with earnestness." The Iyengar, Bryant and Desikachar wordings are under copyright.
  - Guruji's 1994 paraphrase belongs on the orientation page or in captions, not on the wall.

### 2. Guruji: "Do your practice and all is coming"
- **Original (Guruji's English):** Do your practice and all is coming
- **IAST:** none (this is English)
- **Short wall version:** Do your practice and all is coming
- **Plain English line** (for the translators; keep his words on the wall): Do your practice, and all will come. (7 words)
- **Source line:** Sri K. Pattabhi Jois (or "Guruji"; choose on purpose)
- **Source:** a signed message on page 1 of Lino Miele, *Astanga Yoga*. The copy checked is the © 1996 second edition, so **do not paint or cite 1994**: nothing shows the message was in the first printing. Named students also remember him saying it: David Williams ("Practice yoga, and all is coming!", from the 1970s), Eddie Modestini, Bhavani Maki and Kino MacGregor.
- **Risks:**
  - Wikipedia states as fact that Jois sexually abused some students, and Sharath said in 2019 that he witnessed improper adjustments.
  - Remski's 2019 book is titled *Practice and All Is Coming: Abuse, Cult Dynamics, and Healing in Yoga and Beyond*, so some long-time practitioners read the line ironically.
  - Some people read it as a promise of results.
  - It is the most repeated line in the method, so it says "Ashtanga" but not "AYBKK".
  - Keep his Indian English, and tell the TH/ZH/RU translators not to "correct" it.

### 3. Bhagavad Gītā 6.23, second half
- **Original (full verse):** तं विद्याद् दुःखसंयोगवियोगं योगसंज्ञितम् । स निश्चयेन योक्तव्यो योगोऽनिर्विण्णचेतसा ॥ ६-२३॥
- **IAST (wall half):** sa niścayena yoktavyo yogo 'nirviṇṇacetasā
- **Short wall version:** स निश्चयेन योक्तव्यो योगोऽनिर्विण्णचेतसा. Leave out the first half ("severance from union with pain").
- **Plain English line:** Practice with determination. Do not lose heart. (7 words)
- **Source line:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.23
- **Notes:**
  - anirviṇṇa-cetasā means "with a mind that does not get weary or fed up". This is the Gita's direct answer to quitting.
  - The English is AYBKK's own condensed rendering. Sivananda has "practised with determination and with an undesponding mind". Do not paint a modern translator's sentence under their name.
  - Tell the translators that "lose heart" means "get discouraged".
  - The avagraha ऽ in योगोऽनिर्विण्ण must be painted.

### 4. Bhagavad Gītā 2.40, first quarter
- **Original (full verse):** नेहाभिक्रमनाशोऽस्ति प्रत्यवायो न विद्यते । स्वल्पमप्यस्य धर्मस्य त्रायते महतो भयात् ॥ २-४०॥
- **IAST (wall part):** nehābhikramanāśo 'sti
- **Short wall version:** नेहाभिक्रमनाशोऽस्ति. It is a complete sentence on its own: "here there is no loss of effort".
- **Plain English line:** Here, no effort is lost. (5 words)
- **Source line:** Bhagavad Gītā 2.40
- **Notes:**
  - It speaks to the beginner on day one and to the student coming back after a gap.
  - Leave out the second half (स्वल्पमप्यस्य धर्मस्य ...), because "dharma" reads as religion.
  - Purohit's version: "On this Path, endeavour is never wasted."
  - Some may read it as "skipping is fine". The teaching around it should be "come back tomorrow".
  - Tell the translators that "here" means this practice, this room.

### 5. Guruji: "Practice, practice, practice."
- **Original (Guruji's English):** Practice, practice, practice. That's it. This was his answer to "What exactly would mind medicine be?"
- **IAST:** none (this is English)
- **Short wall version:** Practice, practice, practice. You can add his "That's it." at the end.
- **Plain English line:** Practice, practice, practice. That is all. (6 words)
- **Source line:** Sri K. Pattabhi Jois, 1994. The line also works with no credit.
- **Source:** interview with Sandra Anderson, *Yoga International*, Jan-Feb 1994. I read it only in Elise Espat's Ashtanga Yoga Library repost (5 Dec 2024) and found no second copy. See the magazine itself before painting the year.
- **Notes:** this is the only Guruji line that needs no name, so it sidesteps the reputational question. It is the shortest and easiest to translate. Its weakness is that it is generic: any teacher says it.

**Runner-up worth knowing:** अभ्यासात्सिद्धिमाप्नोति / abhyāsāt siddhim āpnoti / "By practice, one succeeds." This is Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā 1.64, and Guruji quotes the verse twice in *Yoga Mala* to say that anyone can practice. It works well as a small second Sanskrit line. Source line: "Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā 1.64, quoted in Guruji's Yoga Mala". If you ever paint the full verse, use the corrected text: युवा वृद्धोऽतिवृद्धो वा व्याधितो दुर्बलोऽपि वा । अभ्यासात्सिद्धिमाप्नोति सर्वयोगेष्वतन्द्रितः ॥ १.६४ ॥

## Lines to avoid

| Line | Why |
|---|---|
| "Without devotion, nothing" | Found on no page at all, not even on quote sites. Treat it as misattributed. |
| "The full ashtanga system practiced with devotion leads to freedom within one's heart." (Jois letter to Yoga Journal, Nov 1995) | The line is real, but it says "devotion", it is 13 words, and it comes from a letter attacking power yoga. |
| "Practice, practice, practice, and all is coming." | Found only on quote sites such as Asivana. It is not a recorded wording. Use #2 or #5. |
| "Yoga is 99% practice, 1% theory." credited to Jois | Rests only on students' memories (Sharath's 2009 foreword, Modestini in 2015). His own recorded words in 1994 were "95 percent practical. Only 5 percent is theory." The house rule says no unchecked number in public. |
| "Anyone ... can practice yoga, except those who are lazy." (Yoga Mala), and the meme "Young man can practice. Old man can practice ... lazy people can't" | "Lazy" scolds the beginners that "Open door" invites. Mallinson says the Sanskrit atandritaḥ primarily means "not tired". The meme has no source, and both versions are long. |
| "Yoga is an internal practice. The rest is just a circus." / "Ashtanga yoga is internal exercise. The rest is all circus." | Both are sourced (Bhavani Maki 2009; Rajaiah interview 2002), but they mock other styles and visitors. |
| "Yoga is for internal purification, not external display." | No source, and the same quote list gets other lines wrong. |
| "Body is not stiff, mind is stiff." | Known only from students' memories (Kino MacGregor), and she adds that it "isn't true for everyone". On a wall it tells a stiff or injured beginner that the problem is in their head. |
| Yoga Sūtra 2.1 (tapaḥsvādhyāyeśvarapraṇidhānāni kriyāyogaḥ) | Every translator renders the third term as surrender to God. Cutting it to "tapaḥ svādhyāya" misquotes the sutra. |
| Yoga Sūtra 1.20 (śraddhā ...) | śraddhā is usually translated "faith", and the line is too long. |
| Gītā 2.47 (karmaṇy evādhikāras te ...) | Overused. "Never for the results" argues against selling the outcome and against the 12-week proof check. It also comes from the battlefield speech and runs 10 words in English. |
| Gītā 6.26, first half alone | It is an unfinished clause: "bring it back" is in the second half. The full verse is too long, and a popular translation ends "focus it on God". |
| Gītā 6.16 | No quarter of the verse stands alone, it ends with "Arjuna", and the short form is only a paraphrase. |
| Any 1.14 translation that uses "devotion" (Bryant, Hariharananda) | Reads as worship in China and Russia. Use "care". |

**Often misspelled or set wrong:**
- सिद्धिं आप्नोति is wrong. Write सिद्धिमाप्नोति / siddhim āpnoti (with m, not ṃ).
- The first word of HYP 1.64 is युवो in the e-texts but युवा in the Adyar edition and in Yoga Mala. A Sanskritist should settle it before the full verse is painted. The short part is not affected.
- HYP verse numbers differ between editions: Adyar and Yoga Mala 1.64, Pancham Sinh 1.66, GRETIL and sanskritdocuments 1.67. Paint 1.64, which is Guruji's number.
- The visarga ः is sometimes typed as a colon (holy-bhagavad-gita.org does this). The avagraha ऽ is sometimes dropped, or typed as an apostrophe inside Devanagari.
- "sthira sukham āsanam" with a space is wrong; write sthirasukham āsanam. "dṛḍha-bhūṃiḥ" (Corigliano's abstract) is wrong; write dṛḍhabhūmiḥ.
- Write "yat tad agre", not "yat tadagre". Next to the Devanagari धनञ्जय write "dhanañjaya", not "dhanaṃjaya". Next to कदाचन write "kadācana", not "kadā cana".
- Do not copy typos from these websites:
  - "perservance" (Gambhirananda)
  - "perfom" (Adidevananda)
  - "Eanimity" (a glitch on the IIT Kanpur site)
  - "contemplation of by discernment" (the site's copy of Satchidananda)
- Do not copy Yoga Mala's own slips "vyadito" and "siddimapnoti".
- Never copy Devanagari from the sacred-texts mirror (it has वयाधितो, करिया, परजायते).
- Under #2, do not paint "1994".
- "You must continue no problem": do not add punctuation and then present it as his exact words. His answer goes on to blame food, so do not present it as a teaching about habit.

## Painting and typesetting checks
1. Set the Devanagari in a font tested for Sanskrit. Peter Scharf (Indology list, 2023) found that Shobhika gets nearly every conjunct right, and judged Mangal, Devanagari MT and Arial Unicode MS "inadequate for Sanskrit". The shala's fonts, Long Cang and IBM Plex Sans Thai, have no Devanagari. IBM Plex Sans Devanagari would match the house type, but it was not in that test, so proof it first.
2. Do not trust Adobe layout without checking it. Balogh (2022) reports that it splits conjuncts and puts the short-i sign in the wrong place. Check र्घ न्त र्य त्का in 1.14, ण्ण in 6.23, and त्सि द्धि in HYP 1.64.
3. Print a full-size proof, and have a Sanskrit reader sign it off before the painter starts. Tape it up and read it from the last mat.
4. Paint the IAST with every diacritic, or leave the IAST line off. Do the same for Sūtra, Gītā and Patañjali in the source lines: all diacritics everywhere or none.
5. Use one spelling throughout: American "practice" for both the noun and the verb.
6. Have native speakers write the TH/ZH/RU lines from the plain English line. For #1, tell them that "no break" means not dropping out, not "no rest days".

# Wall lines in Thai, Chinese and Russian (Top 5)

These are drafts. A native speaker of each language should approve them before painting (painting check 6). None of the lines uses a dash. The Russian lines all use "ты" to match Guruji's direct voice. Print every "ё" with its dots. None of the lines needs the words for asana or Mysore. The Chinese lines do not use 最 or 第一.

- TH: ฝึกให้นาน ไม่ขาดช่วง ด้วยความใส่ใจ แล้วการฝึกจะมั่นคง
- ZH: 长久练习，持续不断，用心对待，根基自然稳固。
- RU: Долго. Не бросая. С душой. Тогда практика обретёт твёрдую почву. (твёрдая почва means "firm ground", which is a direct rendering of dṛḍhabhūmiḥ)

| ZH | 练习，练习，再练习。就这样。 | 再练习 is how Chinese naturally says it three times. Three identical 练习 would read stiff. 就这样 means "That's it". Avoid 仅此而已, which sounds dismissive. |
| RU | Практика, практика, практика. Вот и всё. | This is a noun because he was naming the "medicine". Практикуй would turn it into a command. всё keeps its dots. |

1. STRONGER: In a small study, 16 women aged 35 to 50 did Ashtanga twice a week for 8 months. Their legs pushed 11.4% more weight. Other strength tests, heart and blood pressure numbers did not change.
2. POWER: In endurance studies, not yoga, the total time you train over weeks may be key to building more mitochondria, the tiny power plants in muscle. Harder effort helps each one work better.
3. SUGAR: During exercise and for a while after, muscles take more sugar out of the blood. Over time, more tiny blood vessels grow in muscle. Exercise studies, not yoga. How much is best? Not all agree.
4. HEAT: Sports experts say training in the heat again and again for 1 to 2 weeks helps a body new to the heat get used to it. This is advice for athletes, not yoga.
5. FEEL: Walkers in robot leg braces found the new way of stepping that cost the least energy within minutes. Later tests from the same lab found most people first had to feel the easier way.
6. DAILY: 14 people practised a finger pattern 15 minutes a day for 4 weeks. Activity moved inside a deep part of the brain, from its thinking side to its movement side. Small study, not yoga.
7. SLEEP: A review of 66 studies found that people who exercise regularly fall asleep a bit faster and sleep better, with mostly small effects (not a yoga review).
8. REST: Sports experts agree that hard training pays off only with enough rest, and too little rest can leave athletes tired, slower and moody (linking this to our weekly day off is our reading).
9. COUNTS: In 38 adults, an hour of flow yoga counted as medium exercise, a little easier than fast walking, though heart rate made it look harder. Flow yoga, not Ashtanga.
10. ENOUGH: In about 660,000 adults, moving more went with a longer life, up to 450 to 750 medium-effort minutes a week. Most of the gain came by the basic 150. A link, not proof. Not yoga.

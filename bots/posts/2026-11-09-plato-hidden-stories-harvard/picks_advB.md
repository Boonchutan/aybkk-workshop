## Step 1. Are the asanas really in Advanced B?

Independent source (not Sweeney, not AYI): Yogapedia, "Fourth Series of Ashtanga Yoga" (https://www.yogapedia.com/?p=78758).
Its list: Mulabandhasana, Nahushasana, Vrschikasana, Sayanasana, Buddhasana, Kapilasana, then variations of Padangustha
Dhanurasana and Marichyasana, Parsva Bakasana, and so on. So Shayanasana, Buddhasana and Kapilasana are in the 4th series, in that order.
AYI's own menu (fetched again today) says the same: "Series / 4th Series - Sthira Bhaga" with Mūla Bandhāsana, Nahuṣāsana A B C,
Vṛścikāsana, Śayanāsana, Buddhāsana, Kapilāsana.
Caveat: the place number depends on how you count. AYI counts Nahusasana A, B, C apart (Buddhasana = 7th). Yogapedia counts one
Nahushasana (Buddhasana = 5th). Do not say "7th" on screen. Say "after the scorpion and the elbow balance".

## PICK 1 (C1). Buddhasana. Working title: "Step Four, Pull Your Ears"

Why: the best story of the five and the best fit for Boonchu's own cue (28 Oct): "The hard moment in the practice is not always
because you cannot do it, it is because you are tired." The fail is real and kind: a great student falls asleep while meditating.
The teacher sees it from far away and comes. He does not scold. He gives one small try, then the next. The last step is not
"try harder". It is "rest, then get up quickly". It is very concrete (ears, water, stars), so a 5-year-old gets it.
The asana link is the strongest kind: buddha means "awakened, awake" (MW p. 733), AYI calls it "the posture of the Buddha",
literally "der Erwachte" (the awakened one). The Buddha is the teacher in the story, and the story is about staying awake.
Surprise: Western, Chinese and Russian practitioners almost never know this sutta. Thai readers of dharma may know it
(trueplookpanya.com tells it as "how to beat sleepiness"), but even then the ear pulling and the stars are the funny, fresh part.
Fail, Hero, Payoff: falls asleep (fail), the teacher comes with small steps (hero), rest and get up quickly (payoff).
Habit Gap: people stop because they are tired, feel bad, then stay away. Tired is the signal to change one small thing, not to stop.

Hook: #55 adapted (contrarian, the viewer blames the body): "Hard asana not coming? Many times, you are just tired."
Backup: #86 adapted, "This video is for the morning you are too tired."
Harvard tricks: #1 fork ("Your best student falls asleep. You are the teacher. What you do?"), #21 (your problem is often tiredness,
not your body; it is Boonchu's own cue), #20 (cost: "If tired mean stop, you stop most mornings"), #30 (one comment word). Max 4.
Stakes: if "tired" means "stop", you stop on most hard mornings, and the habit dies quietly.
Why care: every 6 am practitioner has tired mornings and secretly thinks it means they are not good enough.

### Verified facts (I read them myself)
Main text: Anguttara Nikaya 7.61 "Nodding Off" (Pacalāyamānasutta), tr. Bhikkhu Sujato, SuttaCentral, with the Pali on the same page.
- an7.61:1.2 the Buddha was staying in the land of the Bhaggas at "Crocodile's Bellow" (Susumāragira).
- 1.3 "in the land of the Magadhans near Kallavāḷamutta Village, Venerable Mahāmoggallāna was nodding off while meditating."
  Pali "pacalāyamāno nisinno hoti". So: a different land, far away.
- 1.4 "The Buddha saw him with his clairvoyance that is purified and superhuman."
- 1.6 "as easily as a strong person would extend or contract their arm, he vanished ... and reappeared in front of Mahāmoggallāna".
- 1.7 "He sat down on the seat spread out".
- 2.1 "Are you nodding off, Moggallāna? Are you nodding off?" 2.2 "Yes, sir." No scolding anywhere in the text.
- The steps, each one after "But what if that doesn't work?":
  2.3 do not keep your mind on the thing you were thinking about when you got drowsy;
  3.1 think over the teaching you learned; 4.1 say the teaching out loud in detail;
  5.1 "Then pinch your ears and rub your limbs." (Pali "ubho kaṇṇasotāni āviñcheyyāsi": pull both ears);
  6.1 "get up from your seat, flush your eyes with water, look around in every direction, and look up at the stars and constellations.";
  7.1 think of light, "as by day, so by night"; 8.1 "walk mindfully, focusing on the perception of before and behind";
  9.1 "lie down in the lion's posture—on the right side, placing one foot on top of the other—mindful and aware, and focused on the time of getting up."
  9.2 "When you wake, you should get up quickly". Every step ends "It's possible that you'll give up drowsiness in this way."
- Count: SuttaCentral's own markup numbers the tries 1 to 7 (2.3 to 8.1); the lie-down (9.1) is the last fallback, so 8 in all.
  Thai and English retellings say "8 ways". Ears = step 4 in both counts.
- Rank: dn14:1.9.7 "I have a fine pair of chief disciples named Sāriputta and Moggallāna." an1.190 "… with psychic power is Mahāmoggallāna."
- Name: MW p. 733 buddha "awakened, awake ... conscious, intelligent, wise". Sweeney gloss: "Enlightened posture".
- Asana tip (AYI Buddhasana page, German): right after you hold the right shin, lie down diagonally to the left on the forearm
  (elbow about 90 degrees). The foot slides under the armpit much more easily. Then sit up.

### What NOT to say (C1)
- Not "the Buddha's best student". He is one of two chief students (dn14:1.9.7). Say "one of the Buddha's two top students".
- No hard number on screen ("8 steps"). Counts differ (7 numbered tries + the lie-down). "Step four? Pull your ears" is safe.
- Do not put "pull your ears" in quote marks. Sujato's exact words are "pinch your ears and rub your limbs". Retold, no marks, is fine.
- Never "lazy", never "the Buddha was angry" or "scolded". He was meditating and his head dropped.
- Do not explain powers. Say "he saw it from far away" and "he was there, as easy as you bend your arm" (the text's own picture).
- Do not say "his 7th day as a monk" or "he practised too hard" (Thai commentary lines, not in the sutta, unchecked).
- No sleep or health advice ("this cures tiredness", "naps are good for you"). It is an old story about practice.
- No AI image or statue of the Buddha. Show a seat, a lamp, a water bowl, stars, a monk from behind, Boonchu's own ears.
- Never compare Boonchu or AYBKK to the Buddha. Keep "at AYBKK a teacher notices when you miss class" in the caption only,
  never straight after the line where the Buddha comes.
- Leave out the rest of the sutta (visiting families with vanity, arguments, the craving question).
- Do not call Buddhasana "7th". Do not promise the asana "wakes you up".
- Post at least a week apart from Ep. 6 (both have a teacher who sees the student). C1's point is tiredness, Ep. 6's is the teacher's "no".
- Do not use hook #121 (Ep. 6 has it), even though the researcher suggested it.

### Story beats (6 lines)
1. H (#55 adapted, contrarian): "Hard asana not coming? Many times, you are just tired." (On screen: YOU ARE JUST TIRED)
2. DH (open loop): One of the Buddha's two top students fell asleep in practice. His teacher came from far away. Step four? Pull your ears. For real.
3. T1 to FORK: Bedtime story for my son. Moggallana sits to meditate in a village. His head drops. Again. The Buddha is in another land,
   far away, and he sees it. FORK (#1): "Your best student falls asleep. You are the teacher. What you do?"
4. RH (TWIST): He is there in front of him, as easy as you bend your arm. He sits down. "Are you nodding off, Moggallana?" "Yes, sir."
   No scolding. Just one small try. Think of something else. Not working? Pull both your ears. Rub your arms and legs.
5. T2 (worst moment): Still sleepy. Get up. Water on your eyes. Look up at the stars. Walk slowly. Still sleepy. Everything tried.
   (#20 in the story's voice: "If tired mean stop, practice stop.")
6. P: Last step. Lie down on your right side. Keep in mind the time to get up. When you wake, get up quickly.
   Adults: Buddhasana. Buddha means awake. It comes after many hard asanas, so you are already tired. Change one small thing
   [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less]. Close: "Tomorrow you are tired on the mat. You stop? Or you pull your ears?" Then "แล้วเจอกันครับ".

## PICK 2 (C3). Kapilasana. Working title: "The Mother Who Did Not Move"

Why: the asana link is direct (Kapila = "an ancient sage ... considered as the founder of the Sāṃkhya system", MW p. 250;
AYI "the posture of Kapila"). The story answers one of the biggest reasons adults never start or never come back:
"too much time has gone by". The twist is clean and a child gets it: the great teacher's student is his own mother.
The fork is real: the teacher leaves. She does not go looking for a new place. She stays in the same house, does what he taught,
and the book says she got there "soon". Same place, same practice fits the habit message.
Surprise: most Ashtanga practitioners know Kapila only as a name tied to Sankhya. Few know the mother story (Bhagavata Purana, book 3).
The breath line has "sthira" (steady), the same word as Sthira Bhaga.
Why only second: the tension is softer than C1 (the fail is only her feeling that time has gone), and "the teacher leaves" sits a little
against the AYBKK line "a teacher who notices". Frame it as "same seat, the way he taught", never "you don't need a teacher".

Hook: #83 adapted, made contrarian: "You think too much time has gone by for you? She thought so too."
Backup: #104 adapted ("The seat you keep decides how far you go"), only if Boonchu wants the "same place" angle first.
Harvard tricks: #1 fork ("Teacher gone. What you do?"), #27 because (the text gives it: she followed "the path Kapila taught" in "that very"
house, 3.33.13 and 3.33.30), #20 (cost: "If 'too late' keep you off the mat, next year you are one year later"), #30.
Stakes: if you believe you are too late, you never start, and next year you are one year later.
Why care: most adults come to the mat late, after work and family, and many think the good years for practice are gone.

### Verified facts (I read them myself, vedabase.io, Sanskrit on the same pages)
Bhagavata Purana, Canto 3 (Prabhupada translation; use the Sanskrit sense where his English adds ideas).
- 3.23.53: "etāvatālaṁ kālena vyatikrāntena" (enough time has gone by). NOTE: she says this to her HUSBAND Kardama, before Kapila is born.
  The time she means is years of married pleasure (3.23.44 "for many, many years, which passed just like a moment"; 3.23.45).
- 3.24.40 (Kapila to his father): he will teach this knowledge "to My mother", so "she also will be freed from all material fear".
- 3.24.41: Kardama leaves for the forest. 3.25.5: "When Kardama left for the forest, Lord Kapila stayed on the strand of the Bindu-sarovara
  to please His mother". 3.25.6: Kapila sits before her and she questions him.
- 3.25.7: "nirviṇṇā nitarāṁ" (utterly weary); Prabhupada: "I am very sick of the disturbance caused by my material senses ...
  I have fallen into the abyss of ignorance" ("prapannāndhaṁ tamaḥ", fallen into blind darkness).
- 3.25.8: "sac-cakṣuḥ" ("You are my transcendental eye"), "janmanām ante" (at the end of many births).
- 3.28.8: "śucau deśe ... vijitāsana āsanam ... ṛju-kāyaḥ samabhyaset" (in a clean place spread a seat, body straight, practise).
- 3.28.9: breathe in, hold, breathe out (pūra-kumbhaka-recakaiḥ) "cittaṁ yathā sthiram acañcalam" (so the mind is steady, unshaking).
  These are Kapila's general yoga instructions in his teaching to her (chapter 28).
- 3.33.12: "kapilo 'numato yayau" (Kapila, given leave, went). 3.33.33: he left "with the permission of His mother and went towards the northeast".
- 3.33.13: "tasminn āśrama" ("in that very āśrama"), as her son taught her, by the river Sarasvati.
- 3.33.30: "kapiloktena mārgeṇācirataḥ" (by the path Kapila taught, soon) she reached the goal.
- 3.33.31: the place "is known all over the three worlds as Siddhapada" ("nāmnā siddha-padam", the place of the one who made it).
- 3.33.1: Kapila is called the author of the Sankhya system.
- Name: MW p. 250 kapila (also "brown, tawny"). AYI: Kapilasana = Buddhasana plus a forward fold over the straight leg and one more bind
  (right hand around the right thigh to the left wrist).

### What NOT to say (C3)
- Not "his first student". The text never says first, and Sankhya tradition names Asuri as Kapila's pupil. Say "in this story his student is his own mother".
- Do not put "so much time has gone by" in her talk with Kapila. Retold as "One day she says, so much time has gone by" is fine, but do not say to whom.
  Never quote Prabhupada's "wasted ... in sense gratification", and never say her married years were wasted.
- Not "she is old" and not "grey hair". No age is given. 3.33.14 "kapiśa" means tawny, not grey.
- No austerity: leave out bathing three times a day, the thin body, the old clothes (3.33.14).
- No death: leave out 3.33.32 (her body turns into a river). Say "she got there" or "she reached the goal", not "she was liberated".
- Do not use the purports (e.g. 3.33.13 "it is never recommended for a woman to leave her home"). Verses only.
- Respect: Kapila is a form of Vishnu in this book. No jokes about a son teaching his mother.
- Keep the husband to one line or leave him out. No "he left his family" lesson (Thai and Chinese family values).
- Never "you don't need a teacher" or "practise alone". The lesson is same seat, the way the teacher taught.
- Leave out the flying palace, the nine daughters' weddings, and Kapila's other story (the 60,000 sons, BhP 9.8, deaths).
- Do not mix in C4 (the god's-tears lake, Kardama's 10,000 years). One thread. At most one caption line.
- Do not call Sankhya "the philosophy behind yoga" as a fact. Say "a great teacher in the old books".

### Story beats (6 lines)
1. H (#83 adapted, contrarian): "You think too much time has gone by for you? She thought so too."
2. DH (open loop): Then her teacher left her alone. She did not move one step. And the old book says she got there soon.
3. T1 to FORK: Bedtime story for my son. Devahuti lives in a house by a lake. One day she says, so much time has gone by.
   "I feel lost in the dark. You are my eye," she tells a great teacher, Kapila. He teaches her. Clean quiet place. Sit straight.
   Breathe in, hold, breathe out, so the mind is steady. Then he leaves. FORK (#1): "Teacher gone. What you do?"
4. RH (TWIST): Who is this teacher? Her own son. The asana Kapilasana carries his name.
5. T2 (worst moment): Now the house is empty. Son gone. So much time already gone. Nobody to ask.
6. P: She stays. Same house. Same seat. Same breath he taught. And soon she gets there. People name the place Siddhapada, the place where she made it.
   Adults: Kapilasana is Buddhasana plus one more fold, one more bind. Same seat, one more small piece [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less].
   Close: "Too late for you? Or you never stay in one seat long enough?" Then "แล้วเจอกันครับ".

## Rejected
- C2 Shayanasana (Shesha): killed. (1) Weak asana link: Shayana means lying down, rest, couch (MW p. 1055). AYI's note ties it to
  Vishnu resting on the snake Shesha, but the story told is Shesha holding the earth, not the couch. Two steps away, and no text names
  the asana after Shesha. (2) The retelling leaves out a fact that changes it: Brahma's first words are a gentle correction,
  "thou art afflicting all creatures by thy hard penances" (Ganguli Adi Parva XXXVI; CE 1.32.7a "tvaṃ hi tīvreṇa tapasā prajās tāpayase").
  And the job comes with a "but" (1.32.18c "tvayā tv idaṃ vacaḥ kāryaṃ manniyogāt": but at my command do this), so "his wish is what
  made him the one who holds the world" says more than the text. (3) Safety: his motive is "wicked" brothers and a wish to "cast off this body"
  (1.32.12a "mokṣyāmīdaṃ kalevaram") so he never meets them again, and the "practice" he loves is living on air until skin and bone.
  With all that removed, little story is left, and "he left his family" plays badly in Thailand and China.
  (4) Brahma is already the hero of advA pick 2 (Viranchyasana) this week. (5) Medium surprise: Ashtanga people chant to Patanjali as the
  thousand-headed snake. It was also on the 10 Oct myths list (never posted).
- C4 Kapilasana (the lake of tears): not a Reel. The habit link is weak (waiting, no quitting, no teacher who notices). "Ten thousand years"
  and a king in a gold chariot (3.21.36) need hedging. At most one caption line for C3.
- C5 Buddhasana (Culla-Panthaka and the white cloth): killed. The cloth was already PUBLISHED on 5 Oct (carousel B "Find Another Way",
  bots/media/2026-10-05-ripen-another-way/B/slides.json), so it is not new to our followers. Thai Buddhists widely know it.
  "My brother told me go home" is too close to Ep. 6. And it would be a second Buddha story on Buddhasana next to C1.

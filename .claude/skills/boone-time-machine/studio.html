⚙️ Setup

1One-time setup (about 30 minutes, do once)

📸 Boone's reference kit

  1. Photo session with Boone — phone camera, bright indirect daylight (near a window, not direct sun), plain wall behind. Take: front face neutral, front face smiling, slight left profile, slight right profile, full body standing front. 8–10 shots, pick the sharpest.
  2. Choose ONE hero face photo — sharp, eyes open, even light, nothing covering the face. This exact photo is the face reference for every shot in the season. Never switch it mid-season or Boone's face will drift between episodes.
  3. The time-traveler outfit — put Boone in the same outfit for the full-body photo: bright orange t-shirt, khaki shorts, small brown backpack. This outfit appears in every era — it's how viewers instantly spot "the visitor from now" among the ancients, and it keeps Kling consistent.
  4. Keep the reference photos private. They're your production asset — never post the raw reference set anywhere.

2How to get each shot in Kling

⚡ FORMAT UPDATE — the season shoots selfie-vlog

Episode 1 (The Mammoth and the Pencil) is fully built in its card below — recording script, first-frame + motion prompts, sync method per clip. The story source of truth stays in Obsidian → 07 Personal & Family / Boone's Time Machine. Whispered wide-angle selfie from Boone's outstretched arm, deliberately handheld and imperfect. That script's grammar is now the series style; the cinematic shots below are the backup format until each trip is converted.

  • Boone shots (vertical): start every prompt with "Wide angle selfie shot from a child's outstretched arm" + the BOONE token + the scene. End with "natural handheld movement, slightly shaky, vertical format, cinematic" — never "35mm film still".
  • World shots (horizontal): framed as what Boone films — documentary, slightly motion-blurred, photorealistic. Letterboxed in the vertical edit is part of the look; keep the key action center-frame so a crop also works.
  • Series props that never change: the small red backpack + the bright yellow pencil visible in the side pocket. Wardrobe changes per era; the backpack, pencil and face are how viewers know it's Boone.
  • Credit tiers for a ~22-prompt episode: 3.0 Turbo + face reference ONLY for the ~8 Boone selfie shots; cheaper model for every horizontal world shot; pure-scenery establishing shots can be stills with a CapCut move. That turns a ~2,200-credit episode into roughly half.
  • Lo-fi is the budget's friend: in this style, grain, shake and slight AI weirdness read as "real phone footage" — cull for face-likeness and era accuracy, not for polish.

Every episode uses the same 6-shot pattern

  • S1 — Arrival (Boone hero shot): Boone steps out of the glowing portal into the era. Same visual signature every episode.
  • S2 — The world (no Boone): wide establishing shot of the era and the invention site.
  • S3 — The mechanism (no Boone): close-up of the invention actually working.
  • S4 — Boone's job (Boone action): he helps make the invention happen.
  • S5 — The payoff (Boone hero shot): his face lit by the wonder of it.
  • S6 — The stamp (free!): screen-record the app's stamp ceremony at cn.aybkk.net/boone — finish the trip on your phone, record the STAMPED! overlay. Every video ends with it. Zero credits, instant branding.

🅰️ Boone shots — the two-slot rule, then two steps

  1. Two slots, two jobs. REFERENCE images (MASTER-FACE + costume sheet + real photo) tell Kling who. The START FRAME is literally frame 1 of the video — where and how the shot begins. Never put a master sheet in the start-frame slot: you get a boy on a white studio background, animated there.
  2. Step 1 — AI Images. Attach the masters as references → paste the shot's FIRST-FRAME prompt → 9:16 → generate 4 → cull (Grandma test, hands, era, no modern objects).
  3. The first-frame prompt is a freeze of the shot's first second, not the action. The action lives in the motion prompt.
  4. Step 2 — AI Video → Frames tab. Upload the approved still as START FRAME, leave end frame empty → paste the MOTION prompt → check the price on the pill → generate.
  5. Dialogue. Whisper lines: lips barely move, real whisper over it. Sacred lines: Kling Lip Sync with Boone's real audio. Quote-and-mute only where a clip card offers it — and MUTE the clip.
  6. Check the face stays stable; if it melts, simplify to one action. Save as t01-P7b.mp4 style names.

🅱️ Boone action shots (S4) — same recipe, one shortcut

Same steps as 🅰️, but a cheaper model is fine — the face is smaller in frame. If Boone is seen from behind or far away, you can even skip the face reference and just describe the outfit; the orange shirt + backpack carries the identity.

🅲 World shots (S2, S3) — the cheap ones

  1. AI Images, no reference → paste IMAGE prompt → 9:16 → generate → pick the best.
  2. AI Video → Frames tab → upload the still → paste MOTION prompt → switch the model picker (top right) to an older, cheaper model — scenery doesn't need 3.0 Turbo. For very simple motion (steam drifting, gears turning) direct Text-to-Video also works — but still-first wastes fewer credits, because you approve the look before paying for motion.

📱 Video 3.0 Turbo — three things to know

  • Quotation marks make characters SPEAK. Any quoted line in the prompt becomes AI-voiced dialogue (that's what the placeholder text means). Never quote lines — record real Boone instead. Describe sound without quotes: ambient sound: cold wind, distant hammering.
  • Free sound bed. 3.0 clips come with ambient audio — keep it playing low under the narration in CapCut and you may not need library sound effects at all.
  • Save the building blocks as presets. Prompt box → My Presets → Create: make three presets named BOONE, STYLE and NEG with the blocks from section 1. New prompts become two taps instead of copy-paste.

🎙 The voice plan — real dialogue, no fake-voice trap

Narration alone can feel flat. Dialogue is what makes it feel alive — but Boone's mouth only ever speaks Boone's real voice. An AI kid-voice on his AI face is what would actually make it feel cheap: wrong voice, different every clip, and a family channel's whole magic is that it's really him. Three layers instead:

  • Layer 1 — the spine (every video): your narration + Boone's real hook and STAMPED! lines. Already in every script.
  • Layer 2 — reaction dubs (every video): 2–3 short shouted lines by real Boone, laid over action shots where his face is small, turned, or mid-motion — no lip-sync needed, the ear forgives everything when a kid is yelling "IT'S FLYING!". Each episode card below now has a 🎤 recording sheet with the exact lines. Record them in the same Sunday session as a game: say it normal, say it SUPER excited, say it whispering — keep the funniest take.
  • Layer 3 — the wow moment (one per video): Kling Lip Sync on the S5 close-up. Generate the S5 clip first → open Lip Sync on it → upload Boone's recorded line (keep it under ~4 seconds) → the mouth syncs to his real voice. Works best when the face is front-facing and big in frame — which is exactly what S5 is. Costs extra credits, so exactly one per video.
  • Side characters — the one place AI voice is allowed: Amun, Tilly, Gutenberg and friends aren't real people, so one short AI-spoken line each adds cinema for free. This is the single exception to the no-quotes rule: add the quoted line to that shot's motion prompt (each recording sheet says which shot). Maximum one per video — it's a spice, not the dish.
  • Sync methods (as labelled on the Episode 1 clips): A = talk/pause motion + real whisper over it · B = quote-and-mute (quote the exact line so the mouth forms the right words, then MUTE the clip and lay Boone’s take over) · C = Kling Lip Sync with Boone’s recorded audio · D = voice-over a cutaway, no mouth on screen.
  • Mixing rule: narration pauses for dialogue — never talk over Boone's lines. A half-second of silence before "IT'S FLYING!" is what makes it land.

💰 Credit strategy (make the credits last all season)

  • Animate only 5 clips per episode, always 5 seconds — never 10s, the edit only uses ~4s per shot anyway.
  • On Video 3.0 Turbo at 1080p / 5s / 2 outputs, one Generate costs 100 credits — but gives two takes. Spend that on the face shots (S1, S5) where you want a choice of takes; tap the settings pill and drop to 1 output where your plan prices it cheaper.
  • Use 3.0 Turbo only for Boone shots. Switch the model picker to an older model for S2/S3 world shots — same prompt, fraction of the price.
  • Season math at full price: 5 shots × 100 = ~500 credits per episode, ~6,000 for the season — so the cheap-model and 1-output levers matter. If credits run low: S2 can be a still image with a slow CapCut zoom — zero video credits, still cinematic.
  • Generation fails or looks wrong twice in a row? Stop, simplify the prompt (remove one clause), try once more. Don't brute-force — that's the credit killer.

3The weekly rhythm — twice a week for six weeks

One week = two videos, about 3 hours total

  • Sunday (90 min, batch day): generate ALL shots for this week's two episodes — stills first for both, cull, then animate the winners. Record both narrations with Boone while he's fresh: he says the 🎤 lines (the hook and the STAMPED! shout), you read the rest. Phone mic, quiet room, blanket fort = free sound booth.
  • Monday (40 min): edit video 1 in CapCut. Wednesday: it posts.
  • Thursday (40 min): edit video 2. Saturday: it posts.
  • Record VO before editing and cut clips to the voice — never the other way around.

4Edit & upload checklists

✂️ CapCut edit order (same every time)

  1. New project 9:16 → drop the narration audio FIRST.
  2. Lay clips over the voice: S1 under the hook, S2–S4 under the story, S5 under the takeaway, S6 (stamp recording) last 3 seconds.
  3. Captions: use the caption lines from each shot below — bold white with black stroke, bottom-center, never more than 8 words on screen.
  4. Sound effects from CapCut's library: WHOOSH on the portal, CLANG / KA-CHUNK / rocket rumble where the script shouts them.
  5. Music: quiet, playful, from the YouTube Audio Library only (copyright-safe forever). Voice loud, music low.
  6. Export 1080×1920, highest quality.

⬆️ YouTube upload — every box, every time

  • Title format: If a 6-Year-Old Built the Sphinx 🐫 | Boone's Time Machine · Trip 2
  • Made for Kids: YES. Required (COPPA) — this also disables comments, which is why every video ends with a teaser, never "comment below".
  • Altered content / AI disclosure: YES. Realistic AI scenes of a real person must be declared — it's one toggle and it protects the channel.
  • First frame = the hook caption over the portal shot (Shorts use the first frame as the cover).
  • Add every video to the "Boone's Time Machine — Season 1" playlist, in trip order.
  • Description: one line of story + "Play the trip yourself: cn.aybkk.net/boone" (once the PR is merged).

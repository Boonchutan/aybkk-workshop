Add-on: the 4 steps, our way

14 Nov 2026. From a sales Reel by @shelby.sapp, kept only where it passes your pledge.

  1. See how they want to be seen. Ask: "What kind of practitioner, or teacher, do you want to become?" Listen for the words they use about themselves.
  2. Name only what is true. Never flatter. Name a fact you saw: "You came back the morning after your flight." "That's 30 mornings." A true label helps them keep acting like it. A fake one breaks trust.
  3. Let them talk. One question, then quiet. Then ask a follow-up about their answer. People like those who ask follow-up questions (Huang and others, Harvard, 2017), and talking about yourself feels like a reward (Tamir and Mitchell, 2012).
  4. One clear ask, never a put-down. Not "people today are too lazy to...". Saying a bad habit is common can make more people do it (Cialdini's team, petrified wood signs, 2006). Show the habit we want instead: "A dozen people practise side by side every morning."

WHY THIS FRAMEWORK EXISTS

Your goal is simple to state, hard to execute:

  1. Make people believe YOU can help them become better through Ashtanga
  2. Make them willing to pay for your programs, products, and personal instruction

Everything in this framework serves those two outcomes.

This is NOT manipulation. You genuinely believe you can help people. This framework is about making that belief VISIBLE to people who need it, without being "salesy," without ego, without pretending to be someone you're not.

The greatest persuaders in history were not born. They studied human nature systematically, then practiced relentlessly. This is your manual.

THE FIVE LAWS OF BOONCHU PERSUASION

Law 1: CREDIBILITY IS NON-NEGOTIABLE

Source: Carnegie, Art of Public Speaking

"Training in public speaking is not a matter of externals, primarily; it is not a matter of imitation, fundamentally. Public speaking is public utterance, public issuance, of the man himself; therefore the first thing both in time and in importance is that the man should be and think and feel things that are worthy of being given forth." Real, slightly changed

The Application:

Before you can persuade anyone, they must believe you are REAL. Not perfect. Real.

Your story is your credibility:

  • The injuries that led you to understand the body
  • The years of struggle that taught you patience
  • The students you've watched transform
  • The failures that taught you more than successes
Plato note It invites an injury story that is not on record. Plato's notebook already flags the 'back injury' story as invented. Fix: The years of daily practice that taught you the body (real events only, checked with Boonchu).

Rule: Never fabricate. Never exaggerate. Your genuine journey is more persuasive than any polished performance.

Boonchu Application:

  • Tell the story of your back injury BEFORE you teach a single posture
  • Let students see the moment you "didn't know everything" and what you did about it
  • Share failures as teaching tools, not weaknesses
Plato note The back injury story is invented (already flagged in plato.md). It also says 'posture' where our word is 'asana'. Fix: Tell one true story from your own practice before you teach an asana.

Law 2: KNOW WHAT THEY CAME FOR VS. WHAT THEY NEED

Source: Gracian, Art of Worldly Wisdom

Gracian wrote: "The wise man does not reveal everything he knows. Let the mystery grow, and others will come to learn it." Not his words

Plato note This is not Gracian's wording. It is a paraphrase put in quote marks and called a quote. Fix: Gracian, maxim 94 (Jacobs 1892): "Keep the extent of your abilities unknown." Say the rest in our own words, without quote marks.

This is not about hiding. This is about ASKING before TELLING.

Most students come to you with one problem (stiff shoulders, back pain, stress) but their real problem is deeper (a life out of control, body betraying them, feeling invisible in their own bodies).

Plato note It guesses at hidden pain and treats the guess as fact. Said in public, it shames the viewer and goes against the pledge at line 345 ('never make someone feel broken'). Fix: Most students come with one problem: stiff shoulders, back pain, stress. Ask what sits under it. Don't guess it for them.

The Process:

  1. Questions first. Before you fix them, understand what they're actually trying to escape.
  2. Mirror back. "So what I'm hearing is..." shows you listened.
  3. Reframe the problem. Move them from "fix my shoulder" to "reclaim your body."
  4. Then offer the solution. By the time you recommend your program, they already know you understand them.

Boonchu Application:

  • First conversation: Ask more than you tell
  • "What brought you here today?" then listen
  • "What have you tried before?" then listen more
  • Only then: "Here's what I see in you..."

Law 3: LET THEM DISCOVER THE TRUTH THEMSELVES

Source: Strauss, The Game + Carnegie

Plato note It cites a pickup-artist book as a source for teaching. Fix: Source: Socratic teaching + Carnegie.

The seduction community discovered something teachers often miss: People believe what they discover more than what they're told. Wrong author

Plato note Pickup-artist framing. This must never reach public content. Fix: Good teachers know this: people believe what they find out more than what they are told.

Socratic teaching is persuasive teaching. Lead them to the insight. Don't push it at them.

The Framework:

  1. Create an experience (your led class, your workshop, your content)
  2. Ask a question that reveals the gap: "Notice what happened when you tried to breathe here..."
  3. Let the insight land
  4. Then name what they just discovered
  5. Now offer the path forward

Example for Boonchu:

  • Student struggles with forward fold
  • You don't say "You're tight." You say: "What are you noticing in your hamstrings right now?"
  • They say: "I can't breathe properly when I'm here"
  • You say: "That's exactly what most people miss. The breath is the first thing to go when we're fighting our body. What if I told you Ashtanga isn't about touching your toes, it's about breathing first, everything else second?"

They discovered the truth. You just facilitated it.

Law 4: SOCIAL PROOF COMPOUNDS

Source: Strauss, The Game

Plato note It credits social proof to a pickup-artist book. Fix: Source: Cialdini, Influence (1984), 'social proof'.

In persuasion, one converted skeptic is worth more than ten people who already believe. Why?

Because skeptics who convert become your advocates. They tell others: "I was skeptical, but Boonchu actually helped me."

The Accumulation:

  1. Teach with excellence
  2. Students transform
  3. They tell others
  4. New students arrive pre-sold
  5. You spend less time convincing, more time teaching

The Rule: Every student is a long-term investment in your reputation, not a transaction.

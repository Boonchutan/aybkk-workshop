# Brief: Hidden Stories of Ashtanga, Harvard selling structure (9 Nov 2026)

## Who
Boonchu, 53, owner of AYBKK (Ashtanga Yoga Bangkok), authorized Ashtanga teacher. He films himself talking to camera.
Goal of every Reel: build trust and bring serious practitioners to AYBKK (Bangkok shala) and, later, the 180K teacher program.
Audiences: English, Thai, Chinese, Russian. Most viewers and Boonchu are not native English speakers.
Business direction (Habit Gap): people do not lack tools, they quit. AYBKK sells a habit system: same practice, same time,
a teacher who notices when you're gone. CONFIRMED FACT you may say: at AYBKK a teacher notices when you miss class.

## Boonchu's order for this job (his words, 9 Nov)
"use selling technique harvard with hook bank, double hook, tension, hook, tension..then payoff."
So every script runs in exactly this beat order:
1. H  = HOOK (0 to 3 s). Taken or adapted from the hook bank (file hook-bank.txt). Name the number (#n). Contrarian: it flips what the
   viewer believes and makes them question themselves. If it uses the Triple Hook (#121), line 1 must be SAID to camera, SHOWN
   (the picture matches) and WRITTEN on screen in the first second.
2. DH = DOUBLE HOOK (by about 0:05). A second hook: raises the stakes or opens a loop ("the teacher who said no is the one who...").
   Point lands in about 6 seconds total (3 short lines). Then straight into the story.
3. T1 = TENSION. The story starts, told straight, bedtime voice ("Bedtime story for my son."). Build to a FORK.
4. RH = REHOOK (about 0:20 to 0:30). A twist inside the story that resets attention. It stays in the story's voice.
5. T2 = MORE TENSION. The worst moment. What it costs if nothing changes.
6. P  = PAYOFF. The answer, then back to the adults: same thing in your practice, the asana, and a last line that leaves the viewer asking
   themselves a question.

## Harvard selling tricks to use (from Boonchu's note "Harvard and Selling Tricks", full text harvard.md)
Label in the script which trick works where. Use at least 3, never all.
- #1 Decide before you know the ending: stop the story at the fork. One short line: "What you do?" Then tell.
- #2 Sell the hole, not the drill: the viewer wants the result in their life, not "more yoga".
- #20 Ask about the cost of not fixing it (SPIN implication): what happens next year if this never changes.
- #21 Teach them something about their own problem: one insight about their practice they did not see.
- #27 Every claim needs a because.
- #30 Sell only the next step: the Reel sells ONE comment word. Nothing else. No price, no course, no link line.
Caption ask (keyword test, frozen until 22 Nov): Lane B (Bangkok shala, habit, teacher notices) = "Comment aybkk and I'll send you the link."
Lane A (an asana fix for practitioners) = caption ends with the STUCK ask and a 2 s end card "Comment STUCK"; never spoken.
Pick one lane per Reel and say which.

## Standing orders (all strict)
- Series name "Hidden Stories of Ashtanga" on screen and in the caption. Episode numbers: last posted = Ep. 5. The Upamanyu rewrite is Ep. 6
  "The Teacher Who Said No". New stories get NO number until filmed ("Ep. ?").
- ONE THREAD AT A TIME (he was angry once): 1) his point to adults in 2 to 3 lines, 2) the story told straight start to end with no jumping
  out line by line, 3) back to the adults: same thing in your asana, then the close. Rehooks must live inside this flow. Test: read only the
  story part to a 5-year-old; it must make sense alone.
- BEDTIME-STORY ENGLISH: sentences about 12 words or less, one idea per sentence, everyday words, talk to one person ("you"), small real
  details. No colons, semicolons or dashes in spoken lines. No lists in a spoken line. Explain every name the first time.
  Banned words: journey, unlock, transform, elevate, leverage, dive in, game-changer, flow, level up, "here's the thing", "the truth is",
  "let that sink in", "not X but Y" tricks.
- HIS SPOKEN VOICE: Thai-English, short, articles often dropped ("teacher say", "nobody notice"), "Right?", "For real", "Come on",
  "OK, OK..." used sparingly. Clear first. Never a joke about his English. On-screen text follows his words.
- Always "asana", never "pose".
- THE STORY MUST SURPRISE. If the key moment is something every viewer already knows or does, drop the story.
- HOOKS ARE CONTRARIAN AND THE CLOSE LEAVES A QUESTION.
- STORY PATTERN: Fail, then Hero, then Payoff.
- STAKES TEST: the brief opens with "Stakes: what happens if this does not work / does not happen" and "Why care: the reason in the
  viewer's life now". True stakes only: no health fear, no medical promise, no unchecked number, no shaming.
- TOOLKIT PITCH at the top: "Toolkit pick: <hook tool> + <body tool>. Why: ..." Tools: Triple Hook, /hook (5 levers), /story (4-beat),
  Vibe Samurai (flat statement, ladder of 3, turn, flip), Gervais deadpan, Hero's Journey, Fork-First Story, Root and Wobble,
  Question-First, Name-the-Feeling. Then TOOLKIT CHOICE: 2 other options in one line each.
- RETENTION MAP: label every line H, DH, OL (open loop), RH, T (tension), P.
- Sign-off: he ends every Reel with "แล้วเจอกันครับ" (see you then) + small "see you" subtitle.
- Boonchu's own teaching cues beat yours: leave a slot "[BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less]" for the asana line.
- Length: 45 to 60 s, about 110 to 160 spoken words.

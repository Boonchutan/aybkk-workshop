1. SPLITS: Splits were not tested. The stiffest older adults were linked with a higher risk of dying. Past a medium range, no extra survival benefit.
2. SHOES: Tying your shoelaces used about 90 degrees of hip bend (young adults, lab tasks).
3. TURN: Reversing a car used 92% of the neck's full turn. A typical daily task used about a fifth.
4. BENDS: Heart surgeon Nikolai Amosov guessed a desk worker bends his back only 10 to 20 times a day.
5. DECADE: Each 10 years older had about 6 degrees less shoulder and hip range. Small, the authors say.
6. LONGER: Even around age 85, the back of the thigh gained range. 60-second holds gained more than 15-second holds.
7. ONCE: One yoga class a week for 20 weeks, and average spine range grew (no comparison group).
8. MINUTE: For healthy adults, 1 minute in total per main muscle group, at least 2 days a week.
9. STOPPED: Six weeks after they stopped, the new range was gone.
10. KEEP: Rule 1 of China's 2021 activity guideline ends with "keep at it".

## Why episode 1 is the six-day watch

- Strongest and most surprising of the early scenes: five days of nothing, a teacher who cannot speak, danger on the last day, and the one who is thrown into the sea alive comes back years later as the golden deer. A person who has just seen the film knows the golden deer. This is where the deer comes from.
- Strongest link in the whole run: Vishvamitrasana is Vishvamitra's own asana and he is the teacher in the scene.
- Easiest to shoot: one man to camera, the days counted on his fingers, no prop, no delicate line (no woman's honour, no death told, no throne).
- Safe movie line for the double hook of episode 1 only (true on 11 Nov 2026): "There is a new Ramayana movie in the cinema here in Bangkok now." Then: the old book says. Never "like in the movie".
- If he wants an easier asana beat this week than Vishvamitrasana: spare 11 (Vasisthasana; the PDF prints its preparation, a side plank) sits earlier in the story and can open the run. Then the two set-up lines of episode 1 are cut.

## Order

Story order by the place where each story's fork happens. Three places where an episode steps back, each needs ONE plain spoken line and no more:
- Episode 4 (Sita) ends with the bark dress (2.37). That scene is in the father's hall, after the goodbye talk of 2.34. So its last picture is in front of the father.
- Episode 5 (the father) opens one night back (2.11), then tells the goodbye talk (2.34) in that same hall, a little before the bark is tied, then the chariot and the dust (2.40 to 2.42). Spoken line: "Go back one night."
- Episode 8 (Jatayu) is told straight inside itself: the promise (3.14), the day Sita is taken (3.49 to 3.51), then Rama finds him (3.67 to 3.68). His fight happens before episode 7's search (3.64 to 3.66). Spoken line: "Go back to the hour she was taken."

## 1. Vishvamitra: The Teacher Who Went Silent

- char_id: bala-04-maricha
- Where: Bala Kanda sarga 30 (1.30.1 to 26). Set-up: 1.19.17 to 18 (the sage asks the father for the boy, ten nights). Next step: 1.31.6 to 7 (the sages invite Rama to come and see the bow). Echo: Aranya Kanda 3.38.19 to 21 and 3.42 (the golden deer).
- Moment: The sage Vishvamitra has taken the boy Rama and his brother Lakshmana from their father's house to guard his fire rite. At his hermitage he begins the rite and goes silent. It is a vow. The other sages tell the boys: guard this for six nights and days (1.30.4). They do not sleep (1.30.5). The book tells of nothing coming for five days. On the sixth day Rama says to his brother: be ready (1.30.7). The altar fire flares up (1.30.8). A frightening roar in the sky (1.30.10). Two night-walkers like storm clouds come to spoil the fire rite (1.30.11). Rama's Manava arrow throws one of them, Maricha, a hundred yojanas into the sea, "without taking away his life" (1.30.18 to 20, re-read today). The rite is finished and the teacher speaks again (1.30.25). Years later Maricha still remembers that arrow (3.38.19 to 21, re-read today). He is the one who becomes the golden deer (3.42).
- Asana: Vishvamitrasana (Advanced A).
- Link kind: name.
- Link, honest: Vishvamitrasana is "Sage Visvamitra's posture" (Sweeney's glossary), vishva-mitra is the sage's own name (MW), and Vishvamitra is the silent teacher whose fire rite the boys guard in this scene, so the name link is direct and strong. The asana has nothing to do with Maricha, and Marichyasana must never be tied to him (that is the sage Marichi, a different person).
- Hook (bank #10, adapted: the same practice every morning, "boring" is the point):
  Nothing happen in your practice today? Good.
  The day nothing happen, that one is the practice.
- Double hook carries the movie line: Now there is new Ramayana movie in cinema here in Bangkok. The old book tell one small part. Bedtime story for my son.
- Fork: Day five. No sleep. Nothing came. And the teacher cannot say one word to you. What you do?
- Twist (rehook): Day six, the fire jumps up and the sky roars. They come on the last day. One arrow throws one of them, Maricha, far out into the sea. And that one arrow did not take his life.
- Payoff: Answer: the five quiet days were the practice. Day six only showed that the boys were there. And a silent teacher is still a teacher: in a Mysore room nobody calls out the asanas. Asana: Vishvamitrasana, the asana with that silent teacher's name [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less]. Last picture: the one in the sea never forgets that arrow. Years later he comes back as a golden deer. And the teacher speaks again. Next morning the sages say: come with us, in a far city a great bow is kept (1.31.6 to 7). Question left: tomorrow nothing happens again. Are you still there?
- Lane: B (habit, comment aybkk).
- Risks: OUT, all of it: the rain of blood on the altar (1.30.12 to 13), "I wish to kill these demons" (1.30.21), the second demon Subahu struck down (1.30.22), the remaining demons struck down with a third arrow and "eliminated all of the demons" (1.30.23 to 24). So never say "nobody was hurt", never say he chose not to kill, never "the arrow that does not kill". Say only "that one arrow did not take his life". "They came to put out the fire" is in no verse (they pour blood to spoil the rite, 1.30.12, and are called ritual-hinderers): say "they came to spoil the fire rite". "The big one" has no base, both are called monstrous: say "one of them, Maricha". "I am reluctant to eliminate" (1.30.15) is in Rao's text only. Maricha's own later words "That Rama did not wish to kill me then" (3.38.20) are his memory, spoken to Ravana: never make them the storyteller's claim. That Rama spared him "because of the deer to come" is the site's comment, not a verse. "Nothing came for five days" is how the chapter reads (it tells no attack before the sixth day), not a quoted line. The silence is a ritual vow, not a test of the boys. Six days of watch and the six practice days are a number echo only, never a source link. Show one arrow and a splash, nothing more. Vishvamitrasana was scripted before with another story (he loses his tapas and starts again): this scene is new. Movie line: no actor, no review, no picture; re-check showtimes if posted more than two weeks from 11 Nov.

## 2. Parashurama: The Man Who Chose His Loss

- char_id: bala-07-parashurama
- Where: Bala Kanda sargas 74 to 76 (1.74.8 to 24, 1.75.1 to 28, 1.76.1 to 24).
- Moment: On the road home from the wedding, birds cry. Wind, darkness, dust. The whole army stands as if stunned. Out of the dark walks the older Rama, Rama of the Axe, a warrior-sage, with an axe on his shoulder and a second great bow in his hand (1.74.17 to 19). He has heard about the bow that broke and has brought another (1.75.1 to 2, re-read today). The father, palms together: they are only boys, give them safety (1.75.6). He is not answered. The challenge: draw this one (1.75.3). Young Rama takes it from his hand with the arrow already on it (1.76.4) and says: I will not send this at your life (1.76.6). But this arrow does not fall for nothing (1.76.8). Choose: your power to go anywhere as fast as thought, or the heavens you won by your long hard discipline (1.76.7). The first Rama answers: I gave the earth to the sage Kashyapa, and by his word I do not spend a night on it (1.76.13 to 14, "my mentor Kashyapa's order", re-read today). Leave me my going. Take the heavens (1.76.15 to 16). You have beaten me, and it is no shame, for you are the lord of the three worlds (1.76.19). The arrow flies (1.76.21). The dark lifts (1.76.23). He walks around the young man in respect (1.76.24) and is gone to his mountain in a moment (1.76.22).
- Asana: Akarna Dhanurasana A and B (Advanced B).
- Link kind: meaning.
- Link, honest: Akarna Dhanurasana is the "Archer's (bowstring to ear) posture" (Sweeney's glossary) and dhanus means "a bow" (MW); the whole test in this scene is to take and draw a bow, so the bow tie is strong. "To the ear" is only in the translator's bracket at 1.75.3, not in Valmiki's verse, and the asana is not named after Parashurama.
- Hook (bank #106, adapted: asanas are not the point):
  One day you lose the asanas you collected.
  That day you see what you really keep.
- Fork: The arrow is on the string and it does not go back. The first Rama must give up one thing: his power to go anywhere, or every heaven he won by his long hard discipline. What you give up?
- Twist (rehook): The one who lost gets to choose his loss. And he gives away every heaven he won. He keeps only his going, because of one old promise to the sage Kashyapa, his guide: never spend a night on this earth.
- Payoff: Answer: he let go of what he collected and kept the one rule he lives by every night. And he says it out loud: you have beaten me, and it is no shame. Same in practice: asanas come and go, the promise to show up stays. Asana: Akarna Dhanurasana, the bow drawn to the ear [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less]. Last picture: he goes back to his mountain, the family goes home, and quiet years pass until one morning. Question left: if you can keep only one thing from your practice, which one?
- Lane: B (habit, comment aybkk).
- Risks: Words that are NOT in the verses: no verse of 1.74 to 1.76 calls him old (only the translator's own "Annex" at 1.75.6), so never "old warrior", "the old man", "the old champion", "the old one": say "the older Rama" or "the first Rama". His reason for "no shame" is that Rama is "the lord of the triad of worlds" (1.76.19; Griffith "Lord of the Worlds"), not age: never "passed by the younger one". He does not walk home: he goes "with the speed of cerebration" and "vanished in a trice" (1.76.15, 1.76.22): say "goes back to his mountain". The axe is on his shoulder and the bow is in his hand (1.74.17 to 19; Griffith "His axe upon his shoulder lay"). Tapas is "ascesis" in the translation: say "his long hard discipline", never "his long practice", so the old text is not bent toward asana practice. OUT his killing of the warrior clans and his father's murder (1.74.21 to 22, 1.75.23 to 26). OUT the contest of Shiva and Vishnu over the two bows (1.75.14 to 20), god against god. OUT the duel he offers (1.75.4) and "you are eliminable" (1.76.6). Nobody is hurt in the scene. Never "King Rama". He is a sage and honoured as a form of Vishnu: he leaves with honour, no mockery. No number for his wars. "My choice is made" is Griffith's wording, do not put it in Rao's mouth. The meeting in Janaka's court with Lakshmana's sharp words is Tulsidas, not Valmiki. Spelling care: the PDF prints "Akarna" with no long mark, and MW a-karna with short a means "without ears, deaf". The name is aa-karnam, MW: "'up to the ear' or 'from the ear' (generally said of an arrow reaching to or being discharged from the ear in drawing a bow)". Always gloss it "to the ear". Bow rule: see the top of this file. Bow asanas were used before (Dhanurasana posted, Urdhva Dhanurasana script): this is a new asana and a new story. Kashyapa's name comes back in episode 8: say it once here.

## 3. Kausalya: The Mother Who Could Not Come

- char_id: ayodhya-3-kausalya
- Where: Ayodhya Kanda sargas 20, 21, 24, 25 (2.20.14 to 32, 2.21.51, 2.24.9 to 13 and 32, 2.25.1 to 3, 2.25.35 to 39).
- Moment: Dawn. Rama's mother Kausalya has kept a vow all night and stands at her fire in white, praying for her son's great day (2.20.14 to 15). He walks in and tells her: father is held to an old promise, I go to the forest for fourteen years. She offers a seat. He only touches it: "Time has come for me to sit on a seat made of Kusha grass." (2.20.28). She falls (2.20.32). First she holds him: "I do not give permission to you" (2.21.51, re-read today). Then: take me too, "A cow follows its calf wherever it goes" (2.24.9). He says his father needs her. Then the turn. She sips water, puts her grief away (2.25.1) and gives a long blessing: "Let that righteousness, which you are fostering with courage and discipline , protect you" (2.25.3), and may you have the good fortune Vishnu had "while making his three strides" (2.25.35, re-read today). She puts rice on his head and ties a herb on his wrist "by way of an amulet" (2.25.37 to 38). Sad inside, she looks glad outside (2.25.39).
- Asana: Trivikramasana (Advanced A).
- Link kind: name.
- Link, honest: Trivikramasana is the "Three steps (long stride of Vishnu) posture" (Sweeney's glossary) and tri-vikrama is Vishnu "who strided over the 3 worlds in 3 steps" (MW); Kausalya names those three strides in her own blessing (2.25.35). So the tie is real and checked, but small: one line of a long blessing. The asana is not named after Kausalya.
- Hook (bank #27, adapted: the one simple thing that keeps you going):
  You think good teacher carry you? No.
  Nobody can practise for you. So what can a teacher give? One thing.
- Fork: Her son leaves today. Fourteen years, a forest of wild animals. She is his mother, she can say "I forbid you". Hold him, or bless him? What you do?
- Twist (rehook): First she does forbid him. Then she asks to come too. Only when both fail she sips water, puts her grief away, and the same woman gives him a long blessing.
- Payoff: Answer: she has no guard and no map to send. She gives one line: what you keep with courage and discipline, let that keep you. And a herb to keep him safe, tied on his wrist. Same in practice: your teacher cannot go on the mat for you. A teacher can give one thing: the practice you keep every day. That stays with you. Asana: Trivikramasana, three strides, the good fortune she wished him [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less]. Last picture: he walks out with the herb on his wrist. One more person to tell: his wife. Question left: what are you keeping every day, that can keep you on the day nobody is with you?
- Lane: B (habit, comment aybkk).
- Risks: "The longest blessing in the book" was an unchecked claim: say "a long blessing". The herb: the site has "about his wrist", Griffith "Upon the hero's arm", and "healing" (helps take out an arrow) comes only from the translator's bracket: say "a herb to keep him safe, tied on his wrist", no health claim. OUT her threat to starve (2.21.26 to 27) and her bitter words about Kaikeyi and her marriage (2.20.36 to 55). No throne: "his great day", not "coronation"; do not quote 2.25.39 to 41 (the kingdom, the throne). She refuses first: do not say she agreed at once. Never weak or silly. The famous Sanskrit line "dharmo rakshati rakshitah" is NOT this verse: quote only the translation. "Keeps you" must not become a safety or health promise. Trivikrama is Vishnu: respectful, no comparison with anything Thai. Skanda (2.25.11) and scorpions (2.25.18) are named in the same blessing, but one asana only (Vrschikasana is episode 4). The hook says "teacher" before any mother is on screen: the double hook must bring her in at once ("One mother knew this").

## 4. Sita: The Dress She Could Not Tie

- char_id: ayodhya-4-sita
- Where: Ayodhya Kanda sargas 26 to 30 and 37 (2.26.19 to 38, 2.27.1 to 6, 2.28.7 to 23, 2.29.2, 2.30.12, 2.30.28 to 29, 2.37.4 to 14). The chariot: 2.39.9 to 12.
- Moment: Rama comes home with a changed face and tells his young wife Sita, raised in a palace: stay here, look after my parents. She answers, "angry out of love alone" (2.27.1): where you go, I go. He says no and lists the forest: lions in caves, crocodiles, thorns, hunger, the dark, snakes, scorpions, mosquitoes (2.28.7 to 23). She says all of that turns good if you are there (2.29.2); the grass will be like cotton under my feet (2.30.12). He says no again. She weeps. Then he tells her why: "without knowing your entire opinion, I do not like to take you to the forest" (2.30.28, re-read today). She gives her things away, glad. Later, in the father's hall, the bark clothes are brought (2.37.4 to 6). The brothers change right there, in front of their father (2.37.7 to 8). Sita, the brave one, "erred again and again" and stands holding one piece at her neck, shy, not knowing how to tie it (2.37.12 to 13). Rama comes and ties it for her himself, over her silk (2.37.14, re-read today).
- Asana: Vrschikasana (Advanced B).
- Link kind: meaning.
- Link, honest: Vrschikasana is the "Scorpion posture" (Sweeney's glossary) and vrishchika means "a scorpion" (MW); scorpions are one word in the list of forest dangers Rama gives Sita (2.28.21 on valmikiramayan.net, Sanskrit vrishchikaah) and one word in his mother's blessing (2.25.18). No scorpion appears and nobody is named after one. It is one word on a list: a thin tie.
- Hook (bank #15, adapted, with the point of #42: nobody is ready first):
  Scared of the next asana?
  Nobody ready on day one. Somebody tie it for you.
- Fork (Rama's): He loves her. The forest is real: lions, thorns, scorpions, fourteen years. Take her with him, or leave her safe at home? What you do?
- Twist (rehook): He says no. Twice. Then he tells her why: he was waiting to know her whole mind. And then the brave one cannot even tie the forest dress. She stands there holding it. He ties it for her.
- Payoff: Answer: the list was true, and she went. On day one she could not do the first small thing, and somebody tied it for her. Same when teacher gives you the asana you are scared of: your list is true, and on day one somebody ties the first small thing for you. Asana: Vrschikasana, scorpion, one of the words on his list [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less]. Last picture: bark tied over silk, in front of his father. Soon a chariot is brought to the door. Question left: which asana is on your scared list today?
- Lane: A (asana fix, STUCK ask in the caption and end card, never spoken).
- Risks: Story order: the bark scene (2.37) is in the father's hall AFTER the goodbye talk of 2.34. Rama asks for the dress of a forest man (2.37.4), Kaikeyi herself brings the bark (2.37.6; on camera only "the bark clothes are brought"), Lakshmana changes "there itself before his father" (2.37.8; Griffith "in the presence of his sire"). So never "then they walk to the father to say goodbye". The chariot is ordered only after this (2.39.9 to 12): say "soon a chariot is brought", not "is waiting". The scorpion verse is 2.28.21 (the site misprints the English word as "scropious": retell, never quote it). The asana line stays "one of the words on his list", nothing more. Hook safety: the old line "Go anyway" could be heard as "take the hard asana before the teacher gives it" and argued with spares 9 and 10 (keep the teacher's "not yet"): the new hook and the payoff both say that somebody ties it for you, and the payoff says "when teacher gives you". Tender, never funny: no joke about Sita. Never show her leading: in the forest the order is Lakshmana, Sita, Rama (2.52.95 to 96). She does not leave in bark: the family priest stops it and she leaves in her own clothes and jewels (2.37.21 and 34 to 35, 2.39 to 2.40); say only "he tied it over her silk". OUT her taunt (2.30.3), her threats (2.29.21, 2.30.19 to 20), the "husband is a god" teaching, the priest's anger at Kaikeyi (2.37.22 to 33). "He said no first" is close to the unfilmed Ep. 6 "The Teacher Who Said No": do not run them back to back, and do not turn Rama into a teacher who tests. Vrschikasana is a hard asana: no "anyone can" promise, no injury talk.

## 5. Dasharatha: Just One More Night

- char_id: ayodhya-2-dasharatha
- Where: Ayodhya Kanda 2.11.3 to 10 (the oath), 2.12.43 (the hawk and the pigeon, one line), 2.18.3 (one word), 2.34.16 to 60 (the goodbye), 2.40.50, 2.42.1 to 3 and 2.42.33.
- Moment: Go back one night. Rama's old father promised his wife Kaikeyi before he heard the wish, and swore it on Rama himself (2.11.7, re-read today). The wish: Rama goes to the forest for fourteen years. She held him to it with an old story of a ruler who kept his word, "When there was a dispute between a hawk and a pigeon" (2.12.43, re-read today). In the morning he could say only one word, "Rama!" (2.18.3). Now the three stand before him to say goodbye. He runs to his son and falls before he reaches him (2.34.17). Then the father himself offers the way out. Big: stop me, hold me here, and stay (2.34.26). Rama: do not make your word untrue for my sake (2.34.28). Small: "stay at least for this night today", leave tomorrow at dawn (2.34.33 to 34). Rama: "It is not possible for me, Oh father, to stay on even for a moment." (2.34.49, re-read today). The father holds his son and cannot stand (2.34.60). When the chariot leaves he follows it on foot until his ministers stop him (2.40.50), and he watches the dust until it is gone (2.42.1 to 3). At midnight: "Touch me with your hand." (2.42.33).
- Asana: Raja Kapotasana (Advanced A).
- Link kind: meaning.
- Link, honest: Raja Kapotasana is the "King pigeon posture" (Sweeney's glossary): one bird, the king pigeon; kapota means "a dove, pigeon" (MW), and the joined word raja-kapota is not in MW. The only tie is the word "pigeon": on the promise night Kaikeyi holds the father to his word with the old story of the hawk and the pigeon, and the pigeon is one compound word there (shyena kapotiiye, 2.12.43). The asana is NOT named after that king or after Dasharatha, and in that old story the promise is made to the hawk (2.14.4), not to the pigeon. A word link only: thin, the weakest tie of the run.
- Hook (bank #41, adapted: why "only when I feel like it" costs you the habit):
  You never quit practice with a big no.
  You quit with "just today".
- Fork: The father is crying. He says: stay. Just tonight. Leave tomorrow at dawn. One night. Who does it hurt? What you do?
- Twist (rehook): It is the father who begs the son to break the father's promise. First big: stop me, hold me here. Then small: only one night. The son says no to both, to keep his father's word true.
- Payoff: Answer: not even for a moment. A promise does not end with one big no. It ends with the first "just tonight". Same on the mat: the morning you say "just today I skip". Asana: Raja Kapotasana, king pigeon. That night his wife reminded him of an old king who kept his word, in the story of the hawk and the pigeon [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE: 5 words or less]. Last picture: an old man watching the dust until it is gone. And far away another son does not know yet. Question left: what is your "just tonight"?
- Lane: B (habit, comment aybkk).

## Characters dropped, and why (the rule: no honest asana link, no episode)

- Kaikeyi and Manthara (Ayodhya 7 to 9): the most contrarian story of all (she paid for the good news with a jewel), but no asana in Advanced A or B ties to it by name, shape or meaning. One plain line inside episode 5.
- Maricha as the golden deer who said no (Aranya 35 to 42): Marichyasana E to H belong to the sage Marichi, never to the demon. No other asana fits. He is in episode 1 and in one line of episode 7.
- Guha, Sumantra, Lakshmana in the tree, the hermit with Indra's sword, Sita's bundle of jewels, Shabari: no asana by name. (Sumantra only a loose "turned back" with Parivrttasana. The hermit's sword is a pure habit story and could run outside the asana rule if Boonchu wants it.) Shabari also comes after the search has begun.
- Ahalya: only a root link (her curse word is "bhasmashaayinii", lying on ashes, 1.48.29 to 30, and Sayanasana is used in episode 6), and she needs the delicate line.
- Valmiki and the two birds (Bala 2): Viranchyasana is a real name link (Viranchi is Brahma, who visits him), but this scene was rejected on 9 Nov and Viranchyasana already has an unfilmed Brahma script. Owner's call.

- Sutikshna and Agastya: possible (Parivrttasana A for going round and coming back; Tadasana or a bow asana for Agastya), but weaker ties, and Agastya was in posted Ep. 4.

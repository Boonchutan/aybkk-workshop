## 1. Problem + fix: The Part You Cannot See
For: Bangkok shala

**Cover · UNSEEN**  
Same backbend for years? It may not be your body. It may be your blind spot.  
*9 notes · swipe*

**1 · SEE**  
The part you cannot see is often the part that is stuck. In a backbend, that is your back and hips. A teacher at your side sees what you cannot.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 1/9*

**2 · FEEL**  
What you feel and what you do can be very far apart. You feel your legs are straight. From the side, they are bent. A light hand shows you where straight is.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 2/9*

**3 · SHAPE**  
Copying the shape from a photo can keep you stuck. A photo shows the end, not the steps to get there. A teacher shows you the next small step.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 3/9*

**4 · LIGHT**  
Good hands-on help is light, much lighter than most people think. It guides and never forces. The teacher checks first, and you can always say no. Your breath leads.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 4/9*

**5 · FEAR**  
Fear of dropping back is smart, not weak. The floor is behind you, out of sight. Hands at your waist give you something to trust. Go when your breath is ready.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 5/9*

**6 · BREATH**  
You may hold your breath in hard asanas and never know it. The jaw gets tight and the shoulders creep up. One quiet cue, "breathe", and the body often softens.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 6/9*

**7 · MOMENT**  
A cue helps most in the moment you move, not after class. After class, a correction is only words. While you move, your body can feel which part goes which way.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 7/9*

**8 · LEGS**  
A stuck backbend is often a legs problem, not a back problem. Strong legs let the spine lift. A teacher's hand at your thigh shows you where to press.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 8/9*

**9 · HABIT**  
The habit is the fix, not one adjustment. Same practice, same time, and a teacher who notices when you are gone. In your first 12 weeks, we check your progress.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 9/9*

**Caption**

Same backbend for years? It may not be your body. It may be your blind spot.

You cannot see your own back. A teacher at your side can.

An adjustment is light help from a teacher's hands. The teacher checks first, your breath leads, and you can always say no.

We have taught Ashtanga in Bangkok for 14 years. Here are 9 stuck points we see, and the fix for each.

## 2. List with opinion: 9 Notes on Adjustments
For: teachers (180K)

**Cover · LESS**  
You think a teacher's hands should push you deeper. The best adjustments do less, so you do more.  
*9 notes from 14 years teaching in Bangkok · swipe*

**1 · LIGHT**  
Light hands teach more than strong hands. A push moves you for one breath. A light touch shows you where to move, so you can do it alone.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 1/9*

**2 · BREATH**  
Your breath is the boss, not the teacher's hands. When the breath gets short or stuck, the hands come off. A deeper asana with a held breath is not progress.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 2/9*

**3 · ASK**  
A good teacher asks every day, because last week's yes is not today's yes. Even students of many years get asked. You can say no any day, no reason needed.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 3/9*

**4 · MAP**  
A good adjustment draws a body map, not a shape. The hand comes the moment you move: which part, which way, what for. We call it drawing the cortical map.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 4/9*

**5 · FEAR**  
In drop backs, most of the help is for fear, not for flexibility. A steady hand helps you keep breathing. Then it does a little less each week.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 5/9*

**6 · READY**  
No hands can make you ready. In Mysore class, the teacher gives the next asana when you are ready. Pushing to get there faster only slows you down.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 6/9*

**7 · EXPERTS**  
Experts need adjustments too, not only beginners. Skilled gymnasts and ballet dancers still need a coach's hands now and then. My wife has practised for years. She asked me for one.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 7/9*

**8 · TRUST**  
Without trust, even a good adjustment feels like a push. You relax only when you know the hands stop the moment you say stop. That takes weeks, not one class.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 8/9*

**9 · NOTICE**  
The first adjustment is to notice, and it needs no hands. Who came in tired? Who is missing this week? Noticing is why students stay for years.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 9/9*

**Caption**

The best adjustment does less than you think.

9 notes from 14 years of teaching Ashtanga in Bangkok. Light hands. The breath leads. A good teacher asks every day, and anyone can say no.

Teachers, the last note is why students stay for years.

Which one would you argue with?

## 3. Never again: 9 Things Good Hands Never Do
For: teachers (180K)

**Cover · PUSH**  
A good adjustment never pushes. Do you hold your breath when the teacher walks over?  
*9 NEVERS · SWIPE*

**1 · ASK**  
Never put hands on a student before you ask. A yes yesterday is not a yes today. The student can always say no, and no needs no reason.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 1/9*

**2 · BREATH**  
Never go past the breath. When the breath gets short or stops, the hands stop too. They wait there until it is easy again.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 2/9*

**3 · LIGHT**  
Never use more than a light hand. Force can make the body tighten and fight back. Light hands show the way, and the student does the work.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 3/9*

**4 · SHAPE**  
Never chase the shape. A deep asana can sit on a weak base. The legs press and the spine lifts first, then the shape comes. Function over shape.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 4/9*

**5 · LATE**  
Never help too late. After the move, a hand can only remind. During the move, it shows the body this part, this way.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 5/9*

**6 · SAME**  
Never adjust every body the same way. Same asana, different hips, different day. The hands read this body, this morning, then decide.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 6/9*

**7 · RELEASE**  
Never rush the release. In a drop back, letting go too fast can scare the student. Let go slowly, with the breath, until their own legs hold them.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 7/9*

**8 · HELP**  
Never think you are too good for help. Teachers need a teacher too. Nobody can see their own back, not even after many years.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 8/9*

**9 · YEARS**  
Never trade a student's next ten years for one deep asana today. A scared student quietly stops coming. Safe hands bring them back tomorrow, same practice, same time.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 9/9*

**Caption**

A good adjustment never pushes.

9 nevers for hands-on help, from 14 years of teaching Ashtanga in Bangkok.

For teachers who adjust, and for students who receive.

Which never would you add as number 10?

## 4. Debunk: TOUCH
For: trust

**Cover · TOUCH**  
Scared of a teacher's hands? We read the studies. Who stops you pushing too far at home?  
*9 notes · swipe*

**1 · RISK**  
The risk is real. In one online survey, 11% of 2,620 people who do yoga said an adjustment had hurt them. Yet about 7 in 10 called adjustments "very helpful".  
*David Keil, Yoganatomy online survey, 2017*

**2 · ALONE**  
Practising alone is not risk-free either. In a survey of 2,567 Australians who do yoga, some injuries came in home practice. Those hurt often blamed themselves for "pushing it too far".  
*Penman et al., International Journal of Yoga, 2012 (retold)*

**3 · FEEDBACK**  
You learn a movement better with expert feedback. A big 2013 review says this is widely agreed. A video can show an asana. It cannot see yours.  
*Sigrist et al., Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 2013 (retold)*

**4 · EARLY**  
Help may matter most early, when a movement is still new. The same review says lots of guidance looks useful at the start. Its proof for touch was thin.  
*Sigrist et al., Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 2013 (retold)*

**5 · LESS**  
As you learn, good help gets less. The review also warns constant guidance can make learners ignore their own body sense. So the practice stays yours. Hands come now and then.  
*Sigrist et al., Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 2013 (retold)*

**6 · OUTSIDE**  
Even the best need outside eyes. Singer Renée Fleming said: "What we hear as we are singing is not what the audience hears." In a backbend, you cannot see your back.  
*Renée Fleming in Atul Gawande, The New Yorker, 2011*

**7 · COACH**  
In one gym study, the same plan worked better with a coach. Over 12 weeks, men with a trainer gained more strength than men training alone. It was small, and not yoga.  
*Mazzetti et al., Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise, 2000 (retold)*

**8 · TRUST**  
Hands work better with trust. David Keil, who ran the 11% survey, writes that even a skilled stranger's adjustment might go wrong. Trust grows with one teacher over time.  
*David Keil, Yoganatomy, 2017 (retold)*

**9 · FORCE**  
The danger is force, not touch. In our Mysore room, the teacher checks first, the breath leads, and hands stay light. You can always say no.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 9/9*

**Caption**

Afraid of a teacher's hands? We read the studies.

The risk is real. So is the help.

My wife is an experienced practitioner, and she still asked me for an adjustment.

What did a teacher's hands teach you that a video never could?

## 5. Big shift: Check Before the Year Ends
For: Bangkok shala

**Cover · CHECK**  
Practise alone every day, and you can repeat the same mistake every day. Check your practice with 9 questions before the year ends.  
*9 questions · swipe*

**1 · SEEN**  
A practice nobody has seen can drift off track for years. It drifts slowly, so it feels fine every day. Who has watched your whole practice this year?  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 1/9*

**2 · BLIND**  
Getting good does not remove your blind spot. In a backbend, nobody can see their own feet. Skill does not grow eyes in your back. Does anyone check yours?  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 2/9*

**3 · TOUCH**  
A light touch can teach what words cannot. Hands show which part to lift. The teacher checks first, and you can say no. When did a teacher's hands last help you?  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 3/9*

**4 · BREATH**  
Your breath knows you are pushing before you do. When it stops, you are forcing. Good hands wait for it, never force. In your hardest asana, is it long and even?  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 4/9*

**5 · MOMENT**  
At AYBKK, help comes in the moment you move: which part, which way. We call it drawing the cortical map, your brain's body map. Do you get that help at home?  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 5/9*

**6 · READY**  
You may be the last to know when you are ready. In Mysore class, the teacher watches you and gives the next asana. At home, who decides: you, or a video?  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 6/9*

**7 · CHOICE**  
A daily choice is a daily chance to skip. 'When I have time' means deciding again every day. A set time decides for you. Is yours set for January?  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 7/9*

**8 · MISSED**  
One missed day did not stop a new habit from forming, a 12-week study found. So a missed day is not quitting. But who notices when you are gone for a month?  
*Lally et al. · Eur J Soc Psychol, 2010 (retold)*

**9 · PROOF**  
A good feeling is not proof of progress. Film one asana today, and again in 12 weeks. Will you compare the two? At AYBKK, there is a 12-week check.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 9/9*

**Caption**

Every day on the mat is not proof it is working.

Before the year ends, swipe and check your practice with 9 questions.

In Mysore class, you practise your own sequence. A teacher watches, helps with light hands, and notices when you are gone. You can always say no to hands-on help.

## 6. Double benefit: One Touch, Two Lessons
For: Bangkok shala

**Cover · TWICE**  
Not a push: a good adjustment teaches you twice. Do you lose the second lesson by morning?  
*9 notes · swipe*

**1 · WHERE**  
Lesson one: you cannot see where your body can go today. Hands can feel it. Some days that is further, some days not. Your breath decides, not the hands.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 1/9*

**2 · ALONE**  
Lesson two: an adjustment is not finished until you can use it alone. Good hands leave you one small job for tomorrow, like 'lift, then twist'. One job, not ten.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 2/9*

**3 · INSIDE**  
Mirrors and videos show the outside of your asana, never the inside. Hands feel what they miss: a hip that lifts, a breath that stops.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 3/9*

**4 · TODAY**  
Where your body can go changes every day. Yesterday's deep backbend may not come today. Hands read today's body, not last week's.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 4/9*

**5 · ASK**  
Good hands ask first, and 'no' is a full answer. You can also ask for hands. Either way, the touch stays light and nothing is forced.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 5/9*

**6 · BREATH**  
Hold your breath, and the adjustment teaches you nothing. A held breath makes the body tight, and a tight body fights the hands. Breathe out slowly. If you cannot, say so.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 6/9*

**7 · NAME**  
The lesson leaves with the hands, unless you give it a name. Before the hands let go, give the feeling two words, like 'heavy heels'. Tomorrow, those words help bring it back.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 7/9*

**8 · AGAIN**  
The real test is tomorrow, when you try it again with no hands. Same asana, your two words, your own breath. If it does not come, do not force it.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 8/9*

**9 · HABIT**  
Without a habit, even the best adjustment fades. Same practice, same time, and a teacher who notices when you are gone. Plus a 12-week check of your progress.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 9/9*

**Caption**

A good adjustment is not a push. It teaches you twice.

Lesson one: where your body can go. Lesson two: what to do alone tomorrow. Photos: our Mysore class at AYBKK.

You can always say no. Your breath leads.

## 7. Relatable reward: Being Adjusted Is a Skill
For: Bangkok shala

**Cover · BRACE**  
The teacher's hands come and you brace? Being adjusted is a skill, and half of it is your job.  
*9 notes · swipe*

**1 · GUARD**  
When the teacher's hands guide you somewhere new, your body tenses to guard itself. Holding your breath and pushing back are the same guard. It is normal.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 1/9*

**2 · FIGHT**  
Fight the hands and you miss what they came to show you. A tight body mostly feels pressure. A soft body can feel where the hands are guiding it.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 2/9*

**3 · SPEAK**  
Speak before the hands come. Don't wait until it hurts. Tell the teacher about an old injury or a sore knee. Hands help best when they know where not to go.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 3/9*

**4 · EXHALE**  
Exhale when the hands come, not after they go. Holding the breath makes the whole body hold. The breath leads. The hands follow.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 4/9*

**5 · SOFTEN**  
Soften, and the asana gets steadier, not weaker. The Yoga Sutra says asana is 'firm and pleasant', and comes 'by slight effort'. Keep the firm part. Let the rest go soft.  
*Yoga Sutra 2.46-47 · tr. Vivekananda, Raja Yoga, 1896*

**6 · STOP**  
Saying stop is not rude: it is part of the practice. Scared to be touched at all? Say so. A good teacher asks or checks first, uses light hands and never forces.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 6/9*

**7 · NOTICE**  
Notice the new place before the hands leave, or the lesson leaves with them. Feel which part moved, and which way. Tomorrow, look for that feeling on your own.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 7/9*

**8 · ASK**  
You can ask for hands, too. Stuck in one asana? Tell the teacher before you start. Asking is not weak. It tells the teacher where to look.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 8/9*

**9 · BETTER**  
You get better at this, not perfect. Some days you still tense up. It grows class by class: same practice, same time, a teacher who notices when you are gone.  
*AYBKK MYSORE NOTES · 9/9*

**Caption**

Do you hold your breath when the teacher's hands come?

An adjustment is when the teacher's hands help you in an asana. Your half of the job: speak, breathe out, soften, say stop if you need, notice.

You can say no at any time. Light hands, never force.

Same practice, same time, a teacher who notices when you are gone.

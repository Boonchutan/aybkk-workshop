## Notes for whoever picks the final 20

- Strongest for the theme and seen on a page image: 1 (Fukuzawa, Japanese and English), 4 (Lesgaft, Russian), 2 (Ushinsky, Russian, keep the "we may suppose"), 3 (Tolstoy, Russian and English).
- 5 (Sukhomlinsky): the Russian line is now checked in the book (snippet view). For English, the print-checked wording is Judelson 1977; Holly Smith's better-known wording is from a retyped file.
- 6, 7, 8 (Yan Yuan, Zhu Xi, Tao Xingzhi) are about doing and practice, not exercise. Use at most one or two, for the Chinese audience. Tao is the safest name in China; Yan Yuan's passage attacks the Cheng brothers, so cut before that.
- 10 (Pavlov) is real but weak on the theme: it says what he loved, not that the body builds the mind. It belongs with the "weak, at most 2" group.
- Safety calls for the owner: Kano (appointed member of the House of Peers from 1922 and a ministry official; from the researcher's notes, not opened here), Tao (People's Political Council; same), Fukuzawa (sensitive name in China; he worked for the shogunate, never for the Meiji government), Zhu Xi (a scholar-official, used before). None is a minister, president or ruler.
- No medical promise in any of the ten lines.
- Not opened: the 1888 first edition of Lesgaft, the 1969 first edition of Sukhomlinsky (edu.tatar.ru answers 403), Kano's 1937 Maruzen book and the 1932 journal print of his lecture, Вестник АН СССР 1936 № 3, 鄉教叢訊.

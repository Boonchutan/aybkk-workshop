## PICK 1 (N1). Tadasana. Working title: "The Mountain That Lay Down"

Lane B. Ep. ?

Why it survives:
- Every story fact checks out in two old texts (Mahabharata, Ramayana). The lying down comes from a third, later text (Devi Bhagavata). I read all three today.
- The asana link is real and honest. The Ashtanga sequence book calls Tāḍāsana "Mountain posture" (Sweeney, index p. 83). Monier-Williams p. 441
  has tāḍa = "a mountain, L." (L. means it is found only in old word lists). So: mountain asana, mountain story. One step, like Kapotasana and the doves.
- Surprise: Thai, Chinese, Russian and Western practitioners almost never know Vindhya (Thai and Chinese viewers do know Meru,
  เขาพระสุเมรุ / 须弥山). A whole mountain lying flat in front of one man is a big, new picture.
  For Ashtangis the twist is the word. The Devi Bhagavata says he bowed "sāṣṭāṅgaṃ", which means "with eight limbs". It is the same word as Ashtanga.
- Fail, Hero, Payoff: the mountain grows from envy and the gods fail to stop him (fail). One sage walks up (hero). The mountain lies down and
  keeps his word "even today" (payoff).
- Safe on its own. No killing, no curse, a child can hear it.
- Habit Gap: the mountain grew because he wanted the honour Meru got every day (comparison). He became famous for keeping one promise every day.
  The Sun also says something true and useful: he walks the same path every day because it was given to him (CE 3.102.4). Same practice, same time.

Why only Pick 1 with a warning: the teacher goes away and never comes back, the same shape the judge disliked in Kapila.
In this story it is only the ending, not the fork. Keep the spoken close on the mountain's kept promise, never on the teacher being gone.

Stakes: if you practise to match the next mat, you grow the wrong way. The practice turns into anger, and one day you stop.
Why care: everyone compares their mat with someone. A promise you keep every morning lasts longer than envy.

Hook: #112 adapted (bank: "{Goal} is simpler than you've been told"). "Fourth series. Very advanced. Mountain asana? You only stand."
It flips the belief that advanced means bigger and more.
DH (open loop): "Because in old India, one great mountain is famous for lying down. For one old man."
Backup hook: #59 adapted ("Don't {give up thing} just to {small win}"): "Don't grow taller just to be seen. Ask this mountain."
Do not use the researcher's "#106 adapted". The bank's #106 template is "I don't want to {trade-off} just to {benefit}", and his line does not fit it.
Harvard tricks (4, not all): #1 fork ("You are one old sage. Mountain bigger every day. What you do?"), #21 (the mountain asana in
fourth series is not about growing; and sāṣṭāṅga = Ashtanga, same word), #27 (he stays low BECAUSE he gave his word: MBh "compact", Ramayana
"keeping his instruction"), #30 (one comment word only).

### Verified facts (I read them myself)
Mahabharata, Book 3 Vana Parva, Tirtha-yatra Parva, Ganguli section CIV (Gutenberg #15474, Vol. 1), and Critical Edition (CE) 3.102.1-14 (GRETIL mbh_03_u).
Lomasa tells it to Yudhishthira.
- CE 3.102.2: between rising and setting the Sun circled Meru, the golden mountain, keeping it on his right (pradakṣiṇam).
  Ganguli: "The sun between his rising and setting used to revolve round that monarch of mountains--the great Meru of golden lustre."
- CE 3.102.3 (nityaśaḥ, every day): Ganguli "As thou every day goest round Meru and honourest him by thy circumambulations, do thou even the same by me".
- CE 3.102.4 "nāham ātmecchayā ... eṣa mārgaḥ pradiṣṭo me yenedaṃ nirmitaṃ jagat". Ganguli: "I do not of my own will honour this mountain
  by my circumambulations. By those who have built this universe hath that path been assigned to me."
- CE 3.102.5 "krodhāt pravṛddhaḥ sahasā" (grew suddenly, from anger), to block the path of the Sun and the Moon. Ganguli: "the mountain
  suddenly began to increase from wrath". (The researcher put "sahasā" at 3.102.1. That verse is Yudhishthira's question. The story verse is 3.102.5.)
- CE 3.102.6 "devāḥ sahitāḥ sarva eva sendrāḥ samāgamya ... nivārayām āsur ... na ca sma teṣāṃ vacanaṃ cakāra". All the gods, Indra too,
  CAME TO HIM together and tried to stop him. He did not do what they said. Ganguli: "all the assembled gods came to Vindhya ... and tried to dissuade
  him from his course. But he heeded not what they said."
- CE 3.102.7-9: the gods go to Agastya at his hermitage. "No one but you can stop him."
- CE 3.102.10 "sadāraḥ samupasthitaḥ" (came near, with his wife). Ganguli: "he with his wife, having arrived there, came near Vindhya".
- CE 3.102.11-12. Ganguli: "I wish to have a path given to me by thee, as, for some purpose, I shall have to go to the southern region. Until my
  return, do thou wait for me. And when I have returned ... thou mayst increase in bulk as much as thou pleasest."
  CE "yāvadāgamanaṃ mahyaṃ tāvat tvaṃ pratipālaya".
- CE 3.102.13 "evaṃ sa samayaṃ kṛtvā ... adyāpi dakṣiṇād deśād vāruṇir na nivartate" (he made this agreement, and even today he does not come back
  from the south). Ganguli: "having made this compact with Vindhya up to the present day Varuna's son doth not return".
- CE 3.101.15 = Ganguli CIII (the gods speaking to Agastya): "vacas tavānatikrāman vindhyaḥ śailo na vardhate" (not going against your word, the
  Vindhya does not grow). Ganguli: "he hath ceased to increase, as he was unable to disobey thy command."
- Second witness. Valmiki Ramayana (CE) 3.10.83, GRETIL ram_03_u, Rama speaking: "mārgaṃ niroddhuṃ satataṃ bhāskarasyācalottamaḥ / saṃdeśaṃ
  pālayaṃs tasya vindhyaśailo na vardhate". It means the Vindhya keeps Agastya's instruction (saṃdeśaṃ pālayan) and does not grow to block the Sun's path.
- The bow is ONLY in the Devi Bhagavata Purana, Book 10, chapter 7 (Sanskrit djvu text on archive.org, verse numbers printed; English Swami Vijnanananda):
  10.7.15 "agre dadarśa taṃ vindhyam" (he saw the Vindhya ahead).
  10.7.16 "cakampe cācalas tūrṇaṃ dṛṣṭvāgre sthitaṃ munim" (the mountain trembled at once, seeing the sage standing in front).
  10.7.17 "daṇḍavat patito bhūmau sāṣṭāṅgaṃ bhaktibhāvataḥ" (he fell to the ground like a stick, with the eight-limb bow, out of devotion).
  10.7.18 "vatsa evaṃ tiṣṭha tāvat tvaṃ yāvad āgamyate mayā" (child, stay like this until I come back).
  10.7.19 "aśakto 'haṃ ... ārohaṇe tava putraka" (I cannot climb your heights, little son).
  Vijnanananda: "The Bindhya Mountain, seeing the Muni Agastya in front, began to tremble ... became low and dwarfish and bowed down to the Muni and fell
  down with devotion in sastangas ... just like a stick dropped flat on the ground before the Muni." / "O Child! Better remain in this state until I come back."
  10.7.20-21: he crossed the peaks and went south, then built his hermitage on the Malaya mountain.
  In the Devi Bhagavata the gods never talk to the mountain (10.4-10.6: they go to Shiva and Vishnu, then to Agastya).
- Monier-Williams: p. 1212 sāṣṭāṅga "performed with eight limbs ... (as a reverential prostration of the body so as to touch the ground with the hands,
  breast, forehead, knees, and feet)". p. 467 daṇḍavat "like a stick ... prostrating the body in a straight line".
  p. 441 tāḍa "a blow ... sound, noise, L. ... a handful of grass &c., L. ... a mountain, L.".
  p. 4 agasti "said to have been ... of short stature ... compelled the Vindhya mountains to prostrate themselves before him" (a dictionary summary
  of tradition, not a story text).
- Sweeney: index "Tāḍāsana · Mountain posture" (p. 83). Advanced B list p. 46. Vinyasa p. 75: palms together at the chest, 5 breaths, then down to the floor.
- Repeat check: "vindhya", "meru", "sashtanga" and "dandavat" have 0 hits in bots/posts. Tadasana appears only in the unposted 3 Oct research.json.
  Agastya is in posted Ep. 4 (he drinks the ocean, 2026-10-01-plato-chat-ep4-kumbhaka.md). Same sage, different event. Not in stories-used.txt.

### What NOT to say (N1)
- Never say the gods gave "advice from far away". All the gods came TO him (CE 3.102.6 "samāgamya", Ganguli "came to Vindhya"). The
  researcher's best frame ("advice from far away did nothing, one teacher stood in front of him") is FALSE to the text. Drop it.
  True contrast: many gods together tried to stop him and failed. One sage came, and the mountain lay down.
- Never say "hardest series in Ashtanga". There are 5th and 6th series. Say "fourth series" or "very advanced".
- Never say "India's most famous mountain". It is an unchecked superlative (Himalaya, Kailash and Meru are better known). Say "one great mountain".
- Never say the asana is "named after Vindhya" or "named for this story". Say "Mountain asana. And a mountain story."
- Never say Ashtanga MEANS bowing, or that the eight limbs of Ashtanga are the eight body parts. Only "same word". Patanjali's eight limbs are
  different from the bow's hands, chest, forehead, knees and feet. No claim that Pattabhi Jois or anyone linked the asana to this story.
- The bow, "with eight limbs", "like a stick" and "child" are from the Devi Bhagavata only. The Mahabharata has only the request, the agreement and
  the stopping. The caption must name both books. On screen: "One old book says..." for the bow.
- Never say "his teacher Agastya" or "guru". No text calls him that. "Like a student before a teacher" may be said only as Boonchu's picture.
- Do not call Agastya "small" or "short" as a story fact (only MW's summary of tradition says so). Say "one old sage" or "one man".
- Never tell Agastya as a trickster who lied or tricked. Say "He went south. He made his home there." (MBh 3.102.13, DBh 10.7.21).
- Do not make "the teacher never came back" the lesson. Do not link it to the Mysore "stop here until I give you the next asana". It sounds like
  a teacher who leaves you stuck in Primary for good. Close on the mountain's promise.
- Leave out the Kalakeyas killing sages, the "heaps of bones" (CII), Vritra, Nahusha "thrown down from his throne" (CIII), the drained ocean and the war
  after it (CIV second half, CV), the Devi Bhagavata's "some of them died" (10.3), Narada's gossip, and the Vindhyavasini goddess ending.
- Say "great mountain", not "king of mountains". No crowns or thrones.
- No north versus south peoples reading. MW's "conquered and civilized the South" stays out. That reading is political in India.
- Do not number the asana on screen. Do not say the asana sequence was made to match the story. "Down flat like a stick" after Tadasana may be
  said only as "like".
- If Ep. 4 is mentioned, one small screen line only ("Same sage as Ep. 4"). One thread in the spoken story.

## PICK 2 (N2). Buddhasana. Working title: "Between the Floor and the Pillow"

Lane B. Ep. ?

Why it survives:
- Every story fact checks out word for word in the Vinaya (Kd 21) and in Ananda's own verses (Theragatha 17.3). Both texts are on SuttaCentral,
  with the Pali on the same page.
- It is the Habit Gap thesis in one story. People do not lack information, they lack practice. He had 84,000 teachings and was still a trainee.
  What worked was one more night of practice with the body.
- Surprise: the man who learned the most is left out of the 499. And it happens in a gap between two positions: feet off the floor, head not
  yet on the pillow. Western, Russian and most Chinese practitioners do not know it. Many Thai Buddhists do. That is one audience of four, not
  "every viewer".
- Asana link: Sweeney calls Buddhasana "Enlightened posture" (index p. 82). MW p. 733 buddha = "awakened, awake". In the story a man's mind becomes
  free (he becomes enlightened). One step, honest if worded right.
- The Buddha is not a character in the story. He is named once at most, as the teacher Ananda served.
- Fail, Hero, Payoff: not chosen, still a trainee (fail). One night of practice (hero). Free between the floor and the pillow (payoff).

Why only Pick 2: a revered Theravada monk sits close to a sales ask, which is a real risk for Thai viewers. The judge already dropped one Buddhasana
story (Moggallana). The setting of Kd 21 is the time right after the Buddha's death, and many Thai viewers will know that. The script must not say it.

Stakes: if you wait until you know enough, you never start, because there is always one more teaching or one more video.
Why care: many of us know more about yoga than we practise. (Do not say "most viewers". That is unchecked.)

### What NOT to say (N2)
- Never "he gave up", "nothing worked so he quit", or "it came after he stopped trying". Kd 21 says only that he practised most of the night, then at
  dawn bent to lie down. The researcher's retold line "Early morning. Nothing. He gives up" is invented. Say "He lie down to rest."
  "Still not free" just before he lies down is fine. It follows from 1.6.4-1.6.5.
- Never say "letting go" in a way that means "stop practising". Brahmali's "through letting go" is anupādāya, "without clinging".
- Never "Ananda became a Buddha". Say "his mind became free" or "he woke up". Buddhasana link = "Buddha mean awake", nothing more.
- Never say why the meeting was held (the Buddha's death, Kd 21 1.1). No crying monks (1.1.11), no council blaming Ananda (later in Kd 21).
- Never "he knew more than anyone" or "the most learned monk" (not checked by me). Say "84,000 teachings" (Thag 17.3:11).
- Never "lazy", "slow" or "failed". The text says "trainee". Say "still a learner".
- "His friends spoke up" is a stretch. The text says "the monks". Say "the other monks".
- "None of the four postures" is commentary and Thai teaching, not Kd 21. Say only "feet off the floor, head not yet on the pillow".
- Skip "like a shadow that never left". It was already used for Dilipa (Gomukhasana).
- Never "his teacher noticed". In Kd 21 it is the monks who speak up, and the Buddha is not there. Keep "at AYBKK a teacher notices when you miss class"
  in the caption only, far from Ananda's name.
- No AI image or statue of the Buddha. Show Ananda only from behind (robe, bare feet leaving the floor, a pillow), or no person. Do not show Boonchu's
  feet pointing at any monk image. Never compare Boonchu or AYBKK to Ananda or the Buddha.
- No claim that the asana "wakes you up" or "brings enlightenment".
- Do not post in the same week as advA's Kaundinya (also Buddhist).

## KILLED

N3 Akarna Dhanurasana (Drona's grass chain):
1. Sources are mislabelled. The research says only the ring, the dry well, the dinner and the bow are star passages. But "They then began to eye one
   another bashfully" is *1361, "of darkish hue, decrepit and lean" is *1362, and the boys surrounding him is *1363. None of these are in the Critical
   Edition either (GRETIL mbh_01_u, 1.122.13). They are only in Ganguli.
2. The kind "teacher who noticed" frame clashes with the text. In CE 1.122.15 Drona's first words are "aho nu dhig balaṃ kṣātraṃ" ("Shame on your
   Kshatriya might"). In the text around it he comes for a living and has a grudge against Drupada.
3. The asana link is weak. In the Critical Edition there is no bow and no bowstring to the ear. The bow appears only in *1368 (the ring). The chain
   works because of a weapon mantra (1.122.16 "mayāstreṇābhimantritaḥ", "see its power, which no other has"). So the "streak" lesson would be built
   on magic, and a strict checker will call it an invented link.
4. Drona is already the teacher in the used Dhanurasana story (the bird test), and Akarna Dhanurasana is its sister asana. It is a re-run feeling, and
   comments will go to Ekalavya's thumb.
R1 (the oil seller, Guitian Lu): I did not open it. The researcher's own note says every Chinese viewer learned it in school, so it fails surprise for China.

## 3. FULL SCRIPT

*(hook)*
Who made Vritra, the demon who stole the rain?

*(myth, bedtime voice)*
Tvashtr was the craftsman of the gods.
His son Vishvarupa had three heads.
One drank soma, a holy drink.
One drank wine. One ate food.
He was the gods' own priest.
But his mother's family were demons.
In public, he gave the offerings to the gods.
In secret, he gave some to the demons too.
Indra, the king of the gods, found out.
He got scared, and cut off all three heads.
The three heads turned into three birds, and flew away.
Tvashtr lost his son.
He lit a fire and chanted for a new one.
A son who would kill Indra.
The word was "Indrashatru."
Stress the end of the word, and it means "the killer of Indra."
Stress the start, and it means "the one Indra kills."
Tvashtr stressed it the wrong way.
From the fire rose Vritra.
Huge, angry, born to destroy Indra.
And born to die by Indra's hand.
His ending was hidden inside the word that made him.

*(bridge, turn to the adults)*
Tvashtr put the stress in the wrong place.
We do it too, in our asanas.

*(asana)*
In Ashtanga, the thunderbolt comes back. Supta Vajrasana.
Supta means lying down, or asleep. Vajra is the thunderbolt.
Legs in lotus. Hands reach behind your back and hold the toes.
You lie back until your head touches the floor.
Put the stress in your neck, and it can hurt you.
Put it in your legs and your chest. Keep breathing.
Then it makes you strong.
Same asana. Stress in a different place. A different ending.

*(closing question)*
Let me know where the stress goes when you lie back.
Your neck, or your legs and chest?

## 4. What I changed

Your myth text:
1. **Soma line.** "One drank soma, one drank wine, one ate food." became "One drank soma, a holy drink. / One drank wine. One ate food."
   - Why: most viewers do not know the word "soma", so it gets a short explanation. It also keeps one idea per line.
   - The fact is unchanged (TS 2.5.1, SB 1.6.3.2, BhP 6.9.1).
   - I kept "wine". The text says sura, a strong grain liquor, but "wine" is the usual translation and fine for a reel.
2. **Indra line.** "Indra found out, got scared, and cut off all three heads." became "Indra, the king of the gods, found out. / He got scared, and cut off all three heads."
   - Why: new viewers meet Indra here for the first time, so he gets the same explanation as in Part 1.
   - The fact is unchanged (TS 2.5.1 "Indra was afraid", BhP 6.9.4).
   - The weapon is left out. TS says he used his vajra, and that clashes with Part 1, where the vajra is made later.
3. **Birds line.** "The three heads flew away as three birds." became "The three heads turned into three birds, and flew away."
   - Why: the texts say the heads became birds (TS 2.5.1, SB 1.6.3.3-5, BhP 6.9.5). Your "flew away" stays.
4. **Stress lines.** "Stress it one way... / Stress it the other way..." became "Stress the end of the word... / Stress the start..."
   - Why: "one way / the other way" makes viewers guess. Naming the start and the end is clearer.
   - It matches the grammar: accent on the end means "killer of Indra", accent on the start means "the one Indra kills" (Macdonell, Vedic Grammar App. III; Keith's note on TS 2.4.12).
   - It also lets the editor show both versions on screen.
5. **Last line.** "His ending was inside his own name." became "His ending was hidden inside the word that made him."
   - Why: his name is Vritra. "Indrashatru" was the word in the chant (TS 2.4.12, SB 1.6.3.8-10, BhP 6.9.11 and 6.9.18). Viewers would look for the ending inside the name "Vritra".
   - Your original line can also be defended (Apte lists Indrashatru as a title of Vritra), so put it back if you like it better.
6. **Everything else:** line breaks only, words unchanged.

Changes to the draft (not your text):
- The opening line now mirrors Part 1 ("In Ashtanga...").
- Taken from Draft 2: "Supta means lying down, or asleep. Vajra is the thunderbolt."
- "Hands hold the feet from behind" became "Hands reach behind your back and hold the toes". The sources say you catch the big toes.
- The closing question is softer. "Or in the right place?" became "Your neck, or your legs and chest?"

## 6. Caption (English)

Hidden Stories of Ashtanga, Ep. 2 (the Vritra story, part 2)

Who made Vritra, the demon who stole the rain?
His father, Tvashtr, chanted for a son to kill Indra. One word in the chant had the stress in the wrong place, and it gave Vritra the opposite ending.

On the mat, stress works the same way. Supta Vajrasana, the sleeping thunderbolt, can hurt your neck or make you strong. Same asana. The stress decides.

Supta Vajrasana is in the Ashtanga Intermediate Series. Learn it with your teacher. By tradition, a teacher helps in this asana.

## 9. Fact risk left

- **"Stress" is a simplification.** Vedic accent was pitch (the voice rises on a syllable). That is fine for a reel. If a scholar comments, "accent" is the exact word.
- **"Lit a fire and chanted" is the Bhagavata version.** The older Vedic texts say Indra drank Tvashtr's soma by force, and Tvashtr poured what was left into the fire.
- **The neck line is a general safety cue.** Steiner says that dropping onto the head puts load on the neck. The script claims no injury numbers, vinyasa counts, repetitions or dristi. Keep it that way in the comments too.
- **Search results show a different asana.** Most results for "Supta Vajrasana" show a kneeling version. The on-screen tag "in lotus" covers this.
- **Avoid these in comments:**
  - linking the birds to Tittibhasana or Kapotasana
  - putting the vajra in the scene where the heads are cut
  - saying Tvashtr made the vajra
  - citing Yoga Mala for the second series or for tristhana
- **The delay.** Part 1 said "tomorrow's story". If you want to explain the delay, say it in your own words. I did not write a line for you.
- **Timing.** Today, 29 Sep, is R. Sharath Jois's birthday.
- **Chinese version.** The "stress" wordplay does not work in Chinese. Use 重音 (stress on a word) and 重心 (where the weight goes), which share 重.

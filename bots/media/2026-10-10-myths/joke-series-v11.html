Asanas named after long-life stories

The tortoise who held a mountain

Kurmasana, Supta Kurmasana · Primary

The story goes that the gods and the asuras churned the ocean for amrita, the nectar of long life. A mountain was the churning stick, and a tortoise held it on his back. In the Bhagavata Purana the tortoise is Vishnu, and the turning mountain felt like a nice back scratch.

Source: Mahabharata, Adi Parva 18 (Ganguli); Bhagavata Purana 8.7.10

  • 28 Ask the wrong expertI asked my back about Supta Kurmasana. It said the tortoise held a whole mountain and called it a back scratch. Then it asked me to stop calling two legs a mountain.

Ask the tortoise, he is older

Kurmasana · Primary. The crane (baka) is Bakasana · Intermediate

The story goes that a man whose good deeds were forgotten asked the old sage Markandeya: do you know me? The sage sent him to an older owl. The owl sent him to an older crane. The crane sent him to the oldest one, the tortoise Akupara. The tortoise cried and remembered him: the cows he gave away had dug this lake with their hooves.

Source: Mahabharata, Vana Parva 198 (Ganguli)

  • 12 The timelineThe sage sends him to the owl. The owl sends him to the crane. The crane sends him to the tortoise. In my shala, I'm the tortoise now. Slow, and I still can't find my glasses.
  • 39 Hard truth firstIn the story, the oldest one in the lake is the one who remembers kindness. That's the prize of getting old. Not the years. The remembering. I remember every mat in the room. The names are a work in progress.

Three lifetimes, one handful

Bharadvajasana · Intermediate Used in a script, 29 Sep

The story goes that the sage Bharadvaja studied the Vedas for three lifetimes. When he was old and tired, Indra offered him a fourth life. He said he would use it to study again. Indra showed him three shapes like mountains, took one handful from each and said: this is what you learned. The Vedas have no end.

Source: Taittiriya Brahmana 3.10.11 (Muir, 1868, p. 17)

  • 26 The pedantBharadvaja studied for three lifetimes and got three handfuls. I've studied for twenty years. Actually I have one handful. Actually a pinch. Actually it's under my mat.
  • 14 The honest whyPeople ask why I still practise after twenty years. Honest answer: Bharadvaja did three lifetimes and was shown he had a handful. I'm not at the handful yet.

The ray of light in the Big Dipper

Marichyasana A to D · Primary

Marichi means ray of light. He is one of the seven great sages, and in Indian star lore the seven sages are the seven stars of the Big Dipper. Marichi is a star in the handle. A very old Vedic text says the seven sages were once called the Bears.

Source: Monier-Williams, Sanskrit-English Dictionary (1899), p. 790; Shatapatha Brahmana 2.1.2.4

  • 17 Take the cliche literallyMarichi means ray of light. In Marichyasana D, I also see stars. Different reason.

The fish who kept still and listened

Ardha Matsyendrasana · Intermediate

A legend, as B.K.S. Iyengar tells it. Shiva explained the secrets of yoga to Parvati on a lonely island. A fish near the shore heard everything. It listened with full attention and did not move. Shiva saw that the fish had learned yoga, sprinkled water on it, and it became Matsyendra, Lord of the Fishes, who spread yoga.

Source: B.K.S. Iyengar, Light on Yoga (1966), Ardha Matsyendrasana I

  • 10 Good news, bad newsGood news: the first yoga student in the story was a fish. Bad news: it was a better student than me. It kept still.

The old monkey whose tail would not move

Source: Mahabharata, Vana Parva 146 (Ganguli)

Live as long as the story is told

Hanumanasana · Advanced A

A legend from the last book of the Ramayana. Hanuman asked Rama for one wish: to stay alive for as long as people tell Rama's story. Rama hugged him and said it would be so. That is why tradition calls Hanuman a chiranjeevi, a long-lived one, still here while the story is told.

Source: Valmiki Ramayana, Uttara Kanda 40 (Dutt, 1894)

The old bear who could still jump ninety

In the Ramayana, the monkeys must jump across the sea: a hundred yojanas, an old unit of distance. The leaders say they can leap ten, twenty, up to eighty. A hundred autumns

Source: Rig Veda 7.66.16 (Griffith, 1891)

Old book promises

Six months upside down, goodbye grey hair

Sirsasana, Sarvangasana · Finishing. The book means Viparita Karani

Myth. The Hatha Yoga Pradipika says: head on the ground, feet to the sky, 'for a second only the first day, and increase this time daily.' Then: 'After six months, the wrinkles and grey hair are not seen.'

What is true. The first line is great coaching. No study has tested the second. In a Columbia study of 14 people, a few grey hairs got their colour back when stress went down, once after a holiday. It did not test yoga.

Source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 3.80 to 3.81 (Sinh, 1914); Rosenberg et al., eLife 2021

  • 7 Good answer, silly answerHow long until the grey goes? The old book says six months. The new study says one holiday. I teach six mornings a week. I'll never know.

The old man who becomes sixteen

Mula Bandha, Uddiyana Bandha · every series

Myth. The Hatha Yoga Pradipika promises youth from the bandhas. With Mula Bandha, 'even an old man becomes young'. One breath practice makes you young, 'as it were 16 years old'.

What is true. Nobody turns sixteen. But at the Mayo Clinic, 12 weeks of interval training raised the energy power of muscle cells by 69% in people aged 65 to 80. Old muscle still answers training.

Source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.47, 3.64 (Sinh, 1914); Robinson et al., Cell Metabolism 2017

  • 37 Nothing happenedTwenty years of Mula Bandha and I'm still not sixteen. The only part of me acting like a teenager is my hamstrings. Stiff, stubborn, and not listening to the teacher.

The peacock that digests poison

Mayurasana · Intermediate

Myth. The Hatha Yoga Pradipika says Mayurasana removes all diseases, digests too much bad food, and destroys kalakuta. In legend, kalakuta is the poison that rose when the ocean was churned. Shiva held it in his throat to protect the world.

What is true. No study has tested this. Enjoy the arm balance. Keep away from poison.

Source: Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.31 (1.33 in Sinh, 1914)

  • 17 Take the cliche literallyThe old book says the peacock digests even the world's worst poison. I've tested my peacock on som tam from my street. Som tam won.

Sweat, tremble, hop like a frog, walk on air

Utpluthih · Finishing (theme only)

Myth. The Shiva Samhita lists the stages of breath practice. First the body sweats. Then it trembles. Then the yogi jumps about 'like a frog'. Later, the text says, he walks in the air.

What is true. These are the old text's words about pranayama, not a plan for your Tuesday. Nobody walks on air. Tell it as a legend.

Source: Shiva Samhita 3.40 to 3.46 (Vasu, 1914)

  • 12 The timelineStage one: sweat. Stage two: tremble. Stage three: hop like a frog. Stage four: walk on air. In Bangkok I get stage one before I unroll the mat. Twenty years in, I've reached the frog. I'm staying there.

What people believe about age

Ashtanga is only for the young

Every series, from Surya Namaskara A

Myth. Ashtanga is for twenty-year-olds.

What is true. Some said yoga should stop at forty. In Yoga Mala, Pattabhi Jois answered: 'This is not borne out by experience.' And: 'In short, there is no age restriction for the practice of yoga.' He quotes the Hatha Yoga Pradipika: young, old or very old, sick or weak, practice brings success. 'Old', he wrote, means above sixty, and 'very old' beyond ninety. The only one left out is the lazy one.

Source: Pattabhi Jois, Yoga Mala, section 39 Shirshasana (tr. Eddie Stern); Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.66 (Sinh, 1914)

  • 3 They think I'mPeople think I'm getting old. Guruji wrote that 'old' means above sixty. So I'm not old. My hamstrings have lost their best excuse.
  • 40 The closerStart at any age. The old book leaves out only one person: the lazy one. Don't be him. He's very comfortable, and he's not in the book.

Too old to build muscle

Utkatasana · Surya Namaskara B (theme only) Used in a carousel, 9 Oct

Myth. After a certain age, you can't build muscle.

What is true. In a 1990 study, ten frail nursing-home residents, average age 90 and up to 96, did eight weeks of heavy leg training. In the nine who finished, strength rose 174% on average, thigh muscle grew 9%, and walking speed improved 48%.

Source: Fiatarone et al., JAMA 1990;263(22):3029

  • 28 Ask the wrong expertI asked my legs if they're too old to get stronger. They said yes. Then I showed them this study. Average age ninety. They've stopped talking to me.

Too late to start in midlife

Surya Namaskara A, the first thing a beginner learns

Myth. If you didn't exercise young, it's too late.

What is true. A US study of 315,059 adults: people inactive for most of adult life who became active at 40 to 61 had a 35% lower risk of dying during the study than the always inactive. The most active lifelong group: 36%. Almost the same.

Source: Saint-Maurice et al., JAMA Network Open 2019;2(3):e190355

  • 38 The exact numberPeople who started in their forties ended up almost level with the lifelong ones. 35% against 36%. I've set my alarm for four for twenty years. For one percent.

Older women shouldn't lift heavy

Chaturanga Dandasana (body weight, theme only)

Myth. Women with weak bones should not lift heavy.

What is true. In Australia's LIFTMOR trial, 101 women around 65 with low bone mass were split in two. One half lifted heavy, with a coach, twice a week for 8 months. Their spine bone density rose 2.9%. The gentle home group lost 1.2%. Only with a qualified coach and a doctor's OK.

Source: Watson et al., J Bone Miner Res 2018;33(2):211

  • 5 I can, I cannotI can teach fifty people Chaturanga. I cannot pick up my own mat with straight legs.

Unfit? It's just age

Savasana (theme only) Used in a carousel, 9 Oct

Myth. Losing fitness is just age.

What is true. In 1966, five healthy 20-year-old men in Dallas stayed in bed for three weeks. Their aerobic fitness fell 27%. Thirty years of ageing later cost them about 11%. Bed did more harm than birthdays. Six months of training brought them back to their 1966 starting level.

Source: McGuire et al., Circulation 2001; Mitchell et al., Circulation 2019

  • 14 The honest whyPeople ask why I still get up at four. Honest answer: three weeks in bed did more harm than thirty years of birthdays. That's science. My body still votes for the bed.

10,000 steps is a medical rule

Samasthitih (Ashtanga counts breaths, not steps)

Myth. You need 10,000 steps a day.

What is true. The goal likely came from a 1965 Japanese pedometer named manpo-kei, 10,000-step meter. In a Harvard study of 16,741 women, average age 72, about 4,400 steps a day was linked to a 41% lower death rate than 2,700, with the benefit levelling off near 7,500.

Source: Lee et al., JAMA Intern Med 2019;179(8):1105

  • 36 Big word, plain thingTen thousand steps. A pedometer name from 1965. Not a doctor in sight.

Muscle turns into fat

Utthita Hasta Padangusthasana · Primary (theme only)

Myth. Stop training and your muscle turns into fat.

What is true. In a 2007 study, 18 young adults kept one leg off the ground for four weeks. Thigh muscle shrank 7.4%, while fat between the muscles grew 14.5%. Two different tissues moved in opposite directions. Muscle did not become fat.

Source: Manini et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2007;85(2):377

  • 17 Take the cliche literallyThey say use it or lose it. True. But muscle doesn't turn into fat. It just moves out. The fat moves into the empty room.

Flexibility is gone for good after 60

Paschimottanasana · Primary

Myth. After sixty, stiffness is for life.

What is true. A 2019 review of 22 trials in adults with an average age of 60 or more found that yoga, compared with no exercise, improved lower-body flexibility, balance and leg strength. Moderate gains, not miracles. The body still answered practice.

Source: Sivaramakrishnan et al., Int J Behav Nutr Phys Act 2019;16:33

  • 10 Good news, bad newsGood news: research says flexibility still improves after sixty. Bad news: my hamstrings heard "after sixty" and decided to wait.

After 60, balance is just gone

Utthita Hasta Padangusthasana, Ardha Baddha Padmottanasana · Primary

Myth. After sixty, balance is simply gone.

What is true. A 2016 review of six trials with 307 people aged 60 and over: yoga gave a small improvement in balance and a medium improvement in mobility, getting up and moving around. The authors say we do not yet know if it prevents falls.

Source: Youkhana et al., Age and Ageing 2016;45(1):21

  • 24 Head vs mirrorIn my head I put on my socks standing on one leg. In the mirror I'm sitting down and calling it mindfulness.

Getting up off the floor means nothing

Every seated vinyasa, and Utpluthih (theme only)

Myth. Sitting down and standing up from the floor is just a party trick.

What is true. Researchers in Brazil scored 2,002 adults aged 51 to 80 on sitting down and standing up with no help from hands or knees, 0 to 10. Over about six years, each extra point was linked to 21% better survival.

Source: Brito et al., Eur J Prev Cardiol 2014;21(7):892

  • 36 Big word, plain thingLongevity test. Sit on the floor. Stand up. No hands. That's the whole test. I take it every morning. Out loud.

No upside down after 50

Sirsasana, Halasana · Finishing; Downward Dog

Myth. After fifty, going upside down turns dangerous.

What is true. In a 2006 study of 75 yoga practitioners, headstand doubled eye pressure, and age did not predict how much. In a small 2015 study, Downward Dog raised it the most of four head-down asanas, back to normal two minutes after sitting up. Glaucoma or high blood pressure? Ask your eye doctor first.

Source: Baskaran et al., Ophthalmology 2006; Jasien et al., PLOS ONE 2015

  • 35 What I really meanWhen I call Downward Dog a resting asana, I mean my eyes disagree. In the study it raised eye pressure more than Halasana.

What people believe about yoga and exercise

You have to be flexible to start

Paschimottanasana · Primary; Uttanasana in Surya Namaskara

Myth. You must be flexible before you start yoga.

What is true. Patanjali's Yoga Sutra 2.46 defines asana as 'stable-and-easy posture'. Toes are not mentioned. In a 2016 trial, 118 inactive adults, average age 62, did hatha yoga three times a week for 8 weeks. Their flexibility improved, about as much as a stretching and strength group.

Source: Yoga Sutra 2.46 (Woods, 1914); Gothe and McAuley, J Gerontol A 2016

  • 26 The pedantPatanjali's definition of asana never mentions toes. I checked. Twice. Then I tried to reach mine. Also twice.

Yoga is just stretching

Chaturanga Dandasana in Surya Namaskara A and B

Myth. Yoga is only stretching.

What is true. In a small 2001 study, 10 untrained young adults did hatha yoga at least twice a week for 8 weeks. Elbow strength rose 31% and knee-muscle endurance 57%. In 2011, six trained young people doing four rounds of Sun Salutation reached 90% of their predicted maximum heart rate in round four.

Source: Tran et al., Prev Cardiol 2001; Mody, J Bodyw Mov Ther 2011

  • 13 Two voicesGentle stretching, for all levels. (pause) Five Surya Namaskara later, my shirt asks for a transfer.

Sweat means fat is melting

The breath with sound, in every vinyasa

Myth. The more you sweat, the more fat you burn.

What is true. Sweat is mostly water and salt. Burned fat mostly leaves through the lungs: of 10 kg lost, 8.4 kg is breathed out as carbon dioxide. Two Sydney researchers asked 150 doctors, dietitians and trainers where lost fat goes. Only three got it right.

Source: Meerman and Brown, BMJ 2014;349:g7257

  • 16 The ladderSweat isn't fat leaving. It's water. It's salt. It's Bangkok. The fat leaves through my mouth, one breath at a time. Very slowly.

A hot room sweats out toxins

Surya Namaskara A and B, the heat-building start

Myth. A hot, sweaty practice flushes out toxins.

What is true. A University of Ottawa team estimated that two litres of sweat carry less than a tenth of a nanogram of fat-stored pollutants. Your liver and kidneys do the cleaning. The old Hatha Yoga Pradipika says the opposite of detox: rub your sweat back into the skin to make the body strong.

Source: Imbeault et al., Environment International 2018; Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.13

  • 36 Big word, plain thingDetox. Two litres of sweat, a tenth of a nanogram of pollutants, and one towel that needs a holiday.

Twists wring toxins out of your organs

Marichyasana C and D · Primary; Ardha Matsyendrasana · Intermediate

Myth. Twists squeeze toxins out of your organs like a wet towel.

What is true. Herpreet Thind, a UMass Lowell researcher on yoga and chronic disease, told Yoga Journal there is 'no scientific basis' for this. A healthy liver cleans the body without twisting. Twists still help you turn and move.

Source: Yoga Journal, 18 Jan 2022

  • 24 Head vs mirrorIn my head, twenty years of Marichyasana D wrung me clean. In the mirror I'm not cleaner. Just stiffer, from a new angle.

No pain, no gain

Navasana · Primary

Myth. If you're not sore, it didn't work.

What is true. In a 2016 study, ten young men trained their legs for ten weeks. Muscle damage was highest in week one but did not predict growth. Soreness is a poor scorecard. Sharp pain or joint pain is a stop sign, not progress.

Source: Damas et al., J Physiol 2016

  • 17 Take the cliche literallyThey say no pain, no gain. I've been sore after Navasana for twenty years. By that rule I'd be a bodybuilder. I'm a man whose legs still won't straighten in the boat.

Only hard, sweaty exercise counts

Surya Namaskara A, the first step (theme only)

Myth. Gentle movement doesn't count.

What is true. A 2019 BMJ study of 36,383 adults, average age 63, wearing activity trackers: people in the top quarter for light movement had a 62% lower risk of early death than the bottom quarter. The biggest gain came from the first step up.

Source: Ekelund et al., BMJ 2019;366:l4570

  • 22 Bring a cakeScience says the first hour of light movement does the most good. Today I did mine before six. Looking for my glasses. Someone bring a cake.

Stretch first and you won't get hurt

Padangusthasana, Padahastasana · standing

Myth. A quick stretch before exercise prevents injury.

What is true. A 2014 review of 25 trials and 26,610 people found stretching made no real difference to sports injuries. Strength training cut them to under a third. Stretching still helps range of motion. It just isn't armour.

Source: Lauersen et al., Br J Sports Med 2014;48(11):871

  • 13 Two voicesStretch first and you won't get hurt. (pause) I've stretched my hamstrings every morning for twenty years. They still haven't heard.

In headstand, the head just rests

Sirsasana · Finishing

Myth. In headstand, the arms do the work and the head just rests.

What is true. A 2015 study measured 45 practitioners aged 18 to 60. At peak moments the head carried 40 to 48% of body weight. Lifting up with straight legs together tended to load the neck more slowly and more evenly than bent-leg entries.

Source: Hector and Jensen, J Bodyw Mov Ther 2015;19(3):434

  • 21 The confessionI'll be honest. In Sirsasana my head carries almost half my weight. It's the hardest work it does all day.

Yoga is gentle, so it can't hurt

No single asana

Myth. Yoga is too gentle to hurt anyone.

What is true. US emergency rooms saw an estimated 29,590 yoga injuries from 2001 to 2014, most often to the trunk, and the rate was higher in people 65 and over. A 2015 review of the 94 trials that reported side effects found yoga about as safe as usual care and exercise.

Source: Swain and McGwin, Orthop J Sports Med 2016; Cramer et al., Am J Epidemiol 2015

  • 40 The closerYoga can hurt you. So can stairs. Warm up, go slow, tell your teacher about old injuries. Then come back tomorrow. That's the whole safety plan.

Yoga either fixes pain or causes it

Downward Dog, Chaturanga in Surya Namaskara

Myth. Yoga either fixes pain or causes it.

What is true. A one-year study of 354 people from two yoga studios found both. 10.7% got new pain from yoga, mostly in the shoulder, elbow, wrist and hand. Yet 74% said yoga improved pain they already had. Tell your teacher about injuries before class.

Source: Campo et al., J Bodyw Mov Ther 2018;22(2):418

  • 16 The ladderMy hamstrings weren't stiff. They were concrete. Twenty years later they're softer concrete. I count that in the 74%.

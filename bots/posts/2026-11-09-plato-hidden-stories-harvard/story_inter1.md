# Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. ? · "The Rock That Answered" · Asana: Nakrasana (Intermediate Series, Nadi Shodhana, 2nd series) · Lane B · ask word: aybkk

Writer's script, 9 Nov 2026. Story id inter-B-rock-that-answered (key inter1). No episode number until it is filmed. Beat order H, DH, T1, RH, T2, P.

Lane B, and why: the point is the habit. Same practice, same time, a teacher who notices. Nakrasana comes in as a picture of noticing. It is not a fix for an asana people are stuck in. Lane A needs a real asana fix and the STUCK ask, and this story does not give one. So the caption asks for "aybkk" (keyword test, frozen until 22 Nov).

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If your practice changes every day, you have no normal to compare with. A small change goes past you, like hips a little higher or a shorter breath. It goes past your teacher too. So the same mistake stays for years. Or you drift away and start again from day one next year.

Why care: Maybe you feel the same practice every morning is boring, and you look for something new. This story turns that feeling around. The boring road is how the monkey saw the trap. Your same asanas are how you, and your teacher, see what changed.

## 3. Toolkit

Toolkit pick: /hook (contrarian lever, bank #10 flipped) + Fork-First Story.
Why: The hook says the viewer's own thought out loud ("Boring, right?") and flips it in 5 words. Boonchu wants the point to land in about 6 seconds, and it lands at 0:07. The story has a fork built in: the rock looks higher, so jump or not? The viewer chooses before the monkey does (Harvard #1). Then the rehook is the funniest moment in the book. The crocodile answers for the rock. A 5-year-old gets that joke, and it lands at about 0:26 to 0:31.

Toolkit choice:
- Triple Hook (#121 shape: "Same practice every day." / "You think it's boring." / "Boring is how you notice.") + /story 4-beat. SAY, SHOW and WRITE would work, but it costs one more line (about 2 s), and the 4-beat skeleton has no stop where the viewer chooses.
- Gervais deadpan opener ("Rocks don't talk. That's how the monkey knew.") + Vibe Samurai turn. It is short and funny, but it gives away the twist. A joke this early can also feel like we laugh at the monkey, who is the Bodhisatta. And the turn pulls toward a banned "not X but Y" line.

## 4. Hook bank

Bank line, #10 [teach] fit=use goal=trust habit=10:
- template: "How to be happy with {ordinary thing}"
- aybkk: "How to be happy with the same practice every morning"
- note: "Mysore is the same practice each day, so this turns 'boring' into the point; show it, do not promise happiness."

How I adapted it:
- I flipped the how-to into a contrarian claim. The bank's own note says to turn "boring" into the point, so line 1 says "boring" out loud and line 2 makes it the tool.
- I dropped "happy". The note says do not promise happiness.
- Line 1 is the viewer's own inner voice and ends with Boonchu's "right?" (the mirror rule). Line 2 is the flip, "Boring is how you notice." It is not a "not X but Y" line. It makes the viewer ask "notice what?"
- Line 3 is the double hook. It raises the stakes with a claim plus a because (#27), and it opens the loop: what trap, and how does a boring road help? The word "crocodile" stays hidden until line 13.

## 5. Harvard tricks used

- #1 Decide before you know the ending: line 10, "Jump or not? What you do?", then a 0.8 s pause. Line 11 answers it ("No jump.").
- #27 Every claim needs a because: line 3 ("because his road never change") and line 19 ("Same road, so he see"). The proof is planted at line 8 ("He always check..."). The book gives this because itself: "For the story goes that the Bodhisatta always marked the exact height of the water..."
- #21 Teach them something about their own problem: line 2 and lines 20 to 22. The sameness they call boring is the measuring stick that makes a small change easy to see, for you and for the teacher.
- #30 Sell only the next step: the spoken video sells nothing. No price, no course, no link line. The caption has ONE ask (comment aybkk).
Not used: #2 and #20. The close is a question to the viewer, not a cost question.

One thread check: lines 1 to 3 talk to adults. Lines 4 to 18 tell the story straight, start to end, with no jumping out. Lines 19 to 24 come back to the adults. "What you do?" stays in the bedtime voice, the way you ask your child.

5-year-old test (lines 4 to 18 alone): A big monkey lives by a river. There is a mango island and one rock halfway. Every day he jumps there and comes home the same way. He always checks how high the water is and how high the rock is. One evening the water is the same but the rock looks higher. Jump or not? He does not jump. He calls "Hi, rock!" and asks why the rock does not answer today. A crocodile is on the rock. The crocodile thinks the rock always talks, so he answers, "Yes, monkey?" There is no other way home. The monkey says "Open your mouth, catch me when I jump." The mouth opens, the eyes close, and the monkey jumps on the head and goes home. It makes sense alone.

## 10. Flags

1. The asana line is a teaching picture, not from the book. "Hip higher? Teacher see." is Plato's words about Nakrasana. Boonchu should confirm it, or replace line 22 with his own cue. The book only gives the name link (nakra = crocodile). Plato's other asana words, "Low and long", were cut on 9 Nov.
2. The book never says the crocodile lay flat or low. It says he "took his stand on the rock", and the rock "seemed to stand higher". So the story lines say only "On the rock, a crocodile." The low, long shape belongs only to the side-view asana shot at line 21, and nobody says it. Line 9 says "Rock look higher", which follows "seemed".
3. Eyes closing. The narrator says crocodiles shut their eyes when they open their mouths, and the translator's footnote says that is not true. The script says only "His eyes close", about this one crocodile in the story. The caption says the same. Never say it as a fact about crocodiles.
4. The monkey is the Bodhisatta, the Buddha in a past life. He is told kindly. He is smart, he checks every day, and no joke is made about him. He is never called "monkey king", and the pictures give him no crown. The caption says "old Buddhist stories" but does not say the monkey is the Buddha-to-be. If Boonchu wants that line for Thai viewers (they know ชาดก), add it. But then never liken the teacher to the monkey, or Boonchu could look like he compares himself to the Buddha. That is why line 22 says "Teacher see" and does not say "like the monkey". Ask a Thai reader before posting.
5. Compressed, not changed. The book has one "Hi! rock!", then three more calls. The script says it once, and the caption says "He called three times". The book's "How comes it, friend rock..." becomes "Rock, why no answer today?" The "Who are you? I'm a crocodile." exchange is cut. "Then home" stands for "gained the bank", because he lives on the bank. "Same way home" is the book's "returning at evening by the way he came".
6. "Monkey see a trap" (line 3) is a retelling. The book says the crocodile waited "meaning to catch the monkey", and that the monkey "suspected that a crocodile might be lurking there to catch him" (line 9583, which I checked myself). The line is about the monkey. Nothing in the script says that the same practice keeps you safe or prevents injury. The payoff is only about seeing a change.
7. "Boring is how you notice" and the Stakes are Boonchu's view from teaching, not a study. No number anywhere.
8. "Every morning" is loose. Ashtanga has rest days (moon days, and at AYBKK Sunday). Bank #10 uses "every morning". If a purist objects, say "Same practice every day".
9. Do not cast the viewer as the crocodile. Line 22 compares the viewer's hips to the rock that looked higher, and makes the viewer (line 20, "so you see") and the teacher the ones who see, like the monkey. If Boonchu feels it still makes students the crocodile, drop line 22 and keep his cue only.
10. Nakrasana is often taught with hops forward and back in a low position. This script does not describe the hops, because they are not in the checked sources. The side-view shot shows the shape, and his cue can name the action.
11. A purist may say "the crocodile and monkey story is the heart in the fig tree". That is a different, better-known telling (Panchatantra Book 4, Jataka 208). The book's note also points to No. 224 (Kumbhila) and to Chinese (Beal) and Japanese (Griffin) versions, which were not opened. Never say this version is unknown in China or Japan. We tell No. 57 only.
12. The crocodile wants to catch the monkey. That is mild bedtime danger. Nobody is hurt, the crocodile praises him and goes home, and "eat your heart" is never said. Keep the pictures friendly: no teeth close-up, no blood, no chase.
13. Lane B and platforms: plato.md (28 Sep, Two Lanes) says "Lane B never goes on WeChat or Xiaohongshu." That older rule also put Hidden Stories in its own "Lane A (world)". The keyword-test lanes (A = STUCK, B = aybkk) are a different split. Ask Boonchu before making the ZH caption and the XHS title, or make a version for those platforms with no aybkk ask.
14. Keyword test: the "aybkk" ask is frozen until 22 Nov. If this is posted later, check the ask word again.
15. Theme echo: "same and slow every day" was the topic of the 19 Oct cow-pace hooks (bots/posts/2026-10-19-plato-cow-pace.md). The hook line is not the same, but post apart from anything on that theme.
16. Length: 139 words ("Low and long." cut on 9 Nov). About 59 s at a normal pace, about 61 s with the bedtime voice on lines 4 to 18, and about 63 s if every line is slow. For a full bedtime-voice take, use cuts 1 and 2 in part 7.
17. The close "If practice change every day, how you know what changed?" is a question only. It promises nothing from AYBKK. The only AYBKK claim is the confirmed one in the caption, and it does not scold home or online practice.
18. Caption: no price, one ask, no quoted thinkers. The quote marks hold only the book's exact words ("Hi! rock!", "Yes, monkey; what is it?", "Open your mouth and catch me when I jump."). The caption is long because it retells the story for sound-off and translated viewers. If Boonchu wants his short default, keep caption lines 1, 2, 3, 4 (the hook), 23, 24, 25 (Nakrasana), 26 (the source), 27 and 28, plus the hashtags.

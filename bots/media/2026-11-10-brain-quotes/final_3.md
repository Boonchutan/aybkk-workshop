## 1. aristotle-body-before-mind — PASS
- **Print:** Now it is clear that in education practice must be used before theory, and the body be trained before the mind
- **Greek:** ἐπεὶ δὲ φανερὸν πρότερον τοῖς ἔθεσιν ἢ τῷ λόγῳ παιδευτέον εἶναι, καὶ περὶ τὸ σῶμα πρότερον ἢ τὴν διάνοιαν
- **Credit:** Aristotle · Politics VIII.3 (1338b4-6), about 330 BC · tr. Benjamin Jowett, revised by W. D. Ross (Oxford, 1921)
- **Plain:** Aristotle said that in bringing up children, practice comes before theory and training the body comes before training the mind.
- **Opened:** archive.org theworksofaristo10arisuoft (1921 full text); Perseus Greek tlg0086.tlg035 grc2; archive.org politicsofaristo01arisuoft (1885); classics.mit.edu politics.8.eight
- **Evidence (1921):** "To be always seeking after the useful does not become free and exalted souls. Now it is clear that in education practice must be used before theory, and the body be trained before the mind ; and therefore boys should be handed over to the trainer, who creates in them the proper habit of body, and to the wrestling-master, who teaches them their exercises." Next chapter: "for children they should be of a lighter kind, avoiding severe diet or painful toil, lest the growth of the body be impaired." Preface signed W. D. Ross ("in revising his translation").
- **Corrected / notes:** (1) These words are the 1921 revised text only. Jowett's 1885 first edition, vol. I p. 248, reads "in education habit must go before reason, and the body before the mind". Never credit this wording to "Jowett 1885". (2) The MIT web copy has a typo ("roper habit"); do not copy from it. (3) The Greek says literally "by habits before by reasoning"; "practice before theory" is Ross's wording. (4) Plain line changed: the source says "in education", not "a school". (5) Keep the caption honest: the next chapter warns against hard training for children.

## 2. ratey-point-of-exercise — PASS
- **Print:** Building muscles and conditioning the heart and lungs are essentially side effects. I often tell my patients that the point of exercise is to build and condition the brain.
- **Credit:** John J. Ratey, MD, with Eric Hagerman · Spark: The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the Brain, 2008 (Introduction)
- **Plain:** A doctor says he tells his patients that stronger muscles and a fitter heart are side effects, and that the point of exercise is to build up the brain.
- **Opened:** archive.org sparkrevolutiona0000rate (Little, Brown 2008, ISBN 978-0-316-11350-2), read through archive.org full-text search and Open Library search-inside; scan page map (scandata.xml)
- **Evidence (scan text, overlapping hits):** "Introduction Making the Connection WE ALL KNOW that exercise makes us feel better, but most of us have no ... boosting endorphins, and we leave it at that. But the real reason we feel so good when we get our blood pumping is that it makes the brain function at its best, and in my view, this benefit of physical activity is far more important — and fascinating — than what it does for the body. Building muscles and conditioning the heart and lungs are essentially side effects. I often tell my patients that the point of exercise is to build and condition the brain. In today's technology-driven, plasma-screened-in world, it's easy to forget that we are born movers". Contents: "Introduction: Making the Connection 3". About the Author: "John J. Ratey, MD, is an associate clinical professor of psychiatry at Harvard Medical School".
- **Notes:** (1) The scan is lending-only, so the page image was not opened; the words come from the scan's own text layer, checked with five overlapping searches. Page 3 rests on the contents line and the page map (the Introduction starts on p. 3 and the line is about 90 words in). Safer credit: "Introduction" without a page number. (2) It is his opinion ("in my view", "I often tell my patients"), not a study result. Do not present it as proven fact. (3) Publisher's site today calls him "clinical professor"; the 2008 book says "associate clinical professor". Use "psychiatrist, Harvard Medical School".

## 3. bernstein-motor-needs-brain — PASS
- **Print (EN):** Motor tasks (motor needs) and the implacable necessity to move more quickly, accurately, and dexterously were the leading causes for the development of the brain and its accessories during all the millenia of evolution.
- **Print (RU):** Именно двигательные задачи, двигательные потребности, неумолимая жизненная необходимость двигаться все проворнее, все точнее, все ловче — вот что было ведущим началом в развитии мозга и всех его вспомогательных органов почти на всем протяжении его многовековой эволюции.
- **Credit:** Nikolai A. Bernstein · On Dexterity and Its Development (written late 1940s, first printed 1991) · tr. Mark L. Latash, 1996. RU: Н. А. Бернштейн · «О ловкости и её развитии», 1991, с. 126-127
- **Plain:** A Russian scientist of movement wrote that the brain developed over a very long time mainly because animals had to move faster, more exactly and more skilfully.
- **Opened:** elib.old.gnpbu.ru/text/bernshteyn_o-lovkosti_1991 (Russian 1991 full text with page marks); archive.org dexterityitsdeve0000unse (English 1996, search inside)
- **Evidence RU (pp. 126-127, section «Новые задачи и обрастание мозга», Очерк IV «О построении движений»):** "Двигательные задачи, которые животным приходилось решать, становились все более трудными и разнообразными. Именно двигательные задачи ... многовековой эволюции. Разве лишь только в самый последний, относительно очень короткий, отрезок времени условия, быть может, несколько переменились. У человека ... движения утратили свое решающее значение и начали отступать на второй план перед умственными и трудовыми потребностями."
- **Evidence EN (p. 104; next running head "ESSAY 4: ON THE CONSTRUCTION OF MOVEMENTS 105"):** "...became more complex and diverse. Motor tasks (motor needs) and the implacable necessity to move more quickly, accurately, and dexterously were the leading causes for the development of the brain and its accessories during all the millenia of evolution. The conditions might have changed only during the last, relatively very short period of ..." Title page: "by Nicholai A. Bernstein, Translated by Mark L. Latash, 1996, Lawrence Erlbaum".
- **Notes:** (1) The book prints "millenia" (one n). That is the exact text; "millennia" is the normal spelling. Owner's choice. (2) The English says "during all"; the Russian says "почти на всем протяжении" (almost the whole). For Russian posts use the Russian line. (3) It is about evolution of the brain in animals, not about a child's or an adult's brain; Bernstein adds that in humans movement has lately moved to second place behind mental and work needs. Do not caption it as "exercise grows your brain". (4) Do not cut the sentence before "during all the millenia of evolution": the cut hides that it is about evolution. (5) Plain line changed: "leading causes" is "mainly because", not "because".

## 4. fukuzawa-beast-body-first — HELD BACK (text is right; safety for China fails)
- **Print (if used):** Get an animal's body; then develop a man's mind.
- **Japanese:** 先ず獣身を成して後に人心を養う (1899 print: 先づ獣身を成して後に人心を養ふ)
- **Credit:** Fukuzawa Yukichi · The Autobiography of Fukuzawa Yukichi (福翁自伝), 1899 · tr. Eiichi Kiyooka, 1934
- **Plain:** A Japanese teacher said he raised his children by one rule: first build a strong body like a young animal's, then train the mind.
- **Opened:** dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2387720 frame 252 (1899 first edition, page image read); aozora.gr.jp 1864_61590; archive.org in.ernet.dli.2015.172747 (Hokuseido 1934); archive.org autobiography-of-fukuzawa-yukichi (Columbia 1966)
- **Evidence:** 1899 p. 491 (四百九十一), image: "扨又子供の教育法に就ては私は専ら身體の方を大事にして幼少の時から強ひて讀書などさせない先づ獸身を成して後に人心を養ふと云ふのが私の主義であるから生れて三歳五歳まではいろはの字も見せず七八歳にもなれば手習をさせたりさせなかつたりマダ讀書はさせない". 1934 p. 323: "To continue with my ideas on bringing up children, my chief care is always for their physical health. I have said, “ Get an animal’s body ; then develop a man’s mind.” I do not even show them a single letter of the alphabet until after they are four or five years old." 1966 p. 301, side heading "The body must be built first": "Grow an animal's body; then develop a man's mind."
- **Why held back:** every fact checks out (words, page, year, translator, context). The problem is the author's name in China: Fukuzawa wrote "Leaving Asia" (脱亚论) and publicly cheered the 1894-95 war against China as "civilization against barbarism"; Chinese writers often call him an advocate of invading China. The business runs in China and the safety list says no war and no names that are a scandal. Not for Xiaohongshu, WeChat or any Chinese post. Fine for English, Thai, Russian and Japanese readers if the owner decides so.
- **Other notes:** credit the 1934 wording ("Get"); the 1966 edition says "Grow". The line is about raising small children (no reading lessons before about 8 to 10), not about adults.

## 5. xenophon-thinking-needs-the-body — PASS
- **Print:** Why, even in the process of thinking, in which the use of the body seems to be reduced to a minimum, ... grave mistakes may often be traced to bad health.
- **Greek:** ἐπεὶ καὶ ἐν ᾧ δοκεῖ ἐλαχίστη σώματος χρεία εἶναι, ἐν τῷ διανοεῖσθαι, τίς οὐκ οἶδεν ὅτι καὶ ἐν τούτῳ πολλοὶ μεγάλα σφάλλονται διὰ τὸ μὴ ὑγιαίνειν τὸ σῶμα;
- **Credit:** Socrates, in Xenophon · Memorabilia III.12.6, about 370 BC · tr. E. C. Marchant (Loeb, 1923)
- **Plain:** Socrates told a young man to train, because the body is used in everything we do, even in thinking, where bad health often causes big mistakes.
- **Opened:** archive.org XenophonMemorabiliamarchant (Loeb scan text, p. 253); Perseus tlg0032.tlg002 eng2 and grc2
- **Evidence (Loeb p. 253):** "For in everything that men do the body is useful ; and in all uses of the body it is of great importance to be in as high a state of physical efficiency as possible. Why, even in the process of thinking, in which the use of the body seems to be reduced to a minimum, it is matter of common knowledge that grave mistakes may often be traced to bad health. And because the body is in a bad condition, loss of memory, depression, discontent, insanity often assail the mind". Chapter opens: "On noticing that Epigenes, one of his companions, was in poor condition, for a young man, he said : “You look as if you need exercise, Epigenes.”"
- **Notes:** (1) The "..." stands for "it is matter of common knowledge that" (Greek: "who does not know that"). (2) The chapter's first argument is fitness for war (III.12.1-5); keep the caption to the thinking point and do not quote the lines before it. (3) The next sentence lists memory loss, depression and insanity: do not put that on a slide (no medical promise). (4) The full two-sentence quote in the candidate is also exact (III.12.5-6).

## 7. adolph-hoch-motor-enabling — PASS
- **Print:** Motor development is enabling: New motor skills create new opportunities for exploration and learning that instigate cascades of development across diverse psychological domains.
- **Credit:** Karen E. Adolph and Justine E. Hoch · "Motor Development: Embodied, Embedded, Enculturated, and Enabling", Annual Review of Psychology 70:141-164, 2019
- **Plain:** Each new thing a baby learns to do with its body, like sitting, crawling or walking, opens up new things to explore and learn.
- **Opened:** PMC6320716 (NCBI full text) and PubMed 30256718
- **Evidence (abstract):** "(c) Motor development is enculturated: Social and cultural influences shape motor behaviors. (d) Motor development is enabling: New motor skills create new opportunities for exploration and learning that instigate cascades of development across diverse psychological domains. For each of these key features, we show that changes in infants' bodies, environments, and experiences entail behavioral flexibility". Body: "Psychology is the study of mind and behavior, and all behavior is motor behavior." "But motor development should not be quarantined. It is foundational for all the chapters that come after." Both authors: Department of Psychology, New York University. DOI 10.1146/annurev-psych-010418-102836.
- **Notes:** (1) It is a review about infants, not a trial and not about adults. (2) The authors add a limit: "the availability of opportunities for learning does not guarantee that learning occurs ... new motor skills are neither necessary nor sufficient for driving more distant developmental achievements." Keep the caption at "opens new chances to learn", never "makes babies smarter".

## 8. erickson-hippocampus-2011 — PASS
- **Print:** In sum, we found that the hippocampus remains plastic in late adulthood and that 1 y of aerobic exercise was sufficient for enhancing volume.
- **Credit:** Erickson, Voss, Prakash and 15 others (senior author Arthur F. Kramer) · PNAS 108(7):3017-3022, 2011
- **Plain:** In this study, older adults who did a year of regular walking ended with a slightly bigger hippocampus (a brain area used for memory), while it got slightly smaller in the group that did stretching.
- **Opened:** pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3041121 (full text) and PubMed 21282661
- **Evidence:** Abstract: "Here we show, in a randomized controlled trial with 120 older adults, that aerobic exercise training increases the size of the anterior hippocampus, leading to improvements in spatial memory. Exercise training increased hippocampal volume by 2%, effectively reversing age-related loss in volume by 1 to 2 y." Results: "One hundred twenty older adults without dementia (Table 1) were randomly assigned to an aerobic exercise group (n = 60) or to a stretching control group (n = 60) ... increase in volume of the left and right hippocampus by 2.12% and 1.97%, respectively, over the 1-y period, whereas the stretching control group displayed a 1.40% and 1.43% decline". Table 1: age 67.6 (5.81) and 65.5 (5.44). Methods: "Community-dwelling older adults (n = 842) were recruited, and 179 were enrolled. One hundred forty-five participants completed the intervention ... 120 participants had complete MR data from all three sessions"; "between the ages of 55 and 80 y"; "moderate-intensity aerobic exercise 3 d/wk"; "until a duration of 40 min was achieved at week 7". Discussion: "In sum, we found that the hippocampus remains plastic in late adulthood and that 1 y of aerobic exercise was sufficient for enhancing volume."
- **Notes:** (1) Every number in the candidate matches the paper. (2) Do NOT put the abstract's "leading to improvements in spatial memory" on a slide: the Results say "Both groups showed improvements in memory" and "the aerobic exercise group did not improve performance above that achieved by the stretching control group"; PNAS printed a letter on this point (2011;108(18):E89). The volume finding is the measured result. (3) Do not use the paper's words "dementia", "reversing" or "neuroprotective" in a caption (no medical promise). (4) "1 y" is the journal's short form of "1 year"; keep it inside the quote marks and explain it in the caption. (5) Plain line changed: "brisk" is not in the paper (it says moderate-intensity walking, built up to 40 minutes, 3 days a week). (6) Authors also came from Ohio State and Rice, not only Pittsburgh and Illinois.

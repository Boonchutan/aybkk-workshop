# Lane "brainmove": scientists who say the brain is for movement / thinking grows out of movement

Research date: 10 Nov 2026. Every line below was read in the work itself (book scan, paper, transcript).
Downloads and page images: `out/dl_brainmove/`. Helper scripts: `out/scripts_brainmove/`.
None of these authors' lines are in `used.txt` (William James is there with two other lines: "If he keep faithfully busy..." and "Never suffer an exception").

How "read in the work itself" was done, per source:
- FULL TEXT = I have the whole text or the page image (TED transcript, Sperry PDF page images, Sherrington page images, Sechenov scans, Bernstein 1991 HTML, PMC XML, PubMed abstract, Gutenberg).
- SNIPPET = the book is a lending copy on archive.org; I read the words through archive.org full-text search inside that exact scan, stitching overlapping snippets. The words are from the book, but I could not see the whole page, so page numbers come from running heads in the snippets.

## Candidates sent (10)

### 1. Sperry 1952 (FULL TEXT, page images) fit 10
Roger W. Sperry, "Neurology and the Mind-Brain Problem", American Scientist 40(2), 1952, pp. 291-312.
URL: https://people.uncw.edu/puente/sperry/sperrypapers/50s/46-1952.pdf (image PDF; pages rendered and read by eye)
- p. 299: "In both its phylogenetic and ontogenetic histories, mental activity develops out of, and in reference to, overt action."
- pp. 297-298: "To the neurologist, regarding the brain from an objective, analytical standpoint, it is readily apparent that the sole product of brain function is motor coordination. To repeat: the entire output of our thinking machine consists of nothing but patterns of motor coordination." (italics in the original from "the entire output")
- p. 297: "Utilization of this motor approach immediately helps us to view the brain objectively for what it is, namely, a mechanism for governing motor activity. Its primary function is essentially the transforming of sensory patterns into patterns of motor coordination."
- p. 297: "In man as in the salamander the primary business of the brain continues to be the governing, directly or indirectly, of overt behavior."
- p. 299 (his own caveat, right after the main line): "Actually the interrelation of motor and mental activity is one of cyclic and reciprocal interdependence. However, the nature of the problem and current trends in our thinking make it necessary at this time to emphasize particularly the dependence of the mental upon motor activity."

### 3. Sherrington 1940 (FULL TEXT, page images checked) fit 9
Sir Charles Sherrington, Man on his Nature (Gifford Lectures, Edinburgh, 1937-8), Cambridge University Press, 1940.
URL: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.188837 (p. 197 = scan leaf n215; p. 213 = leaf n231)
- ch. VI "A Whole Presupposed of Its Parts", pp. 196-197: "The simpler motor acts were there before the mind—always meaning by that 'recognizable mind'. As we look along the scale of life, whether in time or in order of organization, muscle is there before nerve, and nerve is there before mind, 'recognizable mind'. It would seem to be the conative motor act under 'urge-to-live' which has been the cradle of mind. The conative motor act, mechanically integrating the individual, would seem to have started mind on its road to recognizability."
- same page: "As motor integration proceeds, mind proceeds with it, the servant of an 'urge' seeking satisfaction."
- ch. VII "The Brain and Its Work", p. 213: "Mind, recognizable mind, seems to have arisen in connection with the motor act. Where motor integration progressed and where motor behaviour progressively evolved, mind progressively evolved."
- ch. IV "The Wisdom of the Body", p. 116 (OCR text only, page image not checked): "The importance of muscular contraction to us can be stated by saying that all man can do is to move things, and his muscular contraction is his sole means thereto."

### 4. Bernstein, On Dexterity (Russian FULL TEXT; English SNIPPET) fit 9
Н. А. Бернштейн, «О ловкости и её развитии», М.: Физкультура и спорт, 1991 (written in the 1940s, first printed 1991; publ. prepared by I. M. Feigenberg).
URL (Russian, state pedagogical library): http://elib.gnpbu.ru/text/bernshteyn_o-lovkosti_1991/ (fetched as http://elib.old.gnpbu.ru/textpage/download/html/?book=bernshteyn_o-lovkosti_1991)
- Очерк IV «О построении движений», раздел «Новые задачи и обрастание мозга», с. 126-127:
  «Именно двигательные задачи, двигательные потребности, неумолимая жизненная необходимость двигаться все проворнее, все точнее, все ловче — вот что было ведущим началом в развитии мозга и всех его вспомогательных органов почти на всем протяжении его многовековой эволюции.»
- English, tr. Mark L. Latash, in: Dexterity and Its Development (eds. M. L. Latash and M. T. Turvey), Lawrence Erlbaum, 1996, Essay 4 (https://archive.org/details/dexterityitsdeve0000unse):
  "Motor tasks (motor needs) and the implacable necessity to move more quickly, accurately, and dexterously were the leading causes for the development of the brain and its accessories during all the millenia of evolution." ("millenia" as printed; Latash drops the Russian "почти" = "almost")
- Literal reading of the Russian: "It was precisely motor tasks, motor needs, the implacable vital necessity to move ever more nimbly, ever more precisely, ever more dexterously, that was the leading principle in the development of the brain and all its auxiliary organs through almost the whole of its age-long evolution."
- His caveat in the next sentences: only in the last, very short stretch of time, in humans, did movements start to step back behind mental and labour needs.
- EXTRA, same author, verified in Russian in two books: «...упражнение есть, в сущности, повторение без повторения.» ("exercise is, in essence, repetition without repetition"): «О построении движений» (1947), in the 1990 collection «Физиология движений и активность» (https://archive.org/details/19910_2025310421), and again in «О ловкости», Очерк VI: «Следовательно, правильно проводимое упражнение есть, в сущности, повторение без повторения.» Latash 1996: "...correct exercise is in fact a repetition without repetition." Good for the "same practice every day" message, weaker for the strict theme (fit 5).

### 5. Wolpert, TED 2011 (FULL TEXT, TED's own transcript) fit 8
Daniel Wolpert, "The real reason for brains", TEDGlobal 2011, recorded 13 July 2011, published 3 Nov 2011.
URL: https://www.ted.com/talks/daniel_wolpert_the_real_reason_for_brains/transcript
- "We have a brain for one reason and one reason only, and that's to produce adaptable and complex movements. There is no other reason to have a brain. Think about it. Movement is the only way you have of affecting the world around you."
- "So I am a movement chauvinist. I believe movement is the most important function of the brain -- don't let anyone tell you that it's not true."
- Sea squirt, his wording: "...the humble sea squirt. Rudimentary animal, has a nervous system, swims around in the ocean in its juvenile life. And at some point of its life, it implants on a rock. And the first thing it does in implanting on that rock, which it never leaves, is to digest its own brain and nervous system for food. So once you don't need to move, you don't need the luxury of that brain."

### 8. Bornstein, Hahn and Suwalsky 2013 (PubMed abstract) fit 9, study line

### 9. Sea squirt: who really wrote it (retold) fit 8
Chain, each step read in the work:
1. Rodolfo Llinas, "'Mindness' as a Functional State of the Brain", in C. Blakemore and S. Greenfield (eds.), Mindwaves, Blackwell, 1987, pp. 339-358; index gives "tunicate (sea squirt), 341, 343" (SNIPPET, https://archive.org/details/mindwaves00coli): "This principle is beautifully exemplified by the tunicate (a 'sea squirt') with free-swimming larvae. Herein lies one of the truly extraordinary lessons..."
2. Daniel Dennett, Consciousness Explained, Little, Brown, 1991, p. 177 (SNIPPET, https://archive.org/details/consciousnessexp0000denn): "The juvenile sea squirt wanders through the sea searching for a suitable rock or hunk of coral to cling to and make its home for life. For this task, it has a rudimentary nervous system. When it finds its spot and takes root, it doesn't need its brain anymore, so it eats it! (It's rather like getting tenure.)" Footnote: "The analogy between the sea squirt and the associate professor was first pointed out, I think, by the neuroscientist Rodolfo Llinas".
3. Llinas, I of the Vortex, 2001, ch. 1, pp. 15-17 (SNIPPET): "Once reattached to a stationary object the larva absorbs — literally digests — most of its own brain, including its notochord. It also digests its tail and tail musculature, thereupon regressing to the rather primitive adult stage [...] The lesson here is quite clear: the evolutionary development of a nervous system is an exclusive property of actively moving creatures." (p. 17 ends there)
4. Wolpert, TED 2011 (see 5).
So: the example is Llinas's (1987, then 2001). The "tenure" joke is Dennett's wording, credited by him to Llinas. Wolpert repeats it.
Retold line (label it "retold"): "The young sea squirt swims and has a small brain. When it fixes itself to a rock for life, it digests most of that brain. (Llinas, 2001)"
Biology caveat: "eats its brain" is a simplification. Llinas says "most of its own brain"; the adult keeps a simple nervous system for filter feeding. Do not say "all".

## Dropped (could not be confirmed in a primary source, or wrong for the theme)

- Thelen, "To say that cognition is embodied means that it arises from bodily interactions with the world" (Thelen, Schöner, Scheier and Smith, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 24:1-34, 2001). The article body is behind a paywall; I saw the line only in later articles quoting it. Abstract (PubMed 11515285) does not contain it. Secondary only: dropped.
- "thought grows from action and ... activity is the engine of change", given online to Thelen and Smith 1994. Full-text search inside the 1994 book finds no such sentence ("thought grows from action": 0 hits; "engine of change" appears once, in a sentence about a culture's values and tools). Not confirmed: dropped.
- Sherrington, "Life's aim is an act, not a thought" (The Brain and Its Mechanism, Rede Lecture 1933). Not opened in the lecture itself. In every quotation I found Sherrington introduces it with "It has been remarked that...", so it is not his own line. Dropped.
- Sherrington, "To move things is all that mankind can do; for such the sole executant is muscle, whether in whispering a syllable or in felling a forest" (Linacre Lecture, 1924). I could not open the 1924 lecture; found only later books and magazines quoting it. Use his own 1940 restatement instead (Man on his Nature, p. 116, above).
- Ramon y Cajal, "Todo hombre puede ser, si se lo propone, escultor de su propio cerebro" (Reglas y consejos). Found only in later books quoting it, not opened in his own text here, and it is about will and mental work, not physical exercise. Worse for this theme: in his own memoir he argues the other way. Recuerdos de mi vida, vol. 1, 2nd ed. 1917, pp. 244-245 (https://archive.org/details/recuerdosdemivid01ramuoft): «...el ejercicio físico en los hombres consagrados al estudio debe de ser moderado y breve, sin traspasar jamás la fase del cansancio. [...] los deportes violentos disminuyen rápidamente la aptitud para el trabajo intelectual.» Do not use Cajal as a supporter of "exercise builds the brain".
- Dennett's "It's rather like getting tenure" sea squirt joke: real (Consciousness Explained, 1991, p. 177) but it is a joke at professors and the example is Llinas's. Use Llinas.
- Wolpert's "digest its own brain and nervous system for food": real (TED transcript) but stronger than the biology. Use Llinas's "most of its own brain".
- Rizzolatti and Sinigaglia, "the brain that acts is also and above all a brain that understands" (Mirrors in the Brain, 2008; Italian «il cervello che agisce è anche e innanzitutto un cervello che comprende», So quel che fai, 2006). Found only in other books quoting it. Secondary only: dropped.
- Thelen 1995 "Motor development: a new synthesis" (American Psychologist) and Thelen 2000 "Motor development as foundation and future of developmental psychology" (Int. J. Behavioral Development 24:385-397): full texts not open; only abstracts read. No line taken.

## Safety notes for the whole lane
- No politicians, royalty, war or religion in any chosen line. Sechenov's next sentence names Garibaldi: cut before it. Bernstein's book has a footnote quoting Engels and its preface tells of a political campaign against him: use only the one sentence, keep his bio to "Soviet physiologist of movement".
- No medical promise in any line. Bornstein 2013 and Adolph and Hoch 2019 are about what was observed in children, not a recipe: say "in this study".
- Wolpert's and Sperry's "only reason" wording is a deliberate overstatement by the authors; present it as their view.

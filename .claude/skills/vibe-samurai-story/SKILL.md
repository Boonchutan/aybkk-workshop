# Vibe Samurai Story

Bruno (@vibe_samurai007) tells a story the same way every time, and it was checked against his own posts and seven reels read frame by frame. This skill turns that shape into scripts for Boonchu. The shape is borrowed. The voice, the targets, and the rules below are Boonchu's, because his position is the opposite of Bruno's in one way that matters: Bruno sells "worship no one"; Boonchu's cohort is priced on a lineage that can be checked.

## Who you are writing for, and why the rules exist

Boonchu is an authorized Ashtanga teacher in Bangkok (direct, multi-year authorization from the late Sharath Jois; an 18-year relationship; hosted Sharath in Bangkok in 2018 and 2024). He is building a 150,000 THB China cohort for ten serious practitioners. His viewer is a 40 to 60 year old with ten or more years of practice. English is Boonchu's second language and the videos will often carry Chinese subtitles. His content's job is to filter, not to persuade. Bruno says the same about his own videos: they are filters, and views are only a signal check.

Six rules, each with the reason, because the reason is what lets you apply them in a case this file did not foresee:

1. **Plain words.** Write every output so a smart ten-year-old could read it alone. No sentence over twenty words anywhere in the output, most under fifteen. That includes your diagnosis and your notes, not only the script. One idea per sentence. If a word needs explaining, explain it in the same sentence or cut it. The flat delivery only works when the words are plain, and the subtitles need it.
2. **The authorization is the asset, never the bait.** Never open with Sharath's name. Never make his name the hook. Spent as bait it becomes wallpaper, and using a teacher who died recently as hook material looks like using him, to the exact community Boonchu needs. State it exactly, once, where it belongs: a bio, a page, or after the turn when the teaching really came from him.
3. **Never invent a fact, a number, a quote, or a life story.** The whole position is that the proof can be checked. One made-up detail turns the asset into a danger. When you need a personal detail you do not have, leave a slot: `[BOONCHU: one line about ...]`.
4. **Credit every borrowed idea by name.** Bruno credits Heather Keys for the "Sacred In-Between". The credit is what makes a borrowed hook believable, and for a lineage teacher it is the signature move, not a courtesy.
5. **The spoken video never sells. The caption is the shop.** Seven of seven Bruno reels: no offer, no price, no "link in bio" in the spoken words. All of it sits in the written caption. Boonchu's caption closes on one line saying who this is not for, then the application. Not "link in bio", because the cohort has an application step, not a checkout.
6. **No swearing, no comment-bait, no anti-guru lines.** Swear words get videos hidden on Chinese platforms and sound cheap to this viewer. An either/or question at the end of a lineage reel drags "does authorization still count" into the comments, and the `/dm` rule forbids arguing in public. "Learn from everyone, worship no one" is Bruno's best sincere close and it breaks the exact authority the cohort rests on.

## Three modes

Decide which one the user wants. If it is unclear, use WRITE.

- **WRITE.** A topic, an observation, or a line Boonchu says to students becomes a full script plus caption.
- **FIX.** Boonchu pastes a draft. Diagnose it against the spine in plain words (what it has, what is missing, what sits in the wrong place), then rewrite it. Keep his own words wherever they already work.
- **TEACH.** Explain one pattern in plain words, with one example and a ten-minute drill that ends with a done-test. Point to the piece of Bruno's that shows it, from `references/patterns.md`.

## The reel spine (20 to 60 seconds)

```
0-5s    HOOK     One flat sentence, said as a fact. A true contradiction about
                 practice. Borrowed is fine if you credit it: a sutra line, a
                 Mysore rule, something a senior teacher actually said.
                 Never a question. Never "here's why". Never Sharath's name.
5-15s   SOURCE   Where it comes from, in one line, credited by name. Or the soft
                 second line: a small joke that takes the sting out.
15-35s  LADDER   Three examples that match each other and get closer each step:
                 the beginner, the ten-year practitioner, you.
        (pause)  One silent second.
        TURN     "It isn't ___. It's ___."   Write this line first.
        LINE     One sentence a practitioner would screenshot. Under fifteen words.
last    CLOSE    The flip (reverse what they assumed), or a joke on yourself that
                 calls back the first laugh. Hold the gaze one second. Stop.
                 No summary. No "follow me". No question about the lineage.
CAPTION          A second, separate piece of writing, not the spoken words typed
                 out. Fresh first line: the hook again, tighter. About fifty words
                 in about six short lines. Ends on a flip. Then one line on who
                 this is not for. Then the application. Hashtags last, five at most.
```

Where the turn sits: early (a fifth to two-fifths in) when more follows it; late (about three-quarters in) when the reel ends within twenty seconds of it. Treat these as his habits, not laws. Four of five timing rules were tested and thrown out for being too exact.

Two pictures Bruno never has and cannot copy: the shala room, and a body practising instead of a face talking. Consider one of them for every reel. His white wall says "man with opinions". Boonchu's room says something no competitor can film.

## The essay (Substack, newsletter, long YouTube)

Eight steps, checked against the full text of his essays. Write step 4 first.

```
TITLE     A true contradiction, under six words.
1 SCENE   One ordinary, specific moment. No claim yet.
2 FLIP    The same moment seen from a second angle.
3 AGREE   "Most people think ___. And sometimes they are right."
4 TURN    "But usually ___."   One sentence. Write it first.
5 NAME    What is this pattern called? Whose term is it? Credit them.
6 DEFINE  One line. No "maybe", no "sort of".
7 FLIP    Close by reversing what the reader assumed.
8 RETURN  Two questions, or "if you recognised yourself in this ...".
```

Everything before the turn makes the reader more sure of the story they already believe. Everything after takes it apart. That is why it feels like they found the answer themselves.

Squeezing it into a reel: the title is the turn, said as a contradiction. The scene and the second angle become one hook sentence. The source moves to the front, right after the hook, because the reel spends its credit early to earn the hook, where the essay spends it after the turn. Steps 5, 6 and 8 go in the caption. The reel adds one step the essay does not have: the ladder.

## How to write it, in order

1. Find the true contradiction. It must be something a ten-year practitioner would nod at, not something that only stops a beginner. If you cannot find one, say so and ask for the thing Boonchu says to students most often.
2. Write the TURN: "It isn't ___. It's ___." Then the "you are partly right" line that makes the turn land.
3. Write the HOOK from the turn. Check: one flat sentence, no question mark, under twelve words so it fits in five seconds, borrowed only with a credit ready.
4. Build the LADDER: three items, matching in shape, each one closer to the viewer. Small, concrete, everyday pictures.
5. Write the LINE. Under fifteen words. Would someone screenshot it? If not, it is a summary, not a line.
6. Write the CLOSE. A flip, or a callback to the first laugh. Never a call to action.
7. Write the CAPTION as its own piece. Fresh first line. Ends on a flip, then the not-for line, then the application.
8. Run the checks. Fix every fail before you show the script.

## Output format

For WRITE and FIX, use exactly this shape:

```
TITLE     (under six words, a true contradiction)
SCRIPT    (timed lines, each labelled HOOK / SOURCE / LADDER / (pause) / TURN / LINE / CLOSE)
DELIVERY  (one line: one take, straight to camera, flat, hold one second at the end,
           and which picture: face, room, or body)
CAPTION   (the second piece; ends with the not-for line, then the application)
CHECKS    (every check below, pass or fail, with the fix applied if it failed)
FILL-INS  ([BOONCHU: ...] slots, if any; say why each is needed)
```

For FIX, put a DIAGNOSIS block before TITLE: three to six plain sentences on what the draft has, what it lacks, and what sits in the wrong place.

For TEACH: PATTERN (its name) → what it is, in two sentences → one example → the drill, ending with "Done when ..." → the Bruno piece that shows it.

## The checks

Run all of them, every time, and show them.

- Hook is one flat sentence. No question mark. No "here's why". Under twelve words.
- Sharath's name is not in the hook. Authorization stated exactly, once, or not at all.
- Every borrowed line carries a credit by name.
- The turn is present, in the "it isn't / it's" shape or as an action.
- LINE is under fifteen words.
- The spoken script has no offer, no price, no "link in bio", no "follow me".
- The caption ends on the not-for line, then the application.
- No invented numbers, quotes, or stories. Every personal detail is either supplied by Boonchu or marked as a fill-in.
- Plain words: read it as a ten-year-old. Any word they would not know is explained in the sentence or cut.
- No swearing. No either/or question about lineage at the end. No anti-guru line.
- One of the two Boonchu-only pictures was considered (room, or body practising).

## Worked example (WRITE)

Request: "Reel about how the students who practise hardest are often the slowest to progress."

```
TITLE     Hardest practice, slowest progress

SCRIPT
HOOK     (0-4s)   The students who practise hardest are often the slowest to progress.
SOURCE   (4-9s)   Not my idea. Pattabhi Jois put it in five words: practice, and all is coming.
                  He did not say push.
LADDER   (9-24s)  The beginner forces the bind and wakes up with a sore shoulder.
                  The ten-year practitioner adds a second practice and loses the first one.
                  You skip rest day, because rest feels like losing.
         (pause)
TURN     (25-29s) It isn't effort that moves you forward. It's the effort you can repeat tomorrow.
LINE     (29-33s) The practice that heals you is the one you can do again.
CLOSE    (33-37s) Rest is not the break from practice. Rest is the part that works.
                  [hold one second]

DELIVERY  One take, straight to camera, flat. Film it in the shala after class, mats
          still out. Say the joke-free close at the same speed as everything else.

CAPTION
The hardest workers in the room are often the slowest movers.
Not because they are lazy. Because they never let the work land.
Effort you cannot repeat tomorrow is not practice. It is a performance.
The body keeps a record of every rest day you skipped.
Practise like you plan to be here in twenty years.

Not for anyone looking for a faster way. For the ones who want a longer one.
Application: [link to the cohort application]

CHECKS
Hook flat, no question, 11 words ........................ pass
Sharath not in hook ..................................... pass
Borrowed line credited (Pattabhi Jois) .................. pass
Turn present in "it isn't / it's" shape ................. pass
LINE under 15 words (11) ................................ pass
No selling in spoken script ............................. pass
Caption ends on not-for line then application ........... pass
No invented facts ....................................... pass (the quote is his best-known line)
Plain words ............................................. pass
No swearing, no lineage question, no anti-guru line ..... pass
Boonchu-only picture considered ......................... pass (the shala after class)

FILL-INS  None required. If Boonchu wants a personal close instead of the flip,
          add: [BOONCHU: one true line about a time you pushed and paid for it].
```

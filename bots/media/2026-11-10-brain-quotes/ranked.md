# Ranked: "physical exercise is the beginning of brain development" (judge, 10 Nov 2026)

57 checked candidates in, 26 ranked out: 20 for the set, 6 reserves. Nothing here is posted or approved; this is a ranking for Boonchu to pick from.

## How I ranked
1. Theme fit first: the line itself must say movement, exercise or the body comes first for the mind or brain.
2. Then the mix in the brief, one line per author, no two lines that say the same thing in the same way.
3. Then how well the source was seen (page image beats search snippet), how short the cut is, and safety for Thai, Chinese and Russian readers.
4. Every author was looked up in used.txt. No line below repeats a used line. Authors used before with a different line: Montessori, Aristotle, Dewey, Vivekananda, Fukuzawa, Plato, Galen, Socrates (Xenophon III.12, a different sentence, allowed by the brief), Sushruta (same chapter, different verse), Svatmarama (verse 1.1 is new), Zhu Xi, K. Pattabhi Jois.
5. Spot check by the judge: 24 of the 26 lines were found again, word for word, in the downloaded source texts, page images or scan search snippets (Sperry p. 299 read as an image; Ratey and Jois only as search snippets inside the book scans). The other 2, Lesgaft and Tao Xingzhi, rest on the page images the researcher and the checker read. For Fukuzawa I found the Japanese again, not the 1934 English page.

## Flags for Boonchu (owner's calls)
1. Fukuzawa (12): leave him out of Chinese-language slides and captions. He is disliked in China for his 1885 "leave Asia" editorial. Fine for English, Thai, Russian. For a Chinese edition swap in rank 21.
2. Tao Xingzhi (20): an educator, never a minister, but he sat on a wartime advisory council and was active in the China Democratic League. He is honoured in China today. If the no-politician rule is read strictly, use Zhu Xi (25).
3. K. Pattabhi Jois (26): misconduct allegations, which Sharath acknowledged in 2019. Used four times before. The clean lineage line instead is Krishnamacharya, Yoga Makaranda p. 21: "Asana and pranayama are initially extremely important." (checked, fit 6).
4. Wolpert (22): never clip or subtitle the talk after the first two minutes, and do not link the full talk in Russian posts.
5. Erickson (24): never write "walking improved memory more"; memory improved in both groups. Keep "reversing age-related loss" and "dementia" off slides.
6. Hillman (16): always "in this study"; the effects were small (d about 0.3) and the comparison was a waiting list. Explain PA as physical activity.
7. Kempermann (5): say "in animals". It is a hypothesis built on mouse and rat studies.
8. Ratey (6): his opinion, not a finding. Do not pull the book's treatment claims.
9. Sushruta (17): say "an old Indian medical text says". Keep "increases also the duration of life" off the slide.
10. Vivekananda (11): never use the sentence just before it (football and the Gita).
11. Plato (13): the "soul" benefit he names is calm and courage, not intelligence. Do not caption it "brain".
12. Aristotle (3): name the edition (Jowett revised by Ross, 1921). Do not add the word "brain".
13. Montessori (1): name the 1949 edition; the later Claremont edition has other words.
14. Lesgaft (8), Yan Yuan (19), Tao (20), Ushinsky (23), Zhu Xi (25): the English is the researcher's own translation. Label it so. The original-language line is the quote.
15. Sharath Jois, "Your body is the home you live in": checked, but not ranked (fit 5, it is about long life). Tomorrow, 11 November, is the second anniversary of his death. Whether to post it at all is Boonchu's decision.

## Do not put in the same carousel
- Aristotle (3) with Fukuzawa (12): both say "body first, then mind".
- Montessori (1) with Lesgaft (8): both say the mind's growth depends on the body's.
- Sperry (2), Sherrington (7), Bernstein (9), Wolpert (22): all "the brain came from movement". One or two per carousel.
- Yan Yuan (19) with Zhu Xi (25): Yan Yuan wrote against Zhu Xi's school.
- Jois (26) with the Hatha Yoga Pradipika line (18): Jois is commenting on the same text.

## PICK 1 (C1). Durvasasana. Working title: "The Guest Who Ate Everything, Six Times"

Why: the most surprising story here, and it fits the Habit Gap best. People know Durvasa for his curses
(and the Draupadi pot story). Almost nobody knows Mudgala. Here the "angry" sage is the one watching, and the hero loses everything
six times and goes back to the field with no anger. The real fork ("half a month of work, gone. What you do?") is one everybody has
lived through: a sick week or a trip eats your practice. Clear Fail, then Hero, then Payoff. No killing, no war, no throne.
The asana link is real: the asana is named after Durvasa, and Durvasa is the main actor in this story. In the asana you fall out
and stand up again many times.

Hook: #107 [bold] used almost as written: "'Practise when you feel like it' is ruining your yoga habit." It is contrarian (it flips
"listen to how you feel") and makes the viewer ask themselves something. The story is the because: Mudgala went back to the field
when no one would feel like it (hungry, insulted). Backup: #83 adapted ("How do you start over... six times?").
Do NOT use the researcher's line "'Start over' is not your problem. Being angry is." That is the banned "not X but Y" trick.
Harvard tricks: #1 (stop at the first empty pot: "What you do?"), #21 (what makes you quit is how you take the loss, not the loss),
#20 (cost: every bad week turns into quitting, and next year you start again), #27 (because: the text says Durvasa looked
for anger and found none), #30 (one comment word).
Stakes: if every lost week makes you angry at yourself, one sick week becomes quitting, and next year you start from day one again.
Why care: you have lost practice to a trip or a sick week before, and coming back felt like punishment.

### Verified facts (I read them myself)
Main text: Mahabharata, Vana (Aranyaka) Parva. Ganguli sections CCLVIII to CCLIX (Gutenberg #15474, vol. 1).
Critical edition 3.246 to 3.247 (BORI text on GRETIL).
- 3.246.3 to 3.246.5 (Ganguli CCLVIII): Mudgala lives by "Sila and Unchha", picking up corn and grains "left on the field by
  husbandmen" after the harvest (Ganguli footnote 86). He lives "like a pigeon" (Sanskrit kapotim vrttim). In half a month he gathers
  one drona of rice (3.246.5c "kapotavrttya paksena vrihidronam uparjayat"). Ganguli footnote 85: drona = "a very small measure".
  The sage with his son and wife eats once in half a month (3.246.5 "saputradaro ... paksaharo").
- 3.246.6 to 3.246.8: on the moon days (darsha and paurnamasa) he feeds gods and guests first and lives on what is left.
- 3.246.11 to 3.246.12: Durvasa hears of him and comes "digvasas" (Ganguli "having space alone for his covering", footnote 87 "Naked"),
  "like a madman", hair wild, "uttering ... various insulting words" (3.246.12c "parusa vaco vyaharan vividhah").
- 3.246.13 to 3.246.15: "I have come hither seeking for food." Mudgala: "Thou art welcome!" He gives water for the feet and good food.
- 3.246.16 to 3.246.17: he eats it ALL (krtsnam), Mudgala serves more, he eats that too, rubs the leftovers on his body and goes "as he had come".
- 3.246.18: "when the second moon day came" (dvitiye samprapte parvakale) he comes again and eats all the food.
- 3.246.19 to 3.246.20: "without partaking any food himself, the sage Mudgala again became engaged in collecting corn" (nirahara ...
  uncham arjayate punah). "Hunger could not disturb his equanimity. Nor could anger" (na krodho). He gleans "with his son and his wife".
- 3.246.21: Durvasa came at the right time "six times", "his mind made up" (satkrtvah krtaniscayah).
- 3.246.22 to 3.246.23: Durvasa saw no change at all in his mind (na ... vikaram dadrse). Pleased, he says nobody on earth gives like him
  (tvatsamo nasti loke 'smin data).
- 3.246.30 to 3.246.32: a messenger of the gods comes on a sky chariot yoked with swans and cranes, with bells, to take him to heaven.
- 3.246.33 and 3.247: Mudgala asks what is good AND bad about heaven. 3.247.28 to 3.247.29: you only use up what you earned, and
  at its end you fall (patanam).
- 3.247.38 to 3.247.39: "Bow to you, go in peace ... I do not desire heaven" (tasmat svargam na kamaye).
- 3.247.41 to 3.247.43: he lets the messenger go, takes up the highest calm, praise and blame become equal, "ever in meditation"
  (dhyananityo babhuva). Note: the critical text says he GAVE UP gleaning here (silonchavrttim utsrjya); Ganguli's text differs.
- Name: durvasas = "badly clad, naked"; also N. of a sage "known for his irascibility" (Monier-Williams p. 487).
  Iyengar (Light on Yoga, 1979, pp. 299 to 301, cited on Wikipedia): "named after Durvasa, a proverbially angry sage".
- Asana (AYI Durvasana page): one-legged balance, other foot behind the head. "Richte Dich einatmend langsam auf und vereinige
  Deine Hande vor dem Herzen" (rise slowly on the inhale, hands together at the heart). To make it easier: hands on the left thigh.
- Visual help: real class photos of Boonchu adjusting Durvasasana, 4 Nov (grey tank with red print = him per the 6 Nov note):
  bots/media/2026-11-06-adjust, labels 4Nov-217 (quality 8, best) to 4Nov-222. 4Nov-538 to 542 is a grey tee: check it is him before use.

### What NOT to say (C1)
- Never "naked". Say "came looking wild". No Kling nudity: show the empty pot, the gleaned field, pigeons, a wild-haired figure from behind.
- Not "same day" or "next morning". The text says only "again" (punah, 3.246.19).
- Not "his wife and son starved". The text says the sage ate nothing. The family only gleans with him.
- No "12 weeks" and no "three months". Six moon days is my math, not the text. Say "six times".
- No "test" in quote marks. "He came to see if Mudgala would get angry" is OK as a retold line (3.246.21 to 22).
- Do not say Durvasa smiled. Do not call him crazy or mad, and do not make him a joke.
- Leave out: Kurukshetra (the battlefield). The frame (Vyasa comforting Yudhishthira over his lost kingdom, 3.247.44 to 46).
  The heaven list with "heroes and men bearing marks of battle". Indra eating at his rites. The food that grows by magic (3.246.9 to 10).
  The starred lines with laughing and dancing (3.246.17*, not in the critical text). Durvasa as Shiva.
- Do not tell the Draupadi pot story. It sits right after this one in an appendix (@025), with Duryodhana plotting against his cousins.
- Do not end with "he went back to the field". At the end he sits in meditation every day (3.247.42).
- Do not say the asana is named after Mudgala. Do not use AYI's Brahma and Shiva quarrel legend.
- Hook #107: aim at the advice, never at a studio or app. Never suggest practising through pain. Boonchu's guard line is ready:
  "Aching, yes. Sharp pain, no. That one, you tell your teacher."
- No hooks #121 or #101 (Ep. 6 has them).

### Story beats (6 lines)
1. H (#107): "'Practise when you feel like it' is ruining your yoga habit."
2. DH: One poor man lost all his food six times to the same rude guest. And the guest did it on purpose.
3. T1 to FORK: Mudgala picks up leftover grains like a pigeon. Half a month of picking gives one small measure of rice. A wild sage comes
   shouting, eats it all, rubs the leftovers on his body and leaves. FORK: "What you do?"
4. RH (TWIST): Next moon day he comes back and eats it all again. Six times, on purpose. He is Durvasa, the sage famous for his anger.
5. T2: Each time Mudgala eats nothing and goes back to the field with his wife and son. He starts from nothing six times, with no anger.
6. P: Durvasa looks for anger and finds none: "Nobody on earth like you." A sky chariot comes for heaven. Mudgala says no thank you and
   sits in meditation every day. Adults: in Durvasasana you come down again and again. Rise slowly on the inhale [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE].
   Close: "Next bad week eat your practice. You go back to the field? Or you get angry?"
   (If the take runs over 60 s, cut the heaven part to one line or move it to the caption.)

## PICK 2 (C2). Viranchyasana A (and B). Working title: "Two Sounds in the Water"

Why: a true surprise that also flips a belief. The first teacher of the world did not know how to begin. His whole lesson was two sounds,
"ta" and "pa", from a voice he could never find. He sat down on his own seat and practised. It serves the Habit Gap through "same practice,
same seat": people go looking for a new method, a new app or a new teacher, and here the answer is to sit down and practise.
Most practitioners know Brahma sits on a lotus from Vishnu's navel. Very few know the "ta pa" verse.
The asana link is real: viranci = Brahma (MW). The asana is also a seat with one leg in half lotus, so "his own lotus seat" can be shown on the mat.
It ranks second because the "teacher who notices" part is missing (this one is about the seat, not the teacher).
It also needs care with Thai and Chinese viewers who pray to Phra Phrom (the four-faced Brahma).

Hook: #13 adapted: "The complete guide to Ashtanga is two sounds long." It flips "I need a better, longer method" and opens a loop
(which two sounds?) that the RH pays. DH from #60 adapted ("You're wasting time every week deciding which workout to do").
Harvard tricks: #1 (nobody is there: "What you do?"), #21 (you are not short of information, you are short of sitting), #2 (sell calm
mornings on your own mat, not more methods), #27 (because: the text says he searched every side, found nobody, and sat down), #30.
Stakes: if you keep looking for the next method, you never sit long enough on one for it to work, and next year you are still looking.
Why care: you have tried apps, programs and teachers, and each one felt like the answer for two weeks.

### Verified facts (I read them myself, vedabase.io Sanskrit plus word-for-word glosses)
Spine: Bhagavata Purana 2.9.5 to 2.9.9 (one chapter, one thread).
- 2.9.5: "sa adi-devo jagatam paro guruh" (the first god, supreme teacher of the worlds), on his own seat (svadhisnyam), wanting to create,
  "could not understand the proper direction ... nor ... the process for such creation".
- 2.9.6: "in the water, Brahmaji heard twice from nearby two syllables joined together ... the sixteenth and the twenty-first of the
  sparsa alphabets" (Sanskrit ambhasi upasrnod dvir-gaditam). I counted ka to ma myself: the 16th is ta, the 21st is pa. The purport
  says the same: "the word tapa ... is constructed".
- 2.9.7: "he tried to find the speaker, searching on all sides. But when he was unable to find anyone besides himself, he thought it wise
  to sit down on his lotus seat firmly and give his attention to the execution of penance, as he was instructed".
- 2.9.8: "divyam sahasrabdam" = "one thousand years by the calculations of the demigods". "jita-anila-atma" (breath and mind mastered,
  anila = wind or breath). "tapas tapiyams tapatam" = "known as the greatest of all ascetics".
- 2.9.9: the Lord, pleased, shows him his own world.
- MW p. 437 tapas: "warmth, heat ... religious austerity ... severe meditation". MW p. 983 virinca: "N. of Brahma".
- AYI Viranchyasana A1 page: half lotus on one side, the other leg over the arm and behind the head, hands together at the heart,
  stay 5 breaths. "Virancya ist ein Beiname von Brahma." B1: sit on one heel and fold forward.
- Second telling (only if the writer wants a different spine, never mixed in silently): 3.8.16 (he looks in every direction and gets
  four faces), 3.8.18 to 19 ("Who am I ... on the top of this lotus?", goes down the stem and "could not trace out the root"),
  3.8.21 (back to his own seat, "sanair jita-svasa": slowly mastering the breath, MW sanais = "gently, gradually"), 3.8.22 ("could see in
  his heart ... whom he could not see before with the greatest endeavor"). The purport to 2.9.6 joins the two tellings, but that is a
  commentary. If the script mixes them, the caption says "retold from Bhagavata Purana 2.9 and 3.8".

### What NOT to say (C2)
- Never joke that God was lost, clueless or confused. Say only what 2.9.5 says: he did not know how to begin. Call him
  "Brahma, the first teacher of the whole world" and end on his title, the greatest of all who practise (2.9.8).
- Do not name the speaker. The verse says he found nobody. "The Lord said it" comes from the commentary only.
- "A thousand years of the gods", never human years. No "100 years" for any search. (3.8.22 has a lifetime, puruṣāyuṣā, for the
  meditation, not for the search.)
- Do not call tapa "penance" or "punishment". Say "heat, the hard practice" (MW).
- No unchecked Jois lines ("Practice and all is coming", "99% practice"). Do not add them.
- Do not say Brahma made or did the asana. The name is the link, and the half lotus seat is our picture of it.
- No promise like "practise and God will appear". Vishnu showing his world is a story fact only.
- Leave out 2.9.9's "the supreme planet above all others" and "wealth of the renounced order" (sectarian lines).
- Thai and Chinese viewers pray to Phra Phrom. No cartoon Brahma face in Kling: show a lotus on dark water, light from four sides,
  a seated figure from behind.
- Do not reuse the Vishvamitra script's wording for tapas (28 Sep).

### Story beats (6 lines)
1. H (#13 adapted): "The complete guide to Ashtanga is two sounds long."
2. DH (#60 adapted): You spend every week choosing a new method. The first teacher of the world got two sounds, and nobody to explain them.
3. T1 to FORK: Brahma sits on his lotus. He wants to make the world and does not know how. In the water a voice says two sounds, twice:
   "Ta. Pa." He looks everywhere. Nobody is there. FORK: "What you do?"
4. RH (TWIST): Ta and pa make one word, tapa. It means heat, the hard practice. That was the whole lesson.
5. T2: No second word, no map, nobody to ask. Only him on a flower in the water.
6. P: He sits down firmly on his own seat and practises for a thousand years of the gods. Then Vishnu shows him his world.
   Adults: Viranchyasana is named after Brahma. One leg in half lotus, your own seat [BOONCHU'S OWN CUE].
   Close: "How many new method you try this year? How many morning you sit on your own seat?"

## Rejected
- C3 Kaundinyasana (Kondanna): the quotes check out (Nidanakatha pp. 72 to 73. Vinaya kd1:6.5.5, 6.10.5, 6.11.1 to 2, 6.31.5 to 6).
  Killed anyway. (1) The asana link is a shared family name only (MW p. 315: a patronymic, also a grammarian). Only AYI ties it to the
  Buddha's student. (2) The text weakens the angle: the Buddha thought of his two old teachers first, and went to the five only after
  hearing they had died (kd1:6.2.1, 6.4.1). So "the teacher remembered the ones who quit" claims too much, and it brings in two deaths.
  (3) Thai viewers learn Anna Kondanna and the first sermon (Asanha Bucha) in school, so it is low surprise for half the audience.
  (4) Selling a shala with the Buddha's story is a safety risk in Thailand. (5) Its hook #121 is Ep. 6's hook.
- C4 Kashyapasana (Aditi): the quotes check out (BhP 8.16.2, 8.16.10, 8.16.18, 8.16.25, 8.17.1, 8.17.4). Killed anyway.
  (1) The whole motive is war: demons took heaven and her sons were driven out (8.16.1). The payoff leads straight into Vamana and
  King Bali's kingdom. (2) The 12 days are a milk-only fast (8.16.25), and you cannot tell it honestly without diet talk.
  (3) The one who notices is a husband, not a teacher. In 8.16.18 he smiles and calls her grief Vishnu's power of attachment to children,
  which plays badly. (4) The surprise is weak.
- C5 Galavasana: the quotes check out (Udyoga Parva, Ganguli sec. CVI). Killed: right after the horse demand, Galava wants to die
  ("I will relinquish my life"). Then comes the Madhavi exchange (a king's daughter traded for horses). It would be a second
  Vishvamitra story, its Habit Gap link is weak, and the lesson is against stubbornness.

## 1. montessori-mental-development-movement (educators) — PASS

- **Opened:** https://archive.org/details/absorbentmind031961mbp (full text + page image leaf n218)

## 2. rousseau-learn-to-think (educators) — PASS

- **Print:** "To learn to think we must therefore exercise our limbs, our senses, and our bodily organs, which are the tools of the intellect"
- **Original:** « Pour apprendre à penser, il faut donc exercer nos membres, nos sens, nos organes, qui sont les instruments de notre intelligence »
- **Credit:** Jean-Jacques Rousseau · Emile, or On Education, 1762, Book II · tr. Barbara Foxley, 1911
- **Plain meaning:** Rousseau said our arms, legs and senses are the tools we think with, so we must use them to learn to think.
- **Opened:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5427/pg5427.txt ; 1911 Everyman scan text https://archive.org/details/emile0000rous (p. 90) ; French: Garnier edition https://archive.org/details/mileoudeldu00rous (pp. 121-122) and fr.wikisource 1852 edition, Livre II
- **Evidence:** Foxley: "Before you can practise an art you must first get your tools; ... To learn to think we must therefore exercise our limbs, our senses, and our bodily organs, which are the tools of the intellect; and to get the best use out of these tools, the body which supplies us with them must be strong and healthy. Not only is it quite a mistake that true reason is developed apart from the body, but it is a good bodily constitution which makes the workings of the mind easy and correct." French (Garnier): "Pour apprendre à penser, il faut donc exercer nos membres, nos sens, nos organes, qui sont les instruments de notre intelligence; et pour tirer tout le parti possible de ces instruments, il faut que le corps, qui les fournit, soit robuste et sain. Ainsi, loin que la véritable raison de l'homme se forme indépendamment du corps, c'est la bonne constitution du corps qui rend les opérations de l'esprit faciles et sûres."
- **Problems:** None. English and French exact. The cut stops at a semicolon in the source. Page 90 (and p. 82 for "Would you cultivate your pupil's intelligence...") confirmed in the Everyman scan.

## 3. sherrington-cradle-of-mind (brainmove) — PASS

- **Print:** "As we look along the scale of life, whether in time or in order of organization, muscle is there before nerve, and nerve is there before mind, 'recognizable mind'."
- **Credit:** Sir Charles Sherrington · Man on his Nature, 1940 (Gifford Lectures 1937-8), ch. VI, pp. 196-197
- **Plain meaning:** Looking at living things from simple to complex, a Nobel Prize scientist saw that muscle appeared first, then nerves, then mind.
- **Opened:** https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.188837 (full text + page images leaf n214 = p. 196, n215 = p. 197)
- **Evidence (page scans):** "The simpler motor acts were there before the mind—always meaning by that 'recognizable mind'. It would seem to be the conative motor act under 'urge-to-live' which has been the cradle of mind. Where motor integration progressed and where motor behaviour progressively evolved, mind progressively evolved." Title page: Cambridge, at the University Press, 1940.
- **Problems:** None. Exact, including the second sentence ("cradle of mind") if the owner wants the longer form.

## 4. dewey-mind-by-exercise-of-muscles (educators) — PASS (one word corrected)

- **Print:** nature's "intention" ... is to develop the mind especially by exercise of the muscles of the body
- **Credit:** John Dewey · Democracy and Education, 1916, ch. 9, p. 135
- **Plain meaning:** Dewey said nature does not train the body first and the mind later: it grows the mind above all by using the muscles.
- **Opened:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/852/pg852.txt ; 1916 Macmillan scan https://archive.org/details/democracyeducati00deweuoft (page image leaf n154 = p. 135)
- **Evidence (p. 135 scan):** "(2) The aim of natural development translates into the aim of respect for physical mobility. In Rousseau's words: "Children are always in motion; a sedentary life is injurious." When he says that "Nature's intention is to strengthen the body before exercising the mind" he hardly states the fact fairly. But if had said that nature's "intention" (to adopt his poetical form of speech) is to develop the mind especially *by* exercise of the muscles of the body he would have stated a positive fact. In other words, the aim of following nature means, in the concrete, regard for the actual part played by use of the bodily organs in explorations, in handling of materials, in plays and games."
- **Problems:** The candidate's long quote reads "But if he had said". The printed book reads "But if had said": the word "he" is missing on p. 135 of the 1916 first printing and in the 1919, 1923 and 1928 Macmillan printings I checked. "he" is Project Gutenberg's silent fix. The cut above avoids that spot and is exact in every printing. If the full sentence is ever printed, write "But if [he] had said". "by" is in italics in the book. Plain meaning: added "above all" for Dewey's "especially". "he"/"his" is Rousseau; Dewey states this as his own view ("a positive fact").

## 6. hillman-fitkids-2014 (exercise) — PASS (cut and plain meaning corrected)

- **Print:** "The intervention enhanced cognitive performance and brain function during tasks requiring greater executive control."
- **Credit:** Hillman et al. · Pediatrics 134(4):e1063-e1071, 2014 (FITKids trial: 221 children, 9 months)
- **Plain meaning:** In this study, children who joined an active after-school program for nine months improved more than children on a waiting list at tests of ignoring distractions and switching between tasks.
- **Opened:** PubMed record PMID 25266425 (abstract, authors, journal, DOI 10.1542/peds.2013-3219) ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4179093/ (body)
- **Evidence (abstract):** "METHODS: Two hundred twenty-one children (7-9 years) were randomly assigned to a 9-month afterschool PA program or a wait-list control. ... RESULTS: ... Intervention participants exhibited greater improvements from pretest to posttest in inhibition (3.2%, 95% CI: 0.0 to 6.5; d = 0.27) and cognitive flexibility (4.8%, 95% CI: 1.1 to 8.4; d = 0.35 for group difference in pre-to-post change score) compared with control. ... CONCLUSIONS: The intervention enhanced cognitive performance and brain function during tasks requiring greater executive control. These findings demonstrate a causal effect of a PA program on executive control, and provide support for PA for improving childhood cognition and brain health." Cut changed: the old slide cut ("These findings demonstrate a causal effect of a PA program on executive control") uses the abbreviation "PA" and sits in a sentence that ends in a general health claim ("improving childhood cognition and brain health"). The first sentence of the same conclusion says what was measured and needs no abbreviation. Plain meaning corrected: the two tests were inhibition and cognitive flexibility, not "attention and self-control". The lower end of the inhibition interval is 0.0, so call the gain small. ## 7. yanyuan-practised-through-the-body (east) — PASS (label, date and plain meaning corrected)

- **Print:** "What is clear in the mind, spoken by the mouth and written on paper, if it has not been practised through the body, is all useless."
- **Original:** 心中醒，口中說，紙上作，不從身上習過，皆無用也。
- **Credit:** Yan Yuan (顏元, 1635-1704) · 存學編 (Cunxue bian), 卷二 〈性理評〉 · our translation
- **Plain meaning:** A Chinese teacher saw that he could talk about big ideas but got his times table wrong when he tried it, and said that knowing, saying and writing a thing is useless until you have practised it yourself, with your own body.
- **Opened:** https://archive.org/details/02091872.cn (woodblock page images: leaf n48 chapter head, n50 the passage) ; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/存學編/卷二
- **Evidence (woodblock, leaf 2 of 卷二):** Chapter head: "存學編卷二 / 博陵 顏元 著 / 性理評". Passage: "明道謂謝顯道曰爾輩在此相從只是學某言語故其學心與口不相應盍若行之請問焉曰且靜坐　伊川每見人靜坐便歎其善學" then Yan Yuan's comment: "...吾嘗談天道性命若無甚扞格一着手算九九數輒差王子講冠禮若甚易一習初祝便差以此知心中醒口中說紙上作不從身上習過皆無用也責及門不行彼既請問正好教之習禮習樂却只云且靜坐二程亦復如是噫雖曰不禪吾不信也".
- **Problems:** Chinese exact against the woodblock, read character by character (the block has no punctuation; it prints 着 where Wikisource has 著, the same character in another form). The English is our own translation and must be labelled so; a published one exists (Mansfield Freeman, Preservation of Learning, 1972) but is not online to compare. 身 means the body and also "oneself, in person". The date "written 1669" comes from reference works and I could not confirm it in a primary source, so the credit gives his life dates. His two examples are arithmetic and a rite, so do not caption this as being about exercise. The passage argues against "just sit quietly" and ends with a jab that this is Chan Buddhism in disguise: do not quote the lines after 皆無用也.

## 9. zhuxi-taught-by-doing (east) — PASS (translation wording and plain meaning corrected)

- **Print:** "The ancients' elementary learning taught them through practical tasks, and that in itself nurtured their minds; without knowing or noticing it, they became good."
- **Original:** 古人小學教之以事，便自養得他心，不知不覺自好了。
- **Credit:** Zhu Xi (朱熹, 1130-1200) · 朱子語類 (Classified Conversations of Master Zhu), 卷七 · our translation
- **Plain meaning:** Zhu Xi said that in old times children were first taught things to do, like rites, music, archery and arithmetic, and that this practice shaped their minds before they were taught the reasons.
- **Opened:** https://archive.org/details/06049691.cn (Siku Quanshu page images: leaf n135 first entry, n138-n139 second entry) ; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/朱子語類/007 ; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/周禮/地官司徒
- **Evidence (Siku scan, 朱子語類卷七 學一 小學):** First entry: "古者初年入小學只是教之以事如禮樂射御書數及孝弟忠信之事自十六七入大學然後教之以理如致知格物及所以為忠信孝弟者 驤". Later entry: "古者小學已自暗養成了到長來已自有聖賢坯模只就上面加光飾如今自失了小學工夫只得教人且把敬為主收斂身心却方可下工夫又曰古人小學教之以事便自養得他心不知不覺自好了到得漸長漸更歷通達事物將無所不能今人既無本領只去理會許多閒骨董百方措置思索反以害心 賀孫". 周禮: 「三曰六藝，禮、樂、射、御、書、數」; 「乃教之六藝：一曰五禮，二曰六樂，三曰五射，四曰五馭，五曰六書，六曰九數。」
- **Problems:** Chinese exact in the Siku scan; both recorder names confirmed. The English is our own translation and must be labelled so. I changed "taught them through doing things" to "taught them through practical tasks": 事 means affairs or tasks, and "doing" was added by the translator. In the longer line, "was taught only by doing things" should likewise read "was taught only practical things". These are sayings written down by his students (compiled 1270). Theme caution: he says practice first, theory later; archery and chariot driving are two of the six arts beside writing and arithmetic. Do not caption it as a line about exercise or the body.

# Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. 6 · "The Teacher Who Said No" · Asana: Samasthitih (Primary series, the standing start of every practice) · Lane B · ask word: aybkk

Editor's final, 9 Nov 2026. Fixed the same day after two checkers (see the Fix log at the end). Merged from writer a (out/ep6_a.md) and writer b (out/ep6_b.md). Rewrite of v4 (bots/posts/2026-10-10-plato-chat-upamanyu-rewrite.md, "## v4"). Beat order H, DH, T1, RH, T2, P.

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If nobody notices you stop, one missed week turns into a month, and next year you start again from day one. And if you read a teacher's "no" as "he doesn't care", you may walk away from the one person who would notice you are gone.

Why care: If you quit yoga before, you probably blamed the practice ("too hard") or yourself. This story shows a different reason. Nobody noticed. And the strict teacher who kept saying no is the one who went looking.

## 3. Toolkit

Toolkit pick: Triple Hook (#121) + Fork-First Story.
Why: The Triple Hook puts the point out by about 0:07 (lines 1 to 3 end at 0:06.8) and does SAY, SHOW and WRITE in the first second (Boonchu's 6 second rule and the 4 Nov line 1 check). The fork stops the story after the third "no" ("What you do?", Harvard #1). Most viewers pick "quit". The rehook at about 0:25 is the real answer from the book: he says OK and goes back to the cows.

Toolkit choice:
- /hook contrarian lever with bank #101 ("strict" means someone is paying attention) + /story 4-beat. A good flip, but the flip lands on the teacher and misses the Habit Gap, and the 4-beat has no stop for the viewer to choose.
- Vibe Samurai (flat statement, ladder of 3 no's, turn, flip). The no's make a ready ladder, but its turn pulls toward the banned "not X but Y" line, and its flat opener is slower than #121.

## 4. Hook bank

Main hook, #121 [triple]: "This is why most people quit yoga. You think it's because yoga is too hard. It's not. It's because nobody notices when you stop coming."

How I adapted it:
- Context kept, "most" cut (unchecked, as in v4): "This is why people quit yoga."
- Lean and snapback pressed into one line in his voice: "Too hard? No. Nobody notice you stop." The lean is the viewer's own thought. The snapback is one word. 25 bank words become 13.
- Line 1 SAY, SHOW, WRITE in the first second: said to camera, Boonchu next to one empty mat, the words on screen from frame 1.

Double hook, from #101 [bold]: "When will people realize 'strict' often just means someone is paying attention?"
- Pressed into a question and answer: "Who notice? Teacher who say no." (the article is dropped, as he talks). It flips a second belief (the strict teacher drives you away) and opens the loop "how can that be?" The story answers it at line 16. Line 21 gives the because.
- "When will people realize" is dropped. It scolds and costs 2 seconds. Following the bank note, the line defends nobody and names no review or studio.

## 5. Harvard tricks used

- #21 Teach them something about their own problem: line 2 (quitting was not about "too hard", nobody noticed) and line 21 (a teacher's "no" means he is watching you).
- #1 Decide before you know the ending: line 11 "What you do?" plus a 1 s pause, answered at line 12.
- #27 Every claim needs a because: line 16 (the teacher's own because, retold as I say no to everything, a paraphrase of "restrained by me from the use of everything", adi.txt 1879-1880), line 20 (It was a test, the because for all the no's, adi.txt 1975) and line 21 (the because for the line 3 claim. Line 7 is the proof: every evening the teacher asks what he eats).
- #20 The cost of not fixing it: line 15 inside the story (blind, in a well, night coming, "Who notice?") and line 24 to the viewer ("Your mat empty tomorrow. Who come looking?").
- #30 Sell only the next step: the spoken video sells nothing. The caption has ONE ask (comment aybkk).
Not used: #2.

Story pattern: Fail (no food, blind, in a well at sunset), Hero (the teacher who said no notices and goes looking, Upamanyu prays and sees again), Payoff (he comes home and bows, it was a test, then line 21 and the viewer's own mat).

One thread check: lines 1 to 3 talk to adults. Lines 4 to 20 tell the story straight, start to end. Lines 21 to 24 come back to the adults. "What you do?" and "Who notice?" stay in the bedtime voice, the way you ask your child. 5-year-old test: read lines 4 to 20 alone. A student looks after cows. Every evening he bows to his teacher. The teacher asks what he eats. The teacher takes his food and says no to the milk and to the milk bubbles. He says OK. He gets so hungry he eats leaves, goes blind and falls in a well. At sunset the teacher notices, goes looking and calls him. The boy answers. He prays, sees again, comes home and bows. It was a test. It makes sense alone, and the test line answers the child's "why is the teacher mean?"

Contrarian check: line 2 flips "I quit because it was too hard". Line 3 flips "the strict teacher drives you away". The close is a question.

## 7. Length

Spoken words: 144 (English, the cue slot and the Thai sign-off not counted). Inside 110 to 160.
How it is timed: story lines 4 to 20 at 2.6 words a second (bedtime voice), adult lines 1 to 3 and 21 to 24 at 2.8. Fixed beats on top: the fork pause after line 11 (1.0 s), the silent beat after line 20 (0.5 s), the step into Samasthitih at line 22 (0.5 s), his cue (1.8 s, which is 5 words at 2.8) and the Thai sign-off (1.2 s). Both bows happen while he says the line, so they add no time. Line 22 holds two long names (Upamanyu, Samasthitih), which is one more reason the step gets its own 0.5 s.
Honest end time: about 0:59 (59.3 s). If he says the adult lines at the slow pace too: about 60.4 s. At 2.8 all the way through: about 56.4 s.
If the real take runs over 60 s, cut in this order:
1. Line 10, say "Bubbles from the baby cows? No." (drop "Milk", about 0.4 s).
2. Line 9 "Cow milk? No." (about 1.2 s). Use it only if the take is still over, because the ladder then has only two no's. The milk then lives only in the caption.
Never cut lines 11 to 12 (the fork and twist), line 16 (the teacher's because), line 20 (the test) or line 21 (the payoff).
If the take runs short, add back in this order. Each one was asked for by a checker and cut only for time:

Why Samasthitih stays: the one thing Upamanyu does every evening, even after every no, is come back, stand in front of his teacher and bow. The Sanskrit says it with "sthitvā", from the same root (sthā, to stand) as "sthiti" in Samasthitih. In our room, Samasthitih is the first stand of every morning, at the front of the mat where the teacher sees you. Line 22's "come back" is the book's own "he returned in the evening". The parts also mean "same" and "standing", which fits "same practice, same time". Nothing else in the story (cows, calves, well, leaves, the Aswins) names a real asana. Gomukhasana is already used (Dilipa), and dristi is Ep. 1.

## 10. Flags

1. Hook claim: "This is why people quit yoga" and "Nobody notice you stop" are Boonchu's view from teaching, not a study. "Most" is cut. Never add a number. The spoken lines say it as a plain claim, and the "I think" that softens it is only in the caption, which many viewers never read. There is no number in it, so it is not a statistic problem, but it is a claim. If he wants it softer on camera: "I think this is why people quit yoga." (2 more words, about 0.7 s, so use cut 1 in sec. 7). Line 1 still does SAY, SHOW, WRITE, and the screen then says I THINK THIS IS WHY PEOPLE QUIT YOGA.
2. "Too hard? No." is the approved #121 snapback, but a strict reader could call it a soft "not X but Y" move, and both checkers heard it that way. It stays because it is the contrarian flip the brief asks for, the bank builds #121 this way, and the brief allows #121. Safer option if Boonchu wants it: "Too hard? Maybe. Nobody notice you stop." (same 7 words, same time, no "but"). The cost is that "Maybe" makes the flip softer, so the hook is a little less contrarian. The old option "Maybe. But nobody notice you stop." is dropped, because it is still X but Y.
3. The teacher can look cruel. His rules led to the hunger, the leaves and the fall, and Ashtanga viewers who know its history with harsh teachers will notice. The spoken video now handles it in three places, not only in the caption. Line 16 lets the teacher own it (I say no to everything, a paraphrase of "restrained by me from the use of everything", adi.txt 1879-1880, never shown on screen as a quote). Line 20 says it was a test, the book's own frame ("this was the trial of Upamanyu", adi.txt 1975). Line 22 makes the lesson "come back", not "obey". The caption keeps "A trial is a test." Draw no lesson about fasting or eating less, and never present "it was a test" as a rule for teachers today. If Boonchu feels the test line sounds like an excuse for a harsh teacher, move it back to the caption only (saves about 1.5 s).
4. NEVER say the teacher pulled him out. The book never says how he got out of the well. The script says only "Teacher say, pray. He see again. He come home and bow."
5. Compressed, not changed. The book has four no's: the alms must go to the teacher, the second begging, the milk, the foam. The script shows three (the second begging, adi.txt 1838-1843, is left out), so no line and no screen stamp says a number. In the book, sight comes back after the Aswins' cake test (adi.txt 1958-1971), not right after the prayer. "He see again" skips the cake. The cake is a second lesson (one ordeal per video), and the line "Thy master's teeth are of black iron" can read as mocking the teacher. Keep it out or make it a later episode. The long Rig Veda hymn is also left out. The Aswins are named only on screen, with respect.
6. The milk "no" in the book is "not without my consent". Spoken "No." simplifies it. The caption says "He had not asked first."
7. Ganguli's sunset line is broken ("is, of course, and therefore, doth not come home"). The Sanskrit fills the gap: 1.3.53C "sa niyataṃ kupitaḥ", "he is surely angry". So the teacher thinks his own "no" made the boy angry, and he goes looking anyway. This is the strongest Habit Gap line in the story, but it is not in Ganguli's English. Option only with Boonchu's OK to use the Sanskrit: line 16 becomes "Teacher notice. I say no to everything. Maybe he angry with me. Let's go find him." That is 5 more words, about 1.9 s, so it needs both cuts in sec. 7, and the ladder then has only two no's.
8. "Pit" or "well": the narrator says "pit", Upamanyu himself says "well" twice, and the Sanskrit says kūpa (well). We use his word in line 14. Line 18 now says only "Here I am!".

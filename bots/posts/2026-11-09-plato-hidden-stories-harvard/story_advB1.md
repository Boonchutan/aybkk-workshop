# Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. ? · "Pull Your Ears" · Asana: Buddhasana (Advanced B, 4th series, Sthira Bhaga B) · Lane B · ask word: aybkk

Writer's script, 9 Nov 2026. Story id C1 (key advB1). No episode number until it is filmed. Beat order H, DH, T1, RH, T2, P.

Lane B, and why: the point is the habit. Tired is the signal to change one small thing. If tired means stop, the habit dies quietly. That is the Habit Gap. The story gives no technique fix for one stuck asana. Buddhasana is a 4th series asana that almost no viewer practises, so a STUCK reply would have nothing to land on. The caption's AYBKK line ("a teacher notices when you miss class") is the Lane B promise, and it sits well under a story where the teacher sees from far away (caption only, never spoken). Bank #55's note pairs the hook with Lane A. I kept its "one honest reason" part and dropped the STUCK pairing. So the caption asks for "aybkk" (keyword test, frozen until 22 Nov). If Boonchu wants Lane A: swap the caption ask for the STUCK ask plus the 2 s end card "Comment STUCK", and the cue slot (line 21) must be a real fix.

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If "tired" means "stop", you stop on most hard mornings. You think you are not good enough, so you stay away a little longer each time. The habit dies quietly, and next year you start from day one again.

Why care: Every 6 am practitioner has tired mornings. On those mornings the hard asana does not come, and you blame your body. This story shows one of the Buddha's two top students nodding off in his own practice. His teacher saw it from far away and came. He did not scold. He gave one small try, then the next. Even the last step ends with "get up quickly".

## 3. Toolkit

Toolkit pick: /hook (contrarian lever, bank #55) + Fork-First Story.
Why: #55 says "the real reason you can't". It lets line 1 flip the viewer's own inner voice ("I'm not good enough") into Boonchu's own cue from 28 Oct ("it is because you are tired"). The point is out by 0:03.2, the stakes by 0:05.9 and the loop by 0:08.7. The story starts at 0:09.0. The story has a fork built in. Your student falls asleep, you are the teacher, what do you do? The viewer picks scold or wake him (Harvard #1). Then the twist at 0:22.5 resets attention: the teacher is suddenly there, he does not scold, and the ears come only at step four.

Toolkit choice:
- Vibe Samurai (flat statement "Tired is a signal" + a ladder of three: ears, water, stars + the turn + the flip): the ladder is already inside the sutta. But the turn pulls toward a banned "it isn't X, it's Y" line, and a flat statement is less contrarian than #55.
- Name-the-Feeling ("6 am, body heavy, you think you are not good enough"): a strong mirror. But it lands the point after about 8 s and pushes the story start past 0:12. The 4 Nov rule wants the point in about 6 s.

## 4. Hook bank

Bank line, #55 [warn] fit=use goal=trust habit=2:
- template: "The real reason you can't {goal}"
- aybkk: "The real reason you can't move past Primary series"
- note: "Pair it with Lane A (comment STUCK), give one honest reason like jump-back strength, and never promise the next series."

How I adapted it:
- {goal} became "hard asana not coming". It fits every series, and it promises no next series, as the note asks.
- The "real reason" is said as the answer itself ("Many times, you just tired"), not with the words "the real reason". That fits 3 s and his short voice. The question plus the flip still says "it is not what you think".
- "Many times" keeps it honest. Boonchu's own cue says "not always because you cannot do it" (plato.md, 28 Oct). It never says the body is never the problem.
- One honest reason, as the note asks: tiredness, from Boonchu's own cue. Line 19 gives the because for Buddhasana. It comes after the scorpion (and the elbow balance, told in the caption), so you are already tired.
- Contrarian: the viewer believes the hard asana means "my body can't". Line 1 flips it to "you are tired", and they ask themselves "am I just tired?".
- The note pairs #55 with Lane A. I use Lane B (see the top). The hook works for both.

Backup for the 5-hook test: #86 [story] fit=adapt goal=reach habit=3 ("This video is for the day you want to skip practice."), adapted to "This video is for the morning you are too tired." It is softer and not contrarian, so it is the backup only. Not used: #121 and #101 (Ep. 6 has them), #107 and #13 (advA scripts), #10 and #102 (Intermediate scripts), #42 and #60 (taken in this week's picks).

## 5. Harvard tricks used

- #1 Decide before you know the ending: line 8, "You are the teacher. What you do?", then a 0.6 s pause with eyes in the lens. Lines 9 to 12 answer it (he comes, no scolding, small tries).
- #20 Ask about the cost of not fixing it: line 2, "Tired mean stop? You stop many mornings." It is the stakes, right after the hook (2 Nov rule). The close (line 22, "You stop?") asks it again as the viewer's own question for tomorrow.
- #21 Teach them something about their own problem: line 1 (the hard asana is often tiredness, not your body) and line 19. Buddhasana comes after the scorpion, so you reach it already tired. Line 20 gives the move the story teaches: change one small thing.
- #30 Sell only the next step: the spoken video sells nothing. No price, no course, no link line, no AYBKK line. The caption has ONE ask (comment aybkk).

Not counted: #27 shows up on its own in line 19 (the because for line 1's claim), but I keep the labels to the picker's four. #2 (a result-for-your-life line would be a pitch, and Lane B keeps the pitch out of the video).

## 6. The script

Times are at the bedtime pace, the way Ep. 6 is timed: story lines 4 to 17 at 2.6 words a second (bedtime voice), adult lines 1 to 3 and 18 to 22 at 2.8, with the pauses on top (tools/advB1/t7.py). Story pictures go over his head as in Ep. 5, kid style and calm. NEVER show the Buddha: no AI image, no statue, no face, no figure on the seat. Moggallana is always a monk seen from behind, in a plain old-India robe, in a village of old India (no Thai temple, no modern Thai monk). Never show any hand on someone else's ear. Boonchu tells the Buddha's words as the narrator. He does not act the Buddha's voice.

   (on screen from frame 1: HARD ASANA NOT COMING? / MANY TIMES, YOU JUST TIRED. Shot: Boonchu sitting on his mat after practice, towel next to him, a little sweat, straight to camera. Said flat, like a thing he sees every morning.)

"Sleepy?" ties the Buddha's answer to being sleepy, which is what AN 7.61 is about (flag 1). 13. 0:33.3 [RH] (P + RH) Step four? Pull both ears.

14. 0:35.5 [T2] (T) Still sleepy? Look up at the stars.

No new words, no sound. 0.5 s pause. It pictures the Buddha's own "but what if that doesn't work?", the same way lines 13 and 14 picture the tries. It is not a claim that the stars failed (flag 6). 16. 0:40.8 [P] (P + RH) Last step. Lie down on your right side.

17. 0:43.9 [P] (P) When you wake, get up quick.

Story pattern: Fail (one of the Buddha's two top students nods off in his own practice, again and again), then Hero (the teacher sees from far away, comes, does not scold, and gives small tries one by one), then Payoff (even the last step, lie down, ends with "get up quick", then the viewer's own tired morning and Buddhasana).

One thread check: lines 1 to 3 talk to adults. Lines 4 to 17 tell the story straight, start to end, with no jumping out. Lines 18 to 22 come back to the adults. "What you do?" stays in the bedtime voice. The tries in lines 13 to 17 are the Buddha's own words to Moggallana ("both ears", "your right side"), told by the narrator. Line 3 ("Sleepy?") is still an adult line. It names the story's problem before the story starts, so the story does not need it.

5-year-old test (lines 4 to 17 alone): Moggallana is one of the Buddha's two top students. He meditates. He is sleepy. His head drops. Again. Far away, the Buddha sees it. You are the teacher. What do you do? Then the Buddha is right there, easy, like you bend your arm. He asks, are you nodding off? Yes, sir. No scolding. Small tries, one by one. Step four? Pull both ears. Still sleepy? Look up at the stars. Still sleepy, after all that? Last step. Lie down on your right side. When you wake, get up quick. It makes sense alone. The child's question "did it work?" is answered honestly in the caption: the old book does not say.

Contrarian check: line 1 names the viewer's belief (my body can't do it) and flips it (you are tired). The close is a question, not an answer.

Retention check: one OL before 0:08 (line 3 starts at 0:06.2). Rehooks at 0:22.5, 0:30.6, 0:33.3, 0:40.8 and 0:46.8. The gap from the double hook (0:06.2) to the first rehook (0:22.5) is about 16 s, but the voice drop and the title card at 0:09.0 and the fork at 0:19.2 (eyes in the lens, then the pause) break the pattern inside it, so no stretch runs past about 10 s without a break. From the last rehook to the end is 12.8 s. The silent beat comes before the adult payoff (after line 17). Every loop is paid: line 3 at line 13, line 8 at lines 9 to 12, line 15 at lines 16 and 17, the hook at line 19.

Rhythm check (24 Oct toolkit note): kid English keeps most sentences short. The longer sentences at lines 5, 7, 9, 10, 16 and 17 break the runs. Lines 12 to 15 are short on purpose (the text's own "but what if that doesn't work?" ladder). Say them with small pauses, never rushed.

## 8. Caption (English)

<p>Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. ?</p>
<p>Pull Your Ears</p>
<p>Hard asana not coming? Many times, you are just tired.</p>
<p>Moggallana was one of the Buddha's two top students.</p>
<p>One day he sat to meditate near a village. To meditate is to sit still and watch your mind.</p>
<p>He was nodding off. His head kept dropping, because sleep was coming.</p>
<p>The Buddha was far away, in another land. He saw it.</p>
<p>Then he was right there, as easy as you bend your arm.</p>
<p>He sat down and asked, "Are you nodding off, Moggallana?"</p>
<p>"Yes, sir."</p>
<p>No scolding. He gave small tries, one by one.</p>
<p>Let go of the thought you had when the sleepy feeling came.</p>
<p>Not working? Think about the teaching you learned.</p>
<p>Not working? Say the teaching out loud.</p>
<p>Not working? Pull both ears. Rub your arms and legs.</p>
<p>Not working? Get up. Wash your eyes with water. Look all around. Look up at the stars.</p>
<p>Not working? Think of bright light. Night, the same as day.</p>
<p>Not working? Walk up and down, with your mind on your walking.</p>
<p>Last step. Lie down on your right side, like a lion, one foot on the other. Keep in mind the time to get up.</p>
<p>And when you wake, get up quickly.</p>
<p>The old book does not say which try worked. It only gives the steps.</p>
<p>Buddhasana means the asana of the Buddha. The word buddha means awake.</p>
<p>In the 4th series, it comes after scorpion asana and the elbow balance. So you are already tired when you get there.</p>
<p>Tired? Change one small thing. Then the next.</p>
<p>Aching, yes. Sharp pain, no. That one, you tell your teacher.</p>

## 10. Flags

1. Tired is not the same as sleepy. The sutta is about drowsiness (middha) while meditating. Lines 1 and 2 are about being tired in asana practice. Line 3 now says "Sleepy? The Buddha say, pull your ears.", so the Buddha's answer is tied to being sleepy, which is what the text is about. The bridge from tired (lines 1 and 2) to sleepy (line 3) is Boonchu's own cue (28 Oct), not the text. Never say the Buddha spoke about asana, exercise or a tired body. The Buddha said it to Moggallana, and the story shows that right after.
2. Nodding off, not deep sleep. Pali "pacalāyamāno", Sujato "nodding off". The story says "head drop", "nodding off", "sleepy". It never says "he slept" during the meditation. The caption explains "nodding off" for children.
3. Ear pulling can look like a punishment. In Thai, Chinese and Indian schools, a teacher pulling or twisting a child's ear is an old punishment. That picture would turn "no scolding" into a scolding. So never show a teacher's hand, or anyone else's hand, on an ear. Show only the monk's own hands on his own ears, from behind, or Boonchu's own ear lobes, calm. No funny sound effect on lines 3, 13 or 22.
4. "Pull" or "pinch". Sujato says "pinch your ears and rub your limbs". The Pali is "ubho kaṇṇasotāni āviñcheyyāsi", which the picker reads as "pull both ears". No quote marks on it anywhere. If a purist objects, say "Pinch both ears". It is the same picture.
5. The count. "Step four" is our count. SuttaCentral's markup numbers the tries 1 to 7 (2.3 to 8.1), and the lie-down (9.1) is the last fallback, so 8 in all. Ears are step 4 either way. Never "8 steps" on screen. The spoken story names try 4 (the ears), part of try 5 (the stars; the water is in the caption) and the lie-down. Tries 1 to 3 are "small tries, one by one", and tries 6 and 7 (light, walking) are left out. "After all that" in line 15 covers them. The caption gives them all. Line 3 says no step number, and line 13 gives "step four". "Last step" is true: the lie-down is the last of the steps for drowsiness, and after it the sutta moves to other topics.
6. No result. The sutta never says which try worked, or that Moggallana woke and went on. Never say "it worked" or "he woke up". Line 17's picture (first light, an empty sleeping place) shows the teaching, not an event. Line 15's picture (his head drops again under the stars) shows the Buddha's "but what if that doesn't work?", the same way lines 13 and 14 picture the tries. It is not a claim that the stars failed. So never put "it did not work" in a spoken line, on screen or in the caption. The caption says plainly that the book does not say which try worked.
7. "No scolding" (line 12) is true by absence. There is no word of blame in 2.1 to 9.4. It is retold, never quoted, and never "the Buddha was kind" in quote marks. Never "lazy".
8. Powers. The text says he "vanished ... and reappeared" with clairvoyance. The script says only "far away, he see it" and "right there, easy, like you bend your arm". No "magic", no "teleport", no explaining. Boonchu's arm bend in line 9 is a picture of "easy", not a trick.
9. Respect, for the Thai audience above all. Always "the Buddha" with "the", even in his Thai-English. Boonchu reports the Buddha's words as the narrator and never acts his voice. No image, statue or AI face of the Buddha, and nobody shown on the seat. No joke about the Buddha or the monk. Never compare Boonchu or AYBKK to the Buddha. The fork (line 8) makes the viewer the teacher, not Boonchu.
10. The lying monk (line 16). The lion's posture, on the right side, is the shape of the Reclining Buddha (Wat Pho is in Bangkok), and many viewers read that shape as the Buddha's last day. So the monk must not look like that statue: no gold, no head resting on the hand, no Buddha hair, seen from behind, plain robe, under stars. Never say "like the Reclining Buddha". Never mention how Moggallana died (later tradition, a death with a crime).
11. Monks on screen. Use a monk of old India from behind, in a plain robe and an old village. Not a modern Thai monk or a Thai temple. Thai viewers can be upset by careless pictures of monks.
12. Thai viewers may know this story. trueplookpanya tells it as a way to beat sleepiness. The ear pulling and the stars still surprise. Thai retellings add commentary ("his 7th day as a monk", "he practised too hard"). Keep all of that out.
13. Rank. "One of the Buddha's two top students" follows dn14:1.9.7 ("a fine pair of chief disciples"). Never "best student" or "top student" alone. an1.190 names him first "with psychic power". That is left out (no powers).
14. No sleep or health advice. The lie-down and "get up quick" are the Buddha's words to Moggallana, in the story only. The adult lines never say rest, nap or sleep, and never promise that Buddhasana wakes you up. The caption's pain line keeps "do not stop when tired" from being heard as "push through pain".
15. Buddhasana safety. It is a 4th series asana: one foot behind the head and under the opposite armpit. The Reel never tells a viewer to try it. The cue slot is for people who already practise it. The fallback cue ("Lean left on your elbow.") is AYI's tip, in my plain words. Boonchu's own cue beats it.

AYI counts Nahusasana A, B, C apart, and Yogapedia counts one. AYI's page URL says "3rd-series", but the page header says 4th series. Line 19 now says only "After scorpion asana." That is true: Buddhasana comes after Vrschikasana (scorpion asana), with Sayanasana between them. Never say "right after scorpion asana". The caption keeps both.

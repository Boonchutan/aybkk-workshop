1. HARD: When exercise starts, oxygen supply can take 2 to 4 minutes (longer if the work is hard) to catch up, so muscles borrow quick energy.
2. WARM: In a small study, soccer players who rested at half-time cooled down and sprinted 2.4% slower.
3. CLUMSY: In lab tests, thinking was a bit clumsy in the first 20 minutes of exercise. After that, quick choices got a little better.
4. LOCK: In a small 1983 study, marathon runners' breathing locked onto their steps within 4 or 5 strides. Beginner runners' breathing stayed mostly irregular.
5. AUTOPILOT: Once a practised finger pattern was automatic, people could tap it while counting letters. The same brain areas ran it, with less activity.
6. WATCH: Runners told to watch their steps or breathing used more oxygen at the same speed. Noticing how the body feels cost nothing extra.
7. ZONE: In 11 runners on a 6-hour run, the 'in the zone' feeling rose in the first hour, then faded. The front of the brain got quieter, but the two did not move together.
8. YEARS: In a survey for a PhD thesis, 127 Ashtanga students said they get in the zone in practice more than in another activity, and more with more years of practice.
9. CHEAPER: In a small study of 9 soldiers with no group to compare, the same sun salutations needed about 30% less oxygen at month 11 than at month 3.
10. TOMORROW: Older people who exercised 4 to 5 times a week all their adult lives kept softer, springier hearts. A link, not proof.

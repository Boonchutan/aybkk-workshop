## 1. Lane

Lane B, and why: the point is the habit. Knowing more does not make you ready. Coming back to practise does. That is the Habit Gap in one story. There is no technique fix for one stuck asana, and Buddhasana is a 4th series asana that very few viewers practise, so a STUCK reply would have nothing to land on. The caption's AYBKK line ("a teacher notices when you miss class") stays in the caption only, far from Ananda's name. The caption asks for "aybkk" (keyword test, frozen until 22 Nov).

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If you wait until you know enough, you never start. There is always one more teaching, one more video, one more book. Next year you are still waiting, and your mat is still rolled up.

Why care: Many of us know more about yoga than we practise. This story shows a monk with 84,000 teachings who was still not ready. What changed him was one more night of practice. (Never "most viewers": that is unchecked.)

## 3. Toolkit

Toolkit pick: /hook (contrarian lever, bank #56 adapted, with a pattern interrupt in the double hook) + Fork-First Story.
Why: line 1 flips "learn more and you will be ready" in 3.2 s. For a 10-year Ashtanga practitioner that flip is weaker than it looks (Flag 24), so the opening goes into an A/B test against #73 (sec. 4). Line 2 opens the strangest loop in the story: he wakes up while he lies down. The point is out by 0:03.2 and the loop by 0:06.1. The fork stops the story the night before the meeting ("What you do? Learn more?", Harvard #1), right after Ananda's own reason, "still a learner". Most viewers pick "learn more". The rehook at 0:29.4 is the book's own answer: he keeps his mind on his body, most of the night.

Toolkit choice:
- Vibe Samurai (flat statement "Knowing is the easy part" + ladder of 3: 25 years, 84,000 teachings, 499 chosen without him + turn + flip). The ladder is ready-made, but the turn pulls to the banned "not X but Y" line ("not more teaching, more practice"), and that line leans toward the forbidden "he stopped studying".
- Question-First (open with "How much more you need to know before you practise?"). It is the strongest question for the viewer, but as an opener it gives the lesson away before the story starts. It lands harder as the close.

## 4. Hook bank

Main hook, #56 [warn] fit=adapt goal=shala habit=3:
- template: "The problem with {popular habit} for beginners"
- aybkk: "The problem with learning yoga from videos alone for beginners"

How I adapted it (the picker's line, kept word for word): "He learn 84,000 teachings. Still not ready."
- {popular habit} became "learning more before you start". The story shows the problem as a case, not a lecture: 84,000 teachings and still not ready.
- "for beginners" is dropped. This hook is for the 10-year practitioner who knows a lot (the /hook target).
- The [warn] lever stays: it warns that knowing is not the same as being ready. The note's "videos are a fine start" is kept: nothing in the video knocks learning, videos or books.
- Contrarian: the viewer believes "when I know enough, I will be ready". Line 1 flips it, so they ask themselves "am I waiting to know more?"
- Line 1 is SAID to camera and WRITTEN on screen from frame 1 (84,000 TEACHINGS. / STILL NOT READY.). This is not the Triple Hook (#121, Ep. 6 has it), so no picture rule applies, but the screen follows his words from the first frame.
- Loose fit, said plainly: the adaptation keeps only the [warn] lever and none of the template's words ("The problem with ... for beginners"). See Flag 20.

Double hook (not from the bank; it is the story's own strangest fact, Kd 21 1.6.4): "Then he wake up. Between floor and pillow."

## 6. The script

Timing: the story (lines 3 to 16) is in the bedtime voice, so it is timed at the slow pace, 2.6 spoken words a second. The adult lines (1 to 2 and 17 to 20) are timed at 2.8. Numbers are counted as said ("84,000" = eighty, four, thousand = 3 words; "499" = 4 words; "25" = 2 words). Long names get extra time: +0.2 s for each "Ananda" (lines 4, 7, 9, 11) and +0.5 s for "Buddhasana" (line 18). The fixed beats (fork pause, worst-moment pause, silent beat with the lamp hold, his cue, the sign-off) are added on top, see sec. 7. Start times are to a tenth of a second (tools/advB4/t2.py, v3, re-run in the fix pass). No line runs faster than 2.8 words a second.
Pictures: story pictures go over his head as in Ep. 5, kid style and calm. NEVER show the Buddha: no AI image, no statue, no figure, no face. Ananda is always a monk seen from behind (or no person), in a plain old-India robe, in a simple room of old India. No Thai temple, no modern Thai monk. Boonchu tells Ananda's thought as the narrator. He does not act a monk's voice.

Story pattern: Fail (25 years with the Buddha and 84,000 teachings, still a learner; left out of the 499; the night before, he thinks he is still a learner and it is not right to go; morning comes and he is still not free), then Hero (the other monks speak up, he keeps his mind on his body most of the night), then Payoff (between the floor and the pillow his mind becomes free, he walks in free, then "what change him?" and the viewer's own mat).

One thread check: lines 1 to 2 talk to adults. Lines 3 to 16 tell the story straight, start to end, with no jumping out. Lines 17 to 20 come back to the adults. "What you do? Learn more?" stays in the bedtime voice, the way you ask your child.

5-year-old test (lines 3 to 16 alone): Monk Ananda. Twenty-five years with the Buddha. Eighty-four thousand teachings, and still a learner. There is a big meeting. 499 monks are chosen, and their minds are already free. Not Ananda. The other monks say, invite him too. The night before the meeting, Ananda thinks he is still a learner, so it is not right to go. What would you do? Learn more? Ananda keeps his mind on his body most of the night. Early morning. Still not free, and the meeting is today. He lies down to rest. His feet leave the floor. His head is not yet on the pillow. Right there, his mind becomes free. He walks in, free. It makes sense alone. "Minds already free" (line 6) and "Still not free" (line 12) tell the child what Ananda is missing, and "still a learner" (lines 5 and 9) tells the child why he will not go.

"He" check: lines 1, 2, 13, 15, 16 and 17 use "he" or "him" only when Ananda is the one man in the scene (line 11's "his" comes right after his name). Lines 4 and 5 have no pronoun: line 4 names the Buddha, and line 5 sits on the same monk picture, right after the name tag. Line 8's "him" follows "Not Ananda", and the one who chose the 499 is never named.

Contrarian check: line 1 flips "know enough and you will be ready" for the viewer who keeps waiting to know more. For a 10-year Ashtanga practitioner it is weaker: "practice matters more than knowing" is the method's best-known saying, so line 1 can confirm their belief instead of flipping it, and "Learn more?" has an obvious answer (Flag 24). The A/B test against #73 (sec. 4) tests this on real viewers. Line 2 flips "you wake up when you get up". The close is a question, not an answer, and it is where the viewer questions himself or herself.

## 8. Caption (English)

<p>Hidden Stories of Ashtanga · Ep. ?</p>
<p>Between the Floor and the Pillow</p>
<p>Do you feel you must know more before you practise?</p>
<p>Here is a very old story from the Buddhist books.</p>
<p>Venerable Ananda served the Buddha for 25 years.</p>
<p>In his own verses, Ananda says, "84,000 teachings I've learned".</p>
<p>But he was still a trainee. His mind was not yet free.</p>
<p>499 monks whose minds were already free were chosen for a big meeting. Ananda was not one of them.</p>
<p>The other monks said, "He has learned many teachings and much Monastic Law from the Buddha. Please invite him as well." So he was invited.</p>
<p>The night before the meeting, he felt it was not right to go as a trainee.</p>
<p>He spent most of the night with his mind on his body.</p>
<p>Early in the morning he lay down to rest.</p>
<p>The old book says, "In the interval between his feet coming off the ground and his head hitting the pillow, his mind was freed ..."</p>
<p>He walked into the meeting free.</p>
<p>The story is from the Vinaya, the monks' rule books (Cullavagga XI, numbered Kd 21 on SuttaCentral, sections 1.2 to 1.6, tr. Bhikkhu Brahmali), and from his own verses (Theragatha 17.3, tr. Bhikkhu Sujato). Both are on SuttaCentral.</p>
<p><br></p>
<p>Buddhasana is in the 4th series (Advanced B). It comes after the scorpion asana, Vrschikasana. Buddha means "awakened, awake".</p>
<p>Knowing is the easy part. Coming back to your mat, morning after morning, is the hard part.</p>
<p>At AYBKK, a teacher notices when you miss class.</p>

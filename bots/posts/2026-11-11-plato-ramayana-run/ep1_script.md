## 1. The asana and the link (from the owner's PDF, Advanced A)

- Asana: Vishvamitrasana. It is printed in Boonchu's own PDF (Philippa Asher, Practise Sheets Part 2), p.15, "First Half of Advanced A Series", second asana on the page: "Vishvamitrasana", then "Vishvamitrasana (exit)", dristi "hand". I looked at the page picture and the enlarged label again today. Nothing in this script comes from Advanced C or D.
- Link kind: name. Spoken once, in line 20: Vishvamitrasana. Advanced A. Named after that silent teacher.
- Why it is honest: Sweeney's glossary prints "Sage Viśwāmitra's posture". Monier-Williams gives viśvā-mitra as the name of the sage and says the Ramayana makes him "a companion and counsellor of the young Rāma-candra". In Bala Kanda 30 he is the teacher at whose fire the two boys keep watch. The link is his name and nothing more. The shape of the asana has nothing to do with the watch, the fire or the arrow, and the asana has nothing to do with the one who is thrown into the sea.

## 2. Stakes and why care

Stakes: If you only count the days when something happens (a new asana, a bind, a word from the teacher), the quiet days look like wasted days, and one of them becomes the day you stop. Then the day something does come, you are not on the mat.

Why care: If your practice feels like nothing is happening and your teacher says little, you may think you are stuck, or that nobody is teaching you. This story reads it another way. The quiet days were the practice. And a quiet teacher is still a teacher.

## 3. Toolkit

Toolkit pick: /hook contrarian lever (bank #10) + Fork-First Story.
Why: The contrarian line lands in 2.5 s and stops a long-time practitioner, because everybody has had the morning where nothing happened. The story has a natural stop at day five (no sleep, nothing came, the teacher silent), so the viewer has to choose before the book answers ("What you do? Sleep?", Harvard #1). Most viewers pick "relax". The rehook is the book's own answer: on day six Rama says "be ready".

Toolkit choice:
- Triple Hook (#121) + Hero's Journey. #121 is already the hook of the unfilmed Ep. 6, so it would repeat, and a full hero's journey does not fit in 60 seconds.
- Vibe Samurai (flat statement, ladder of three days, turn, flip). The days make a ready ladder, but its turn pulls toward the banned "not X but Y" line and it has no stop for the viewer to choose.

## 4. Hook bank

Main hook, #10 [teach]: "How to be happy with the same practice every morning". Bank note: Mysore is the same practice each day, so this turns "boring" into the point; show it, do not promise happiness.

How I adapted it:
- Turned from a how-to into a contrarian question and answer in his voice: "Nothing happen in your practice today? Good." 7 words, 2.5 s. It flips the belief "a practice where nothing happens is a wasted practice". It promises no happiness.
- The second line of the map's hook ("The day nothing happen, that one is the practice.") is not said at the top. It is paid at line 17 ("Five quiet days. That was the practice."). So "Good." stays an open loop for about 43 seconds and the hook stays inside 3 s. It is no longer on the add-back list (Flag 3).

Double hook (line 3): the old book. It starts at 0:02.5 and the point lands at 0:06.4.
- Line 3 names the book and puts the danger up front: "In old Ramayana book, boys wait for monsters. Five days, nothing." It opens the loop: do the monsters come? Paid at line 12. "Nothing" is the echo of line 1. "Ramayana book" explains the name the first time it is said.
- The movie is not spoken any more. It is small on-screen text over line 3 (line 2, words only) and one caption line: NEW RAMAYANA MOVIE · IN BANGKOK CINEMAS NOW. It says why this story now. It names no actor, gives no review, and says nothing about what the film shows. Checked today (sec. 9).

The book tells of nothing coming until the sixth day.

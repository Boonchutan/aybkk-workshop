## HOW TO USE THIS PROGRAM

**27 minutes of video → 27 days of application.** One technique per day. Read the day in 10 minutes, do the drill in 30–60.

Each day gives you four things:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The technique** | What Suby actually teaches, stated in one line |
| **Why it works** | The mechanism — so you can adapt it instead of copying it |
| **Your drill** | The specific thing you do today, for AYBKK, not in the abstract |
| **Done means** | A test you either pass or fail. No "I thought about it." |

The days are **sequenced, not a menu.** Day 4 assumes Day 1 exists. Build in order.

Three days carry a **⛔ REFUSE** mark. Those are techniques that work for Suby and would actively damage you. Read them anyway — you need to recognise them when a competitor uses them on your students.

# PART 2 — THE ADAPTATION LAYER

Suby sells marketing services to business owners. You sell **transmission from a lineage** to serious practitioners, at 150,000 THB, to ten people.

Those are not the same sale, and three of his techniques will cost you the exact asset your whole position rests on. This section is the filter.

## The structural difference

Suby's system is built to **maximise conversion**. Yours is built to **filter** — that is literally what `/qualify` exists to do. A high-ticket cohort of ten does not fail from too few applicants; it fails from the **wrong** ten. One student who should not be in the room damages the other nine and the room itself.

So every technique below gets read through one question:

> **Does this help the right person recognise themselves — or does it help the wrong person talk themselves into it?**

Keep the first kind. Refuse the second.

# BLOCK 1: THE DREAM BUYER
## *Days 1–3 · You cannot filter for someone you cannot describe*

---

### DAY 1 — Name One Person, Not a Segment

**The technique:** Suby's Phase 1 — know your dream buyer's age, frustrations, likes, dislikes and where they spend time, in enough detail that you can write an offer only they respond to.

**Why it works:** Copy written for "serious practitioners in China" reaches nobody, because nobody thinks of themselves in the plural. Copy written for one specific person reaches everyone who resembles them. Precision is not a narrowing — it is what makes recognition possible.

**Your drill:** Write one page describing a single real person who should be in the China cohort. Not invented — pick someone from the Huizhou 43 or from your DMs. Name them. Then fill in:

- How many years have they practised, and how far into the series?
- What made them start, and what nearly made them stop?
- What do they currently believe about their practice that is wrong?
- What have they already paid for that didn't work?
- Who do they currently follow, and what do they distrust about them?
- What would they have to believe to pay 150,000 THB?
- What are they afraid the money buys — and what are they afraid it doesn't?

**Done means:** You can read the page aloud and name three real people it fits. If it fits everybody, it's a segment, not a buyer — rewrite it.

---

### DAY 2 — Find Where They Already Are

**The technique:** Phase 1, second half — know where your dream buyer gathers, so you spend attention-money where they already stand.

**Why it works:** Attention is cheapest where it's already concentrated. A serious Chinese practitioner is not discoverable by broad interest targeting — they're in shalas, in WeChat groups, at workshops, in the orbit of teachers they already trust.

**Your drill:** List every place your Day 1 person can actually be found. Be concrete — a named group, a named shala, a named teacher's audience, a named event. For each one, write how you'd reach it: paid, organic, or through a person. Mark which of the three it is.

Then mark the ones where **you already have standing** — the Huizhou 43, your Bangkok students, the hosts who've brought you in. That column is your first traffic source, and it costs nothing.

**Done means:** Ten named places, each tagged paid / organic / relationship. At least three where you already have standing.

---

### DAY 3 — Write the Disqualification List

**The technique:** This is `/qualify`'s Name-Disqualify-Release, not Suby's — and it's the day the two systems part ways.

**Why it works:** Suby's step 1 calls out *who this is for*. For a ten-person cohort, naming who it's **not** for does more work: it removes the wrong applicants before they cost you a conversation, and — counterintuitively — it makes the right ones certain. A serious practitioner who reads a hard disqualification list and still recognises themselves arrives already sold.

**Your drill:** Run `/qualify`. Write the "this is not for you" section for the China cohort. Force yourself to include at least one disqualifier that costs you real money — a type of student who could pay but shouldn't come.

**Done means:** A list where at least one line genuinely stings to write. If nothing on it costs you anything, you've written marketing, not a filter.

# BLOCK 2: THE PERFECT BAIT
## *Days 4–6 · Give away the teaching, not a brochure*

---

### DAY 4 — Build the HVCO

**The technique:** Phase 2 + the HVCO — a free asset of genuine value (report, guide, video, cheat sheet) that over-delivers on its promise, given in exchange for contact details. Suby's rule: not a website with an About page. Something that solves a real problem before any money changes hands.

**Why it works:** It inverts the burden of proof. Instead of claiming you can teach, you teach — and the prospect evaluates the actual thing rather than a description of it. For a lineage teacher this is close to free, because the asset is what you already know.

**Your drill:** Choose one and build it this week. Candidates, in order of strength:

1. **The practice audit** — the specific errors you see most in intermediate practitioners, how to spot each in yourself, and what to do about it
2. **What Sharath actually corrected** — the corrections you received directly, and what they meant. This one only you can make
3. **The honest readiness guide** — how to tell whether you're ready for the next series, written to talk some readers out of it

Option 2 is the strongest. It's unfalsifiable proof of transmission, it's impossible to copy, and it gives value in the giving.

**Done means:** A finished asset a stranger could use without you. Not an outline.

---

### DAY 5 — Over-Deliver on the Promise

**The technique:** Phase 2's actual bar — the bait must exceed what it promised, not merely meet it.

**Why it works:** The free asset is a sample of how you teach. A prospect deciding on 150,000 THB is not evaluating your content — they're extrapolating. Thin free thing implies thin paid thing. A free thing better than most people's paid course implies the opposite, and requires no claim from you at all.

**Your drill:** Take yesterday's asset and add the thing you were holding back. There's always one — the correction you thought was too valuable to give away, the mistake you didn't want to name. Put it in.

Then test: would a practitioner who received this feel they owed you something? If not, add more until they would.

**Done means:** You feel slightly uncomfortable about how much you gave away. That discomfort is the correct calibration.

---

### DAY 6 — One Asset, Repeated

**The technique:** Traffic §7 — Suby anchors everything to one asset, repeated relentlessly, functioning as both authority proof and low-barrier entry.

**Why it works:** Repetition builds recognition; a scattered library builds none. One asset, named consistently, becomes a thing people refer to and pass on. Ten assets become a blur.

**Your drill:** Name your Day 4 asset properly — a real title, not a description. Then write the one sentence you'll use every single time you reference it, in every channel, without variation. Put both in `/remotecontrol`'s context so every future piece of copy uses the same words.

**Done means:** One title, one sentence, written down where you'll reuse them. Say the sentence out loud without reading it.

# BLOCK 3: CAPTURE
## *Days 7–9 · Own the relationship*

---

### DAY 7 — Get Off Rented Land

**The technique:** Phase 3 — capture contact details. Move the relationship off platforms you don't control and onto a list you do.

**Why it works:** Instagram can throttle you, WeChat can restrict you, and neither will tell you first. A list you own is the only marketing asset that appreciates. Suby's point is blunt: if you can't contact them directly, you don't have them.

**Your drill:** Map your current capture. For each channel — Instagram, LINE, WeChat, the shop at `cn.aybkk.net`, the workshop orientation pages — answer: *when someone shows interest here, what do I end up holding?* A name? A LINE ID? A WeChat contact? Nothing?

Find the leaks. Every place someone can show interest and vanish is a leak.

**Done means:** A written list of every capture point, and every leak, ranked by how many people flow through it.

---

### DAY 8 — Build the Capture Path

**The technique:** Phase 3, applied — one path from interest to contact details, short enough that nobody falls out of it.

**Why it works:** Every extra step loses people. The path from "I want that free guide" to "you have my contact" should be the shortest thing you build.

**Your drill:** Build one path for the Day 4 asset. Given your channels, the realistic shape:

- **China:** WeChat QR → follow → asset delivered in-chat. Email is weak into China; don't build on it
- **Thailand / international:** LINE or email capture → asset delivered
- **In person:** QR card at the shala and at workshops. You already generate QR cards (`generate-qr-cards.py`) — reuse that

Note the constraint that already shapes this: fonts are self-hosted in `public/fonts/` because the Google CDN is blocked in China. Whatever you build must load behind the Great Firewall. Test it there before you trust it.

**Done means:** One path, working end to end, tested from a phone. Not designed — tested.

---

### DAY 9 — Segment on the Way In

**The technique:** Nurture §8 — right message, right person, right time. Segment by where they actually are.

**Why it works:** A 3-year practitioner and a 15-year practitioner need opposite messages. One list treated identically will under-serve both. Suby segments by buying-journey stage; you should segment by **practice depth**, because that's what determines whether the cohort is even appropriate.

**Your drill:** Add one question to the capture path. One only — every extra question costs you completions. The highest-value single question:

> *How many years have you practised, and where are you in the series?*

That single answer routes them: cohort candidate, workshop candidate, or too early. Your `/dm` command already classifies tourist / beginner / serious — use the same three buckets so the systems agree.

**Done means:** Contacts arrive tagged into one of three buckets, automatically.

# BLOCK 4: THE OFFER
## *Days 10–13 · Where Suby's system needs the most surgery*

---

### DAY 10 — The Rationale: Why This, Why Now

**The technique:** Godfather component 1 — a clear rationale. Every offer must answer *why does this exist and why now*, or it reads as a trick.

**Why it works:** An unexplained offer creates suspicion in proportion to its size. At 150,000 THB the suspicion is large. A true rationale dissolves it faster than any amount of persuasion, because it gives the buyer something to verify rather than trust.

**Your drill:** Write the honest answer to three questions, in plain language:

1. **Why does this cohort exist at all?** Not "to teach Ashtanga" — what specifically can you transmit in this format that a workshop or a Mysore room cannot?
2. **Why ten?** The real constraint. If it's the number you can genuinely hold to this standard, say that. If it's arbitrary, change the number or change the reason
3. **Why 2026?** What is true now that wasn't before? Be careful here — Sharath's passing is the honest answer to why direct authorization matters more now, and it must be stated with restraint or not at all

**Done means:** Three answers a sceptical practitioner could check. Delete any sentence you couldn't defend to someone who knew the lineage well.

---

### DAY 11 — Run the 17 Steps

**The technique:** The 17-Step Selling System — Suby's full sales-page skeleton, linked in the video's own description. Steps 1→17, in order.

**Why it works:** It's a sequence of objections handled in the order a reader raises them. Skip a step and the reader stalls at the objection you skipped. The structure is sound even where the tone isn't.

**Your drill:** Draft the China cohort page against all 17 steps. Structure from Suby, tone from `/qualify` and `/hook`. Three steps need explicit handling:

| Step | Suby says | You do |
|---|---|---|
| 7 — Credentials | Show credentials | The Sharath authorization, stated **exactly**: direct multi-year authorization, an 18-year personal relationship, hosted in Bangkok 2018 and 2024. Not one word beyond the facts. This is the asset — inflating it destroys it |
| 11–12 — Bonuses & value stacking | Add bonuses, itemise, total | ⛔ Neither. State clearly what's included. **Do not total it.** Pricing a lineage into a dollar figure cheapens the thing you're selling |
| 14 — Scarcity | Inject scarcity | Ten places. That's true, so say it. Add nothing else — no timer, no "spots filling fast" |

Steps 1–6, 8–10, 13, 15–17 run close to Suby's original.

**Done means:** A complete draft where every one of the 17 steps is either present or deliberately struck with a written reason.

---

### DAY 12 — The Power Guarantee, Redefined

**The technique:** Godfather component 6 — move risk off the buyer and onto yourself.

**Why it works:** Risk reversal converts because the buyer's real objection is rarely price — it's *what if I'm wrong*. Removing that fear removes the reason to delay.

**Your drill:** Here is the trap. You **cannot** guarantee an outcome. You cannot promise a student will land a posture, deepen a practice, or be changed. Any teacher who guarantees attainment is lying, and a serious practitioner knows it instantly.

So guarantee what you actually control:

- ✅ The **teaching** — hours, attention, direct instruction, the format as described
- ✅ The **access** — that you personally teach it, not a substitute
- ✅ The **honesty of the filter** — if the application reveals they're not ready, you say so and don't take the money
- ⛔ Never the **result**

That third one is the strongest guarantee available to you, and almost nobody offers it. *"If I don't think this is right for you, I'll tell you before you pay."* It is a genuine risk reversal, it costs you only the wrong students, and it is impossible for a hype-driven competitor to copy.

**Done means:** A written guarantee containing zero claims about student attainment.

### DAY 13 — ⛔ REFUSE: The Two Techniques That Would Cost You Everything

**The technique:** 17-Step §11 (bonuses) and §14 (injected scarcity), plus Traffic §5 (parody creative). Suby uses all three. All three work. You use none of them.

**Why it works — for him:** Suby sells marketing services in a market where everyone is shouting and attention is the scarce resource. Bonus-stacking, urgency and meme-jacking are rational responses to that environment.

**Why it destroys you:** Your entire position rests on an asset that is credible precisely because it is *not* for sale in the ordinary way — direct authorization from Sharath. Authority of that kind is asymmetric: built over eighteen years, spent in a single afternoon. A 10-year practitioner who sees a countdown timer on a lineage program doesn't think "I should hurry." They think *"this person is selling something,"* and they never come back. There is no recovery from that, because the thing you lost was the reason they were listening.

**Your drill:** No building today. Instead, an audit:

1. Go through everything currently live — the shop, the orientation pages, any DM template, any past post
2. Find every instance of urgency, bonus, stack, or "limited" language
3. For each, ask: **is this literally true?** If yes, keep it and state it plainly. If no, delete it today
4. Write the three refused techniques on something you'll see while writing copy

**Done means:** Zero untrue urgency claims live anywhere. Anything you couldn't verify is gone.

# BLOCK 5: TRAFFIC
## *Days 14–17 · Buy attention below what it's worth*

---

### DAY 14 — Know What a Student Is Worth

**The technique:** Phase 5 — traffic is only rational when you know your numbers. Buy attention below customer value.

**Why it works:** Without this number, ad spend is gambling and every result is unreadable. With it, every decision becomes arithmetic. This is the single least glamorous day here and the one that decides whether the rest works.

**Your drill:** Calculate three numbers:

1. **Cohort value** — 150,000 THB per student. Ten students = 1,500,000 THB
2. **What you can afford per applicant** — if 20 qualified applications yield 10 students, and you'll spend 10% of revenue on acquisition, that's 150,000 THB ÷ 20 = **7,500 THB per qualified application**
3. **What you can afford per lead** — if 1 in 10 leads becomes a qualified application, that's **750 THB per lead**

Those are placeholder ratios. Replace them with your real ones as data arrives — but write today's numbers down now, because a wrong number you're tracking beats no number.

**Done means:** Three numbers written down, and you can say your maximum cost per lead from memory.

---

### DAY 15 — The Hook Is the Ad

**The technique:** Traffic §2 — the opening line or visual decides everything downstream. Suby's own example: *"This One Strategy Generated $1 Million in Sales in Just 30 Days."*

**Why it works:** Nothing after the first second is read by anyone the first second didn't stop. Every other variable is downstream of the hook, which is why it deserves more time than all of them.

**Your drill:** Run `/hook` on three topics from your Day 4 asset. That command already does this better-adapted than Suby's version — five levers (authority, contrarian, stakes, question, pattern interrupt), under 12 words, no hype, tested against whether a 10-year practitioner would stop.

Note the difference in standard: Suby's example hook leads with a revenue number. Yours can't and shouldn't. **Your authority lever is the strongest one you have and the one no competitor can copy** — you received corrections directly from Sharath. Lead with the transmission, never with a number.

**Done means:** 15 hooks generated, 3 chosen — one per topic.

# BLOCK 6: THE MAGIC LANTERN
## *Days 18–21 · Walk them from cold to hot — the block that fits you best*

---

### DAY 18 — Light the Path

**The technique:** Phase 6, the Magic Lantern — the prospect is in the dark; you walk ahead with the lantern and they follow. A sequence of 2–3 genuinely valuable messages that move them forward *before* anything is sold.

**Why it works:** Trust cannot be asserted, only demonstrated repeatedly. Each piece of real value given without a request compounds; by the time an offer appears, it reads as the obvious next step rather than an interruption.

# BLOCK 7: SELL LIKE A DOCTOR

### DAY 22 — Diagnose Before You Prescribe

**The technique:** Phase 7, *Sell Like a Doctor* — don't push, diagnose. Ask questions, listen, then prescribe. Suby's sequence: **uncover goals → explore pain → give a small win → close with confidence.**

**Why it works:** A prescription arriving before a diagnosis is untrustworthy no matter how correct it is — the buyer has no evidence you understood their situation. Diagnosis first makes the same recommendation land as considered rather than generic.

**This is the closest fit in the entire system.** It is the Mysore method described in sales language. You already watch a student for weeks before changing anything. You already ask before you adjust. Suby is teaching businesspeople to do what you do every morning.

**Your drill:** Write your diagnostic questions for a cohort conversation — the ones a doctor would ask. Suggested spine:

1. *Tell me about your practice. How long, how often, where?* — **uncover**
2. *What made you start?* — **origin, always ask this**
3. *What's not working right now?* — **pain**
4. *What have you already tried for it?* — **what's failed**
5. *What do you want your practice to be in five years?* — **goal**
6. *What's stopping you?* — **the real objection, in their words**

**Done means:** Six questions written. You talk for less than a third of the conversation.

### DAY 23 — Give a Small Win First

**The technique:** Phase 7, step three — deliver a real result inside the conversation, before any close.

**Why it works:** It converts a claim into evidence at zero risk to them. One genuine correction proves more about your teaching than any page of copy, because they *felt* it.

**Your drill:** Build the small win into every cohort conversation. For you it's obvious and nearly free: **give one real correction.** From their answers to Day 22, name the thing you can already see — the error their description reveals, the thing their teacher hasn't told them.

Give it fully, with no condition attached. If they take it and never speak to you again, you did your job.

**Done means:** A written rule that no conversation ends without one real correction given, sale or no sale.

### DAY 24 — Listen More Than You Talk

**The technique:** Nurture §5 — the diagnosis is in what they say. Listen more than you talk.

**Why it works:** The objection blocking the sale is almost never the one they state first. It surfaces in what they return to unprompted — and only if there's enough silence for them to get there.

**Your drill:** In your next three real conversations — students, host inquiries, anyone — count your talking time against theirs. Aim under one-third.

Use the two most useful phrases you already have from `30-Day-Master-Persuasion-Program.md`, Day 3:

- *"It seems like..."*
- *"It sounds like..."*

Then stop. The pause after a label is where the real answer arrives, and most people fill it themselves.

**Done means:** Three conversations where you spoke less than a third, and in at least one, a labelled pause produced something you didn't already know.

### DAY 25 — Close by Filtering, Not Persuading

**The technique:** Phase 7's close — "confidently close." This is the last place Suby's system needs inverting.

**Why it works — inverted:** For a ten-person cohort, the close isn't *convince them to say yes*. It's *make sure the right ten say yes and the wrong ones don't.* A confident close, for you, is the willingness to say no.

**Your drill:** Write your close. It should contain, honestly:

- What you observed in the diagnosis, said plainly
- Whether you think the cohort is right for them — **including when it isn't**
- What you'd recommend instead, if it isn't
- The application, if it is
- No pressure, no deadline that isn't real, no follow-up sequence disguised as concern

The strongest close available to you is the one almost nobody can afford to use: *"I don't think this is right for you yet. Here's what I'd do instead. Come back in a year."*

Say that to the wrong applicants and the right ones will hear about it.

**Done means:** A written close containing an explicit path for turning someone down.

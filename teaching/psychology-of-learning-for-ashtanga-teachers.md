# The Psychology of Learning for Ashtanga Teachers: An Evidence Map for Prospects, New Students, and Long-Term Practitioners

*Prepared for the AYBKK teacher training module. English with brief 简体中文 renderings. Direct and factual. No sales language.*

> [!abstract] How to use this note
> Reference material for the teacher-training module. Sections **A–F** are the evidence, tagged by which group they serve (PROSPECTS / NEW / EXPERIENCED / TEACHER). Section **G** is the myths to drop. The **Recommendations** at the end are the practical playbook. Each finding carries a 一句话 Chinese one-liner you can say to students. Slide deck built from this: see the AYBKK presentations folder.

## TL;DR
- The strongest, most replicated tools for a teacher are: build real success experiences (mastery), use an external focus of attention, support autonomy, reduce feedback frequency as skill grows, and use motivational-interviewing-style conversations instead of persuasion with prospects. These are backed by meta-analyses, not single studies.
- Several popular ideas are weak or wrong and should be dropped from the module: learning styles, the strong version of growth mindset, the "21 days to a habit" claim, the fixed "10,000 hour rule," and left-brain/right-brain learner types.
- The three groups need different handling: prospects respond to autonomy and resolving ambivalence; new students need mastery, habit cues, and dropout protection in the first 3 to 6 months; experienced students need less feedback, an external focus under pressure, and protection from the expertise reversal effect.

## Key Findings

### The single most useful frame
Most of what predicts whether a student stays and improves is not the cleverness of the cue or the verbal instruction. It is (1) whether they experience genuine success, (2) whether they feel some choice, and (3) the relationship with the teacher. Hold this frame while reading the rest.

一句话: 学生能否坚持，主要看三点：真实的成功体验、一定的自主选择、和老师的关系。

## A. Motor Learning and Physical Skill (mostly NEW and EXPERIENCED students)

### 1. Distributed practice beats massed practice
- **What the research shows:** Practice spread over time produces better long-term retention than the same practice crammed together. For motor skills, Lee and Genovese (1988) reported distributed practice improved both acquisition (d = 0.96) and retention over massed practice. Donovan and Radosevich (1999) meta-analysis confirmed the effect, though its size depends on the task. Cepeda et al. (2006) reviewed over 300 experiments: the effect is large and robust for memory, more variable for complex motor skills. A note of honesty: Wiseheart, D'Souza and Chae (2017), in PLOS ONE, taught a 17-note piano sequence and found no spacing effect, concluding "the effect may not always be demonstrable for complex motor skills."
- **Evidence strength:** Strong and replicated for simple skills; mixed for complex motor skills.
- **Practical action:** The traditional Mysore format (daily, moderate-length, self-paced practice) is already aligned with this. Short daily practice beats occasional long workshops.
- 一句话: 每天短时间练习，胜过偶尔的长时间练习。
- **Group:** NEW and EXPERIENCED.

### 2. Contextual interference: random practice hurts today, helps later
- **What the research shows:** Practicing several variations in random order produces worse performance during the session but better retention and transfer than blocked practice (all of one thing, then all of another). Origin: Shea and Morgan (1979), building on Battig (1966). Czyż, Wójcik and Solarská (2024), in Frontiers in Psychology, reviewed 42 studies (34 in the meta-analysis) and found "the overall CI effect on transfer in motor learning was medium (SMD = 0.55), favoring random practice," larger in adults (SMD around 0.54) and older adults (large), but small and non-significant in applied sport settings and negligible in young participants.
- **Important caveat:** High interference can overwhelm novices. Several studies (basketball, volleyball, soccer) failed to find the effect in beginners.
- **Practical action:** For brand new students, keep practice fairly blocked and repetitive (this is what the fixed Ashtanga sequence does). For experienced students, introduce variation and do not panic when messy practice sessions feel worse; that difficulty is often productive.
- 一句话: 打乱顺序练习，当下更难，但长期记得更牢。新手先重复，老手再变化。
- **Group:** NEW (blocked) vs EXPERIENCED (introduce variation).

### 3. Feedback frequency and the guidance hypothesis
- **What the research shows:** Salmoni, Schmidt, and Walter (1984, Psychological Bulletin) proposed the guidance hypothesis: feedback given too often creates dependence, so performance collapses when feedback is removed. Reduced feedback frequency produces worse practice-day performance but better long-term learning. Confirmed by Winstein and Schmidt (1990) and many others. Concurrent (constant, real-time) feedback is especially prone to creating dependence for simple tasks.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong, foundational, replicated. Boundary condition: for very complex tasks, more feedback can help (Wulf and Shea, 2002).
- **Practical action:** Do not correct every student on every pose every day. As students gain skill, deliberately fade your corrections so they learn to feel the posture themselves. This directly serves the daily Mysore method.
- 一句话: 不要每次都纠正。随着进步，减少纠正，让学生自己感受体式。
- **Group:** NEW (more feedback, but not constant) shifting to EXPERIENCED (much less).

### 4. External focus of attention beats internal focus
- **What the research shows:** Gabriele Wulf and colleagues have shown across dozens of studies since about 1998 that directing attention to the effect of a movement (external) produces better performance and learning than directing it to the body part (internal). Example: "reach toward the mat" beats "straighten your arm."
- **Evidence strength:** One of the most replicated findings in motor learning.
- **Practical action:** Cue outward and toward effects, not inward toward muscles, especially for balance and complex postures. "Press the floor away" rather than "engage your quadriceps."
- 一句话: 把注意力放在动作的效果上（如"把地板推开"），比放在肌肉上更有效。
- **Group:** ALL, but especially NEW and EXPERIENCED performing under strain.

### 5. OPTIMAL theory: autonomy + expectancies + external focus
- **What the research shows:** Wulf and Lewthwaite (2016), in Psychonomic Bulletin and Review, combined three factors into the OPTIMAL theory of motor learning: enhanced expectancies (belief you will succeed), autonomy support (giving choices), and external focus. Each helps alone, and studies show they add together (Wulf, Lewthwaite, Cardozo, and Chiviacowsky, 2018, found all three together beat two).
- **Evidence strength:** The individual components are well-replicated. The unified theory is influential but newer; treat the "all three add up" claim as promising rather than settled (it rests on a small number of studies).
- **Practical action:** Give students small choices (which variation, how many breaths), set them up for success experiences, and cue externally. These are free and stack.
- 一句话: 给选择、让学生相信能成功、注意力向外，三者叠加效果最好。
- **Group:** ALL.

### 6. Self-controlled practice and autonomy
- **What the research shows:** Learners who control some aspect of their own practice (when to get feedback, order of tasks, use of aids) learn better than yoked controls. This is a pillar of OPTIMAL theory and is supported by multiple motor-learning experiments (Chiviacowsky, Wulf, and colleagues).
- **Practical action:** Let students choose within a frame. In Mysore, this is natural: they set their own pace and decide when to ask.
- 一句话: 让学生对练习有一定掌控，学得更好。
- **Group:** ALL.

### 7. Kluger and DeNisi (1996): feedback can make things worse
- **What the research shows:** A meta-analysis of 607 effect sizes and 23,663 observations found feedback improved performance on average (d = 0.41), but over one third of feedback interventions actually decreased performance. Feedback that directs attention to the self (ego, self-worth) rather than the task tends to backfire.
- **Evidence strength:** Large, influential meta-analysis (Psychological Bulletin).
- **Practical action:** Keep corrections about the task, not the person. "The hand goes here" not "you always get this wrong." Assume roughly a third of your corrections could hurt if aimed at the self.
- 一句话: 反馈要针对动作，不要针对人。针对人的反馈，约三分之一会起反作用。
- **Group:** ALL.

### 8. Choking and self-focus in skilled performers
- **What the research shows:** Beilock and Carr (2001) and Beilock et al. (2002) showed that under pressure, experts who turn attention inward to monitor their own well-learned movements perform worse (self-focus / explicit monitoring). Novices show the opposite: they are hurt by distraction, not by self-focus. Skilled movement has become automatic, and consciously controlling it disrupts it.
- **Evidence strength:** Well-replicated across golf, baseball, soccer.
- **Practical action:** For experienced students attempting hard postures or being watched, steer them to an external focus or a simple breath rhythm, not to step-by-step body monitoring. For brand new students, the opposite: give them concrete steps to focus on.
- 一句话: 熟练者在压力下过度关注自己的身体反而会失常；应关注呼吸或外部目标。新手则需要具体步骤。
- **Group:** EXPERIENCED (self-focus harms) vs NEW (steps help).

## B. Motivation, Adherence, and Dropout (mostly PROSPECTS and NEW students)

### 9. Exercise dropout: the first 3 months are the danger zone
- **What the research shows:** Sperandei, Vieira, and Reis (2016, Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport), tracking 5,240 fitness-center members in Rio de Janeiro over nine years, found that "63% of new members will abandon activities before the third month, and less than 4% will remain for more than 12 months of continuous activity" (measured by membership payment). Attendance-based studies show lower but still high dropout: Clavel San Emeterio et al. (2019) found about 51% dropout over twelve months. Predictors of dropout: younger age (25 and under), weight-loss as the main motive, and lack of prior activity. Supervised, small-group formats retain far better.
- **Evidence strength:** Consistent across multiple observational studies; exact numbers depend on how "dropout" is measured (payment overstates it; attendance shows roughly 50%).
- **Practical action:** Treat the first 90 days as a retention emergency. Front-load relationship, success, and habit-building. The shala's supervised group format is itself a strong retention tool: lean on it. Watch for the weight-loss-only motive as a red flag for early dropout.
- 一句话: 前三个月最容易放弃。头90天要重点建立关系、成功感和习惯。
- **Group:** NEW (and PROSPECTS deciding).

### 10. Habit formation takes about 66 days, not 21 (and missing a day is fine)
- **What the research shows:** Lally et al. (2010, European Journal of Social Psychology), tracking 96 people forming daily habits, found the time to reach 95% of automaticity "ranged from 18 to 254 days" (median 66), and that "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process." The "21 days" claim is a misreading of Maxwell Maltz's 1960 plastic-surgery book and has no support. A 2024 systematic review (Singh et al.) put durable health habits at roughly two to five months.
- **Evidence strength:** Lally is a single well-designed real-world study; 66 days is a median, not a law. The debunking of "21 days" is solid.
- **Practical action:** Tell new students to expect two to three months before daily practice feels automatic, and that one missed day does not undo progress. Set expectations to prevent early discouragement.
- 一句话: 养成习惯平均约66天（不是21天）。漏掉一天没关系。
- **Group:** NEW.

### 11. Implementation intentions: "if-then" plans
- **What the research shows:** Gollwitzer's implementation intentions are specific if-then plans ("After I wake up, I will roll out my mat"). Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006) meta-analysis of 94 studies found a medium-to-large effect (d around 0.65) on goal attainment across behaviours. For physical activity specifically, effects are smaller (d roughly 0.14 to 0.31) and depend on the person already intending to act.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong overall meta-analysis; weaker and more variable specifically for exercise.
- **Practical action:** Help students write a concrete plan tying practice to an existing daily cue and a fixed time and place. Do not rely on it alone; it works best when the student already wants to practice.
- 一句话: 用"如果…就…"计划：把练习和固定的时间、地点、日常动作绑定。
- **Group:** NEW (and PROSPECTS who have decided).

### 12. Self-Determination Theory: autonomy, competence, relatedness
- **What the research shows:** Deci and Ryan's SDT holds that motivation lasts when three needs are met: autonomy (choice), competence (feeling effective), and relatedness (connection). In exercise, a systematic review (Teixeira et al., 2012) found autonomous motivation and competence consistently predict participation; identified regulation predicts short-term adoption while intrinsic motivation predicts long-term adherence. Meta-analytic work (Gillison et al., 2019) found SDT interventions produce large gains in autonomy support (g = 0.84) and need satisfaction and medium gains in autonomous motivation (g = 0.41).
- **Evidence strength:** Very large, mature evidence base.
- **Practical action:** For long-term retention, help students find enjoyment in the practice itself (intrinsic), not just results. Support choice and connection. See section F for the cross-cultural caveat with Chinese students.
- 一句话: 长期坚持靠三点：自主、胜任感、归属感。让学生爱上练习本身。
- **Group:** ALL, especially PROSPECTS and NEW.

### 13. Identity-based motivation
- **What the research shows:** Daphna Oyserman's identity-based motivation theory holds that people act in ways that fit who they believe they are, and that how they interpret difficulty matters: if a hard task feels "not for someone like me," they quit; if it feels important, they persist. Strong experimental support exists in education (Horowitz et al., 2018); the exercise-specific causal evidence is thinner and mostly correlational.
- **Evidence strength:** Robust theory with strong education evidence; suggestive for exercise.
- **Practical action:** Help students move from "I am trying yoga" to "I am a practitioner." Pre-frame difficulty as a normal sign the practice matters, not proof they do not belong.
- 一句话: 帮学生从"我在尝试瑜伽"转变为"我是一个练习者"。
- **Group:** NEW and EXPERIENCED.

### 14. Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation and the overjustification effect
- **What the research shows:** Rewarding an already-enjoyable behaviour with external incentives can reduce intrinsic motivation (the overjustification effect; Deci, and Lepper, Greene, and Nisbett, 1973). SDT research shows the same pattern.
- **Practical action:** Avoid turning practice into a system of external rewards, badges, or heavy praise for outcomes. Protect the student's own reasons for practicing.
- 一句话: 用外部奖励刺激本来喜欢的事，反而会削弱内在兴趣。
- **Group:** ALL.

### 15. Behaviour change techniques with the strongest evidence
- **What the research shows:** Michie et al. developed a behaviour change technique taxonomy. For physical activity, the techniques with better evidence include self-monitoring of behaviour, goal setting, action planning, and feedback on behaviour (Michie et al., 2009, on self-regulation techniques). Self-monitoring is consistently among the strongest.
- **Practical action:** Encourage simple self-monitoring (a practice log or calendar), specific goals, and action plans. These are cheap and evidence-backed.
- 一句话: 有证据支持的方法：记录练习、设定目标、制定计划。
- **Group:** NEW.

## C. Self-Efficacy, Belief, and Expectation (ALL groups)

### 16. Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy
- **What the research shows:** Bandura (1977, 1997) identified four sources of self-efficacy, in order of power: (1) mastery experiences (actually succeeding), by far the strongest; (2) vicarious experience (seeing similar others succeed); (3) verbal persuasion (credible encouragement); (4) physiological and emotional states (how you read your own arousal and fatigue). Physical-activity studies confirm mastery is the dominant predictor (Warner et al., 2011, in older adults).
- **Evidence strength:** Foundational and well-supported.
- **Practical action:** Above all, engineer real success: scale postures so students genuinely achieve them. Use peer modeling with relatable students, not just advanced demos. Encouragement helps but cannot replace felt success. Teach students to read fatigue and shaking as normal, not as failure.
- 一句话: 自信最强的来源是真实的成功。设计让学生真正做到的体式。
- **Group:** ALL.

### 17. Expectancy effects: how framing changes outcomes
- **What the research shows:** Enhanced expectancies improve motor performance. Wulf and colleagues showed that telling learners they are doing well, or setting easier success criteria, improves both performance and learning (Palmer, Chiviacowsky, and Wulf, 2016, on golf putting; Chauvel, Wulf, and Maquestiaux, 2015, showed even a visual illusion of an easier task helped).
- **Practical action:** Frame tasks as achievable and highlight genuine progress. Honest positive framing is a performance tool, not just kindness.
- 一句话: 让学生相信"这个能做到"，表现和学习都会更好。
- **Group:** ALL, especially PROSPECTS and NEW.

### 18. Growth mindset: real but much smaller than the popular version
- **What the research shows:** Carol Dweck's growth mindset (believing ability is malleable) was hugely popular, but the effect is small. Sisk et al. (2018), two meta-analyses (273 studies, 365,915 participants), found mindset explains about 1% of variance in achievement, and interventions had an average effect of d = 0.08, meaningful only for specific subgroups (low-income or academically at-risk students). Li and Bates (2019) found mindset interventions changed beliefs but did not change achievement.
- **Evidence strength:** Large meta-analyses; the popular strong claims failed to replicate.
- **Practical action:** You may encourage an effort-focused view, but do not build the module on growth mindset or promise it transforms outcomes. Be honest that its real effect is small.
- 一句话: "成长型思维"是真的，但效果比流行说法小得多，别夸大。
- **Group:** relevant to ALL, mainly a "do not oversell" warning.

## D. Instruction, Expertise, and the Teacher's Blind Spots (about the TEACHER)

### 19. Cognitive load theory and worked examples
- **What the research shows:** Sweller's cognitive load theory holds that working memory is limited. Novices learn better from worked examples and full guidance than from being made to problem-solve, because problem-solving overloads them.
- **Practical action:** For beginners, demonstrate fully and break postures into small steps. Do not overload them with many cues at once.
- 一句话: 新手记忆容量有限。多示范、分步骤、别一次给太多提示。
- **Group:** NEW.

### 20. The expertise reversal effect
- **What the research shows:** Kalyuga, Chandler, Tuovinen, and Sweller (2001) showed that heavy guidance and worked examples that help novices become useless or even harmful for more advanced learners, because the extra information is now redundant and adds load.
- **Evidence strength:** Well-replicated.
- **Practical action:** Reduce detailed instruction as students advance. What helps a beginner (constant step-by-step) holds back an experienced student. This mirrors the feedback-fading point (section 3).
- 一句话: 对新手有用的详细指导，对老手反而有害。进步后要减少指导。
- **Group:** the boundary between NEW and EXPERIENCED.

### 21. Curse of knowledge and expert blind spot
- **What the research shows:** Hinds (1999, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied) found experts systematically underestimate how hard a task is for novices; novices took about twice as long as experts predicted, and more expertise meant worse predictions. Debiasing techniques did not fix it. Nathan and Petrosino (2003) found teachers with more advanced subject knowledge were more likely to misjudge what is easy or hard for beginners (the expert blind spot).
- **Evidence strength:** Solid experimental findings.
- **Practical action:** Assume you are underestimating beginner difficulty by roughly a factor of two. Do not trust your own sense of "this is easy." Ask beginners directly and watch them. This is one of the most important warnings for a skilled teacher training other teachers.
- 一句话: 老师会严重低估新手的难度（约两倍）。别相信"这很简单"的直觉，多观察多问。
- **Group:** about the TEACHER, applied to NEW students.

### 22. Desirable difficulties, retrieval, and the testing effect
- **What the research shows:** Robert Bjork's "desirable difficulties" principle: conditions that make practice feel harder and slower (spacing, interleaving, retrieval, reduced feedback) often produce better long-term learning. Retrieval practice and the testing effect are strongly supported for memory and apply to motor skills through spaced and varied practice.
- **Practical action:** Do not equate a smooth, easy class with good learning. Recall-based practice (having students perform a sequence from memory rather than always following) strengthens learning. Some struggle is productive.
- 一句话: 感觉困难、缓慢的练习，往往长期效果更好。让学生凭记忆做序列。
- **Group:** NEW (mildly) and EXPERIENCED.

### 23. Deliberate practice: real but overstated
- **What the research shows:** Ericsson, Krampe, and Tesch-Römer (1993) introduced deliberate practice (structured, effortful, feedback-rich practice). It was popularized by Gladwell as the "10,000 hour rule," which Ericsson himself rejected. Macnamara, Hambrick, and Oswald (2014) meta-analysis found deliberate practice explains only part of performance: 26% of variance for games, 21% for music, 18% for sports, 4% for education, under 1% for professions. Ericsson disputes the method and argues the figure is higher for strictly defined deliberate practice (up to about 29 to 61%), but even his high-end estimate leaves much variance unexplained.
- **Evidence strength:** Meta-analytic; genuine but bounded, and the exact size is disputed.
- **Practical action:** Practice quality matters more than raw hours, but be honest that practice alone does not determine outcomes. Individual differences (body, starting age, life circumstances) matter a great deal. Do not promise mastery purely as a function of hours.
- 一句话: 刻意练习重要，但只能解释运动表现的约18%。练习质量比时长更重要，但练习不能决定一切。
- **Group:** EXPERIENCED, and a warning for the whole module.

### 24. The learning styles myth (debunk explicitly)
- **What the research shows:** Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, and Bjork (2008) reviewed the evidence for "learning styles" (matching teaching to visual/auditory/kinesthetic preference, the meshing hypothesis) and found almost no properly designed studies support it, and several contradict it. Conclusion: "there is no adequate evidence base to justify incorporating learning-styles assessments into general educational practice." Later reviews agree.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong consensus against.
- **Practical action:** Do not sort students into learning styles or tailor teaching to them. Instead, use multiple representations (show, tell, physically guide) for everyone because the content demands it, not because of a student's "type."
- 一句话: "学习风格"（视觉型、听觉型）是没有科学依据的错误概念，不要用它给学生分类。
- **Group:** a warning for the whole module.

## E. Relationship, Retention, and Talking to Prospects (mostly PROSPECTS)

### 25. The working alliance predicts outcomes
- **What the research shows:** In psychotherapy, the quality of the relationship (the working alliance: agreement on goals, agreement on tasks, and the personal bond) is one of the most consistent predictors of outcome across all methods. Flückiger, Del Re, Wampold, and Horvath (2018) meta-analysis of 295 studies and over 30,000 patients found a correlation of r = 0.28 (d = 0.58) between alliance and outcome. Wampold's common-factors work argues the relationship often matters more than the specific technique.
- **Evidence strength:** Very large meta-analysis.
- **Practical action:** Invest in the relationship and in shared agreement about goals and methods. For a shala, the teacher-student bond is not secondary to technique; it is a primary driver of whether students improve and stay.
- 一句话: 师生关系是学习效果最强的预测因素之一。关系不是次要的，而是核心。
- **Group:** ALL, foundational for PROSPECTS and NEW.

### 26. Motivational interviewing beats persuasion and advice
- **What the research shows:** Miller and Rollnick's motivational interviewing (MI) is a guiding, non-confrontational style that helps people resolve their own ambivalence rather than being told what to do. Rubak et al. (2005) meta-analysis of 72 randomized trials concluded MI "outperforms traditional advice giving in the treatment of a broad range of behavioural problems and diseases." MI works by drawing out the person's own reasons for change.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong meta-analytic base; this is the key evidence-based alternative to sales tactics.
- **Practical action:** When talking to prospects, do not pitch or pressure. Ask open questions, listen, reflect back their own reasons, and let them talk themselves toward the decision. Ask permission before giving advice.
- 一句话: 对潜在学员，用"动机式访谈"：提问、倾听、反映他们自己的理由，而不是推销。
- **Group:** PROSPECTS.

### 27. Reactance: pushing people triggers resistance
- **What the research shows:** Psychological reactance theory (Brehm, 1966) holds that when people feel their freedom to choose is threatened, they push back and value the threatened option less. Pressuring a prospect toward a decision can produce the opposite of the intended effect. MI is built partly around avoiding this.
- **Practical action:** Preserve the prospect's sense of choice. Say "it is completely your decision" and mean it. Pressure tactics backfire.
- 一句话: 越推越抗拒。保护对方的选择自由。
- **Group:** PROSPECTS.

### 28. Goal framing: approach and process goals sustain effort
- **What the research shows:** Elliot's work on approach vs avoidance goals shows approach goals (moving toward success) support persistence and positive affect, while avoidance goals (avoiding looking bad) breed anxiety and worse outcomes. Meta-analyses in sport (Lochbaum and colleagues, e.g., Lochbaum and Gottardy, 2015) confirm the pattern, with mostly small-to-medium effects. Process goals (focus on executing the movement) generally support performance and lower anxiety better than pure outcome goals (Jackman et al., 2022).
- **Practical action:** Frame goals as approach and process: "focus on steady breathing through this sequence" rather than "do not fall" or "achieve the full posture." Help students set process goals, not just outcome goals.
- 一句话: 目标要"趋向"和"过程"导向（如"专注呼吸"），而不是"回避"或只看结果（如"别摔倒"）。
- **Group:** ALL, especially NEW and EXPERIENCED.

## F. Chinese Learners Specifically

### 29. The paradox of the Chinese learner
- **What the research shows:** Watkins and Biggs (1996, 2001) documented that Chinese learners, often assumed to rely on rote memorization, actually frequently combine memorization with understanding and show deep approaches to learning. Marton, Dall'Alba, and Tse (1996) found Chinese learners see memorizing and understanding as linked, not opposite: repetition is a route to understanding. Confucian-heritage culture highly values education and treats effort as more important than innate ability.
- **Practical action:** Do not dismiss repetition and memorization of the sequence as shallow. For these students, disciplined repetition can be a path to depth and fits both Ashtanga and their learning tradition. Frame progress as a matter of effort, which aligns with their existing attributions.
- 一句话: 中国学生把"记忆"和"理解"看作相连。重复练习序列，对他们是通向理解的路径。
- **Group:** ALL Chinese students.

### 30. Face (mianzi) and public correction
- **What the research shows:** Mianzi (面子), social dignity and reputation, strongly shapes behaviour. Research on Chinese classroom silence finds students often stay silent, avoid asking questions, and avoid admitting difficulty to protect face, especially in front of peers. Asking a "bad" question or being corrected publicly can cause loss of face.
- **Practical action:** Correct privately and gently where possible. Do not single students out for public correction in ways that expose incompetence. Create low-risk ways to ask questions (private moments, the one-on-one Mysore format, which suits this well). Normalize difficulty for the whole group so no individual loses face.
- 一句话: 面子很重要。尽量私下纠正，不要当众让学生难堪。Mysore一对一的方式很适合。
- **Group:** ALL Chinese students, critical for NEW.

### 31. Collectivism and group dynamics
- **What the research shows:** Collectivist cultural patterns place weight on group harmony and interdependence. Public individual correction or singling out can disrupt harmony and cause discomfort beyond the individual.
- **Practical action:** Frame corrections and encouragement in ways that respect the group. Group-level cues and shared norms may land better than individual spotlighting.
- 一句话: 重视集体和谐。集体层面的引导，往往比单独点名更有效。
- **Group:** ALL Chinese students.

### 32. Does autonomy support (SDT) work in China? The cross-cultural caveat
- **What the research shows:** SDT claims autonomy is a universal need, but this has been challenged cross-culturally. Some researchers (drawing on Markus and Kitayama; Iyengar and Lepper) argue autonomy matters less in collectivist cultures, and some studies found autonomy a weak predictor for Chinese students. However, SDT theorists distinguish autonomy (acting with a sense of volition) from independence (acting alone). Nalipay, King and Cai (2020, Journal of Adolescence), using PISA data on 92,325 students across five Western and five Eastern societies, found "relatedness and autonomy support were equally important for student achievement in both Western and Eastern cultures, whereas competence support was found to be more important to students in the West than in the East." A key nuance: choices made by trusted in-group members (a respected teacher) can be motivating for Chinese learners, whereas purely individual choice may matter less.
- **Practical action:** Autonomy support still works, but frame it correctly. Autonomy here means the student endorses and owns the practice, not that they practice in isolation or reject the teacher's authority. A respected teacher can guide choices, and this enhances rather than undermines motivation. Emphasize competence (real success) heavily.
- 一句话: 自主支持在中国依然有效，但"自主"是指学生认同练习，而不是脱离老师。可信任的老师引导选择，同样能激发动力。
- **Group:** ALL Chinese students.

## G. What Is Weak or Wrong (say this plainly in the module)

> [!warning] Drop these from the module — popular but unsupported
> Learning styles · the strong version of growth mindset · "21 days to a habit" · the fixed "10,000-hour rule" · left-brain/right-brain learner types · "more feedback is always better" · "practice alone determines success."

| Popular claim | Reality | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Learning styles (visual/auditory/kinesthetic) improve learning if matched | No credible evidence; several studies contradict it | Pashler et al., 2008 |
| Growth mindset transforms achievement | Real but tiny effect (d ≈ 0.08; ~1% of variance) | Sisk et al., 2018; Li and Bates, 2019 |
| 21 days to form a habit | Median is about 66 days, range 18 to 254; missing a day is fine | Lally et al., 2010 |
| 10,000 hours makes an expert | Ericsson rejected it; practice explains only ~18% of variance in sport | Macnamara et al., 2014 |
| Left brain vs right brain learners | No evidence people are globally left- or right-brained | Nielsen et al., 2013 (1,011 brain scans, Univ. of Utah) |
| More feedback is always better | Over a third of feedback interventions make performance worse | Kluger and DeNisi, 1996 |
| Practice alone determines success | Individual differences (body, starting age, circumstances) explain most variance | Macnamara et al., 2014 |

一句话总结: 学习风格、强版成长型思维、21天习惯、一万小时定律、左右脑分型，这些流行说法都缺乏科学依据。

## Recommendations (staged and concrete)

### For PROSPECTS (not yet committed)
1. Do not sell. Use motivational interviewing: ask what draws them to practice, listen, reflect their own reasons back. Ask permission before advising. (Rubak et al., 2005)
2. Protect their sense of choice explicitly to avoid reactance. (Brehm, 1966)
3. Begin the relationship now; the alliance predicts outcomes. (Flückiger et al., 2018)
4. If they mention weight loss as the only motive, gently broaden the conversation, because that motive predicts early dropout. (Sperandei et al., 2016)
- **Threshold to change approach:** if a prospect voices clear ambivalence, stay in MI mode longer; do not move to logistics or commitment until they voice their own reasons to start.

### For NEW students (first weeks and months)
1. Treat the first 3 months as a retention emergency. (Sperandei et al., 2016)
2. Engineer frequent, genuine success to build self-efficacy. (Bandura, 1997; Warner et al., 2011)
3. Keep practice fairly blocked and repetitive; do not overload with variation or too many cues. (Sweller cognitive load; contextual interference caveat for novices)
4. Give more feedback than for experts, but never constant real-time feedback. (Salmoni et al., 1984)
5. Cue externally ("press the floor away"). (Wulf)
6. Set expectations: about two to three months to feel automatic, and one missed day is fine. (Lally et al., 2010)
7. Build an if-then plan tying practice to a daily cue, time, and place. (Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006)
8. Encourage simple self-monitoring (a practice log). (Michie et al.)
9. For Chinese students: correct privately, protect face, normalize difficulty across the group. (mianzi research)
- **Threshold to change approach:** once a student practices consistently for roughly two months and postures look automatic, begin fading feedback and adding variation.

### For EXPERIENCED students (years of practice)
1. Fade your corrections sharply; over-instruction now harms them. (Expertise reversal: Kalyuga et al., 2001; guidance hypothesis)
2. Introduce variation and desirable difficulties (recall sequences from memory, vary conditions). (Bjork; contextual interference)
3. Under pressure or on hard postures, steer them to external focus or breath, not inward self-monitoring. (Beilock and Carr, 2001)
4. Support autonomy and let them own their practice. (OPTIMAL theory; SDT)
5. Reinforce identity ("you are a practitioner") and reframe plateaus as normal. (Oyserman)
- **Threshold to change approach:** if an experienced student starts choking or over-controlling under observation, that is the cue to shift them from internal to external focus.

### For the TEACHER and the module itself
1. Assume you underestimate beginner difficulty by about two times; verify by watching and asking, not by intuition. (Hinds, 1999)
2. Keep all feedback task-focused, not person-focused. (Kluger and DeNisi, 1996)
3. Build the relationship and shared goals deliberately. (Flückiger et al., 2018)
4. Explicitly teach the debunked myths so new teachers do not spread them.
5. Be honest about evidence quality: distinguish meta-analyses from single studies when teaching.

## Caveats
- Effect sizes vary by task and population. Motor-learning findings come largely from lab tasks (putting, timing, positioning); the transfer to a full Ashtanga practice is reasonable but not directly measured.
- Some contextual interference and spacing effects are weaker or absent for complex motor skills and for novices; the recommendations reflect this by keeping novice practice more blocked (Wiseheart et al., 2017; Czyż et al., 2024).
- SDT's autonomy claim is genuinely contested cross-culturally; the recommendation reflects the nuanced position, not a blanket claim.
- OPTIMAL theory's "all three factors add up" claim rests on a small number of studies; the individual components are much better established.
- Identity-based motivation's causal evidence in exercise specifically is thin; treat as promising theory.
- The deliberate-practice variance figures are disputed by Ericsson; the honest summary is that practice matters but does not determine outcomes.
- Dropout percentages depend heavily on how dropout is measured (payment vs attendance); the "less than 4% at 12 months" figure is the high end from payment data (Sperandei et al., 2016), while attendance-based studies show around 50% (Clavel San Emeterio et al., 2019).

## Part 5. AYBKK Weekly Letter Playbook

This is Halbert's system bent to fit your shala.

### Read this first: 3 weak spots

1. **Halbert's readers paid for his letter. Yours read it for free.** So the letter itself earns nothing. The P.S. does the earning. An issue with no P.S. pointing at one offer is a diary entry.
2. **Three languages every week is a trap.** That's three letters, three checks and three chances for a mistake, every week, forever. That's hustle, not a system. The fix is in his own [Boron 9](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/TChapter9.htm): write one core letter, then change only about a third of it for each language. One native speaker per language reads it out loud before it goes out.
3. **I don't know the size of your list.** Halbert's #1 rule is the crowd. Before issue 1, count how many Thai, Chinese and Russian students you can actually reach, and on which app. That number decides how much effort this deserves.

**Hormozi check (value for the reader).** Their dream: steady progress, and feeling close to a real lineage. Their doubt: "I'm too stiff, too old, too busy." The delay: they want to feel progress this week, not someday. The effort: so make it a 3-minute read with one tiny task. Short. One idea. One task.

**Munger check (what kills it).**
- Skipping weeks. Trust dies quietly.
- Selling every week. People mute you.
- A clumsy Chinese translation. It looks cheap to the exact people you want paying 180K.
- One health promise. That's a real risk.

The protection: always keep 4 issues written ahead, put at most one offer in each issue, and have a native speaker check every language, every time.

---

### What the letter is for (3 jobs, in order)

1. **Keep students on the mat.** That's shala revenue.
2. **Warm up the next teacher training cohort.** That's the 180K engine.
3. **Fill your graduates' workshops in China.**

**How to score it:** count replies, bookings from the P.S. link, and "TT" replies. Don't count opens. Halbert judged a page by what share of readers *acted*, not how many visited. ([Most Valuable Skill](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/newsletters/zjll_most_valuable_skill.htm))

### The shape (the same every week)

```
Subject: [one line from the subject bank in Part 2]

[Day], [time]. [Place].

Dear [first name],

[Hook: 1 to 3 lines]

[Story: one real moment, 100 to 200 words]

[Lesson: one sentence, on its own line]

This week, try this: [one tiny task]

[Sign-off]
Boonchu

P.S. [one offer, one link]
P.P.S. [tease next week]
```

300 to 500 words. A 3-minute read.

### The 4-week loop

| Week | Body shape | Hook | P.S. sells |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Boron letter (a story) | Confession or dateline | Mysore membership |
| 2 | Fact, bridge, lesson | Surprising fact | A workshop (China or Bangkok) |
| 3 | Student hot seat, or a graduate writes the issue | A question | TT early list ("reply TT") |
| 4 | Fodder box, or story then numbered rules | One number, or a list | Bring a friend |

**When TT opens:** switch to the sales letter shape (Body H in Part 2) for 2 or 3 issues, then go back to the loop.

**The TT P.S. is never a hard sell.** It's only: *Reply "TT" and you'll hear first.* The list gets early access before the public. ([Big Announcement](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/newsletters/2009/BigAnnouncement/BigAnnouncement.html))

### One core, three versions

From [Boron 9](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/TChapter9.htm): keep the core, change about a third.

**Keep the same:** the story, the lesson, the task.

**Change for each language:**
- The dateline and the examples (a Bangkok morning, a Chengdu workshop, a Moscow student)
- The greeting and how warm it sounds
- The P.S. offer (Thai: the Bangkok shala. Chinese: the China workshop or graduate host. Russian: [your Russia offer])
- The app. Thai on LINE (@aybkk). Chinese on WeChat. Russian on [Telegram? **please confirm**]. Whatever app they actually read is your envelope.

**Guesses to test, not facts:** Chinese readers may care most about lineage and proof. Russian readers may want depth and discipline. Thai readers may want warmth and belonging. Let the replies tell you who's right.

### The weekly routine (about 30 minutes of your time)

This is the system, so it doesn't depend on you feeling like writing.

| Day | What happens | Your time |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Pick one story prompt below. Record 5 minutes on your phone. | 5 min |
| Tue | I turn the recording into the English core, in your words, on Halbert's bones. | 0 |
| Wed | Native readers check the Thai, Chinese and Russian versions out loud. | 0 |
| Thu | You read, fix, approve. Then it goes out. | 15 to 20 min |
| Always | Keep 4 issues ready ahead. | |

This is Halbert's [By The Numbers](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/newsletters/zjkj_by_the_numbers.htm) method: talk first, trim later. Nothing goes to students without your OK.

### Story prompts (record, don't write)

1. The morning you almost didn't practice. What got you on the mat?
2. Your first morning in the shala in Mysore. What did you notice first?
3. A student who surprised you. What changed for them?
4. The worst class you ever taught. What did you learn?
5. One thing your teacher told you that you still think about.
6. An asana you disliked for years. What changed?
7. A mistake from your first 5 years of teaching.
8. What you say to a student who tells you "I'm too stiff."
9. The hardest week of your teaching life.
10. A travel day that went wrong.
11. What moon days mean to you.
12. Something a graduate in China did that made you proud.
13. Why you still practice at the time you do.
14. Your practice now vs 10 years ago.
15. Something a beginner taught you.
16. A question students ask that most teachers answer wrong.
17. What you do on a day your body says no.
18. What "authorized" means, and what it doesn't.
19. A small thing you noticed in the room this week.
20. Why you teach Mysore style and not only led classes.

### P.S. bank

**Shala**
- *P.S. Mysore runs [days] at [time]. Book here: [link].*
- *P.S. If you haven't come in a while, come back this week. No need to explain. Just come. [link]*

**Workshop**
- *P.S. I'll be in [city] on [dates], hosted by [graduate]. [N] places. Readers of this letter can book first until [date]. [link]*

**Teacher training**
- *P.S. The next teacher training opens in [month]. 10 to 12 places. Reply "TT" and you'll hear before anyone else.*

**Bring a friend**
- *P.S. Know someone who keeps saying they'll "start yoga next month"? Forward this to them. That's the whole favor.*

**Reply (this builds your list data)**
- *P.S. Reply with one word: the asana you're stuck on. I'll answer the most common one next week.*
- *P.S. Reply with your city. I'm planning next year's trips.*

**P.P.S. teases**
- *P.P.S. Next week: the one thing I watch in every new student. It isn't their hamstrings.*
- *P.P.S. Next week: the answer to this week's guess.*
- *P.P.S. Yes, I hit snooze this morning. Once.*

### Before you send: checklist

1. One idea only?
2. One real detail (a day, a time, a number)?
3. Read out loud. Nothing trips?
4. A native speaker read each version out loud?
5. Short words, short lines?
6. "Asana," never "posture."
7. No health promise anywhere?
8. One P.S. offer, one link. Did you test the link?
9. A P.P.S. that teases next week?
10. A test sent to your own phone in each app?
11. Hungry, angry, lonely or tired? Then wait until tomorrow. (HALT, [Boron 20](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Newletter-archives-protected/Boron/TChapter20.htm))

## Part 6. Issues 1 to 3, Drafts

**How to read these drafts**

- English is the core. The notes at the end of each issue say what to change for Thai, Chinese and Russian.

### Issue 1: the first letter of a series

Built on: [Boron 1](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/TChapter1.htm)

**Subject:** Why I'm going to write to you every week

`dateline` [Thursday], [9:12 am]. The shala, right after Mysore.

Dear [first name],

`what this is` This is the first of many letters. Every week I'll write down one thing that 16 years of teaching and about 20 years of practice have taught me.

`what's inside` Practice, breath, rest, getting up early, being stuck. And once in a while, the strange things students say in the room. One letter a week. Three minutes to read.

`lower the bar` These won't be polished. I'd rather write to you every week than write one perfect letter a year.

`today's small story` Anyway, let's start. This morning [one real moment from the room, with one exact number. Example of the shape: "there were 23 mats down by 6:15, and the one by the window belongs to a woman who has come every Monday for 4 years"]. ([one small honest correction])

`before and after` You know what? [N] years ago, I couldn't [X]. Now [Y].

`bridge to the one habit` That brings me to the one habit I'd give you if I could give you only one:

**Practice at the same time every morning.**

`the steps, then "That's it."` Alarm. Water. Mat. First breath. That's it.

You don't need more motivation. You need fewer decisions. When the time is fixed, there's nothing left to decide.

`kill the scary word` By the way, "every morning" doesn't mean the full series every morning. On a bad day, five sun salutations count. The time is the habit. The length can change.

`why I keep going on` If it sounds like I'm making a big deal out of a small thing, I am. In 16 years I haven't found anything that matters more.

`the ask` **This week, try this:** tomorrow, practice at the same time you practiced today. Then do it again the day after.

See you on the mat,
Boonchu

P.S. One small favor. Reply with two things: your city, and how many years you've practiced. One line is enough.

P.P.S. Next week: the question almost every new student asks me. Most teachers answer it wrong.

**Language notes**
- All versions: only promise "I read every reply" if it's true. If someone else sorts them first, say "Your replies come to me."
- Chinese: think about adding one line after "what this is": *I was authorized to teach by Sharath Jois in Mysore in 2012.* Test whether lineage proof lifts replies.
- Thai: make the dateline and the morning moment very local (the street, the weather, the sound).
- Russian: [your notes].

### Issue 2: the short flip

Built on: [Does Size Really Matter?](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/newsletters/does_size_matter.htm) (shape only)

**Subject:** Does flexibility really matter? (The real answer)

`dateline` [Day], [time]. [Place].

Dear [first name],

`last week I said` Last week I promised you the question almost every new student asks me. Here it is:

"Am I flexible enough for Ashtanga?"

`yes... and no` The answer is yes... and no.

`scene where it doesn't matter` Let me explain. You're at work. You're carrying shopping bags up the stairs. You're on the floor playing with your kids. Does it matter if you can put your foot behind your head? Not at all. (It would make the meetings more interesting, though.)

`scene where it seems to matter` Now you're on the mat. Day one. Next to you is someone who folds in half like a towel. Now it matters, right?

It feels like it does. But not the way you think.

`the memory` [80 to 150 words. Your own stiff early months, or a stiff student who came anyway. Change the name and ask first. What they said on day one. What you saw six months later. One exact detail.]

`"Why? It's simple."` Why does this happen? It's simple. [Your real answer from 16 years. If it matches what you see, something like: the flexible student can skip the hard work. The stiff student can't. So the stiff student learns to breathe, to wait, and to come back. Those three things are the practice.]

`can't change A, can change B` The truth is, you can't choose the body you walk in with. You can choose the time you set your alarm.

And that's the one that counts.

`the task` **This week, try this:** count how many mornings you practice. Write the number down. Next week, add one.

See you on the mat,
Boonchu

P.S. Know someone who says they're "too stiff for yoga"? Forward this to them. Beginners start [day], [time]. [link]

P.P.S. Next week: why we don't practice on full moon days, and what that has to do with you.

**Language notes**
- The "meetings" joke may not land in every language. Swap in a local everyday scene if it doesn't.

### Issue 3: fact, then bridge

Built on: [Interesting Facts](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/newsletters/ac_interesting_facts.htm) and [Boron 2](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/TChapter2.htm)

**Subject:** Why we rest on moon days

`dateline` [Day], [time]. [Place].

Dear [first name],

`the fact` In the Mysore tradition, we don't practice on full moon and new moon days.

`the bridge` That's not as strange as it sounds. You see, rest isn't a break from the method. Rest is written into the method. [One line on why, the way your teacher explained it to you.]

`most people... but` Most students treat rest like a reward they haven't earned yet. So they skip it. Or they rest badly: up late the night before, then guilty all day.

`I do it myself` I take moon days off too. [What you actually do on a moon day.]

`the matched pair` Practice sets the order of your day. Rest sets the order of your week.

`watch the people around you` Watch the students who've been in the room the longest. How often do they skip a moon day? Almost never. How often do they rush? Almost never.

`the task` **This week, try this:** look up the next moon day and put it in your calendar now. That day, don't practice. The next morning, notice one thing that feels different. Reply and tell me what it was.

See you on the mat,
Boonchu

P.S. I'll be in [city] on [dates], hosted by [graduate]. [N] places. Readers of this letter can book first until [date]. [link]

P.P.S. Next week: 12 things I noticed this month. One involves a [gecko]. Don't ask.

**Language notes**
- Chinese P.S.: the workshop. Thai P.S.: swap in the Bangkok Mysore booking. Russian P.S.: [your Russia offer].
- Check the moon day dates for each country's time zone before sending.
